# 7-OH legal status note for the "naturally occurring 7-OH" opinion piece

Checked: 2026-11-24

## Where it stands today

- The DEA notice at 91 FR 40917 (2026-07-06) is no longer the operative document. DEA withdrew it on 2026-10-14 and replaced it with a new notice of intent at 91 FR 65370 that uses a different test: more than 1 mg per gram (solids) or 1 mg per milliliter (liquids), or a mitragynine-to-7-OH ratio below 100. ([Federal Register API record, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-20943.json?fields[]=citation&fields[]=publication_date&fields[]=dates&fields[]=docket_ids&fields[]=abstract))
- The notice says the temporary order would be published "on or after November 13, 2026." As of 2026-11-24 no such order appears in the Federal Register: a search of federalregister.gov for "hydroxymitragynine" and for "kratom" returns nothing published after 2026-10-14, and DEA's latest Federal Register documents in the index are dated 2026-11-19. The Federal Register's public inspection list for the next issues, checked the evening of 2026-11-24, holds no 7-OH or kratom document either. 7-OH above the threshold is therefore not federally scheduled yet. ([Federal Register public inspection, checked 2026-11-24](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/current)) ([Federal Register search, checked 2026-11-24](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions[term]=hydroxymitragynine&conditions[publication_date][gte]=2026-10-15&order=newest))
- The AKA suit (D.D.C. 1:26-cv-02997) is about the separate MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 order, not 7-OH as such. It was stayed through 2026-11-13, and the latest confirmed docket entry is a joint status report of 2026-11-13; no order extending the stay was found. It is still open. ([PacerMonitor docket, updated 2026-11-23](https://www.pacermonitor.com/public/case/66470597/AMERICAN_KRATOM_ASSOCIATION_v_UNITED_STATES_DRUG_ENFORCEMENT_ADMINISTRATION_et_al))
- States are ahead of DEA on concentration and ratio tests: New York signed a 2 percent of total alkaloids or 1 mg per serving law on 2026-11-23, and Florida's labeling rule 5KER26-16 took effect 2026-11-23 on top of an emergency rule that already schedules 7-OH.

## DEA temporary scheduling

**Current notice (the one that matters now)**
- 91 FR 65370, "Schedules of Controlled Substances: Temporary Placement of 7-Hydroxymitragynine and Mitragynine Pseudoindoxyl Above a Specified Threshold in Schedule I," published 2026-10-14, Docket No. DEA-1570, 12 pages. It is a notice of intent, not an order. ([Federal Register API record, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-20943.json?fields[]=citation&fields[]=publication_date&fields[]=dates&fields[]=docket_ids&fields[]=abstract))
- It covers 7-OH and MGPI, with isomers, esters, ethers and salts. Control applies when either substance alone, or their combined amount, is above the threshold recommended by HHS in an October 8, 2026 letter. ([Federal Register API record, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-20943.json?fields[]=citation&fields[]=publication_date&fields[]=dates&fields[]=docket_ids&fields[]=abstract))
- Threshold in the notice's terms: "greater than 1 mg per gram" for solid forms or "greater than 1 mg per milliliter" for liquid preparations, OR a ratio test in which the mass of mitragynine divided by the mass of 7-OH (or MGPI, or their combined mass) "is less than 100." ([Federal Register full text, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/14/2026-20943.txt))
- DOJ's release states the same test in plain words: more than 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquid products, or a mitragynine to 7-OH/MGPI ratio "less than 100 to 1." ([DOJ press release, 2026-10-09, updated 2026-10-19](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid))
- Natural leaf: the notice does not carve out leaf by name. It says the substances have "supposedly been found in trace amounts in some botanical kratom products," which is why control is limited to above-threshold levels, and it says individuals should not infer that DEA views below-threshold levels as safe. ([Federal Register full text, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/14/2026-20943.txt))
- Notice also says "neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels." ([Federal Register API record, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-20943.json?fields[]=citation&fields[]=publication_date&fields[]=dates&fields[]=docket_ids&fields[]=abstract))
- Earliest date: "The temporary scheduling order will be published in the Federal Register on or after November 13, 2026." It cannot issue before 30 days after the notice. Once published it takes effect on publication and lasts two years, with a possible one-year extension. ([Federal Register full text, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/14/2026-20943.txt))
- "On or after" is a floor, not a promise. The July notice had a floor of 2026-08-05 and no 7-OH order followed. ([Federal Register full text of July notice, 2026-07-06](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/07/06/2026-13580.txt))
- The notice sets no comment period or hearing-request deadline and says APA notice-and-comment does not apply. ([Federal Register full text, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/14/2026-20943.txt))

**Original July notice (withdrawn)**
- 91 FR 40917 to 40924, document 2026-13580, published 2026-07-06, Docket DEA-1570, a notice of intent. ([Federal Register API record, 2026-07-06](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents/2026-13580.json?fields[]=citation&fields[]=publication_date&fields[]=docket_ids&fields[]=abstract&fields[]=start_page&fields[]=end_page&fields[]=type))
- Its test was different: botanical material covered if it "contains more than 0.050 percentage of 7-hydroxymitragynine on a dry weight basis"; synthetic and processed articles covered above 0.050 percent or above 1.00 mg in the article. The withdrawal notice calls it the "July 7, 2026" notice in the summary, while its footnote says July 6. The Federal Register shows July 6. ([Federal Register full text, 2026-10-14](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/text/2026/10/14/2026-20943.txt))
- The July notice covered 7-OH only. MGPI was handled in a separate notice (2026-13581, published 2026-07-06). ([Federal Register search listing, 2026-07-06](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13581/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-and))

Notes on the table
- The Florida AG rules moved from a 1 percent by total weight test (2025) to two alternative triggers (2026): above 1 mg/g or 1 mg/mL, or under 100 parts mitragynine per part 7-OH. In the AG notice the two categories are joined by "and" because both are added to Schedule I; the Agriculture notice spells out that either one makes a product Schedule I. ([AG emergency rule notice, 2026-08-20](https://flrules.org/Gateway/View_notice.asp?id=31270974); [Agriculture notice 5KER26-16, 2026-11-23](https://flrules.org/Gateway/View_Notice.asp?ID=31650632))

## What the opinion piece can safely say

- The federal test in the October 14 notice is a concentration and ratio test: above 1 mg per gram (solids) or per milliliter (liquids), or mitragynine to 7-OH ratio below 100. That is the notice of intent as published, not a final order.
- Do not write that DEA "has scheduled" or "banned" 7-OH, or that a ban "took effect November 13." The notice said an order would come "on or after" that date, and no order appears in the Federal Register as of 2026-11-24. Re-check the Federal Register on the day of publication.
- Do not use the old July numbers (0.050 percent dry weight, or 1 mg per article) as the current federal test. That notice was withdrawn on 2026-10-14.
- The DEA notice does not use the phrase "naturally occurring" as a legal category. It describes 7-OH as "supposedly found in trace amounts in some botanical kratom products" and says it makes no finding that below-threshold levels are safe. That supports the argument that the legal line is a measured number, not a source.
- The AKA suit is about the MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 order, not 7-OH. It is open and not decided. It was stayed through 2026-11-13, and the latest confirmed docket entry is a joint status report filed that day; no order extending the stay was found. Do not call it "stayed" or "resolved" today without a fresh docket check.
- New York's law is signed (2026-11-23) but not yet in effect, and Florida's labeling rule took effect on 2026-11-23 and is temporary (expires 2027-02-21).
