Two New York counties moved this week toward local restrictions on retail kratom sales, according to local reporting, shifting some of the regulatory activity around the plant from the state and federal level down to the county level. In Warren County, officials are weighing a local law that would prohibit smoke and vape shops from selling kratom products, and in Oneida County a local law to ban kratom sales has been introduced.

According to [The Post-Star](https://poststar.com/news/local/government-politics/collection_3fdf8b58-61b0-44a9-94b9-b7b883dacb63.html), Warren County officials are debating an ordinance that would bar the retail sale of kratom products, alongside nitrous oxide, in smoke and vape shops. The paper [reported](https://poststar.com/news/local/government-politics/article_d43178fd-aca6-4e2d-95ea-c5f92e8dfff6.html) that a nonprofit advocate told the county's Health Services Committee that the substances are dangerous, while a county health official drew a distinction between natural kratom leaf and synthetic or highly concentrated products, saying concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) formulations are the ones that concern the department. That distinction tracks the line federal and state regulators have drawn between whole-leaf kratom and products with 7-OH concentrated well above the trace levels found in raw leaf.

The Warren County discussion also touched on kratom pharmacology, according to The Post-Star. The committee's conversation referenced concepts including biased signaling at the opioid receptor and a proposed ceiling effect on respiratory depression, points that kratom advocates frequently cite, while also raising concerns about withdrawal, psychosis, and the variability of synthetic or concentrated 7-OH products. The Post-Star's account described a split between advocates of an outright retail ban and officials who emphasized the difference between natural leaf and high-potency preparations.

In Oneida County, a local law was introduced to ban the sale of kratom, [AOL News reported](https://www.aol.com/news/oneida-county-introduces-local-law-171214646.html). The associated public-health order and the proposed law cite concerns about mitragynine, the main psychoactive alkaloid in kratom, according to the report. The AOL account framed the county's move as local-law and enforcement-oriented activity rather than a state or federal rule, and noted that critics described kratom and 7-OH as public-health and substance-abuse concerns.

Both county efforts are at the proposal stage, and neither report describes a measure that has taken final effect. The local activity comes after New York enacted a statewide framework addressing the category. State lawmakers [passed a ban on synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban] earlier this year, and the county-level proposals in Warren and Oneida would reach further by restricting retail sale of kratom products broadly, beyond 7-OH formulations alone.

The county proposals also surface as the federal posture toward the category is shifting. The Drug Enforcement Administration [announced on July 1 that it will move to temporarily place high-7-OH products and several synthetic analogs in Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], an action federal officials framed as targeting concentrated and synthetic products rather than natural leaf. The Warren County health official's framing, as reported by The Post-Star, echoes that same distinction, even as the Warren and Oneida proposals under discussion would apply to kratom retail sales generally within those counties.
