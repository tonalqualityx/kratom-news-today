Suffolk County's legislature is expected to take a final vote on July 14 on a revised local bill that would ban kratom sold in pill, capsule, and liquid vial form and prohibit 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, while leaving natural loose leaf kratom legal for sale, [according to the New York Post](https://www.aol.com/articles/long-island-county-mulls-bans-225534000.html). The measure targets formulated products of the kind commonly sold in gas stations and smoke shops, including concentrated 7-OH preparations that contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts naturally present in raw kratom leaf. The exemption for loose leaf preserves that distinction in the bill's structure.

The Post reported that the public comment period remains open through July 14 and that a final vote is expected at the legislature's July 14 general meeting. The measure [drew its fourth public hearing on June 23][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], a session also covered by [News 12 Long Island](https://longisland.news12.com/2026/06/23/suffolk-county-legislature-to-hold-public-hearing-today-on-proposed-kratom-ban/1PPJH8O8IGdzyGYQB5rIHD). Deputy Presiding Officer Jim Mazzarella, the bill's sponsor, said "there are so many chemicals, alkaloids, and other substances that we're finding in the market being sold along with kratom," adding that the legislature "has to take a stand," according to the Post. Suffolk County already restricts kratom sales to buyers 21 and older, according to the same report.

The bill, [Introductory Resolution 1279](https://www.scnylegislature.us/DocumentCenter/View/128016/Introductory-Resolution-1279-26-PDF), was amended as of June 18, according to the version posted by the Suffolk County Legislature. Mazzarella, a Republican from Moriches, introduced the measure with co-sponsors Leslie Kennedy, RJ Renna, Chad Lennon, and Jason Richberg, [Long Island Life & Politics reported](https://lilifepolitics.com/news/long-island-news/county-legislature-debates-kratom-ban/) in April. That outlet's account of an April general meeting described more than 50 speakers, including doctors, attorneys, law enforcement officers, parents, and members of the recovery community, with near-unanimous support among speakers for prohibiting 7-OH products and open debate over whether natural kratom leaf should remain legal. The legislature recessed the bill at that meeting to work through enforcement questions, according to the report. The revised version now headed for a vote answers that April debate by drawing the line between formulated products and loose leaf.

The Post reported that the Suffolk measure mirrors state legislation that Governor Kathy Hochul has not yet signed, though the county version goes further by sweeping pill, capsule, and vial forms into its definitions. State lawmakers [passed a ban on synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban] earlier this year, and that bill has been awaiting the governor's signature since. Neighboring Nassau County has already implemented a complete kratom ban, according to the Post, giving Long Island's two counties divergent models if Suffolk's exemption for natural leaf survives the final vote.

Suffolk's vote would extend a run of county-level activity in New York. Warren and Oneida counties [moved toward local restrictions on retail kratom sales][new-york-counties-weigh-local-bans-on-retail-kratom-sales] earlier this month, and in the Warren County discussion a county health official drew the same distinction between natural leaf and concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products that Suffolk's revised bill now writes into its text. The county measures are advancing as the Drug Enforcement Administration [moves to temporarily place high-7-OH products and several synthetic analogs in Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], a federal action framed as targeting concentrated and synthetic preparations rather than natural leaf kratom.
