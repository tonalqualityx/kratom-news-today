California Governor Gavin Newsom said on July 15 that the state has removed more than 6,700 kratom and concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from the shelves of licensed retailers, [according to a statement from the governor's office](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/07/15/california-leads-the-nation-in-cracking-down-on-deadly-kratom-goods-the-feds-are-now-following-our-lead/). The concentrated 7-OH products at issue are distinct from natural kratom leaf, which contains the alkaloid only in trace amounts. The statement said nearly 98 percent of state-licensed alcohol retailers are now in compliance with California's restrictions on kratom and 7-OH product sales.

The governor's office framed the enforcement campaign as a model for the rest of the country, saying the DEA's recent temporary scheduling of concentrated 7-OH compounds amounts to the federal government "following our lead." The statement cited the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) as part of the enforcement rationale, describing the products as linked to "overdose, addiction, and other adverse health outcomes," according to the release. The state's [prior enforcement actions included product seizures and an attorney general lawsuit][california-kratom-enforcement-seizures-ag-lawsuit] targeting retailers and distributors.

The timing of the announcement aligns with broader federal activity. The DEA [filed notices of intent on July 1 to temporarily place concentrated 7-OH and three synthetic analogs in Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], with the orders published in the Federal Register on July 6. Under the proposed thresholds, products containing more than 0.05 percent 7-OH by dry weight, or more than 1 mg per serving, would fall under the scheduling action. Natural kratom leaf with only trace 7-OH would remain outside the scope of the federal order, according to DEA and HHS statements.

HHS and FDA formally commended the DEA's action on July 15, [according to a report from the Presidential Prayer Team](https://www.presidentialprayerteam.org/2026/07/15/hhs-and-fda-respond-to-deas-action-on-kratom-related-products/). The DEA administrator was quoted as saying the action targets "highly concentrated, synthetic 7-OH products, which pose a growing threat to public safety and health." HHS published a Request for Information (RFI) in the Federal Register on July 6 inviting public comment on the scientific basis for the concentration thresholds. The comment period closes July 31.

The American Kratom Association (AKA) responded to the RFI on July 13 with a press release warning consumers and stakeholders "not to let the '7-OH industry' hijack" the comment process, [according to a release distributed via Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/press-releases/american-kratom-association-warns-consumers-hhs-rfi-2026-07-13/). The AKA stressed that the RFI is focused on scientific evidence about the 7-OH concentration threshold and urged submissions of "toxicology, pharmacology, analytical chemistry, product-testing data, and other legitimate scientific research." The group said DEA, HHS, and FDA have all drawn a line between natural kratom leaf and products with chemically elevated or synthesized 7-OH, and that natural leaf products containing 7-OH below the proposed threshold are not covered by the scheduling action.

California's compliance figures suggest that state-level enforcement targeting concentrated 7-OH products at retail is producing measurable results, at least among retailers already operating under state alcohol licenses. The governor's statement did not address compliance among unlicensed retailers, online sellers, or smoke and vape shops operating outside the alcohol licensing framework. The state joins [Oklahoma][oklahoma-warns-retailers-liability-kratom-alkaloid-limits], which warned retailers of liability for products exceeding state alkaloid limits on July 13, in a growing pattern of state-level enforcement ahead of the federal scheduling deadline.
