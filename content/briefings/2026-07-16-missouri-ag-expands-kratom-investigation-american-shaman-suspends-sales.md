Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway announced that her office is expanding a statewide investigation into kratom and concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products to include 10 additional entities across the supply chain, [according to the attorney general's office](https://ago.mo.gov/press-releases/). The concentrated 7-OH products under investigation are distinct from natural kratom leaf, which contains the alkaloid only in trace amounts.

The announcement also disclosed that Shaman Botanicals, LLC, which operates under the brand name American Shaman and was described by the AG's office as the largest distributor of kratom products in Missouri, has agreed to suspend all in-state sales, according to the same release. The AG's office did not specify whether the suspension covers all kratom products or is limited to those containing concentrated 7-OH.

The expansion brings the Missouri investigation deeper into the supply chain, targeting entities beyond the retail level. The AG's office framed the action as part of ongoing enforcement against products that present public health risks, with the investigation focusing on distribution, manufacturing, and retail operations, according to the release. The specific allegations and legal theories underlying the investigation were not detailed in the announcement.

Missouri's enforcement posture adds the state to a growing list of jurisdictions tightening oversight of kratom products ahead of the DEA's expected August 5 federal scheduling deadline. The DEA [filed notices of intent on July 1 to temporarily place concentrated 7-OH and three synthetic analogs in Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], targeting products above a 0.05 percent 7-OH concentration threshold (or more than 1 mg per serving). [Oklahoma warned retailers of liability for products exceeding state alkaloid limits on July 13][oklahoma-warns-retailers-liability-kratom-alkaloid-limits], and [California reported on July 15 that it has removed more than 6,700 kratom and 7-OH products from licensed retail shelves][california-claims-enforcement-lead-7oh-crackdown].

The voluntary suspension of sales by American Shaman is a notable business development. The company operates a franchise model with retail locations across multiple states. A voluntary sales halt by the state's largest distributor, before the federal scheduling order takes effect, signals that at least some operators are choosing to exit markets where attorney general scrutiny has intensified rather than contest enforcement.
