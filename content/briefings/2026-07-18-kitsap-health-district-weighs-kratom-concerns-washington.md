The board of the Kitsap Public Health District, which serves Kitsap County in western Washington, discussed safety concerns around kratom at its July 7 meeting, [according to a July 15 report in the Kitsap Daily News](https://www.kitsapdailynews.com/2026/07/15/kphd-board-discusses-safety-concerns-around-kratom/). District health officer Dr. Herbie Duber briefed the board on the plant, its alkaloids, and the rise of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts that occur naturally in kratom leaf.

Duber cited national poison center data showing kratom-related exposure reports increased roughly 1,200 percent over the past decade, from 258 cases in 2015 to 3,434 in 2025, a record level, the Kitsap Daily News reported. Serious medical outcomes, including hospitalizations, have risen alongside the call volume, and Duber pointed to deaths involving concentrated 7-OH products specifically, according to the report.

## What the board heard

Duber described kratom as a tropical Southeast Asian tree with stimulant effects at some doses and sedative effects at others, and walked the board through its two most-discussed alkaloids: mitragynine, which is dominant in the leaf but less potent, and 7-OH, which is more potent but present at low concentrations in natural leaf, the paper reported.

The health officer drew a line between the leaf and the concentrated products now sold at retail. Concentrated 7-OH products are available as pills, gummies, tonics, and vapes in convenience stores, and they contain the alkaloid at levels far exceeding natural leaf content, Duber told the board, according to the Kitsap Daily News. He said these products act on opioid receptors and can cause respiratory depression that is reversible with naloxone, per the report.

## Washington's regulatory gap

On the legal landscape, Duber told the board that kratom is not controlled under the federal Controlled Substances Act and that the FDA has not approved it for any medical use, the paper reported. Thirty states plus Washington, D.C. have some form of kratom regulation, he said, and Washington state is not among them.

Some Washington jurisdictions have moved on their own. Grant County, in the central part of the state, passed a resolution urging governments to restrict kratom sales, according to the report.

The district does not yet have data on kratom use within Kitsap County, the paper reported. The board's policy committee plans to discuss kratom further at its September 29 meeting.

## Federal backdrop

The Kitsap discussion comes as the federal picture is shifting for one category of products the briefing singled out. The DEA has moved to temporarily place concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products in Schedule I, with the order able to take effect [as early as August 5][dea-7oh-order-august-timeline-patient-pushback]. The DEA, the Department of Health and Human Services, and the FDA have said the action targets high-concentration and synthetic 7-OH preparations rather than natural kratom leaf, which contains only trace amounts of the alkaloid. Botanical kratom itself remains unscheduled at the federal level, a point the 2024 DEA drug fact sheet cited in the Kitsap briefing also notes, according to the Kitsap Daily News.

The federal action does not cover leaf kratom, leaving decisions about the plant itself to states and localities. In Kitsap County, the next scheduled step is the policy committee's September 29 discussion, according to the Kitsap Daily News.
