The Tisbury Board of Health voted unanimously this week to ban the manufacturing, sale, and distribution of kratom within the town, [according to a July 17 report in The Martha's Vineyard Times](https://www.mvtimes.com/2026/07/17/tisbury-board-of-health-bans-kratom/). The ban covers kratom in all forms and does not differentiate between natural leaf products and the concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations that are the focus of pending federal action, per the report. That makes the local rule broader than the DEA's planned scheduling, which federal officials have said targets high-concentration and synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf, where the alkaloid occurs only in trace amounts.

Tisbury is the second town on Martha's Vineyard to prohibit kratom sales. Edgartown banned the products on June 9, the Times reported.

## What the board decided

"At this time, allowing the regulated sale of kratom in any form is not feasible due to the lack of oversight," assistant health agent Frederick Hehre said, according to the Times. Kratom had previously been for sale at a Tisbury storefront, the paper reported.

The ban applies to local commerce only. Mail-order purchases remain available to residents, and board member Betsy Carnie noted during the hearing that people who use kratom would still be able to obtain it from sources they trust, per the report.

The board indicated it may revisit the regulation depending on future federal guidance, the Times reported.

## Testimony from both sides

The public hearing drew comments from off-island callers on both sides of the question, according to the Times. Dan Gibbs, calling in from Ohio, told the board: "My son believed what many consumers believed, that because kratom was so openly marketed as natural, it was safe." Melody Woolf, a kratom consumer from Michigan, told the board that kratom had restored her mobility, per the report.

## Federal and regional backdrop

The Times report cites the DEA's recent move on 7-OH as context for the town's action. The agency [announced on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] that it intends to temporarily place concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and three related compounds in Schedule I, and the temporary order can be published [as early as August 5][dea-7oh-order-august-timeline-patient-pushback]. The DEA, the Department of Health and Human Services, and the FDA have described the action as aimed at high-concentration and synthetic preparations, with natural kratom leaf remaining unscheduled at the federal level.

Local governments have been drawing that line in different places. Suffolk County, New York, advanced a retail ban this month that [exempts natural loose-leaf products][suffolk-county-kratom-ban-vote-exempts-natural-loose-leaf], and Fountain Valley, California, voted this week to [restrict products above a 7-OH concentration threshold][fountain-valley-restricts-high-7oh-kratom-products]. In Massachusetts, Revere held a hearing on a [proposed local ban][revere-massachusetts-kratom-ban-hearing] in June. Tisbury's regulation sits at the restrictive end of that spectrum, covering all kratom products regardless of form or alkaloid content.
