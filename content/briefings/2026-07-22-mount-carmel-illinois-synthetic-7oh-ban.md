The Mount Carmel City Council moved forward Monday with plans to ban the sale of synthetic kratom products within the southeastern Illinois city, [according to a July 21 report from local radio station WSJD](https://wsjd.fm/new-blog-1/2026/7/21/city-council-moves-forward-with-plans-to-ban-sale-of-kratom-in-mt-carmel). The effort targets products the report describes as "7-O," a reference to concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, along with other synthetic gray-market drugs. Federal regulators have said such products contain 7-OH at levels far above the trace amounts that occur naturally in kratom leaf. The WSJD report does not specify whether an eventual ordinance would also cover natural leaf kratom.

## What the mayor proposed

Mayor Joe Judge told council members he had requested the removal of synthetic kratom products from local stores, saying his responsibilities include protecting the health and safety of residents, per WSJD. Judge said the city will not wait for the Illinois legislature to act, according to the report.

Judge said he directed Police Officer David Houser to hand-deliver letters asking retailers to voluntarily remove the products, WSJD reported. The mayor also said he had consulted officials at Wabash General Hospital, who reported no current increase in cases locally, per the report.

Council members voiced support for the effort, according to WSJD. The station did not report a formal vote.

## Next steps

Judge and City Clerk Ryan Turner will work with the city attorney to draft an ordinance, which could come before the council within two meetings if approved for consideration, WSJD reported. The report does not give an effective date or describe penalties.

## Federal backdrop and local trend

The WSJD report cites pending federal restrictions as context for the city's move, noting that the DEA and the FDA have pursued temporary limits on highly concentrated synthetic kratom compounds because of their opioid-like effects. The DEA [announced on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] that it intends to temporarily place concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and three related compounds in Schedule I, and the temporary order can be published [as early as August 5][dea-7oh-order-august-timeline-patient-pushback]. Federal officials have described the action as aimed at high-concentration and synthetic preparations, with natural kratom leaf remaining unscheduled federally.

Mount Carmel joins a growing list of local governments acting ahead of or alongside the federal timeline, with each drawing the product line differently. Tisbury, Massachusetts, [banned kratom in all forms][tisbury-marthas-vineyard-kratom-ban] this month, while Fountain Valley, California, [restricted products above a 7-OH concentration threshold][fountain-valley-restricts-high-7oh-kratom-products] and Suffolk County, New York, advanced a ban that [exempts natural loose-leaf products][suffolk-county-kratom-ban-vote-exempts-natural-loose-leaf]. Where Mount Carmel's ordinance lands on that spectrum will depend on the text the city attorney produces.
