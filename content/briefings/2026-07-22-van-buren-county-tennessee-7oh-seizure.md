The Van Buren County Sheriff's Office in Tennessee seized 120 units of synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, concentrated preparations distinct from the trace 7-OH found naturally in kratom leaf, during a July 16 search of a convenience store, [according to a July 21 report from WKRC](https://local12.com/news/nation-world/van-buren-county-speed-mart-liquors-search-warrant-538-thc-pre-rolls-26-vapes-120-units-synthetic-7-hydroxymitragynine-70h-cash-bmw-seized-possession-intent-manufacture-sell-deliver-charges-filed-cincinnati-illegal-addictive-drugs-opioid). Charges of possession with intent to manufacture, sell, or deliver have been filed, per the report, which is based on a sheriff's office release that did not name a defendant.

## What was seized

Deputies executed the search warrant just after 2 p.m. on July 16 at Speed Mart Liquors, located at 16972 State Route 111, with assistance from the district attorney's office, WKRC reported. In addition to the 120 units of synthetic 7-OH, the seizure included 538 THC pre-rolls, 26 vape products with THC content the release described as up to 50 percent, $20,307 in cash, and a 2021 BMW X3, per the report.

"This effort takes thousands of dollars of illegal substances out of our community and helps make our community safer," the sheriff's office said in the release quoted by WKRC. Sheriff Michael J. Brock said the office is committed to making Van Buren County "the safest place for all of our citizens and families," per the report.

## The Tennessee context

The WKRC report does not specify which statutes the charges fall under. Tennessee's statewide kratom prohibition, enacted as HB 1649 and known as Matthew Davenport's Law, [took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], and the State Gazette reported earlier this month that the ban [is now in effect across the state][georgia-kratom-study-committee-tennessee-ban-in-effect]. The law gave Tennessee one of the most restrictive state postures in the country ahead of the DEA's pending federal action, which [targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] rather than natural leaf kratom and could take effect as early as August 5.

The seizure adds to a series of recent state enforcement actions against retail sellers of concentrated 7-OH products, including [California's multi-agency enforcement campaign][california-claims-enforcement-lead-7oh-crackdown] reported earlier this month. In the Van Buren County case, the sheriff's release grouped the 7-OH products with unregulated THC items as "illegal substances," per WKRC.
