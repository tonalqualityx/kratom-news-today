Dauphin County Coroner Graham Hetrick issued a public health warning about kratom after the death of an intern in his office who had used kratom, [according to PennLive](https://www.pennlive.com/news/2026/07/dauphin-county-coroner-claims-kratom-is-deadly-after-his-intern-dies.html). The reporting does not specify whether the products involved were concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that materially changes the products' risk profiles: concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural leaf and are the focus of the pending federal scheduling action.

## The coroner's warning

Hetrick described kratom as neither safe, natural, nor beneficial, pushing back on how the products are commonly presented, per PennLive. He listed liver damage, seizures, and dangerous interactions with SSRI antidepressants such as Prozac and Zoloft among his concerns, according to the report.

The coroner called for removal of kratom products from retail stores, clear labeling requirements, and a prohibition on marketing directed at children, PennLive reported.

## Screening and case counts

Hetrick's office has started routine screening for mitragynine, kratom's primary alkaloid, in its toxicology panels, and has identified three kratom-associated cases, two of them in the current year, according to PennLive. Routine screening adds kratom exposure to the substances the office checks in death investigations, where it has not historically been a standard panel item.

## Legal backdrop

Kratom and 7-OH products are currently legal in Pennsylvania, and PennLive notes pending legislation in the state House. The article lists Alabama, Arkansas, Indiana, Louisiana, Vermont, Wisconsin, Connecticut, and Tennessee as states that have banned kratom.

At the federal level, the FDA has called 7-OH products "illegal and dangerous," phrasing that state and local officials have repeated in public warnings, per PennLive, and the DEA [has moved to temporarily place][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products in Schedule I. Families of people who died after using concentrated 7-OH products [described those cases publicly][families-describe-7oh-deaths-addiction-ahead-of-scheduling] as the scheduling date approaches; the Dauphin County reporting, by contrast, does not identify the product type involved in the cases Hetrick cited.

Hetrick's call for retail removal parallels municipal steps elsewhere, including [prohibitions advancing in Massachusetts][hopkinton-kratom-ban-natick-weighs-prohibition].
