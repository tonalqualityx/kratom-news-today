Two more Massachusetts towns are moving against kratom sales. The Hopkinton Board of Health voted unanimously to prohibit the sale of kratom and synthetic THC products, [according to the Hopkinton Independent](https://hopkintonindependent.com/board-of-health-passes-regulations-prohibiting-sale-of-kratom-synthetic-thc/). In Natick, the Board of Health is considering a prohibition on both natural and synthetic kratom, with draft regulations already written and a public hearing scheduled for September 14, [the Natick Report reported](https://www.natickreport.com/2026/07/natick-board-of-health-considering-prohibition-of-natural-and-synthetic-kratom/). The Natick draft singles out high-concentration 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts that occur naturally in kratom leaf, while also covering unaltered plant material.

## Hopkinton's vote

The Hopkinton board's regulation removes the category from local shelves: retailers in town can no longer legally sell kratom or synthetic THC products, the Independent reported. In adopting the prohibition, the board cited FDA warnings that extracted forms of kratom can cause liver toxicity, seizures, and substance use disorder, according to the report. The report does not give an effective date for the regulation.

## Natick's draft regulations

Natick's draft would prohibit the sale of both natural and synthetic kratom, with escalating fines and penalties for sellers, per the Natick Report. The draft defines natural kratom as any unaltered form of Mitragyna speciosa, including whole, crushed, or powdered leaves and stems, and defines synthetic kratom as products altered through chemical synthesis, synthetic alkaloid analogs, or concentrates that exceed naturally occurring levels, particularly high-concentration 7-OH products, according to the report.

Board materials state that kratom "is not regulated by the FDA or the State of Massachusetts," leaving regulation to individual municipalities, the Natick Report reported. The board describes mitragynine and 7-OH, kratom's principal psychoactive alkaloids, as capable of causing psychotic symptoms and dependence with opioid-like features, and notes that the DEA is considering classifying synthetic kratom as a Schedule I substance, per the report. The DEA [announced on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] that it intends to temporarily place concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and three related compounds in Schedule I, an action federal officials have described as targeting high-concentration and synthetic preparations rather than natural leaf.

The public hearing is scheduled for September 14 and will be held via Zoom, according to the Natick Report.

## A widening municipal patchwork

Natick board materials say about 20 Massachusetts communities have already adopted complete kratom prohibitions, while others target synthetic products only, per the Natick Report. Tisbury [banned kratom in all forms][tisbury-marthas-vineyard-kratom-ban] earlier this month, and boards of health in Rockland and Hanover [moved on local bans][white-house-drug-strategy-kratom-massachusetts-local-bans] this spring, a town-by-town pattern that has developed in the absence of a statewide framework.

The municipal activity is not limited to Massachusetts. In Southern California, La Habra is [weighing a ban on kratom and nitrous oxide products][la-habra-weighs-kratom-whippet-ban], citing the pending federal scheduling action. Where each locality draws the line between natural leaf and concentrated or synthetic products continues to vary: Hopkinton's regulation covers kratom broadly, while Natick's draft names both categories explicitly.
