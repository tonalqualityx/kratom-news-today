Top law enforcement leaders in North Carolina sent a letter to House lawmakers on Monday urging passage of House Bill 328, [according to WRAL](https://www.wral.com/news/nccapitol/north-carolina-top-law-enforcement-leaders-are-pressing-lawmakers-to-pass-thc-regulation-bill-july-2026/). The bill's primary focus is regulation of CBD and THC products, but it would also establish regulations for kratom, synthetic kratom, and xylazine, the veterinary sedative known as tranq, while updating the state's Controlled Substances Act, per the report.

## What the bill would do

As described by WRAL, HB 328 would ban the sale or delivery of intoxicating hemp-derived products to anyone under 21, make products containing more than 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container a Schedule VI controlled substance, and create civil penalties for violations. The bill would also align parts of state law with federal hemp regulations scheduled to take effect in November, which limit total THC per container to 0.4 milligrams.

The WRAL report does not quote specific bill text on mitragynine or 7-hydroxymitragynine, the kratom alkaloids that have driven much of this year's regulatory activity. The legislative approach places kratom and synthetic kratom in a dedicated framework alongside synthetic cannabinoids and xylazine, treating them as part of a broader class of emerging psychoactive substances, per the report.

## Opposition from retailers

Hemp retailers and manufacturers strongly oppose the bill, arguing it goes far beyond protecting minors and would constrain their current product lines, WRAL reported. Law enforcement leaders backing the letter emphasized youth protection and control of intoxicating products, while business groups pointed to economic harm from the bill's breadth.

## Kratom keeps landing on state dockets

The North Carolina push reflects a pattern of kratom being bundled into broader legislation on intoxicating products rather than addressed through standalone consumer-protection acts. In New York, Assembly Alcoholism and Drug Abuse Committee Chair Phil Steck advanced a bill banning synthetic kratom that passed the Assembly unanimously and is being sent to the governor, [City & State New York reported](https://www.cityandstateny.com/power-lists/2026/07/2026-upstate-power-100/414939/) in a profile of the legislator. Steck previously sponsored a kratom regulation law signed last year that treats non-synthetic kratom as a regulated herbal supplement, per the profile.

State and local activity has accelerated alongside the federal action against concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine products the DEA [announced July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products]. In Massachusetts, local boards of health [have moved on outright kratom sales bans][hopkinton-kratom-ban-natick-weighs-prohibition] in recent weeks, citing FDA warnings about extracted forms of the plant.
