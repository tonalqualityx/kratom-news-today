A multi-county law-enforcement operation in north Alabama executed 12 search warrants across eight counties and seized 1,528 bottles and containers of kratom products, along with other suspected kratom-related items, [according to 256 Today](https://256today.com/eight-county-kratom-investigation-leads-to-seizure-of-more-than-1500-products-in-north-alabama/). Kratom is banned in Alabama, which the report notes is one of the states where possession, sale, and manufacture of the plant are criminal offenses. Authorities described the investigation as ongoing, with potential additional charges or enforcement actions once evidence analysis is complete, per the report. The report does not specify whether the seized items were natural leaf products or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, a distinction that matters in most states but not under Alabama's blanket prohibition, which covers both.

## Lawrence, Kansas outlaws kratom and 7-OH

The city of Lawrence announced in a July communication that kratom and 7-OH are now illegal within city limits following city action, [according to the city's CityBeat publication](https://lawrenceks.gov/citybeat/jul-2026/kratom-and-7-oh-now-illegal-in-kansas/). The announcement cited an official FDA warning issued in 2025 about the health and addiction risks of 7-OH and framed the local ban as a harm-reduction measure. The city said the change removes kratom and 7-OH products from convenience-store and smoke-shop shelves locally. The Lawrence ordinance names both natural kratom and 7-OH, going further than the pending federal action, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations while leaving natural leaf kratom unscheduled.

## Marblehead board of health debates a ban

The Marblehead, Massachusetts Board of Health debated a proposal to prohibit the sale, manufacture, and distribution of kratom products, explicitly including both natural leaf and synthetically derived products, [The Daily Item reported](https://itemlive.com/2026/07/28/marblehead-board-of-health-debates-kratom-sales/) on July 28. The proposal would group kratom with synthetic cannabinoids such as delta-8 and delta-10 THC under a ban on what the board described as unregulated novel intoxicating products, citing inconsistent labeling, dosage information, and ingredient transparency. Marblehead joins a cluster of Massachusetts towns moving on the category: [Hopkinton banned kratom sales earlier this month and Natick is weighing a prohibition][hopkinton-kratom-ban-natick-weighs-prohibition] with similar natural-and-synthetic scope.

## Poison-center data and the August 5 backdrop

A document posted to the DEA's website on July 27 described dramatic increases in kratom-related poison control center calls in [Missouri from 2024 to 2025](https://www.dea.gov/documents/2026/2026-07/2026-07-27/kratom-and-7-oh-poisonings-increase-missouri), which the document characterized as consistent with a nationwide trend. Local boards of health and city councils have cited rising poison-center figures in recent ban debates.

The enforcement and municipal activity lands days before August 5, the earliest date the DEA can issue its temporary order placing concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products in Schedule I, [Medical Daily reported](https://www.medicaldaily.com/dea-7-oh-kratom-schedule-i-august-5-timeline-476378). That order does not apply to botanical kratom leaf containing naturally occurring 7-OH below the agency's 0.050 percent threshold, according to the notice. [Retailers of concentrated 7-OH products have been discounting inventory ahead of the date][7oh-retailers-discount-inventory-ahead-august-deadline], while jurisdictions like Lawrence and Marblehead are drawing their own lines more broadly than the federal one.
