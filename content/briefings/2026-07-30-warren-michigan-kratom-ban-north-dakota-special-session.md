The Warren City Council approved the first reading of an ordinance that would prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom products within the city, [The Detroit News reported](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/news/local/macomb-county/2026/07/29/warren-city-council-moves-towards-regulating-kratom-amid-safety-concerns/91091700007/) on July 29. The council acted after receiving reports from the city's police department and community members about safety concerns, according to the report. The ordinance requires a second reading before it can become law. If adopted, it would bar convenience stores, gas stations, smoke shops, and specialty retailers in the Macomb County city from selling or distributing kratom products, per The Detroit News.

The local measure advances while a statewide kratom ban bill remains pending in the Michigan Senate, according to the report, which framed Warren's ordinance as a local government moving ahead of stalled state legislation. The Detroit News also noted that the DEA's July 1 move toward temporary scheduling of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products forms part of the backdrop for the local debate.

## North Dakota weighs a special session

In North Dakota, Gov. Kelly Armstrong and Republican legislative leaders are considering a special session that could take up a kratom ban, [according to reporting carried by Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/gov-armstrong-lawmakers-eye-special-003329418.html). A kratom ban is explicitly named as a potential agenda item, alongside an unrelated funding matter, per the report. [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/07/30/1334925/) on July 30 that state leaders discussing the session pointed to the DEA's July 1 announcement of its intent to temporarily place 7-OH and three related synthetic substances into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act.

North Dakota considered banning or regulating kratom during its 2025 regular session but instead passed a bill directing a study of kratom's uses and regulation, according to the Yahoo News report. Legislative leaders ultimately did not select kratom as an interim study topic, the report said, leaving the state without formal study work underway as ban discussions resume.

## Island County asks retailers to pull kratom voluntarily

The Island County, Washington Board of Health backed a voluntary advisory letter asking retailers to remove kratom products from shelves or post warnings about them, the [South Whidbey Record reported](https://www.southwhidbeyrecord.com/2026/07/28/board-of-health-backs-voluntary-kratom-limits/) on July 28. The letter is a public health advisory and voluntary request that creates no new regulations, per the report. The board is also exploring age limits and possible future ordinances, according to the Record, and its discussion referenced the DEA's slated temporary scheduling of synthetic 7-OH and three related substances. The advisory follows [kratom deliberations at Washington's Kitsap Public Health District earlier this month][kitsap-health-district-weighs-kratom-concerns-washington].

## Local lines drawn wider than the federal one

The three actions extend a run of sub-federal activity in the final days before the DEA's temporary scheduling order can issue. [The agency's July 1 notices of intent][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] cover concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products above a 0.050 percent dry-weight threshold, plus three lab-made compounds, and do not reach natural kratom leaf containing trace 7-OH below that level, according to the notices. The DEA has said the order cannot issue before August 5.

The measures in Warren and Island County, and the ban under discussion in North Dakota, apply to kratom products broadly rather than tracking that federal distinction between concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations and natural leaf. The pattern matches earlier activity this week, when [Alabama investigators seized more than 1,500 kratom products and Lawrence, Kansas outlawed both kratom and 7-OH][north-alabama-kratom-seizure-lawrence-kansas-ban], with local jurisdictions drawing lines wider than the pending federal one.
