Utah officials issued a public warning after one death and several severe health incidents, including overdoses, were linked to tablets sold as "Buzzers Cat's Claw" that contained mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15, [FOX 13 News Utah reported](https://www.fox13now.com/news/local-news/death-connected-to-illegally-sold-kratom-product-in-utah). The two compounds are lab-made, opioid-active substances derived from kratom alkaloids, a category distinct from natural kratom leaf and the trace alkaloids it contains, and both are classified as Schedule I drugs under Utah law, according to the report. The tablets were mislabeled and did not disclose the compounds, per the warning.

[KSL reported](https://www.ksl.com/article/51604214/officials-issue-warning-after-mislabeled-kratom-product-linked-to-death-in-utah-county) that the death occurred in Utah County and that officials framed the tablets as illegally sold. Utah officials stressed that all kratom products other than pure leaf varieties are banned in the state under recent legislation, and the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food is working with law enforcement to remove the mislabeled products from store shelves, according to FOX 13. The involvement of the Utah Poison Control Center in the warning framed the episode as a poisoning risk and public health matter rather than solely a regulatory violation, per the report.

## The same compounds are in the DEA's federal action

Both compounds named in the Utah warning appear in the federal scheduling pipeline. Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15 are two of the three synthetic analogs, alongside MGM-16, that the DEA moved on July 1 to [temporarily place into Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] together with concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. That temporary order cannot issue before August 5, and the action does not reach natural kratom leaf below the DEA's concentration thresholds.

The Utah case also fits a pattern regulators have flagged before. [FOX 13 Tampa Bay reported](https://www.fox13news.com/news/lethal-loophole-manufacturers-kratom-compounds-florida-ban) that after [Florida placed concentrated 7-OH into state Schedule I by emergency rule][florida-emergency-rule-7oh-schedule-one], manufacturers allegedly synthesized new kratom-derived compounds, including MGM-15, to sidestep the prohibition. Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier said in that coverage that bad actors will "try to skirt the system" and develop new compounds to keep profiting.

State enforcement against kratom-derived products moved on other fronts this week as well: Virginia's attorney general [launched a Regulated Products Enforcement Unit][virginia-ag-regulated-products-enforcement-unit] covering kratom among other regulated categories.
