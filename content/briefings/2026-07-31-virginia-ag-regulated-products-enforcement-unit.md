Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones announced a new Regulated Products Enforcement Unit on July 30, tasked with policing kratom, vapes, THC and hemp-derived products, and products marketed to children, [WSLS reported](https://www.wsls.com/news/local/2026/07/30/ags-new-regulated-products-enforcement-unit/). The unit will handle civil enforcement, retail compliance, product directories, and public education, according to the attorney general's office. The office said the unit will focus on illegal and unsafe products sold across the Commonwealth.

The enforcement push builds on a 2026 Virginia law that already restricts kratom sales to adults 21 and over, requires labeling and behind-the-counter or locked-display storage, bans synthetic and semisynthetic kratom compounds along with kratom products intended for vaping or injection, and limits 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) content in kratom products, [according to Marijuana Moment](https://www.marijuanamoment.net/virginia-attorney-general-launches-enforcement-effort-on-hemp-thc-products-kratom-and-emerging-intoxicants/). For retailers, the new unit signals that those product standards now come with a dedicated enforcement arm rather than statute-book requirements alone.

## Industry advocates back the enforcement framing

The American Kratom Association is quoted in the Virginia announcement supporting strong enforcement against manufacturers and retailers selling what it called "chemically manipulated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) opioid products" falsely marketed as kratom, per WSLS. The association said highly concentrated 7-OH products are "fundamentally different from traditional natural kratom leaf" and pose distinct safety concerns that justify regulatory action. The AKA's stated regulatory agenda, summarized in the coverage, centers on age limits, testing, labeling, good manufacturing practices, and third-party certificates of analysis, while urging regulators to remove chemically manipulated products from the marketplace.

Prevention groups are pressing in the same direction at the federal level. The Partnership for a Drug-Free New Jersey issued a statement on July 30 supporting the DEA's plan to temporarily schedule concentrated and synthetic kratom-derived opioids, [naming 7-OH and related compounds](https://zeestmedia.com/newsroom/pdfnj-supports-dea-action-on-kratom-derived-opioids/) and framing the products as contributing to opioid-type harms.

## The federal clock behind the state action

The Virginia unit arrives in the final stretch of the federal 7-OH scheduling process. The DEA filed its [notice of intent on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] to temporarily place concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, plus three lab-made analogs, into Schedule I, an action that targets products above defined concentration thresholds and does not reach natural kratom leaf containing trace 7-OH. The public comment window on the related HHS request for information closes July 31, and the temporary order cannot issue before August 5, [as KNT reported earlier this week][7oh-retailers-discount-inventory-ahead-august-deadline] when online sellers were already discounting 7-OH inventory ahead of the deadline.

Separately on the enforcement front, Utah officials this week [linked a death to mislabeled tablets containing synthetic kratom-derived compounds][utah-death-mislabeled-synthetic-kratom-tablets], another instance of state agencies moving against lab-made kratom derivatives while federal scheduling is still pending.
