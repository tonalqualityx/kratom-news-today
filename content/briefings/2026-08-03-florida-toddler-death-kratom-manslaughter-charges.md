Police in Florida arrested two adults on charges of aggravated manslaughter of a child and child neglect causing great bodily harm after a two-year-old died of alleged kratom poisoning, [the New York Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/07/31/us-news/florida-toddlers-tragic-death-sparks-manslaughter-charges-after-alleged-kratom-poisoning/) on July 31. The reporting does not specify whether the product the child ingested was a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that materially changes the products' risk profiles.

Coverage carried by [Local 21 News](https://local21news.com/news/nation-world/jonnie-jordan-jerome-charlene-rea-oconnell-7-hydroxymitragynine-7oh-narrested-after-toddler-dies-kratom-ingestion-mitragynine-intoxication-police-say-adults-failed-seek-medical-care-aggravated-manslaughter-child-neglect-cincinnati-crime-criminal-activity) reported that police said the adults failed to seek medical care for the child, and that the death was tied to kratom ingestion and mitragynine intoxication. Mitragynine itself remains unscheduled under the federal Controlled Substances Act, and the DEA's pending temporary scheduling action targets 7-OH above specified concentration thresholds along with three synthetic compounds, according to the agency's notices of intent.

## Charges land days before the federal 7-OH order can issue

The arrests come as the federal action on concentrated and synthetic 7-OH reaches its final procedural window. The DEA's July 1 notices of intent, [published in the Federal Register on July 6][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule], would place 7-OH above 0.050 percent by dry weight, or more than 1 milligram per finished unit, into Schedule I along with mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, with natural leaf containing trace 7-OH carved out, according to the notices. [The public comment windows closed on July 31][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], and the temporary order can issue any day on or after August 5. A [News 12 New Jersey investigation](https://newjersey.news12.com/2026/08/03/kiyc-feds-look-to-ban-gas-station-heroin/WRau48bV82Gm8VhAPMcZx) published August 3 reported that restrictions on synthetic kratom compounds "could take effect as early as this week."

The Local 21 News report on the Florida case recounted that federal backdrop, noting that the DEA relied on a determination by the Department of Health and Human Services that synthetic, concentrated 7-OH and the three related substances have no accepted medical use and a high potential for abuse. Once the temporary order takes effect, manufacture, distribution, sale, and possession of the covered compounds will carry federal criminal, civil, and administrative penalties, per the report.

## Deaths attributed to kratom products draw wider official attention

The Florida case adds to a series of kratom-attributed deaths receiving official scrutiny this week. In North Dakota, [state epidemiology data tied 23 deaths since 2019 to kratom, 7-OH, or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl][north-dakota-kratom-deaths-emergency-rules], and that state's pharmacy board is pursuing emergency rules that would mirror the pending federal scheduling, according to the North Dakota Monitor.
