There is "enormous confusion" among patients, advocates, and businesses as the earliest effective date arrives for the DEA's temporary scheduling of concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, [Pain News Network reported](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/8/3/enormous-confusion-as-deadline-nears-for-7-oh-kratom-bannbspnbsp) on August 3. The pending order covers concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf, and much of the reported confusion centers on whether the order's reach stops at that line.

The temporary order, which would place 7-OH above specified thresholds and three related compounds into Schedule I alongside heroin and LSD, [can issue any day on or after August 5][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], following the close of the public comment window on July 31.

## The pseudoindoxyl measurement problem

The American Kratom Association, which Pain News Network identifies as a group representing natural leaf kratom vendors, told the outlet there is "enormous confusion among some members of Congress and even staff at the DEA" about the differences between natural leaf kratom, 7-OH concentrates, and other alkaloids. The group's specific concern involves mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, one of the three related compounds named in the [scheduling notices published in the Federal Register on July 6][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule].

Pseudoindoxyl occurs naturally in kratom at trace levels, and Pain News Network reported the AKA's warning that, because there is no current way to reliably measure pseudoindoxyl levels in botanical material, a blanket ban on pseudoindoxyl products could in practice prohibit the natural levels present in kratom leaf. The federal notices describe natural leaf as outside the scope of the action, with the 7-OH control applying above 0.050 percent by dry weight or 1 milligram per finished unit.

## Support for the crackdown, criticism of its design

The AKA supports the federal move against what it calls chemically manipulated products. In a [News 12 New Jersey investigation](https://newjersey.news12.com/2026/08/03/kiyc-feds-look-to-ban-gas-station-heroin/WRau48bV82Gm8VhAPMcZx) published August 3, the group said the government is doing the right thing in cracking down on synthetic kratom products like 7-OH, while insisting that kratom itself should be treated differently. "The science has now demonstrated conclusively that natural Kratom leaf is safe," an AKA representative told News 12, adding that "it's the chemically manipulated 7-OH opioids that needed to be banned." The FDA has not approved kratom for any use, and the agency's support for the 7-OH scheduling rests on an HHS finding that synthetic 7-OH and the related compounds have no accepted medical use and high abuse potential, according to DEA press materials.

Policy groups have challenged the order's design from opposite directions. The R Street Institute, in a [public comment](https://www.rstreet.org/outreach/r-street-comments-on-placement-of-7-oh-on-schedule-i/) submitted before the July 31 deadline, argued that available evidence supports prompt action against concentrated and semisynthetic 7-OH but does not establish the 0.050 percent or 1 milligram thresholds as the point at which 7-OH poses an imminent hazard. The group recommended focusing on label accuracy, potency disclosure, rapid-delivery formulations, child-appealing products, unsafe manufacturing, and unapproved medical claims rather than a single numerical cutoff.

Public Citizen, in an August 3 release, criticized the action as a ["fake crackdown"](https://www.citizen.org/news/trump-administrations-fake-crackdown-leaves-most-gas-station-heroin-on-the-shelves/) that targets 7-OH while leaving mitragynine, kratom's most abundant alkaloid, without a federal limit, and urged broader regulation covering both compounds.

## The state picture as the order nears

The scope question is already playing out at the state level. North Dakota's governor on Monday [ordered a statewide ban on all kratom products effective August 5][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom], reaching natural leaf as well as the concentrated and synthetic products targeted federally. The federal order, once issued, would last at least two years, according to Pain News Network, while the DEA pursues permanent scheduling.
