North Dakota Gov. Kelly Armstrong issued an executive order on Monday declaring a public health emergency and prohibiting the sale, use, and possession of all kratom and kratom products statewide effective 5 p.m. on August 5, [according to the governor's office](https://www.governor.nd.gov/news/armstrong-wrigley-announce-executive-actions-ban-unregulated-drug-kratom-amid-growing-concerns). The order reaches natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. That makes it broader than the pending federal action, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations rather than the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf.

Armstrong announced the actions jointly with Attorney General Drew Wrigley, framing them as "aimed at protecting public health and safety by curbing access to kratom," per the governor's office. The announcement cites "fatal overdoses and imminent public health threat" from kratom, mitragynine, and 7-OH as the basis for the emergency declaration.

## What the orders do

The package has two parts, according to the governor's office. An emergency rule issued by the North Dakota Board of Pharmacy and signed by the governor classifies 7-OH as a Schedule I controlled substance under state law, valid for 180 days while permanent rulemaking proceeds. KFGO reported that the rule takes effect Wednesday, "consistent with the DEA's action," and [Knox Radio reported](https://knoxradio.com/2026/08/03/1361147/) that it aligns state law with the expected federal scheduling. The executive order separately prohibits the sale, use, and possession of all kratom products statewide. [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/03/1336402/) that the prohibition runs for 30 days starting August 5.

Armstrong also called the Legislature into special session on September 2 to codify the kratom prohibition, enact the 7-OH rule into state law, and clarify enforcement authority, per the governor's office. The announcement directs people concerned about kratom or 7-OH dependence to the 988 crisis lifeline and the 211 helpline.

State officials described kratom as currently unregulated in North Dakota, arguing that the absence of minimum-age or product restrictions created a public health risk, according to the governor's office. The orders follow [state epidemiology data tying 23 deaths since 2019 to kratom, 7-OH, or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl][north-dakota-kratom-deaths-emergency-rules], data that as reported did not distinguish which deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations versus natural leaf kratom. Legislative leaders had been [weighing a special session with a kratom ban on the agenda][warren-michigan-kratom-ban-north-dakota-special-session] since late July.

## A state ban wider than the federal action it cites

Armstrong explicitly cited the DEA's move to temporarily place 7-OH and three related compounds into Schedule I, along with FDA support for restricting certain 7-OH products, as the trigger for the state's emergency response, per KFGO. The federal action covers 7-OH above 0.050 percent by dry weight or more than 1 milligram per finished unit, plus three synthetic compounds, and [its earliest possible effective date is August 5][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], the same day North Dakota's ban takes hold. Natural kratom leaf is described in the DEA's notices as outside the scope of the federal action.

North Dakota's order prohibits all kratom products rather than tracking the federal line between leaf and concentrated or synthetic 7-OH. Advocacy groups have warned that federal and state actions risk sweeping in natural leaf products; the American Kratom Association's [concerns about the scope of the federal scheduling][aka-warns-pseudoindoxyl-scheduling-could-reach-natural-leaf] are covered in a separate briefing today.

## Retail impact and the wider patchwork

KFGO reported that convenience stores, vape shops, and wellness retailers in North Dakota will have to pull kratom items from shelves for at least 30 days, pending decisions at the special session. Violations after 5 p.m. on August 5 would expose sellers and possessors to controlled-substance and executive-order penalties, per KFGO.

Other states continued adjusting their kratom rules in the same window with narrower tools. In Minnesota, the legal age to purchase kratom has been raised to 21, [MPR News reported](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/03/kratom-purchase-age-raised-to-21-in-minnesota) on August 3. The divergence between total prohibition in North Dakota and age-and-potency regulation elsewhere continues the state-by-state split that industry and advocacy groups have tracked throughout the federal scheduling process.
