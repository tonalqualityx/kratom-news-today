A Franklin County judge declined this week to block Ohio's ban on processed kratom products, a ruling Signal Ohio characterized as a win for Governor Mike DeWine's administration. Common Pleas Judge Mark Serrott denied the preliminary injunction sought by Botanic Tonics, the maker of the kratom-and-kava tonic Feel Free, and retailer Krazy Daze on Tuesday, [Signal Ohio reported](https://signalohio.org/kratom-illegal-ohio-judge-rules-handing-dewine-a-win-on-drugs/). The ruling leaves the Ohio Board of Pharmacy's rule in force while the case proceeds to a trial scheduled for June 2027.

The rule at issue prohibits the sale of processed kratom products, including liquids, candies, and pills, according to Signal Ohio. Raw plant material remains outside the prohibition for now, with the DeWine administration pursuing traditional rulemaking on that front, the outlet reported. The board [proposed a Schedule I classification for mitragynine compounds in June][ohio-board-of-pharmacy-proposes-schedule-i-mitragynine], a step that would extend Ohio's restrictions to natural kratom leaf, after its [rule covering synthetic kratom products took effect in May][ohio-synthetic-kratom-ban-takes-effect].

## The court's reasoning

The plaintiffs argued that the pharmacy board's rules were impermissibly vague and that the agency exceeded its authority, per Signal Ohio's account of the case. Serrott rejected both arguments, writing that the board "followed the proper procedure" and that the rule "appears to be beneficial to the public in attempting to prevent the ill effects that may arise from abuse," citing kratom's "addictive effects" and "possibility for abuse."

The judge acknowledged the rule was "detrimental to plaintiff's business interests" but found the public-health rationale sufficient to let it stand pending trial, according to the report. Signal Ohio noted that the Ohio Department of Health attributes roughly 200 unintentional fatal overdoses to kratom, and that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recorded about 3,400 kratom-related poison control exposure reports last year, an increase the outlet put at roughly 1,200 percent since 2015. The reporting does not break down how many of those cases involved concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products rather than natural leaf kratom, a distinction that has become central to the federal scheduling debate.

## A difficult week for Botanic Tonics

Botanic Tonics told Signal Ohio it plans to appeal. The Oklahoma-based company has been active in courtrooms on both sides of the docket this year: it joined the Global Kratom Coalition in [challenging Utah's kratom regulation law][utah-kratom-law-court-challenge-beverage-ban], and a [proposed class action against the company was dismissed in New York in June][botanic-tonics-case-dismissed-new-york-alabama-kratom].

The Ohio ruling arrived in the same week that Senator Ron Wyden [demanded documents from the company][wyden-inquiry-kennedy-kratom-vape-policy] as part of a Senate Finance Committee inquiry into the administration's kratom and flavored-vape policy, a separate development covered in today's companion briefing.

Unless an appeals court intervenes, Ohio's processed-product prohibition will remain in effect for at least the ten months before the June 2027 trial date, per Signal Ohio.
