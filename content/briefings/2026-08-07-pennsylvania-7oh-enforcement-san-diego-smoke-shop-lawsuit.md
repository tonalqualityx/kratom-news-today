State and local enforcement moved to the foreground this week on two coasts. In Pennsylvania, Attorney General Dave Sunday said his office is prepared to enforce the DEA's temporary scheduling of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), [Fox56 reported](https://fox56.com/news/local/pennsylvania-kratom-sales-face-scrutiny-as-dea-lawmakers-weigh-new-rules). In California, San Diego City Attorney Heather Ferbert filed a civil lawsuit against the owner of 11 smoke shops over alleged kratom sales that the city says violate its local ban, [according to Times of San Diego](https://timesofsandiego.com/crime/2026/08/06/san-diego-targets-owner-of-11-smoke-shops-for-selling-kratom/).

## Pennsylvania treats the federal action as live, though no order has published

Fox56's August 5 report stated that concentrated 7-OH products, a category that includes extracts, tablets, and shots containing the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural kratom leaf, can no longer be sold as of August 5, and that natural leaf kratom remains unscheduled federally. Sunday said retailers should "take the time to figure out if what they're selling is lawful" and that compliance would be monitored, per the station.

The formal status of the federal action remains unsettled. A Kratom News Today review of the Federal Register on August 7 found no published temporary scheduling order for 7-OH; the DEA's July 6 notices established August 5 only as the [earliest date an order could take effect][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5]. The gap between official statements treating the scheduling as accomplished and the absence of a published order extends the confusion that Pain News Network [documented earlier this week][aka-warns-pseudoindoxyl-scheduling-could-reach-natural-leaf] among patients, advocates, and retailers.

State Senator Tracy Pennycuick added her endorsement on August 6, calling synthetic kratom "a fast-growing, unregulated market that poses serious risks to consumers" and saying she "will continue to seek commonsense guardrails in Pennsylvania," [according to a statement from her office](https://senatorpennycuick.com/2026/08/06/pennycuick-applauds-fed-ruling-to-temporarily-control-synthetic-kratom/). Pennycuick sponsored Senate Bill 233, a Kratom Consumer Protection Act that would bar sales to buyers under 21 and prohibit what the bill terms unsafe kratom products; the measure passed committee in 2025, per her office.

Fox56 also reported the public-health figures driving the state's attention: the Cambria County coroner has investigated six kratom-related deaths since 2019, and the Pennsylvania Department of Health recorded 38 deaths in 2024 in which kratom was a contributing factor. The reporting does not specify whether those cases involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that materially changes the products' risk profiles. Mac Haddow, senior fellow for public policy at the American Kratom Association, drew that line in the same report, arguing that dependence associated with natural leaf kratom resembles caffeine dependence rather than opioid addiction and saying that vape and convenience stores should sell only "safely formulated, properly labeled products" and enforce age restrictions. Haddow's characterization of natural leaf dependence is his organization's position; the FDA has not approved kratom for any use.

## San Diego sues a multi-store operator

Ferbert's office sued Wadeea Wadeea, whom the city describes in its filing as one of San Diego's largest kratom sellers, with stores in Imperial Beach, Normal Heights, Ocean Beach, North Park, Mira Mesa, and South San Diego, Times of San Diego reported on August 6. The suit seeks a court injunction prohibiting kratom sales at his establishments and monetary penalties. [CBS 8 reported](https://www.cbs8.com/article/news/local/san-diego-sues-smoke-shop-owner-alleged-kratom-sales/509-83959bab-9861-490a-90b1-972b70161178) that the alleged sales violate San Diego's municipal ban on kratom.

The action is not the office's first against Wadeea: in December 2025, the city filed a civil enforcement action over alleged sales of unlicensed cannabis products from a concealed back room at his Mira Mesa location, per Times of San Diego. The outlet cited CDC figures recording at least 233 kratom-associated deaths nationally from 2015 to 2025, with more than ten percent of those occurring in San Diego County in the past five years. As with the Pennsylvania figures, the reporting does not distinguish between concentrated 7-OH products and natural leaf kratom in those counts.

The suit adds a municipal layer to California enforcement that has so far run through the state: Attorney General Rob Bonta [sued a San Diego kratom manufacturer in May][california-kratom-enforcement-seizures-ag-lawsuit] as part of a state campaign that has seized more than $5 million in products. Courtroom enforcement advanced elsewhere this week as well, with an Ohio judge [declining to block that state's processed-kratom ban][ohio-judge-declines-block-processed-kratom-ban], covered in today's companion briefing.
