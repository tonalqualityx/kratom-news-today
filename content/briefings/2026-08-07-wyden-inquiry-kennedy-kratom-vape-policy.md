Senator Ron Wyden of Oregon, the ranking Democrat on the Senate Finance Committee, opened an inquiry this week into the administration's handling of flavored vape and kratom policy, [according to an announcement from the committee's minority office](https://www.finance.senate.gov/ranking-members-news/wyden-investigates-trump-and-rfk-jr-corruption-favoring-big-tobacco-and-kratom-companies). Wyden sent letters to Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., Botanic Tonics chief executive Jerry Ross, and Reynolds American chief executive David Waterfield, demanding documents and written responses by August 31.

The inquiry alleges what [STAT described](https://www.statnews.com/2026/08/05/senator-ron-wyden-investigation-kennedy-flavored-vapes-kratom/) as a pattern of quid pro quos: substantial donations from the tobacco and kratom industries to the presidential campaigns of Donald Trump and Kennedy, followed by policy decisions favorable to those industries. "Both industries will flood gas stations and convenience stores with products that pique the curiosity of kids and teens and launch them into addiction," Wyden wrote in an August 3 letter to Kennedy, per the announcement. In the same letter, he told Kennedy that "just like President Trump, your door is always open for corporate donors with demands and a checkbook."

## The kratom thread

On kratom, Wyden's office said the administration has pushed to make kratom products widely available in retail stores nationwide despite health concerns raised by federal agencies. The announcement also renewed attention on Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, who [reportedly holds a financial stake of up to $1 million in Botanic Tonics][dhs-secretary-mullin-holds-stake-in-kratom-company], the Oklahoma company behind the kratom-and-kava tonic Feel Free. Wyden's office said Mullin has advocated restrictions on kratom's synthetic competitors while holding that interest, framing the combination as a conflict of interest. The Daily Beast first reported the Mullin holding in May, based on his federal ethics filing.

The reference to synthetic competitors points at the concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products that are the subject of the DEA's pending temporary scheduling action, a category distinct from the natural leaf products Botanic Tonics sells. Neither the committee announcement nor STAT's report describes any response from Botanic Tonics, Reynolds American, or HHS to the letters.

## The vape timeline, per the letters

Most of the specific timeline in the announcement concerns flavored e-cigarettes rather than kratom. According to Wyden's letters, Reynolds American donated $5 million to the MAGA Inc. political committee on April 30, a company executive met with Trump on May 2, and the administration issued a directive on May 8 permitting flavored e-cigarettes in retail stores. The letters also state that FDA Commissioner Marty Makary resigned on May 12 after opposing the change. Those characterizations are Wyden's; the companies and the administration have not been quoted addressing them in the materials reviewed for this briefing.

The document demand lands during an already crowded week for Botanic Tonics, which [lost a bid to block Ohio's processed-kratom ban][ohio-judge-declines-block-processed-kratom-ban] in a Franklin County courtroom, a ruling covered in today's companion briefing.
