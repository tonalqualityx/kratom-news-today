Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway announced on August 6 that The Green Dragon, LLC and its owners, David Jadwin and Matthew Jadwin, have agreed not to own, operate, or hold a financial interest in any Missouri business that markets, advertises, or sells 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) or any other kratom product to consumers or distributors in the state, [according to the attorney general's office](https://ago.mo.gov/st-louis-area-distributor-to-suspend-sales-of-kratom-products-and-7-oh-in-missouri/). The office describes Green Dragon as a St. Louis-area distributor of 7-OH, the concentrated and synthetic preparations at the center of the state's enforcement campaign, which are distinct from the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf. The agreement's terms nonetheless reach kratom products generally, covering leaf products alongside the concentrated alkaloid formats.

The [Missouri Independent reported](https://missouriindependent.com/2026/08/07/missouri-ag-bars-st-louis-company-from-kratom-sales-as-federal-7-oh-ban-looms/) that the resolution took the form of an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance filed in the Circuit Court of Cole County, formally ending the office's investigation into the company.

## The agreement's terms

Under the agreement, Green Dragon must remove the covered products from its Missouri retail premises and cease sales, distribution, offering, transfer, delivery, marketing, and advertising to any consumer or distributor located in the state, per the attorney general's office. The company also agreed not to sell any dietary supplement without first obtaining a scientific analysis of its active ingredients, a condition the office framed as ensuring consumers are not exposed to substances like 7-OH without their knowledge.

"These unregulated, highly addictive drugs have taken lives and fueled addiction across our state," Hanaway said in the announcement, calling the resolution "another decisive step to protect Missouri families from the deadly impact of gas-station opioids" and saying her office "will continue pursuing any distributor or retailer that puts Missourians in jeopardy." The characterization of the products as addictive and deadly is the attorney general's; the office's statement does not distinguish which specific product formats the fatality and addiction claims refer to.

## A widening series of exit agreements

The office said the Green Dragon deal is the latest in a series of agreements it has secured with distributors and retailers of kratom alkaloids, naming CBD American Shaman, Relax Relief Rejuvenate Trading LLC (EDP Kratom), and Big Dreams Ventures, LLC (VEGA 7X), and noting that other agreements are not yet public. The pattern extends the campaign Hanaway's office [expanded in July][missouri-ag-expands-kratom-investigation-american-shaman-suspends-sales], when it added 10 entities across the supply chain to its statewide investigation and announced American Shaman's suspension of in-state sales. Taken together, the agreements amount to a negotiated contraction of the kratom and 7-OH distribution channel in Missouri, accomplished through civil investigations rather than legislation or court rulings.

## The federal backdrop

The office tied its action to the federal timeline: the Drug Enforcement Administration filed notice on July 1 of its intent to add 7-OH, MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl to Schedule I, and the 30-day comment period on that notice expired August 1, meaning the U.S. Attorney General may now schedule the substances and impose federal criminal penalties, according to the release. Hanaway said she "applauds federal officials for recognizing these drugs as what they are: synthetic, addictive opioids that carry grave risks."

The formal federal order had not published as of Kratom News Today's most recent review; the DEA's July notices set August 5 as the [earliest date a temporary scheduling order could take effect][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5]. The Missouri Independent reported that the proposal would cover processed or synthetic products containing more than 0.05 percent 7-OH or more than 1 milligram per article, and botanical kratom containing more than 0.05 percent 7-OH by dry weight, leaving natural kratom with only trace 7-OH outside the federal action.

State-level pressure continued elsewhere this week: a North Dakota legislative workgroup met August 7 to weigh whether that state's [total kratom ban should give way to regulation][north-dakota-lawmakers-favor-kratom-regulation-over-ban], covered in today's companion briefing.
