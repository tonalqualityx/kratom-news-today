Five days into North Dakota's statewide kratom ban, no violations had been reported, Fargo officials said in coverage published August 11 by [Valley News Live](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/08/11/fargo-officials-urge-kratom-education-five-days-into-statewide-ban-no-violations-reported-yet/). The officials described an enforcement posture built around education rather than citations, directing residents with questions or in crisis to the 988 and 211 lines. North Dakota's emergency order prohibits the sale, use, and possession of all kratom products, a scope that reaches natural leaf kratom as well as the concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations that federal regulators have targeted.

The public-health messaging accompanying the ban has centered on people who were already using kratom when it took effect. State behavioral health officials urged residents experiencing kratom withdrawal to seek outpatient medical support, [AM 1100 The Flag](https://www.am1100theflag.com/news/health-official-urges-kratom-users-to-seek-medical-support-amid-north-dakota-ban/) reported August 10. Health officials also told [Inforum](https://www.inforum.com/health/health-officials-say-narcan-may-help-in-kratom-overdoses) that naloxone, sold under the brand name Narcan, may help in kratom overdoses. That guidance parallels a recommendation the [American College of Medical Toxicology made this week][acmt-position-statement-restrict-nonprescription-7oh-sales] for naloxone access and emergency treatment of suspected 7-OH toxicity.

The ban itself took effect this month under [an emergency executive order from Gov. Kelly Armstrong that declared a public health emergency][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom]. [Medical Daily](https://www.medicaldaily.com/north-dakota-kratom-ban-7-oh-state-approaches-477147) reported that the governor's order pulled kratom products from shelves for 30 days, while the state Board of Pharmacy separately classified 7-OH as a Schedule I controlled substance for 180 days as emergency rulemaking proceeds. [MPR News](https://www.mprnews.org/story/2026/08/09/north-dakota-banned-kratom-and-set-a-special-session-for-september) reported that a special legislative session is set for September to decide the state's longer-term approach.

What the legislature does in September remains the open question. [Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle have said they favor regulating kratom rather than a permanent prohibition][north-dakota-lawmakers-favor-kratom-regulation-over-ban]. North Dakota's all-products approach also stands apart from the direction most jurisdictions took this week: [Dutchess County, New York's new ban][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products] applies to concentrated and synthetic products while leaving leaf kratom outside its scope.
