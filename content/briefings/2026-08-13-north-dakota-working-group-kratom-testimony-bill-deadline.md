North Dakota's Kratom Working Group heard about four hours of testimony on Wednesday, August 12, in Bismarck as lawmakers worked against a noon Friday deadline to file bills for the special session that convenes September 2, [InForum reported](https://www.inforum.com/news/fargo/bills-deadline-for-north-dakota-special-session-approaches-as-more-testify-on-kratom-ban). The central question remains where to draw the line: Senator Michelle Axtman, a Bismarck Republican, proposed a complete ban on kratom and its derivatives, while other legislators argued for prohibiting synthetic products containing high concentrations of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and regulating natural kratom instead. 7-OH occurs only in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf; the concentrated and synthetic preparations sold in smoke shops and convenience stores are the products the Drug Enforcement Administration [moved in July to place temporarily in Schedule I][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], an action whose published thresholds would not reach natural leaf containing only trace 7-OH.

The working group is drafting the state's longer-term policy while Governor Kelly Armstrong's [executive order banning all kratom products][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom], in effect since August 5, temporarily outlaws the substance statewide until the Legislature acts, per InForum. At the group's first meeting on August 7, several members [signaled a preference for regulation over a flat ban][north-dakota-lawmakers-favor-kratom-regulation-over-ban], with lawmakers distinguishing natural leaf products from the concentrated 7-OH pills they said are driving the state's problems. Axtman's complete-ban proposal on Wednesday indicates that prohibition remains a live option heading into the filing deadline, which falls at noon on Friday, August 14.

## Testimony pulls in both directions

The witnesses who traveled to Bismarck gave the working group sharply different accounts. Misty Brown, who came from Denver, told lawmakers "I never in a million years thought I could get out of active addiction," according to InForum. Jennifer Gillis of Memphis pointed to the effect of a ban in her own state: "In Tennessee, we were turned into criminals overnight just for trying to have a better quality of life," she said, per the outlet.

Shelly Gunderson of Bismarck, whose son died in 2019, testified on the other side of the question. "No opioids were found in his system, but mitragynine was," she said, according to InForum. Mitragynine is kratom's primary alkaloid. The reporting does not indicate whether the products involved in her son's death were natural leaf kratom or concentrated preparations.

The ban's commercial effects also surfaced. Mohammed Wazwaz of Moe's Smoke Shop told the working group "I lost half the sales because kratom is gone," per InForum. Retailer disruption has been a consistent thread since the executive order took effect with roughly three days' notice, stranding inventory that store owners moved out of state or handed to disposal sites, per earlier InForum and KFGO reporting.

## Wrigley: wait for the experts

Attorney General Drew Wrigley urged legislators not to commit to conclusions before the special session assembles its evidence, [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/12/n-d-attorney-general-drew-wrigley-wants-legislators-to-wait-get-kratom-information-from-experts-during-special-session/) on August 12. "I implore the legislature to not do what too many are already doing: start to make judgements based on something they heard anecdotally or an article they read," Wrigley said, per the station. "Wait until you have the opportunity to hear from medical experts, hear from people at the North Dakota Crime Lab, addiction folks."

Wrigley's office has firsthand experience with the product-labeling question. The state crime lab tested kratom that Wrigley purchased in Bismarck and found the packaging mislabeled, and a retailer warned him during the purchase that the substance is highly addictive, according to KFGO. State officials have attributed 23 deaths to kratom, per the station, figures that [underpinned the state's emergency rulemaking][north-dakota-kratom-deaths-emergency-rules] earlier this month.

Enforcement of the current ban has so far been quiet. Fargo officials [reported no violations in the ban's first days][north-dakota-kratom-ban-no-violations-education-push] and described an education-first posture, as covered in yesterday's briefing. Whether the special session produces a permanent ban, a 7-OH-focused prohibition with regulated natural leaf sales, or another framework will start to come into focus once bills are filed Friday.
