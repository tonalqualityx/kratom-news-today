Massachusetts Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein issued an emergency order on Wednesday, August 13, temporarily designating all forms of kratom as Schedule I substances under state law, [Boston.com reported](https://www.boston.com/news/local-news/2026/08/13/healey-temporarily-restricts-kratom-sales-statewide/). The order takes effect after a 14-day notice period, can remain in force for up to a year, and gives local boards of health and municipal officials additional authority to take enforcement action against retailers selling kratom products, per the outlet. The action, announced by Governor Maura Healey's administration, covers natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, a broader sweep than the pending federal action, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while leaving botanical kratom with the alkaloid's naturally occurring trace levels below its thresholds unaffected, [according to WGBH](https://www.wgbh.org/news/health/2026-08-13/emergency-order-issued-to-crack-down-on-kratom-products-in-mass).

In announcing the order, Healey said dangerous kratom products are being sold with little oversight and that consumers often do not know what they are buying or how potent it is, [the Greenfield Recorder reported](https://recorder.com/2026/08/13/deerfield-enacts-kratom-ban/). The state's framing describes kratom as largely unregulated and cites the potential for dependence, overdose, and other serious side effects, [per WWLP](https://www.wwlp.com/news/massachusetts/massachusetts-issues-emergency-order-to-restrict-kratom-sales/). The governor's office paired the order with public education and treatment outreach, and law enforcement groups publicly backed what the administration called common-sense regulation, according to the [announcement from the governor's office](https://www.mass.gov/news/governor-healey-takes-action-to-protect-young-people-and-residents-from-dangerous-kratom-products).

## Broader than the federal action

The Massachusetts order sweeps wider than the Drug Enforcement Administration's pending federal move. The DEA filed notices of intent in early July to temporarily place 7-OH and related substances into Schedule I, describing the covered substances as an imminent threat to public safety, and that scheduling does not apply to botanical kratom products whose naturally occurring 7-OH falls below the published threshold, per WGBH. The [federal comment window closed at the start of August][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], with a final temporary order possible at any time.

Massachusetts becomes the second state this month to use emergency authority against kratom in all its forms. North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong's [executive order banning all kratom products][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom] took effect August 5 and remains in place while that state's legislature prepares for a September 2 special session. The Massachusetts order arrives through a different mechanism, an emergency scheduling action by the public health commissioner rather than a gubernatorial executive order, and it carries its own clock: a 14-day notice period before it takes effect and a maximum duration of one year, per Boston.com.

## Beacon Hill weighs a narrower line

The legislative track in Massachusetts may draw the line differently than the emergency order does. The House Ways and Means Committee is reviewing a bill that would ban synthetic kratom and products with 7-OH above two percent of total alkaloids, a threshold the Greenfield Recorder notes is above what the FDA says is typical in kratom leaves. Lawmakers have also considered a proposal requiring kratom product labels to carry the manufacturer's name and address, an ingredient list, and directions for use, per WGBH.

WGBH's report also cited the FDA's position that 7-OH is substantially more potent at the mu-opioid receptor than mitragynine, kratom's primary alkaloid, and morphine. That potency framing has anchored the federal case for distinguishing concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products from natural leaf, where 7-OH appears only in trace amounts.

## Local bans continue alongside the state action

The town of Deerfield enacted its own ban on all kratom products, the Greenfield Recorder reported. The move extends a run of municipal action in the state: Natick board materials cited in July reporting counted about 20 Massachusetts communities with complete kratom prohibitions, and [Hopkinton banned kratom sales that month][hopkinton-kratom-ban-natick-weighs-prohibition] while Natick weighed its own prohibition. County governments outside Massachusetts are moving on a similar track; Dutchess County, New York [banned concentrated and synthetic kratom products][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products] earlier this week.

## Reaction and what comes next

Shatterproof, the national addiction-focused nonprofit, said it supports the Massachusetts action to protect consumers, especially young people, and framed the order as a response to the widespread availability of concentrated and synthetic products sold with limited oversight and no age restrictions, according to the organization's [statement distributed by PR Newswire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260813ny26299/shatterproof-applauds-massachusetts-effort-to-restrict-dangerous-kratom-products-and-protect-public-health).

For operators, the immediate stakes are inventory and shelf space. The order is described as intended to remove kratom products from store shelves statewide, per WWLP, and the enforcement authority it hands to local boards of health means retailers face oversight from the officials closest to them once the 14-day notice period runs. If the order takes effect on that schedule, the restriction would reach every kratom product sold in Massachusetts by late August, while the legislature considers whether the durable rule should target all kratom or only the synthetic and high-7-OH products at the center of the federal action.
