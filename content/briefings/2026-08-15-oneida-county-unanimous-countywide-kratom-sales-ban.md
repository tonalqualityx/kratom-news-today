The Oneida County Board of Legislators voted unanimously on Wednesday, August 12, 2026 to ban the sale of kratom and kratom-derived products throughout the upstate New York county, [the Utica Observer-Dispatch reported](https://www.uticaod.com/story/news/politics/government/2026/08/12/oneida-county-kratom-ban-prohibits-sales-imposes-fines/91270736007/). The prohibition reaches all kratom products, natural leaf included, going beyond the concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations that are the focus of the pending federal scheduling action and of narrower bans elsewhere in New York. Violations carry a civil penalty of $500 for the unlawful distribution or sale of kratom, and each subsequent violation draws a penalty of up to $1,000 per item confiscated, per the outlet.

The new local law strengthens a public health order issued by the Oneida County Health Department that took effect June 5, 2026 and already banned the sale of kratom and kratom-derived products, according to the Observer-Dispatch. County Executive Anthony Picente Jr. first announced his intent to pursue a ban during his State of the County Address on May 8, 2026, identifying kratom as an emerging public health concern, and announced the legislation in June. Under the law, compliance checks may occur during routine inspections, in response to complaints, or as part of criminal investigations, and prohibited products will be confiscated and destroyed under health department policy, per the report. The report also notes that the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has not approved kratom for medical use, a point local governments across the country have cited in enacting restrictions.

## A broader line than Dutchess County

Oneida County's prohibition sweeps wider than the measure [Dutchess County legislators passed on August 10][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products], which banned concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while leaving natural leaf kratom legal for adults. WAMC [reported](https://www.wamc.org/news/2026-08-12/dutchess-county-kratom-ban-leaves-advocates-unhappy-as-some-products-are-still-legal) that the narrower Dutchess approach left some kratom advocates unhappy, with concerns that restricting certain products while others remain legal could push consumers toward less regulated sources. Oneida County drew no such line: the sale or offer for sale of kratom, products containing kratom, and products derived from kratom is now prohibited throughout the county, per the Observer-Dispatch.

## Debate over natural leaf at the vote

The line between natural leaf kratom and other kratom products surfaced during the board's public comment period. One resident spoke against the measure, saying she wanted regulation of leaf kratom rather than an outright ban and citing her own use of the plant in place of opioid painkillers, the Observer-Dispatch reported. District 13 Legislator Christopher Newton voted for the ban but said he understood the case for a carve-out. "We could split this out so the leaf is still available to be used medicinally for people who don't want to take opiates, because the leaf contains far less of the active ingredient than what's being sold in the smoke shops," Newton said, per the outlet, while adding that keeping newer products off the market would be "a continuing chase."

District 14 Legislator Chad Davis cited a 2026 Centers for Disease Control and Prevention study in support of the ban, saying United States poison control centers have reported a 1,200 percent increase in kratom-related exposures from 2015 to 2025, according to the Observer-Dispatch. Davis said 30 states and the District of Columbia regulate kratom or its components in some manner, six states have total bans, and product contents are a central uncertainty: "You don't know who is putting those products out there or what's in them." A University of Florida analysis published this week reported findings along those lines, [documenting mislabeled alkaloid content and undisclosed semisynthetic compounds in commercial kratom products][university-of-florida-study-mislabeled-kratom-products].

## Local action continues while federal rules remain pending

The county vote lands amid a run of state and local measures enacted while federal rules are still unsettled. The Drug Enforcement Administration announced on July 1, 2026 that it intends to temporarily place 7-OH and three related substances in Schedule I, and the agency has said botanical kratom products whose naturally occurring trace 7-OH falls below its published thresholds would not be targeted, [according to U.S. News & World Report](https://www.usnews.com/news/health-news/articles/2026-08-14/states-enact-emergency-orders-on-kratom-while-awaiting-federal-rules). With the federal order still pending, states have moved on their own, per the report. Massachusetts issued an [emergency order placing all forms of kratom in state Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] last week, and North Dakota's statewide ban remains in place ahead of a September special session.

For retailers in Oneida County, enforcement now runs through the county health department, with confiscation, destruction of inventory, and escalating per-item penalties, per the Observer-Dispatch. The county becomes the latest New York jurisdiction to remove kratom from retail shelves entirely, while others in the state continue to draw the line at concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, leaving sellers with county-by-county rules that differ on whether natural leaf may be sold at all.
