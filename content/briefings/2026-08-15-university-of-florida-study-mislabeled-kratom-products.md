Researchers at the University of Florida College of Pharmacy examined more than 40 commercial kratom formulations and reported that many contained alkaloid concentrations that did not match their labels, along with psychoactive compounds not listed on packaging, [according to a summary published August 14, 2026 by Technology Networks](https://www.technologynetworks.com/drug-discovery/news/whats-really-inside-commercial-kratom-products-415642). The unlisted compounds included semisynthetic kratom-related alkaloids that the summary identifies as 7-HMG, MGPI, and MGM-15, which go beyond the mitragynine and trace 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) profiles of natural kratom leaf, per the summary. Products marketed as single-alkaloid formulations often contained multiple unlisted compounds.

Among the specific findings, tablets sold under the name "SUDO" and labeled as raw kratom contained 22 milligrams of 7-hydroxymitragynine, which the study abbreviates as 7-HMG, per tablet, according to Technology Networks. [According to U.S. News & World Report](https://www.usnews.com/news/health-news/articles/2026-08-14/states-enact-emergency-orders-on-kratom-while-awaiting-federal-rules), kratom leaves naturally contain only trace amounts of 7-OH, and the compound appears at high concentrations in concentrated extracts and lab-made preparations, which are the focus of current federal and state regulatory action. The researchers also reported what the summary describes as the first detection of 3-dehydromitragynine (3DMTG), a metabolite the researchers associated with high lethality in animal models, at doses of 15 to 80 milligrams in several unlabeled liquid shots.

## Calls for testing, labeling, and restrictions on semisynthetics

The investigators recommended mandatory pre-market testing and full-spectrum ingredient labeling for natural botanical kratom products, along with commercial restrictions on unapproved semisynthetic compounds such as 7-HMG, MGPI, and MGM-15, according to Technology Networks. The recommendations track the distinction the researchers drew throughout the analysis: natural botanicals with known alkaloid profiles on one side, and products containing concentrated or semisynthetic compounds at levels far beyond natural leaf on the other.

## The compounds at issue are already regulatory targets

Several of the compounds flagged in the analysis overlap with those regulators are moving against. The Drug Enforcement Administration's [pending temporary scheduling action][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule] covers 7-OH above 0.050 percent by dry weight in botanical material, processed products exceeding that concentration or containing more than 1 milligram of 7-OH per unit, and three synthetic kratom-derived compounds. States and localities have been acting on the same distinction while the federal rules remain pending, per U.S. News & World Report. Dutchess County, New York [banned concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products on August 10][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products], a measure that names the derivatives MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, [according to the New Pine Plains Herald](https://www.newpineplainsherald.org/dutchess-county-passes-ban-on-sale-of-concentrated-kratom-products/). Oneida County legislators went further this week, [banning kratom sales countywide][oneida-county-unanimous-countywide-kratom-sales-ban] in a unanimous August 12 vote.

For manufacturers and retailers, the analysis adds product-level composition findings to a policy debate in which poison-center and overdose statistics have featured heavily, and it focuses on the product categories regulators have singled out: tablets labeled as one thing that test as another, and liquid shots carrying compounds their labels do not disclose, per the Technology Networks summary.
