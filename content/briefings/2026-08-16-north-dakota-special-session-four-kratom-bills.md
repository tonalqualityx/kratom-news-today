Twenty pieces of legislation have been submitted for the special session of the North Dakota Legislature that convenes September 2, and four of them deal directly with kratom, [InForum reported](https://www.inforum.com/news/fargo/20-pieces-of-legislation-submitted-for-historic-north-dakota-special-session-targeting-kratom). The filings follow the noon August 14 deadline that lawmakers were working against when the state's kratom working group [heard four hours of testimony in Bismarck][north-dakota-working-group-kratom-testimony-bill-deadline] earlier in the week. According to [KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/15/data-centers-vapes-childcare-aid-emerge-for-north-dakota-special-session-consideration/), the drafts prepared for the session include proposals to classify synthetic mitragynine-derived products as controlled substances, restrict sales to buyers 21 and older, impose licensing and labeling requirements, and expand the attorney general's enforcement authority.

The bills arrive in a legislature that has leaned toward a split approach. The working group favors banning synthetic kratom alkaloids while it continues to weigh how to handle natural botanical products, [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/13/north-dakota-lawmakers-kratom-group-leans-toward-synthetics-ban-ponders-natural-products/) on August 13. The synthetic and concentrated products at issue center on 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), a compound present only in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf. The concentrated and semisynthetic preparations sold in smoke shops and gas stations contain it at far higher levels, and those preparations are the target of the [DEA's pending temporary Schedule I action][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], which as proposed would not reach natural leaf below its concentration thresholds.

## Mortality data and the attorney general's purchases

The State Epidemiology Office's mortality figures continue to anchor the debate. Kratom-related compounds, including mitragynine, 7-OH, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, were part of the primary cause of 23 deaths in North Dakota since 2021 and a contributing cause in one additional death, according to KFGO's account of the office's data. The reporting groups natural alkaloids and semisynthetic derivatives together and does not specify whether individual deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that [figured in the state's emergency rulemaking][north-dakota-kratom-deaths-emergency-rules] earlier this month.

Attorney General Drew Wrigley, who urged legislators on August 12 to wait for testimony from medical experts and the state crime lab before settling on a framework, purchased a range of kratom products at a North Dakota store and displayed them in the context of the legislative debate, per InForum. The outlet's coverage notes lawmakers' concern that kratom had been widely available at gas stations and smoke shops with no statewide regulation or age restrictions before Governor Kelly Armstrong's [executive order banning all kratom products][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom] took effect August 5. For retailers, the emergency ban and the attorney general's disposal instructions have meant removing products from shelves and surrendering inventory, which InForum described as functionally a forced exit from the kratom market for at least the emergency period.

## States move while the federal order waits

The special session preparations fit a pattern that HealthDay documented in an August 14 report carried by [U.S. News & World Report](https://www.usnews.com/news/health-news/articles/2026-08-14/states-enact-emergency-orders-on-kratom-while-awaiting-federal-rules): states are enacting emergency kratom orders while the federal scheduling process remains unfinished. The Drug Enforcement Administration announced its intent on July 1 to temporarily place 7-OH and three related compounds in Schedule I, but the temporary scheduling order itself had not yet been issued as of the report's writing, per HealthDay. North Dakota's Board of Pharmacy has separately classified 7-OH as a Schedule I substance under an emergency rule lasting 180 days, according to the same report, and Massachusetts [placed all forms of kratom in state Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] by emergency order on August 13. Both states' emergency actions sweep more broadly than the federal proposal, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf.
