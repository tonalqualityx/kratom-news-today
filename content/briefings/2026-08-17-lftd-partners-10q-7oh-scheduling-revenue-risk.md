LFTD Partners warned investors that the Drug Enforcement Administration's proposed temporary scheduling of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) could materially reduce the company's revenue, "potentially by approximately half or more," in a quarterly report filed August 14, 2026 and [published via StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/LIFD/10-q-lftd-partners-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-59ff2cdbf166.html). The 10-Q, covering the six months ended June 30, 2026, states that "Schedule I placement or other significant federal regulatory restrictions may occur, as the FDA and the DEA have expressed concerns regarding products containing 7-hydroxymitragynine ('7-OH')."

The filing recounts the federal actions that produced the risk. In July 2026, according to the 10-Q, the Department of Health and Human Services initiated a Request for Information on proposed scheduling thresholds for 7-OH, and the DEA issued [two notices of intent to publish temporary scheduling orders][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule] in the Federal Register. The first would place 7-OH into Schedule I above specified concentration thresholds in kratom and processed products; the second would place three synthetic kratom-derived compounds (mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16) into Schedule I without concentration thresholds. The proposals target concentrated and synthetic products, and botanical kratom leaf below the 7-OH thresholds is excluded, per the DEA's published notices. If finalized, the filing states, the proposals "could materially restrict or prohibit the manufacture, distribution, and sale of certain kratom-derived products sold by the Company."

## A $1.4 million reserve against kratom inventory

The regulatory uncertainty has already reached the company's balance sheet. LFTD Partners recorded a $1,434,458 reserve against kratom-derived inventory as of June 30, 2026, according to the filing. Kratom-derived products are one of the company's two major lines of business, alongside hemp-derived products, per the 10-Q.

The disclosure is among the more concrete quantifications to date of what the pending federal action could mean for operators with 7-OH exposure. The final temporary scheduling order has been possible since [August 5, the statutory earliest date][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5], and the Federal Register listing still showed the 7-OH action as a proposed temporary placement as of mid-August, according to ScienceAlert. The pending order is drawing clinical attention as well: a case report covered by ScienceAlert this week [documents opioid-like withdrawal in a patient who abruptly stopped daily high-dose 7-OH use][case-report-documents-opioid-like-7oh-withdrawal].
