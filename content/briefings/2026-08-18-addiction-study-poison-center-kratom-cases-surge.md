U.S. poison center cases involving kratom rose from 19 in 2010 to 1,242 in 2023, a roughly 65-fold increase, according to a study published in the journal Addiction and described in a [release distributed by ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260814235902.htm) on August 18. The study, by Grant Comstock, Anthony P. Gulotta, Lisa E. Rein, and senior author Ryan Feldman of the Medical College of Wisconsin, also reported that severe outcomes rose from zero reported cases in 2010 to 158 in 2023. The poison center data as reported do not specify whether the exposures involved natural leaf kratom or concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, a distinction that materially changes the products' risk profiles and that sits at the center of current federal and state regulatory activity.

Among exposures involving kratom alone, nearly one in seven resulted in hospital admission and roughly one in 16 required critical care, the researchers found, per the release. Feldman said in the release that "evidence shows kratom can cause serious health effects like seizures, irregular heart rhythms, liver damage, and breathing problems."

The study also examined regulation. States that banned kratom showed lower rates of exposure, severe outcomes, and healthcare use than states with permissive rules or no kratom-specific regulation, according to the release. The release notes that kratom is not scheduled under the federal Controlled Substances Act and is not approved for any medical use by the FDA, leaving regulation largely to the states.

## Data ends before the current 7-OH regulatory wave

The study period runs through 2023, before the wave of federal and state action aimed at concentrated 7-OH products. The DEA's temporary Schedule I order for concentrated and synthetic 7-OH [remained unpublished as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished], and states have moved on their own in the interim: Massachusetts placed [all forms of kratom into state Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] by emergency order this month, citing kratom-associated deaths. Whether the poison center trend the study documents reflects leaf products, concentrated derivatives, or both is a question the release does not address, and it is likely to figure in how the findings are used in the policy debate.
