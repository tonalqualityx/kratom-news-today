The DEA's temporary order placing concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) into Schedule I had not been published as of August 18, according to [Federal Register document listings](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13580/schedules-of-controlled-substance-temporary-placement-of-7-hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified) for the compound, which still show the agency's July 6 notices of intent as the most recent entries. The pending action targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations above specified thresholds, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf, and botanical leaf below those thresholds is excluded, per the DEA's [published notices][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule].

[Pain News Network reported on August 17](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/8/17/the-waiting-game-dea-scheduling-of-7-oh-could-come-soon) that the order could publish around August 24. The outlet based the projection on the DEA's recent temporary scheduling of O-desmethyltramadol, an opioid the agency moved against on a 49-day timeline, from a June 24 notice of intent to an August 12 classification. Applying the same pace to the 7-OH notices published July 6 yields a late-August window. Pain News Network framed the date as an estimate rather than anything the DEA has announced.

The statutory clock has been open since [August 5, the earliest date the order could issue][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5] under the 30-day minimum in the notices of intent. Once published, the temporary Schedule I placement would take effect immediately and run for two years, with a possible one-year extension, per the notices. The companion notice covers three synthetic kratom-derived compounds, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, at any concentration.

## Stakeholders describe a waiting game

Pain News Network described stakeholders as caught in a waiting game, with consumers who use 7-OH products for self-managed pain fearing the loss of access, according to the outlet's reporting. Jeff Smith, executive director of the Holistic Alternative Recovery Trust, disputed the emergency rationale for the action, telling Pain News Network that "the data doesn't appear to bear out that there is great harm occurring, or that this is any kind of emergency."

The federal delay has not slowed state action. Massachusetts placed [all forms of kratom into state Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] by emergency order effective late August, a broader sweep than the pending federal action, and North Dakota's Legislative Management committee [voted unanimously on August 17][north-dakota-legislative-management-advances-kratom-package] to send a kratom package to that state's September 2 special session. New data is also feeding the policy debate: a study in the journal Addiction reported a [65-fold rise in U.S. poison center kratom cases][addiction-study-poison-center-kratom-cases-surge] between 2010 and 2023.
