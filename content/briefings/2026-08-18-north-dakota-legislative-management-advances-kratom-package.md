North Dakota's Legislative Management committee voted 16-0 on August 17 to send a two-bill kratom package to the special legislative session convening September 2, [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/17/1341872/). The unanimous vote narrows the field from the [four kratom bills filed for the session][north-dakota-special-session-four-kratom-bills] and settles the working group's approach: Schedule I status for synthetic kratom derivatives, including concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, alongside a regulated market for natural leaf kratom. Concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations are distinct from the trace amounts of 7-OH that occur naturally in kratom leaf, and the package treats the two categories differently.

The first bill, sponsored by Sen. Michelle Axtman, would add chemical derivatives of kratom such as 7-OH to the state's Schedule I controlled substances list, according to KFGO. Penalties would be tiered: an infraction for a first offense, a Class A misdemeanor for a second, and a Class C felony for subsequent offenses. Axtman said the graduated structure is meant to give young adults time to adjust to changing laws while public health campaigns roll out, rather than immediately imposing felony charges, per KFGO. Separate special session coverage from [KNOX Radio](https://knoxradio.com/2026/08/17/1364486/) reported that the proposal would place mitragynine synthetic products on the same level as heroin and LSD, with the Class C felony tier carrying up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine for repeat possession offenses.

The second bill, sponsored by Rep. Pat Heinert, would regulate natural leaf kratom rather than ban it, KFGO reported. It would restrict sales to buyers 21 and older, mandate product labeling, and place retailer licensing and enforcement under the state attorney general. The bill would also allocate $20,000 to the state Department of Health and Human Services for a youth-focused public health outreach campaign. KNOX Radio reported that selling to minors would carry infractions with fines up to $1,000.

## From emergency ban to statutory framework

The package would replace the current emergency posture with a permanent statutory one. Gov. Kelly Armstrong's [executive order banning all kratom products statewide][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom] took effect August 5, and the special session he called for September 2 is tasked with enacting a lasting framework. If the two-bill package passes as advanced, natural leaf kratom would return to North Dakota's retail market under licensing, labeling, and age controls, while synthetic derivatives would remain prohibited as Schedule I substances.

Not every legislator has endorsed the special session route for every item on the agenda. Senate Majority Leader David Hogue, discussing the session's agenda broadly, told KNOX Radio that matters able to wait should benefit from the more thorough review of the regular legislative process.

The committee's vote comes as the federal picture remains unsettled. The DEA's temporary scheduling order for concentrated 7-OH and three synthetic kratom-derived compounds [had not been published as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished], leaving states such as North Dakota to legislate ahead of the federal action their officials have cited.
