Local boards of health across Massachusetts are preparing to enforce the state's [emergency order placing all forms of kratom into Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] when it takes effect on August 28, according to municipal notices and local reporting published this week. The [Shrewsbury Post reported](https://shrewsburypost.com/2026/08/18/kratom-possession-and-sales-to-become-illegal-in-massachusetts/) that possession, distribution, and sale of kratom become illegal statewide on that date, with violators subject to imprisonment or fines, and that the order may remain in force through August 28, 2027.

A [notice posted by the Town of Williamstown](https://williamstownma.gov/massachusetts-emergency-order-classifies-kratom-as-a-schedule-i-controlled-substance/) details the order's scope. Issued by the Department of Public Health under Chapter 94C, Section 2A, the order declares kratom "an imminent hazard to the public safety" and temporarily designates it a Schedule I drug under state law. Per the notice, the order covers any product held out to be kratom and any product containing Mitragyna speciosa, mitragynine, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, the analogs known as MGM-15 and MGM-16, and their isomers, esters, ethers, and salts. Williamstown's community development and health officials plan to begin enforcement against establishments that continue selling kratom after August 28, according to the notice.

## Boards of health gear up before the deadline

[iBerkshires reported](https://www.iberkshires.com/story/83616/Local-Authorities-Responding-to-State-s-Action-on-Kratom.html) that Berkshires-area health officials are preparing for shelf removal statewide, with one official explaining that "effective the 28th, they can't have kratom in the entire state on their shelves." The one-year emergency period is intended in part to give the Legislature time to consider permanent regulation, per the outlet. The Shrewsbury Post reported that municipalities and local boards of health are explicitly empowered to take enforcement action against licensed businesses, and that the order could affect licenses held by food, retail, and other commercial establishments that possess or distribute kratom during the order period.

Some towns are moving ahead of the state timeline. The [Franklin Observer reported](https://franklinobserver.town.news/g/franklin-town-ma/n/384931/franklin-beats-state-kratom-crackdown), under the headline "Franklin beats state kratom crackdown," that Franklin's local action on kratom preceded the state's, with the town engaging directly with the Department of Public Health around enforcement. The [Cambridge Public Health Department circulated an update](https://www.cambridgepublichealth.org/public-health-updates-as-of-august-19-2026/) on August 19 describing kratom as a dangerous, largely unregulated product and noting that state public health officials had launched new kratom resources. Editorial support has followed the order as well: the [East Boston Times-Free Press](https://eastietimes.com/2026/08/19/gov-healey-does-the-right-thing-to-ban-kratom/) praised Governor Maura Healey's administration for removing what it called "a dangerous and highly-addictive substance from store shelves," arguing that manufacturers sell highly-potent extract powders, tablets, and gummies that isolate and concentrate 7-OH.

## A broader sweep than the pending federal action

The Massachusetts order covers natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, a reach the Williamstown notice makes explicit by listing the plant, its principal alkaloids, and the synthetic analogs together. That approach goes further than the DEA's pending federal action, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations above specified thresholds and excludes botanical kratom below them, and [which had not yet published in the Federal Register as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished]. The trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf is distinct from the concentrated and synthetic preparations that federal regulators have moved against, and the Massachusetts order removes that distinction within the state by scheduling all of it.

The federal timing is shaping decisions elsewhere. In Florida, [352today reported](https://352today.com/news/257752-marion-county-just-hit-pause-on-a-kratom-ban-heres-why/) that Marion County officials paused consideration of a county kratom ban, in part because the DEA's temporary scheduling order for concentrated 7-OH had not yet taken effect in the Federal Register, leaving the county to work from the Florida attorney general's synthetic-focused emergency rule in the meantime.
