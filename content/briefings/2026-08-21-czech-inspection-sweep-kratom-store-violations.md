Czech authorities have completed a nationwide inspection of roughly 1,000 stores selling kratom and similar substances the country treats as borderline legal, and found violations at approximately 63 percent of the outlets inspected, [Expats.cz reported on August 20](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/czech-news-in-brief-for-august-20-2026-thursday-top-morning-headlines). According to the outlet, 373 of the cases were serious enough to be classified as criminal offenses.

The scale of the seizures was substantial. Expats.cz reported that authorities confiscated nearly half a million products in the course of the campaign and blocked 25 online stores. Interior Minister Lubomír Metnar said the sweep exposed gaps that let sellers skirt existing rules, according to the outlet, and the findings have prompted 26 proposed legislative changes.

The enforcement campaign feeds into a broader regulatory effort. Expats.cz reported that the results will inform a proposed law known as "lex kratom," expected to take effect in January, which would formalize stricter regulation of the category and introduce standardized age verification for online sales.

The reporting does not detail what share of the violations involved kratom specifically, as the inspections covered a range of substances sold in the same retail channels, and it does not describe the nature of the individual violations beyond the criminal classifications. The sweep arrives as retail-level scrutiny of kratom intensifies in the United States as well, where [Naperville, Illinois's city council is debating a retail sales ban][naperville-council-debates-kratom-retail-ban] amid a wave of municipal action.
