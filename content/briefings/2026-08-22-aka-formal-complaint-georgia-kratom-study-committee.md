The American Kratom Association (AKA) filed a formal complaint with Georgia House Speaker Jon Burns on August 21 regarding Chairman Rick Townsend's conduct as head of the House Blue-Ribbon Study Committee on Youth Exposure to Kratom and Retail Available Substances, according to a [report carried by Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/american-kratom-association-files-formal-220600049.html).

The complaint alleges bias in how the chairman is administering the study committee, per the report. The association's position, as described in the report, is that the committee is predisposed toward portraying kratom as a public health threat rather than neutrally evaluating scientific evidence and regulatory options. Those characterizations are the AKA's; Townsend's response, if any, was not included in the report.

The study committee's remit covers youth exposure to kratom and the broader category of retail-available substances in Georgia. The complaint does not cite specific bill text, according to the report, which noted that study committees of this type are typically precursors to formal legislation. The dispute reflects a tension between consumer-advocacy framings focused on adult access to natural leaf kratom and risk-focused framings centered on youth access, addiction, and safety, per the report.

The complaint lands amid sustained attention to kratom in Georgia. Earlier this month, [state-level reporting examined proposed 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) limits and a testing gap in Georgia's enforcement apparatus][georgia-kratom-investigation-7oh-limits-testing-gap]. Any recommendations the study committee produces, on age restrictions, labeling, or specific alkaloids, would arrive against that backdrop.
