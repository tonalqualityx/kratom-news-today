Albany County, New York will ban the sale and distribution of all kratom products under a public health order announced by the county executive on August 22, [News10 reported](https://www.news10.com/news/albany-county/public-health-order-to-ban-the-sale-of-kratom-in-albany-county/). The order, to be issued by the county Department of Health, takes effect seven days after it is signed, according to the station. Health officials cited at least one death in Albany County in 2026 linked to a kratom product, News10 reported. The station's reporting does not specify whether the product involved was a concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that materially changes a product's risk profile.

The order covers all kratom products, according to News10, which described kratom as a plant whose leaves contain the alkaloid mitragynine along with trace amounts of 7-OH. County officials characterized kratom as a danger to life and health and pointed to FDA warnings about serious risks associated with concentrated 7-OH, including respiratory depression, addiction, seizures, and death, per the station. The order also cites the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) announced intent to temporarily place certain high-potency 7-OH products in Schedule I as an "imminent hazard to public safety," while noting that the federal action had not yet taken effect. Concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in raw leaf; Albany County's order reaches natural leaf products as well. The [DEA's temporary scheduling order remained unpublished in the Federal Register as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished].

The ban targets sale and distribution rather than individual possession, News10 reported, with enforcement vested in the Albany County Department of Health, which will oversee compliance by businesses in the county.

In Oneida County, County Executive Anthony J. Picente Jr. signed a local law on August 19 prohibiting the sale of kratom and kratom-derived products throughout the county, according to a [county press release](https://oneidacountyny.gov/news/picente-signs-local-law-prohibiting-sale-of-kratom/). The signing follows the [county Board of Legislators' unanimous August 12 vote][oneida-county-unanimous-countywide-kratom-sales-ban] to adopt the ban. The law prohibits the sale or offer for sale of kratom, products containing kratom, and products derived from kratom by any person or business countywide, with an exception for any FDA-approved kratom product dispensed by a licensed pharmacist pursuant to a physician's prescription, per the release. The FDA has not approved any kratom product for medical use. The release lists the Oneida County Health Department as the contact point for businesses seeking compliance information.

The two actions extend a run of county-level restrictions in New York this summer. Earlier in August, [Dutchess County moved to ban concentrated and synthetic kratom products][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products], a narrower approach than the all-product bans in Albany and Oneida counties. Municipal deliberations continued elsewhere the same week: in Illinois, [nurses urged the Streator city council to ban kratom sales][streator-nurses-urge-city-council-kratom-ban], citing emergency room visits and poison center data.
