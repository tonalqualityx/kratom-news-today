Nurses urged the city council in Streator, Illinois on August 20 to ban kratom sales, citing emergency room visits and withdrawal cases, [Shaw Local reported](https://www.shawlocal.com/illinois-valley/2026/08/20/streator-nurses-urge-city-council-to-ban-kratom-citing-er-visits-withdrawal-cases/). Much of the data the nurses presented concerned concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, preparations that contain the alkaloid at concentrations far above the trace levels naturally present in raw kratom leaf, though the ban they requested would cover kratom sales generally.

According to Shaw Local, the nurses presented data showing 13 percent of recorded kratom exposures resulted in severe outcomes, and that states with retail bans had significantly lower poison-center exposures and hospitalizations. Since national tracking of 7-OH began, 53 poison center cases related to 7-OH were reported in a three-month span, and 7-OH was 13 times more likely than regular kratom to cause severe outcomes, the nurses told the council, per the outlet. Poison-center figures have drawn national attention this month: [a study in the journal Addiction reported a roughly 65-fold rise in U.S. poison center kratom cases from 2010 to 2023][addiction-study-poison-center-kratom-cases-surge].

The nurses also pointed to the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) stated intent to classify high-concentration 7-OH products as Schedule I, a category that includes heroin, LSD, and MDMA, Shaw Local reported. They told the council there is no FDA-approved treatment for kratom withdrawal and no reversal agent, leaving hospital care limited to symptomatic management, per the outlet.

Streator's deliberation adds to municipal activity on kratom in Illinois and beyond. The [Naperville city council debated a retail kratom ban][naperville-council-debates-kratom-retail-ban] the same week, and in New York, [Albany County announced a public health order banning kratom sales countywide][albany-county-public-health-order-kratom-ban].
