The Arlington, Massachusetts Board of Health voted unanimously on August 19 to adopt regulations prohibiting the manufacturing, sale, and distribution of kratom in the town, with the prohibition taking effect August 20, [according to a town announcement](https://www.arlingtonma.gov/Home/Components/News/News/16208/16). The town framed the regulations as a public health measure adopted in coordination with the statewide effort to restrict kratom sales, per the announcement.

More than 25 Massachusetts communities have already adopted similar local bans, according to the Arlington announcement, which noted that the forthcoming statewide restriction will overlay the local regulations. [Massachusetts Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein issued an emergency order on August 13][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] temporarily placing all forms of kratom into Schedule I under state law. Governor Maura Healey announced the order alongside public education efforts about kratom health risks and connections to treatment and recovery services, [according to the Massachusetts Municipal Association](https://www.mma.org/gov-announces-kratom-sale-restrictions-public-education-efforts/).

The statewide restriction takes effect August 28 after a 14-day notice period and remains in place for up to one year, making the distribution and sale of kratom illegal across the commonwealth, [according to a municipal notice posted by the town of Dalton](https://dalton-ma.gov/statewide-restriction-on-kratom-effective-august-28-2026/). Once the order is in effect, local boards of health and other municipal officials gain authority to take enforcement action against retailers selling kratom products, per the Massachusetts Municipal Association. [Boards of health across the state have been preparing for statewide shelf removal][massachusetts-boards-of-health-prepare-kratom-enforcement] ahead of the effective date.

The Massachusetts order covers natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. That reach is broader than the Drug Enforcement Administration's pending federal action, which targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations above specified thresholds and excludes natural leaf kratom below those thresholds.

State approaches continue to diverge. In Wyoming, [retailers are clearing concentrated 7-OH products from shelves][wyoming-stores-clear-7oh-products-new-state-law] under a state law that caps 7-OH content at 2 percent and bans synthetically adulterated products rather than prohibiting kratom sales outright.
