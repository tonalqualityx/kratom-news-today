Retailers in Wyoming are removing concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from their shelves as new state restrictions take hold and a federal scheduling order remains pending, [according to an August 21 report from Narcotics.com](https://www.narcotics.com/wyoming-stores-clear-7-oh-kratom-products-under-new-bans/). The products being cleared are concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations, a category distinct from the trace amounts of 7-OH naturally present in raw kratom leaf, which the pending federal action does not cover.

The Wyoming law described in the report bars retailers from selling any kratom product to people under 21, bans products adulterated with synthesized or semi-synthesized alkaloids, and bans products with 7-OH composition above 2 percent. Violations are misdemeanors punishable by up to one year in jail, a $1,000 fine, or both, according to Narcotics.com. The law does not criminalize possession, the report notes, and enforcement falls to law enforcement and the state department of health.

The retail pullback comes ahead of federal action that has not yet taken effect. The [Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) issued a notice of intent on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] to temporarily place 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act. Narcotics.com reported that producing, dispensing, or possessing these substances would become a federal crime once the temporary order takes effect, and that the notice specifically states raw leaf kratom is not covered. The [temporary order had still not been published in the Federal Register as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished]. Retailers are treating the notice of intent as a strong signal and pulling high-potency 7-OH products pre-emptively to avoid future federal criminal liability, according to the report.

Wyoming's controlled substances schedules automatically adopt federal scheduling changes unless the state attorney general objects within 30 days of the federal classification taking effect, Narcotics.com reported. The report describes the mechanism without indicating that the attorney general has taken or signaled any such action. If no objection is filed, concentrated 7-OH and the three related compounds would become Schedule I substances under Wyoming law once the federal order takes effect, per the report.

Federal health officials have drawn the same line between concentrated products and natural leaf. The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) commended the DEA's notices of intent and stated that the actions "are not intended to regulate natural leaf kratom that does not contain enhanced levels of 7-OH," [according to USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/life/health-wellness/2026/08/21/rfk-jr-kratom-7-oh/91374043007/), which reported on the federal posture in a piece updated August 21.

Wyoming's framework, as described by Narcotics.com, caps 7-OH content and targets adulterated products rather than prohibiting kratom sales outright, an approach narrower than the broader restrictions taking hold elsewhere. In Massachusetts, [Arlington's Board of Health adopted a townwide ban on all kratom products][arlington-board-of-health-townwide-kratom-ban] on August 19, days before a statewide order placing all forms of kratom in state Schedule I takes effect.
