The Justice Department announced on August 25 that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is temporarily placing three synthetic kratom-related compounds into Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act, according to a [department press release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds). The compounds are mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15, and MGM-16, which the department describes as "7-hydroxymitragynine-related substances." The release frames the emergency scheduling as directed at deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products, not traditional botanical kratom. The action finalizes an order the DEA [first announced on July 1][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] and that [had not appeared in the Federal Register as of August 18][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished].

The temporary order will be published in the Federal Register on August 26, 2026, take immediate effect, and remain in force through August 26, 2028, according to a [press release from the Global Kratom Coalition](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/global-kratom-coalition-applauds-dea-230500305.html) (GKC). Under Schedule I placement, the Justice Department said, the manufacture, distribution, and possession of the three compounds become subject to the controls and criminal and civil sanctions that apply to that schedule.

The GKC, which applauded the order, emphasized its scope in the coalition's release. The order names three specific synthetic substances by chemical structure, compounds the coalition says do not occur naturally in the kratom plant, and it does not schedule natural kratom leaf or leaf-based products such as powders, capsules, and teas that have not undergone post-harvest chemical manipulation. The action "is not a ban on unaltered natural kratom leaf itself," the coalition said, characterizing the order as a targeted move against novel synthetic alkaloid products.

One piece of the DEA's July initiative remains open. The agency's July notice also announced an intent to schedule concentrated synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), the alkaloid that occurs only in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf, and that portion has not been finalized, according to the GKC release, which said the final comment period runs until September 10, 2026. The federal Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health [extended the public comment period on the proposed 7-OH threshold through September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10], a move Pain News Network reported will likely push back the DEA's timing on the concentrated 7-OH portion.
