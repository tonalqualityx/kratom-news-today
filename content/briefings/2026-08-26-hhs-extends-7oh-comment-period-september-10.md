The Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health (OASH) at the Department of Health and Human Services is extending the public comment period on the proposed threshold for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) allowed in commercial kratom products, [Pain News Network reported on August 25](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/8/25/expansion-of-public-comment-period-buys-7-oh-advocates-more-time). The extension adds 15 days and will be formalized in a notice to be published in the Federal Register, reopening the comment window through September 10, according to the outlet.

The extension is not binding on the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), Pain News Network noted, but it likely means the agency will postpone its plan to classify concentrated 7-OH products as Schedule I substances until the extended comment period is over. The outlet reported that the additional time gives pain patients and advocacy groups more room to argue for the continued sale of certain concentrated kratom alkaloid products, 7-OH in particular.

The threshold at issue defines which products would fall under the DEA's pending temporary scheduling action. The framework [published in the Federal Register in July][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule] covers products containing more than 0.050 percent 7-OH by dry weight or more than 1 milligram of 7-OH per finished unit. Those thresholds are designed to reach concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations, a category distinct from natural kratom leaf, which contains the alkaloid only in trace amounts. An [earlier comment window on the proposal closed July 31][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5].

The extension arrived in the same news cycle as a federal action on a related front. The DEA [finalized its temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-derived compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] on August 25, while the concentrated 7-OH portion of the agency's July initiative remains unfinalized, according to a [Global Kratom Coalition release](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/global-kratom-coalition-applauds-dea-230500305.html) that put the final comment period's close at September 10, 2026.
