Four Massachusetts store owners have filed suit to block the state's one-year emergency ban on kratom sales, arguing that the public health commissioner exceeded his authority in issuing the order, [Law360 reported on August 24](https://www.law360.com/articles/2516867/mass-retailers-sue-to-block-kratom-sales-emergency-ban). The retailers are seeking to stop the ban before it takes effect this week, according to the outlet. The challenge targets the [emergency order Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein issued on August 13][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one], which temporarily designates all forms of kratom as Schedule I substances under state law for up to one year.

The order takes effect August 28, The Berkshire Eagle [reported on August 25](https://www.berkshireeagle.com/news/local/massachusetts-kratom-ban-berkshire-officials-treatment-concerns/article_2af1ff29-f668-4a42-8dcf-8984de7dd23c.html). The Department of Public Health cited the lack of regulation, the potential for addiction, and the opioid-like effects of the alkaloids mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) as its rationale, per the paper, and the order is designed to give local boards of health additional enforcement authority against retailers who continue selling kratom products. A [public health notice from the town of Whitman](https://www.whitman-ma.gov/167/Public-Health-Information) states that the rule, codified at 105 CMR 726.000, makes possession, distribution, or sale of kratom illegal in Massachusetts without a state controlled-substances registration as of August 28, with violators subject to imprisonment or fines.

Local enforcement is already under way. The Westfield and Southwick boards of health are ordering kratom products removed from convenience stores and gas stations, with a deadline of Friday, August 28, [MassLive reported on August 25](https://www.masslive.com/westfieldnews/2026/08/local-health-boards-remove-kratom-from-store-shelves.html). The shelf-clearing orders follow weeks of preparation by [local boards across the state][massachusetts-boards-of-health-prepare-kratom-enforcement] and come days after [Arlington's Board of Health adopted its own townwide ban][arlington-board-of-health-townwide-kratom-ban] ahead of the statewide effective date. For gas stations, smoke shops, and other retailers, the order ends kratom sales statewide once it takes hold, and the Law360 report described the pending lawsuit as an effort to win relief before that happens.

The Massachusetts order sweeps more broadly than current federal action. It covers natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, while the federal scheduling [finalized by the Drug Enforcement Administration this week][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] covers three synthetic kratom-derived compounds and leaves unaltered natural leaf unscheduled, according to the Justice Department's announcement. MassLive noted the distinction in its coverage: kratom leaf typically contains 1 to 2 percent mitragynine and only trace amounts of 7-OH, while the newly scheduled federal compounds are lab-created analogues the paper described as more potent.
