The American Kratom Association (AKA) has filed a lawsuit in federal court over the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds, [Bloomberg Law reported](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/kratom-group-warns-government-ban-threatens-market-in-lawsuit) on August 27. According to the report, the suit warns that the order covering mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15, and MGM-16 could sweep up natural kratom products unless it is clarified to exclude them, and it seeks to exempt the association's members from the temporary ban. The order [took effect on August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] upon publication in the Federal Register and runs through August 26, 2028.

The filing arrived one day after the association publicly applauded the same order. In an [August 26 statement distributed through AP's press release service](https://apnews.com/press-release/ez-newswire/press-release-0b3fee20e707b9dcdf71484c88a95a9c), the AKA praised the Justice Department and DEA for the emergency scheduling of what it called "dangerous 7-OH-related opioid compounds," describing the three substances as potent opioids chemically related to 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and distinct from unaltered natural kratom leaf. The two moves reflect the same stated position: the association supports scheduling the synthetic derivatives and wants federal action drawn narrowly enough to leave natural leaf products outside it.

In the [Federal Register notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-08-26/pdf/2026-17364.pdf), published August 26 in Volume 91, No. 164, the DEA states that placing the three compounds in Schedule I is "necessary to avoid an imminent hazard to public safety" and describes them as derivatives of the indole alkaloids of the Mitragyna speciosa plant. The Justice Department has characterized the action as directed at "deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products, not traditional botanical kratom," according to [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/feds-schedule-kratom-derivatives-as-opioids). Courthouse News also reported that the department said it would use enforcement discretion in cases where only incidental trace amounts of MGPI are found in otherwise botanical kratom products.

The federal suit lands amid litigation over kratom regulation at the state level. Four [Massachusetts retailers have sued][massachusetts-retailers-sue-emergency-kratom-ban] to block that state's emergency order placing all kratom products in state Schedule I, which takes effect August 28. The DEA's separate proposed action on concentrated 7-OH above a specified threshold also remains unfinalized, and the [public comment period on the proposed threshold runs through September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10].

Bloomberg Law's report places the lawsuit within a broader stretch of scrutiny for the industry, citing the Massachusetts litigation alongside an inquiry into industry ties inside the administration. [NutraIngredients reported](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/08/26/senators-inquiry-questions-kratom-industrys-influence-on-trump-administration/) on August 26 that a senator has opened an inquiry into the kratom industry's influence on the Trump administration, including Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin's financial stake in Oklahoma-based Botanic Tonics. Accounts differ on the stake's size: NutraIngredients described it as worth up to $1 million, while [The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/rfk-jr-bails-on-maha-event-with-maga-senator-ashley-moody-after-being-cornered-on-kratom/), citing earlier reporting by The New York Times, put it at more than $1 million.
