[WAER reported](https://www.waer.org/news/2026-08-25/oneida-county-moves-to-ban-sales-of-kratom) on August 25, 2026 that Oneida County Executive Anthony Picente Jr. has signed legislation prohibiting the sale of kratom and related products throughout the upstate New York county. The signing follows the [unanimous August 12, 2026 vote by the county Board of Legislators][oneida-county-unanimous-countywide-kratom-sales-ban] to enact the ban, which covers kratom products and derivatives of the plant and carries a civil penalty of $500 for a first violation, with subsequent violations drawing up to $1,000 per item confiscated, per the Syracuse public radio station.

Picente cited the products' availability in explaining the move, telling WAER that kratom is "sold right over the counter in local establishments" and noting that critics have called it "gas station heroin" because of its accessibility and low cost. The station reported that the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has advised against kratom use, citing risks including seizures and liver toxicity, and that no federal regulation of the products currently exists. Legislator Christopher Newton, who supported the ban, expressed hope for alternative solutions for chronic pain sufferers who use kratom, per WAER, echoing the position he took at the August vote.

## Search warrants in Utica two days later

On August 27, 2026, the county announced that a joint law-enforcement investigation had led to [search warrant executions at two Utica businesses](https://oneidacountyny.gov/news/joint-law-enforcement-investigation-leads-to-search-warrant-executions-at-two-utica-businesses/). According to the county's release, investigators seized 589 flavored vapor products and 30 packages of kratom products from UT-One Grocery and Deli on Williams Street, along with untaxed cigarettes, suspected cannabis, and U.S. currency. The release does not specify whether the seized kratom products were concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom. At Romano's Convenience on Oneida Street, the release lists roughly 50 packages of suspected synthetic cannabis, untaxed cigarettes, suspected cannabis, and currency among the items seized.

The investigation involved the Oneida County Sheriff's Office, the Oneida County District Attorney's Office, the Utica Police Department, the New York State Office of Cannabis Management, the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance, the Oneida County Health Department, the Utica Fire Department, Utica City Codes, and National Grid, per the release. Both businesses were sanctioned by the Office of Cannabis Management, and Romano's Convenience was shut down with its power disconnected. No criminal charges had been filed at the time of the release, which describes the investigation as ongoing.

The release also does not state the legal basis for the kratom seizure, whether the county's new local law, the health department order in effect since June 5, 2026, state law, or another authority. The product-type distinction noted above matters elsewhere in New York, where some jurisdictions restrict only concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, but not under Oneida County's ban, which covers natural leaf as well.

## County enforcement while the federal picture stays unsettled

The signing and the seizures land in a county where kratom sales were already barred under a health department order in effect since June 5, 2026, which the new local law strengthens. Approaches differ across New York: [Dutchess County banned concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while leaving natural leaf legal for adults][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products], and [Albany County issued a public health order barring kratom sales][albany-county-public-health-order-kratom-ban] last week. At the state level, WAER noted that Governor Kathy Hochul signed legislation in December restricting kratom sales to buyers 21 and older and requiring ingredient labeling with addiction warnings.

The county-level activity proceeds against an unsettled federal backdrop. The Drug Enforcement Administration's [temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds took effect August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] upon publication in the Federal Register, the [comment period on the proposed federal 7-OH threshold runs through September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10] per the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health's extension notice, and questions about the administration's kratom industry ties [cut short a press event with the HHS secretary in Tampa this week][rfk-tampa-press-event-kratom-donation-questions]. For retailers in Oneida County, the practical picture is narrower: the county's new local law and the health department order in effect since June 5, 2026 together bar kratom sales countywide, and this week's warrants show county and state agencies acting on retail inventory in the meantime.
