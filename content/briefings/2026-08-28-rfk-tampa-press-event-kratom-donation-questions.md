A press availability with Health and Human Services (HHS) Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. at a Make America Healthy Again (MAHA) event in Tampa ended abruptly this week after questioning turned to his ties to the kratom industry, [The Daily Beast reported](https://www.thedailybeast.com/rfk-jr-bails-on-maha-event-with-maga-senator-ashley-moody-after-being-cornered-on-kratom/) on August 26. According to the report, Sky News correspondent Martha Kelner pressed Kennedy about a $1 million donation to the MAHA PAC from the owner of Feel Free, a kratom-infused tonic made by Botanic Tonics, and about the Justice Department's decision to drop its case against the company. Kennedy responded, "I don't know anything about either of the things that you just said," and added, "I don't make decisions for the Justice Department," per the outlet.

Florida Senator Ashley Moody, who appeared alongside Kennedy, ended the event after Kelner asked whether the secretary was "in the pocket of Feel Free," The Daily Beast reported. [Geo.tv reported](https://www.geo.tv/latest/679533-rfk-jr-exits-press-event-after-kratom-company-donation-question) that the press gaggle, held at a Tampa elementary school, lasted roughly 12 minutes before Moody cut it short. Tampa community radio station [WMNF likewise tied](https://www.wmnf.org/rfk-jr-ends-tampa-qa-when-asked-about-kratom-ties/) the disruption to questions about Kennedy's links to Botanic Tonics and Feel Free.

## The donation and the dropped case

According to Geo.tv, Kelner's question concerned whether the Justice Department's move to drop its case against Botanic Tonics was related to a subsequent $1 million donation to Kennedy's MAHA PAC from company founder J.W. Ross. Geo.tv, citing reporting by The New York Times, said the contribution represented roughly 44 percent of the PAC's fundraising through April. Kennedy said he had no knowledge of the donation or the dropped case, per both outlets.

The exchange adds to a stretch of scrutiny of kratom industry connections inside the administration. As [reported in yesterday's briefing][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order], a senator has opened an inquiry into the industry's influence on the Trump administration, including Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin's financial stake in Botanic Tonics, which The Daily Beast, citing earlier reporting by The New York Times, described as worth more than $1 million. Geo.tv reported that a Democratic member of the Senate Finance Committee has opened an investigation into the connection between the dropped case and the donation.

## Why the questions land at HHS

Kennedy's department sits at the center of pending federal kratom policy. The Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health, within HHS, requested the extension of the [public comment period on the proposed 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) threshold][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10], which now runs through September 10, while the Drug Enforcement Administration's [temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] took effect August 26. Geo.tv noted that the administration's announced action targets synthesized 7-OH, a concentrated compound distinct from the trace 7-OH in natural kratom leaf, and that such products are sold by competitors of Botanic Tonics rather than by the company itself.

Feel Free has drawn criticism of its own: The Daily Beast reported that critics have labeled the product "gas station heroin." Botanic Tonics has positioned itself on the natural-leaf side of the category's dividing line. In an [August 25 statement](https://www.eznewswire.com/newsroom/botanic-tonics-commends-dea-action-synthetic-opioids-kratom), the company commended the DEA's scheduling of mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15, and MGM-16, said its flagship Feel Free Classic is made from natural kratom leaf and noble kava root and contains none of the three compounds, and said the federal action does not apply to traditional, natural kratom leaf formulations. "What the DEA acted on this week were lab-made opioids dressed up in kratom packaging to fool people looking for a natural alternative," the company said in the release.

The Tampa exchange comes during a week in which kratom policy has moved at every level of government, from the federal scheduling action and its litigation to state and local enforcement, including [Oneida County's newly signed countywide sales ban and a seizure of kratom products in Utica][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures]. None of the outlets reported a timeline for the Senate Finance Committee inquiry's next steps.
