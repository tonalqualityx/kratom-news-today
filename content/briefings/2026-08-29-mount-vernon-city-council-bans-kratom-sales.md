The city council in Mount Vernon, Washington voted unanimously on August 26 to ban the sale and distribution of all kratom products within city limits, [My Bellingham Now reported](https://mybellinghamnow.com/news/297792-mount-vernon-moves-to-ban-sale-distribution-of-kratom/). The ordinance adds a new chapter to the municipal code prohibiting any person or organization from selling, distributing, or advertising kratom in the Skagit County city, according to the [Skagit Valley Herald](https://www.goskagit.com/news/mount-vernon-city-council-bans-crypto-kiosks-sale-of-kratom-products/article_6daa61ab-5da1-4ca2-b1bb-7d0d36caa127.html). The council banned cryptocurrency kiosks at the same meeting, per the Herald.

The Herald reported that the ordinance cites the Food and Drug Administration's determination that 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) is a dangerous alkaloid, along with the Drug Enforcement Administration's new Schedule I placement of three related products. Those federal reference points are narrower than the city's ban. The [DEA's temporary Schedule I order that took effect August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] covers three synthetic kratom-related compounds, and the Justice Department has said the action targets deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. Concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products are also distinct from the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf. Mount Vernon's ordinance, as described in both reports, draws no such line: it covers all kratom products, natural leaf included.

Neither report describes penalties, an effective date, or enforcement mechanics, and neither includes comment from retailers currently selling kratom in the city.

## Another municipality moves ahead of state and federal rules

Mount Vernon joins a growing list of municipalities acting locally while state and federal frameworks remain unsettled. The pattern has accelerated through August: [Arlington, Massachusetts' board of health enacted a townwide ban][arlington-board-of-health-townwide-kratom-ban] before that state's blanket order took effect, and [Oneida County, New York signed a countywide sales ban last week][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures] as local enforcement began seizing products. Local ordinances of this kind vary on the question Mount Vernon's council answered with a full ban: whether to restrict only concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products or to bar natural leaf as well.
