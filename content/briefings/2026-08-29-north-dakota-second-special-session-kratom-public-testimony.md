North Dakota's Legislature is preparing for its second special session of the year, called by Governor Kelly Armstrong specifically for lawmakers to address kratom and synthetic products, the [North Dakota Monitor reported via KFGO](https://kfgo.com/2026/08/29/north-dakota-legislature-prepares-for-2nd-special-session-this-year-with-public-testimony-tuesday/) on August 29. Public testimony on the kratom bills is scheduled for Tuesday, followed by a House-Senate joint policy committee on Wednesday, per the report.

The session will take up two kratom bills, according to the Monitor. One would restrict natural kratom sales to adults 21 and older. The other would prohibit synthetic products, including 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), the alkaloid present only in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf but sold in concentrated and synthetic forms that have become the focus of federal and state regulators. That bill also sets criminal penalties and enforcement mechanisms, per the Monitor. The two-track structure matches the package [North Dakota's Legislative Management committee advanced on a 16-0 vote][north-dakota-legislative-management-advances-kratom-package] earlier this month, which paired a synthetic-derivatives scheduling bill with a natural-leaf regulation bill, and follows the [broader slate of kratom proposals submitted for the special session][north-dakota-special-session-four-kratom-bills].

## Armstrong frames the session as closing a loophole

Armstrong has linked kratom and synthetic derivatives to overdoses and deaths in the state, and the Monitor reported that he called the session so the Legislature can address the products directly. He described his earlier emergency measures as temporary steps pending legislative action, per the report. The Monitor also reported that officials are targeting products that were readily available in gas stations and smoke shops before the governor's executive order.

The governor is positioning the state effort as consistent with federal policy. According to the Monitor, Armstrong said his actions align with the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary scheduling of several kratom-related compounds, and lawmakers working the bills are presenting the legislation as closing perceived loopholes that federal action leaves open. The [DEA's temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds took effect August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], and the Justice Department has said that order targets deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

The KFGO report is the only account of the session preparations in this briefing's source set, so the framing above is the Monitor's. What the reporting describes, though, is a state taking the split-track approach several others have debated: age-gating natural leaf while prohibiting synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products outright. North Dakota convenes in a week when [Massachusetts' broader emergency order, which covers natural leaf and synthetic products alike, took effect][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect] amid a retailer court challenge.
