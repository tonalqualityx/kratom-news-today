The district attorneys of Bucks and Chester counties sent formal letters to Pennsylvania Secretary of Health Dr. Debra Bogen on August 31, 2026, urging the state Department of Health to "immediately exercise its emergency powers to schedule 7-Hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and related kratom derivatives as controlled substances" under state law, according to a [joint news release](https://www.buckscounty.gov/m/newsflash/home/detail/1612) from the two offices. The request from Bucks County District Attorney Joe Khan and Chester County District Attorney Christopher de Barrena-Sarobe targets concentrated 7-OH products, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf. The release draws that line itself, describing 7-OH as a concentrated alkaloid found naturally in trace amounts in the Southeast Asian plant and stating that products containing it produce "opioid-like effects on the body comparable to morphine or heroin."

The letters follow the Drug Enforcement Administration's [temporary Schedule I order covering three synthetic kratom-related compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, which took effect August 26, 2026. The release notes that the federal action did not schedule 7-OH itself, and argues that local law enforcement generally cannot act on federal violations without a matching state statute. "We can't allow a loophole in the law to leave our residents vulnerable," de Barrena-Sarobe said in the release. "7-OH is a dangerous substance." At the federal level, a separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above specified concentration thresholds remains pending, with the [public comment period open through September 10, 2026][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10].

The prosecutors center their case on youth access. According to the release, 7-OH products are marketed without age restrictions, packaged as "gummies, vapes, and candy bars specifically designed to appeal to minors," and sold at smoke shops, gas stations, and other retailers without ID verification. "With school back in session and smoke shops open for business selling 7-OH and kratom products to our kids, there is no time for delay," Khan said in the release.

The letters cite recent state actions as precedent. The release points to Massachusetts, where an [emergency public health order designating kratom and five related substances as Schedule I took effect August 28, 2026][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect], and says North Dakota and Florida have implemented similar restrictions. In North Dakota, [the legislature is preparing a special session called specifically to address kratom and synthetic products][north-dakota-second-special-session-kratom-public-testimony], with the governor describing his earlier emergency measures as temporary steps pending legislative action. The Pennsylvania request is narrower in scope than the Massachusetts order: the letters ask for scheduling of a specific alkaloid and its derivatives, and the release does not describe a request that would cover natural leaf kratom products.

Kratom advocacy organizations have drawn a similar line around the federally scheduled compounds. The American Kratom Association's [federal lawsuit over the DEA's emergency scheduling order][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order] does not contest the scheduling of the three compounds. Mac Haddow, the group's senior fellow on public policy, said "the AKA supports aggressive enforcement against chemically manipulated MGPI, MGM-15, and MGM-16 products," according to [kratom.news](https://kratom.news/aka-mgpi-lawsuit-botanical-kratom-schedule-i-2026/), while the suit asks the court to declare that the federal order does not reach botanical kratom containing only trace, naturally occurring amounts. The coverage available does not include an advocacy or industry response to the Pennsylvania letters.

The request lands in a state with no kratom-specific statute and growing pressure to act. Attorney General Dave Sunday [said in early August that his office was prepared to enforce the DEA's temporary scheduling of concentrated 7-OH][pennsylvania-7oh-enforcement-san-diego-smoke-shop-lawsuit], and the Pittsburgh Post-Gazette has reported [mounting kratom-associated deaths in Allegheny County as state lawmakers weigh regulation][allegheny-county-kratom-deaths-pennsylvania-regulation]. The district attorneys' offices prosecute under existing law; authority over emergency scheduling rests with the Department of Health, which had not acted on the request as of the release's publication.
