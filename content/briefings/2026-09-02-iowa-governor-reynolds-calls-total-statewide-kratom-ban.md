Iowa Governor Kim Reynolds called for a total statewide ban on kratom on September 1, 2026, in a joint announcement with the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services and the Department of Public Safety, according to a [state news release](https://hhs.iowa.gov/news-release/2026-09-01/governor-reynolds-state-officials-warn-public-about-rising-dangers-kratom-and-synthetic-7-oh) and reporting from the [Iowa Capital Dispatch](https://iowacapitaldispatch.com/2026/09/01/reynolds-calls-for-a-total-ban-on-kratom-and-synthetic-variants-in-iowa/). "Kratom is not a supplement, it is not a medicine, and it is not safe," Reynolds said in the release, which warned of what officials called increasing dangers posed by kratom and newly emerging, highly concentrated synthetic kratom-related products. In follow-up remarks reported by [KTIV](https://www.ktiv.com/2026/09/02/why-iowa-governor-wants-product-banned-thats-sold-some-gas-stations/) on September 2, the governor said she would support a full ban covering both organic and synthetic versions of the product.

The state's case rests on mortality and exposure data. According to the Iowa HHS release, officials have counted 45 kratom-associated deaths across 23 Iowa counties since 2024, including 16 deaths in the first seven months of 2026. The release also reported that Iowa Poison Control has received 68 kratom exposure calls this year, which the department characterized as a 467 percent increase over two years. The release did not break down how many of the reported deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products rather than natural leaf kratom, a distinction that has shaped the federal response to date.

The announcement leans on recent federal action while asking for more at the state level. The release cites the Drug Enforcement Administration's [temporary Schedule I order covering mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], three synthetic compounds derived from 7-OH, which took effect August 26, and notes that 7-OH itself remains under federal review for emergency scheduling, with a [public comment period on a proposed threshold approach open through September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10]. The [Des Moines Register](https://www.desmoinesregister.com/story/news/politics/2026/09/01/kratom-iowa-drug-ban-kim-reynolds/91554323007/) reported that Reynolds argued the federal scheduling supports stricter state action. Her proposal would reach further than the DEA's order, which federal officials have repeatedly described as targeting deliberately manufactured and concentrated products rather than botanical kratom. The American Kratom Association has drawn the same line from the other direction, [supporting enforcement against the scheduled synthetic compounds while suing to keep botanical kratom outside the order's reach][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order]. The Iowa release does not include a response from the AKA or any other advocacy organization to the state's announcement.

Kratom remains legal in Iowa for now. The Iowa Capital Dispatch reported that Senate File 2192, the chamber's kratom regulation bill, never reached a floor vote, leaving no state-level restrictions in place beyond the substances the DEA has separately scheduled. In the meantime, the HHS release said the state will expand its public health campaign with new messaging on kratom and synthetic 7-OH, and it urged retailers to review their shelves closely, cautioning that reliance on packaging claims is not sufficient to avoid selling federally controlled substances.

Iowa's move adds a governor's office to a lengthening list of state and local officials pressing beyond the federal action. A [statewide emergency order treating kratom as Schedule I took effect in Massachusetts in late August][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect], [prosecutors in two Pennsylvania counties have asked their state health department to emergency-schedule 7-OH and related derivatives][bucks-chester-das-urge-pennsylvania-emergency-7oh-scheduling], and a [county-level ban on kratom sales took effect in Albany County, New York, the same day as the Iowa announcement][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect].
