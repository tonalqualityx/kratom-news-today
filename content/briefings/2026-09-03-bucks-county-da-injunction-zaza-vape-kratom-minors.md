The Bucks County District Attorney's Office obtained an emergency preliminary injunction on September 2 against Zaza Vape & Cigar, Inc., shutting down sales of kratom and tobacco products to minors at the Buckingham Township, Pennsylvania business, according to the [office's announcement](https://crimewatch.net/us/pa/bucks/da/29567/post/bucks-county-da-shuts-down-kratom-sales-minors-historic-civil-enforcement-action-against). The office described the case as a historic civil enforcement action. The announcements do not specify whether the kratom products involved were concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that materially changes the products' risk profiles.

The civil action goes beyond the immediate shutdown. According to the DA's announcement, the office is seeking a permanent ban on the sale of controlled substances and kratom by the defendant business, along with civil penalties of up to $1,000 per violation, or $3,000 per violation where victims are 60 or older. [Fox29 Philadelphia reported](https://www.fox29.com/news/bucks-county-smoke-shop-shut-down-after-da-finds-illegal-kratom-tobacco-sales-youth) that the shop was found selling kratom, tobacco, and high-potency cannabis products to minors. [NBC Philadelphia](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/news/local/bucks-county-leaders-to-announce-civil-enforcement-action-to-protect-children/4457437/) covered the action as an enforcement move aimed at protecting children.

The DA's statement said the office will continue using civil and criminal enforcement tools to curb unregulated substances sold to minors, a category in which it explicitly included kratom. The injunction came two days after District Attorney Joe Khan and Chester County District Attorney Christopher de Barrena-Sarobe [urged Pennsylvania's health secretary on August 31 to emergency-schedule 7-OH and related kratom derivatives][bucks-chester-das-urge-pennsylvania-emergency-7oh-scheduling] under state law. In those letters, the prosecutors argued that the federal scheduling of three 7-OH-related compounds left an enforcement gap for local authorities; the Zaza Vape action shows the office reaching for civil enforcement remedies in the meantime.

The case adds Pennsylvania to a week in which state and local authorities moved against kratom retail through channels other than new legislation, from county public health orders in New York to statewide compliance sweeps in California.
