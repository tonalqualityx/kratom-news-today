California enforcement efforts have removed nearly 15,300 illegal hemp, kratom, and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from store shelves statewide, [Governor Gavin Newsom's office announced on September 2](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/02/governor-newsom-announces-nearly-15300-illegal-hemp-kratom-and-7-oh-products-removed-from-california-store-shelves/). The total breaks down to 7,888 prohibited kratom and 7-OH products and 7,403 illegal hemp products, according to the release. The announcement covers products in a combined category, and it does not specify how many of the removed kratom and 7-OH items were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations as opposed to natural leaf kratom products, a distinction that separates the trace 7-OH present in raw leaf from the high-potency formulations that have drawn most recent regulatory attention.

The governor's office described an enforcement campaign of 8,038 site visits focused on kratom and 7-OH compliance that produced 181 violations. Among businesses licensed by the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control, the state reported 97.75 percent compliance with the prohibition on kratom and 7-OH products and 99.33 percent compliance on illegal hemp products. Licensees that continue selling prohibited products face administrative discipline, including suspension or revocation of their licenses, according to the release.

The announcement frames California as "among the first states to take aggressive statewide enforcement action" against products containing kratom and 7-OH, and Newsom's office presented the campaign as part of an effort at "strengthening safeguards around intoxicating products." The release states that the targeted products have not been tested for safety, consistency, effectiveness, or accurate labeling and have not been approved for sale by the Food and Drug Administration. It also cites the California Department of Public Health's position that products containing kratom and 7-OH are linked to serious health risks, including overdose, addiction, and other adverse health outcomes. Those characterizations are the state's; the release does not cite specific case data.

For operators and retailers, the compliance figures sketch the practical effect: near-universal removal of the category from ABC-licensed venues, which include convenience stores, bars, and smoke shops. No major kratom manufacturer or trade organization was quoted in the announcement.

The sweep lands in a week of state and local action on the category.
