The Naperville, Illinois city council voted 8-0 on September 2 to ban the retail sale of kratom in the city, the [Chicago Tribune reported](https://www.chicagotribune.com/2026/09/02/naperville-council-ban-kratom-sales-products/). The ordinance takes effect October 1 and prohibits businesses from selling any products containing natural leaf or synthetic kratom, according to [NCTV17](https://www.nctv17.org/news/naperville-city-council-approves-ban-on-retail-sale-of-kratom/). Possession is treated differently from sale: adults 18 and older may still possess and consume kratom in the city, the Tribune reported.

The vote concludes a deliberation the council [opened in mid-August][naperville-council-debates-kratom-retail-ban], when members debated whether and how to restrict retail sale of products containing mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The outcome is a full retail ban covering both natural and synthetic forms, which puts Naperville in a stricter posture than jurisdictions that have limited their bans to concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products.

That breadth was the point of contention for the industry voice at the meeting. Allison Smith, director of government affairs for the Washington, D.C.-based Global Kratom Coalition, addressed the council before the vote, according to the Tribune. Smith argued that synthetic forms of the drug are "the root cause of the problems" rather than natural botanical products, and said that 7-OH levels found naturally in kratom leaves "never have a concentration greater than 2%," per the paper. Any product above that level should be considered synthetic or adulterated, Smith told the council. The position reflects a distinction the major kratom advocacy organizations have pressed throughout the year's regulatory wave: support for restrictions on concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, paired with opposition to measures that also remove natural leaf from shelves. The council's unanimous vote extends the ban to both.

[ABC7 Chicago's coverage](https://abc7chicago.com/post/what-is-kratom-naperville-illinois-city-council-votes-ban-sale-can-have-opioid-like-effects/19777791/) connected the local action to the Drug Enforcement Administration's recent [temporary Schedule I placement of three synthetic kratom-related compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], which took effect in late August. For Naperville retailers, the practical effect arrives in four weeks: under the ordinance, smoke shops, vape shops, and convenience stores carrying the category have until October 1 to clear kratom products from their shelves.

The vote came the same day [California's governor announced a statewide enforcement campaign][california-statewide-enforcement-removes-kratom-7oh-products] that has removed thousands of kratom and 7-OH products from store shelves.
