A North Dakota legislative committee has advanced a bill that would permanently ban all kratom and synthetic kratom products in the state, [KFGO reported on September 3](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/03/kratom-bills-advance-including-a-permanent-full-ban-in-north-dakota/). According to the station, lawmakers introduced amendments expanding Senate Bill 2408, which initially targeted synthetic derivatives, to include natural kratom, with the stated goal of a permanent full ban. The bill would classify kratom alkaloids and related derivatives at the same level as heroin and LSD under state law, KFGO reported.

A competing proposal also moved. That bill would allow natural kratom but regulate it, with sales limited to adults 21 and older, licensing and labeling requirements, criminal penalties, and enforcement authority for the attorney general, per KFGO. The two bills give the special session a clean choice between prohibition and a regulated adult market, the same two-bill structure the session [convened around last week][north-dakota-second-special-session-kratom-public-testimony].

The committee action followed Governor Kelly Armstrong's State of the State address on September 2, in which he urged lawmakers to ban kratom "in all its forms" and to avoid "political grandstanding" during the special session, [KFGO reported separately](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/02/armstrong-asks-the-legislature-to-ban-kratom-avoid-political-grandstanding-in-special-session-in-his-state-of-the-state-address/). If a full ban proved out of reach, Armstrong asked the legislature at a minimum to ban kratom sales to anyone under 21 and to pass a "truth and labeling" bill so consumers know what is in the products they buy, according to the station.

Federal scheduling is shaping the state debate. In joint House-Senate committee deliberations, the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary Schedule I placement of the synthetic mitragynine derivatives mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15, and MGM-16 was cited as justification for treating synthetic kratom compounds similarly under state law, according to [News From The States](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/governor-urges-permanent-ban-all-kratom-north-dakota). That federal order is itself in litigation: the American Kratom Association [filed a federal lawsuit in late August][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order] over its scope, and in a [September 2 press release](https://apnews.com/press-release/ez-newswire/press-release-be1eb80b4f68d39e04be222b49d7bc3c) the association said it is seeking declaratory and injunctive relief confirming the order does not apply to traditional botanical kratom products in which only incidental trace amounts of MGPI might be detectable by modern testing.

Both North Dakota bills remain pending. Either would need passage by both chambers and the governor's signature before taking effect.
