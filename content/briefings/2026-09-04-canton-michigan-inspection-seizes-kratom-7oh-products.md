Canton, Michigan police, the Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development (MDARD), and Michigan State Police inspected a Canton-area tobacco shop on August 28 and found hundreds of kratom and concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, along with hundreds of THC products allegedly being sold illegally, [WDIV reported](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2026/09/04/canton-police-crack-down-on-kratom-7-oh-sales-as-legal-questions-remain/) on September 3. MDARD seized the products, and the case will be referred to the Wayne County prosecutor's office for possible enforcement action, according to the station. The station did not name the retailer.

The station described the 7-OH items as concentrated products sold as tablets, gummies, powders, shots, and liquid extracts under the kratom label. Those preparations are distinct from the trace amounts of 7-OH that occur naturally in kratom leaf, a point WDIV made in its own explanation of the compound. The report groups the seized kratom and 7-OH items together and does not say how many of the products were natural leaf kratom versus concentrated 7-OH preparations.

## The legal basis

Michigan has not enacted a statewide ban specific to 7-OH, per WDIV. The station reported that state regulators instead take the position that kratom and other unapproved psychoactive substances cannot legally be marketed as food or dietary supplement ingredients under current Michigan law, which is the footing MDARD is using for the seizure. As KNT previously reported, a statewide kratom ban bill [passed the Michigan House earlier this year and remains pending in the state Senate][warren-michigan-kratom-ban-north-dakota-special-session], and Warren, Michigan advanced a local sales ban in July.

Federal action on 7-OH is also unresolved. WDIV noted that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is considering whether to place 7-OH under Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act and that the federal action has not taken effect. As KNT has reported, the DEA's [temporary Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] took effect August 26 without scheduling 7-OH itself, and the public comment period on the proposed 7-OH threshold [runs through September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10].

Canton police indicated that additional local enforcement may follow based on the alleged violations found in the inspection, WDIV reported.

The Michigan action is one of several state and local moves this week. In North Dakota, the Senate [passed a full kratom ban 46-1 while the House approved a regulation bill][north-dakota-senate-passes-full-kratom-ban-house-passes-regulation-bill] on September 3, as KNT reported separately, with final votes set for Friday.
