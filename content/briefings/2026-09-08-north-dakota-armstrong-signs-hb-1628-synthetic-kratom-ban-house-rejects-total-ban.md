North Dakota Governor Kelly Armstrong signed House Bill 1628 into law on the afternoon of September 4, 2026, [KFGO reported](https://kfgo.com/2026/09/04/1348823/), closing a three-day special session that ended with the House rejecting a permanent ban on all kratom. The new law classifies synthetic and semi-synthetic kratom derivatives, including concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, as Schedule I controlled substances under state law, and creates a licensing, testing, and labeling system for natural leaf kratom sold to adults 21 and older, according to the [governor's office](https://www.governor.nd.gov/news/updated-armstrong-signs-bill-banning-synthetic-kratom-restricting-natural-kratom-21-and-older). Natural leaf kratom contains 7-OH only in trace amounts, and the law caps that content at 0.05 percent on a dry-weight basis, [KVRR reported](https://www.kvrr.com/news/politics-elections/update-governor-armstrong-signs-bill-banning-synthetic-kratom/article_96e4b453-577c-43cf-b334-3d97fe055a7b.html).

The signing resolves the split KNT reported last week, when the [Senate passed a total ban 46-1 while the House passed the regulation bill 86-6][north-dakota-senate-passes-full-kratom-ban-house-passes-regulation-bill] and the chambers swapped bills for Friday votes.

## How the total ban failed

Senate Bill 2408, sponsored by Sen. Michelle Axtman, R-Bismarck, would have permanently banned kratom in every form and made kratom alkaloids and derivatives controlled substances at the same level as heroin. The House rejected it on the morning of September 4 by a 46-47 vote, [InForum reported](https://www.inforum.com/news/north-dakota/north-dakota-passes-kratom-regulation-bill-rejects-full-ban). [Prairie Public](https://news.prairiepublic.org/2026-09-04/north-dakota-house-kills-total-ban-on-kratom-complicating-special-session) put the tally at 47 opposed and 46 in favor, and [Valley News Live](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/09/04/house-chamber-kills-bill-2408-banning-all-forms-kratom/) recorded it as 46-47-1.

Lawmakers on both sides agreed that synthetic products should be banned, per InForum. The disagreement was over natural leaf. Rep. Kathy Frelich, R-Devils Lake, told the chamber that "to put a full ban in place without matching our citizens' needs is irresponsible," according to InForum. Rep. Daniel Johnston, R-Kathryn, urged colleagues, "Do not criminalize the leaf and our citizens." Rep. Eric Murphy, R-Grand Forks, pushed back on pressure from the governor's office, saying, "This is the legislative branch of government," per InForum. Supporters of the ban included Rep. Donald Longmuir, R-Stanley, who said, "By doing the full ban, we are helping people," and Rep. Austin Foss, D-Fargo, who described the bill plainly: "At its core, it is a full ban on kratom and synthetic products." Prairie Public reported that opponents argued the ban had not received a proper hearing and left out key voices, including members of the public who use kratom for pain.

With the ban dead, the Senate amended HB 1628 to add the synthetic products ban and passed it unanimously, and the House then concurred 91-2, according to InForum. As the House passed it on September 3, the bill had regulated natural leaf but did not address synthetic products, a gap InForum had flagged at the time.

## What the law requires

HB 1628, sponsored by Rep. Pat Heinert, R-Bismarck, sets a minimum purchase age of 21, requires ingredient labeling that lists mitragynine and 7-OH content verified by an accredited laboratory, prohibits packaging that resembles candy or appeals to children, and requires annual retailer licensing through the Attorney General's Office, according to the governor's office. Selling without a license is a Class B misdemeanor, per the release. KFGO reported that the retailer application fee is $10,000, that labels must include serving size and health disclaimers, and that the bill funds a $20,000 public health campaign. InForum described an over-the-counter clause that requires licensed seller assistance to access kratom products.

For the synthetic derivatives now scheduled under state law, KFGO reported that penalties start at an infraction with a fine of up to $1,000 for a first offense and rise to a Class C felony for three or more possession offenses, carrying up to five years in prison and a $10,000 fine. Heinert said banning synthetics mattered "because it's not good for our people," per KFGO.

The state's fiscal projections, as reported by KFGO, estimate $7 million in revenue from roughly 700 licensed retailers, up to $2.5 million in expenditures over two years, and as many as nine full-time employees for inspections and testing.

## When sales can resume

The law does not reopen the market immediately. Attorney General Drew Wrigley, whose office is responsible for rulemaking, told KFGO that "kratom remains banned in all of its forms" until the administrative process has been completed. Senate Majority Leader David Hogue, R-Minot, estimated the process at about six months, per KFGO. Rep. Brady Pyle, R-Casselton, vice chair of the rules committee, told InForum that the Legislature's administrative rules committee does not meet until December and that licensing rules may not be approved until March. Sen. Jeff Magrum, R-Hazelton, who has said publicly that he uses natural kratom, raised concerns about rulemaking delays, KFGO reported.

Accounts differ on the status of Armstrong's [August executive order banning all kratom][north-dakota-executive-order-bans-all-kratom], which took effect August 5. KFGO and InForum reported that Armstrong rescinded the emergency ban upon signing the bill. The American Kratom Association's account, by contrast, stated that the executive order would be rescinded once administrative rules are implemented, to allow compliant products to return. The governor's office release said the synthetic ban took effect with the signing and that natural kratom sales cannot resume until rules are in place. All three accounts agree that kratom sales remain prohibited in the state until rulemaking is complete.

## Reactions

Armstrong, who had asked lawmakers to ban kratom "in all its forms," called the result "a victory for public health and safety, keeping dangerous synthetic kratom products off the shelves and protecting our young people from the adverse and unknown long-term effects of natural kratom," in a statement from his office. "While we preferred a total ban on kratom, we appreciate the Legislature convening in special session and working hard to pass a meaningful law that protects the well-being of our citizens." He told reporters, "We got 90% of what we wanted," per InForum, and KFGO reported he indicated a total ban could return in the regular session that begins in January.

The American Kratom Association, which supported the regulation bill and the synthetic ban it now contains, issued a [press release](https://www.finanzwire.com/press-release/american-kratom-association-commends-north-dakota-legislature-for-rejecting-total-kratom-ban-and-passing-consumer-protection-legislation-ylaMw8M5tWf) commending the Legislature. Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy, said lawmakers "chose a responsible path that targets the products creating the greatest risk," and added, "This legislation represents meaningful progress, but implementation and enforcement will determine its success." The association's stance separates the two product categories: it backed the state's ban on concentrated and synthetic 7-OH while opposing a ban on natural leaf.

## The federal overlay

North Dakota's list of banned derivatives mirrors the three compounds in the Drug Enforcement Administration's [temporary Schedule I order that took effect August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. The state law goes further by scheduling concentrated 7-OH itself, which the DEA has not yet done; a federal threshold proposal for 7-OH remains open for public comment through September 10, 2026. In Colorado, where no comparable state law applies, a [Steamboat Pilot report this week tied two deaths to concentrated 7-OH tablets][yampa-valley-deaths-concentrated-7oh-tablets-colorado-shops] still sold in local shops.

The legislative record for HB 1628 is available on the [North Dakota Legislative Branch site](https://ndlegis.gov/assembly/69-2025/special-2/bill-overview/bo1628.html), which lists the Joint Policy Committee's 7-1-0 do-pass recommendation and the House's initial 86-6 vote.
