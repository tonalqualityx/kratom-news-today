Police in Greenbrier, Tennessee seized more than 350 packages of suspected illicit products in a raid on a commercial building, and preliminary state lab testing found a compound associated with products marketed as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) alongside fentanyl analogues, [WSMV reported](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/09/06/police-seize-350-packages-greenbrier-drug-raid-warn-public-these-specific-product-labels/) on September 6, 2026. The station's account, which credits local outlet Smokey Barn News, does not say whether the products were concentrated 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom, a distinction that separates trace-level 7-OH in raw leaf from the high-potency formulations at the center of federal scheduling action.

## What the testing found

The Greenbrier Police Department executed a search warrant on Friday, September 4, as part of an ongoing drug and money-laundering investigation, WSMV reported. The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation tested the seized products, and according to police, preliminary results indicated the presence of mitragynine; dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, which police said is commonly associated with products marketed as "7-OH"; a fentanyl analogue and p-fluorofentanyl; and traces of other scheduled substances.

Mitragynine is the primary alkaloid in natural kratom leaf, while dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine is not among the three compounds the [Drug Enforcement Administration placed in Schedule I on a temporary basis in late August][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], which were mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16. WSMV's report does not indicate whether the fentanyl analogues were found in the same packages as the kratom alkaloids or in separate products among the 350-plus seized.

## Product labels and the public warning

Police told Smokey Barn News that some of the products containing the substances may be marketed or labeled as "Klaz" or "Cat's Claw," according to WSMV. The Greenbrier Police Department is "strongly warning the public against having or consuming the products or any illicit or mislabeled substances," the station reported, and asked anyone with information about the sale or distribution of the products in the city to contact the department.

The report names no business, no defendant, and no charges, and it does not say whether the products were being sold at retail or held for distribution.

## Tennessee's enforcement backdrop

The seizure comes roughly two months after [Tennessee's statewide kratom prohibition took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one]. Local agencies have moved on retail inventories since then; in July, [the Van Buren County Sheriff's Office seized 120 units of synthetic 7-OH products from a liquor store on State Route 111][van-buren-county-tennessee-7oh-seizure]. The Greenbrier case differs in that the state lab's preliminary results include fentanyl analogues, which WSMV's report treats as a mislabeling and adulteration concern rather than a kratom-specific one.
