The Rensselaer County Legislature voted 15-2 on the night of September 8, 2026, to ban the sale of kratom throughout the county, [News10 reported](https://www.news10.com/news/rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-sale-of-kratom/). The two votes against the local law came from District 6 Legislator Lark Barnett Rutecki and District 1 Legislator Ken Zalewski, according to the station's account of the meeting in East Greenbush. The reporting does not give an effective date for the ban.

## What the local law does

The measure prohibits the sale or distribution of kratom in the county and carries a $500 fine for a first offense and $1,000 for subsequent offenses, or a misdemeanor charge under state Public Health Law, the [Times Union reported](https://www.timesunion.com/health/article/rensselaer-county-kratom-ban-bill-22412664.php) ahead of the September 2 public hearing. The bill makes an exception for kratom products regulated by the Food and Drug Administration and dispensed by a licensed pharmacist under a physician's prescription, according to the paper. Legislator Bruce Patire, a Hoosick Republican on the legislature's health committee, sponsored the bill after reviewing annual overdose statistics, the Times Union reported. "We were going through the overdose deaths," Patire told the paper. "I kept seeing kratom, kratom, kratom."

The law covers all kratom products, and Patire told the Times Union that a ban limited to 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) or other synthetic compounds would not be enough. "This is a dangerous drug that should be regulated totally," he said, adding that it "shouldn't be sold at a corner gas station." The Times Union's account did not report Patire specifying whether the overdose cases he reviewed involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural kratom leaf, or natural leaf products.

## The dissent and the floor debate

Zalewski moved to table the vote and form a subcommittee to study the issue further, and the motion failed, News10 reported. He then spoke against the law before voting no. "We've all learned there's two very distinct versions of it, and it's very important to draw that line between this natural leaf kratom and this synthetic kratom," Zalewski said, according to the station. "Through this whole process, I guess I have found myself to be a kratom advocate, and I didn't even know what kratom was one month ago."

Supporters pointed to testimony from the earlier public hearing. "After listening to the three ladies that were here last week, I will be voting no against the amendment and I will be voting for the law as written," District 2 Legislator Robert Bayly said, per News10. The station identified the three women as Janice Coppola, Wendy Lee, and Cari Scribner, whose sons each died with kratom in their systems. They were the only speakers on kratom at the September 2 hearing, and no supporters of the product spoke that night, News10 reported. The station's reporting does not specify whether the products involved in those deaths were concentrated 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

The speakers at the September 8 meeting were the reverse: all three who addressed kratom supported it and objected to the ban, according to News10.

## Industry and advocacy response

"Disappointed. Very disappointed," Allison Smith, director of government affairs for the Global Kratom Coalition, said after the vote, per News10. "I think there's very clear guidance on how to address the difference between traditional kratom products and synthetics."

Tracey Hudson, who owns a kratom retail shop in Schenectady, told News10 she wished Rensselaer County had followed Suffolk County, which she said invited experts and ended up banning only synthetic products. Suffolk County's legislature [advanced a bill in July that targeted pill, capsule, and liquid vial forms and concentrated 7-OH products while exempting natural loose leaf][suffolk-county-kratom-ban-vote-exempts-natural-loose-leaf].

The American Kratom Association had staked out a similar position before the hearing. David Carlucci, a former state senator who lobbies for the association, told the Times Union that 7-OH is "poisoning the well of the kratom community" and pointed to state legislation that would ban high-concentration 7-OH products while allowing regulated kratom sales. "Proper regulation is so important," Carlucci said. "But not to do it negatively like a de facto ban."

News10 cited a [study indexed by the National Library of Medicine](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40762659/) reporting alkaloid content of two to five percent in raw kratom leaf and up to 60 percent in concentrated extracts. The authors wrote that "products marketed as kratom but containing much higher alkaloid concentrations than found in the natural kratom leaf, or semi-synthetic isolates of highly potent alkaloid derivatives not typically found in the plant, represent a growing public health concern," according to the station.

## New York's county-by-county map

Rensselaer joins a lengthening list of New York counties acting on their own since Governor Kathy Hochul signed two bills in December that barred sales to anyone under 21 and required warning labels, the Times Union reported. Nassau, Washington, and Oneida counties have banned sales outright, Dutchess and Suffolk counties banned synthetic and concentrated versions, and Ulster County lawmakers are weighing a ban, according to the paper. [Albany County's emergency public health order took effect September 1][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect], [Oneida County's ban was signed in late August alongside an enforcement sweep in Utica][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures], and [Dutchess County's executive has said he wants to go beyond that county's synthetic-product restrictions to a full ban][dutchess-county-executive-eyes-full-kratom-ban].

The Times Union also reported state figures behind the local activity: kratom was found in 131 of 10,192 overdose deaths in New York in 2023 and 2024 and was listed as a cause of death in 100 of those cases, and the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention counted 3,434 poison control center reports for kratom last year, up from 258 in 2015. Those figures do not separate concentrated 7-OH products from natural leaf.

Enforcement in counties that already have bans continued the same day. Nassau County police arrested an 18-year-old employee of a Valero gas station in Valley Stream on September 8 after officers found flavored vape products, kratom, and cannabis products being sold there, [News 12 Long Island reported](https://longisland.news12.com/2026/09/09/valley-stream-gas-station-employee-arrested-for-illegal-vape-kratom-sales/6dAtNJvWdoKl5dXcA04Ui8). The charges include sale of restricted products, criminal possession of a controlled substance, and unlicensed cannabis sale, according to the station, which did not specify which law covers the kratom count.

Across the state line, [Springfield, Massachusetts officials are working out how to enforce that state's emergency kratom order after the governor's office advised against confiscating products][springfield-500-notices-kratom-ban-no-confiscation-license-revocation].
