Springfield, Massachusetts has sent more than 500 notices to businesses informing them that selling kratom is now illegal under the state's emergency order, but city officials have been told by the governor's office not to remove products from store shelves, [New England Public Media reported](https://www.nepm.org/regional-news/2026-09-07/crackdown-in-springfield-on-kratom-an-herbal-supplement-with-opioid-effects) on September 7, 2026. The order, which [took effect August 28][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect], temporarily places all forms of kratom in Class 1 under state law for up to one year, according to the station.

## The confiscation question

"The governor's office has told us not confiscate. We cannot take the products off the shelf," Springfield Health Commissioner Helen Caulton-Harris told NEPM. "Because they're advising against that, then the question becomes 'what does the city do to say that we're serious?'" The station's report, by Phil Bishop, did not include a response from the governor's office or an explanation for the guidance. The emergency order authorizes local boards of health and municipal officials to enforce it, and [boards across the state had been preparing shelf-removal enforcement before the effective date][massachusetts-boards-of-health-prepare-kratom-enforcement].

The instruction leaves Springfield with notices as its main tool so far. Caulton-Harris said at least 12 businesses in the city came under scrutiny last year when Springfield banned synthetic cannabis products, and that those stores are also likely selling kratom, per NEPM.

## License revocation and a trigger ordinance

City Councilor Brian Santaniello, who chairs the council's Health and Human Services Committee, floated pulling the license of any store found selling kratom, NEPM reported. A measure of that kind would need approval from the full council, according to the station.

Springfield had been considering its own municipal ban on the main chemical compound in kratom before the state acted, and that proposal is now on the back burner because state law takes precedence, per NEPM. "Right now, the governor's action supersedes all of the local action we were going to take," Caulton-Harris said. Councilors are instead considering what the station described as a "trigger" ordinance that would institute a citywide ban whenever the state emergency order is lifted.

NEPM's report does not describe any seizures or citations in Springfield to date, and it does not distinguish between natural leaf kratom and concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which contain that alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in raw leaf. The state order itself covers all forms of kratom, so the distinction does not change what is prohibited in Massachusetts, though it has been central to policy debates elsewhere.

## The court challenge

The enforcement posture is taking shape while [four retailers' lawsuit challenging the emergency order remains pending in Suffolk County Superior Court][massachusetts-retailers-sue-emergency-kratom-ban]. The retailers argued the public health commissioner exceeded his authority in issuing the order. NEPM's report did not address the litigation.

The city's deliberations come the same week that [Rensselaer County, New York's legislature voted 15-2 to ban kratom sales outright][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2], a vote in which two dissenting legislators argued for separating natural leaf from synthetic products.
