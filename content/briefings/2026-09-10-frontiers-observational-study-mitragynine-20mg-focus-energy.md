An observational study published in Frontiers in Pharmacology reported that participants who took about 20 mg of mitragynine equivalent per serving over two weeks described statistically significant improvements in focus, problem-solving, mental clarity, and energy, [NutraIngredients reported](https://www.nutraingredients.com/Article/2026/09/09/scientists-say-kratom-extract-offers-goldilocks-dose-for-energy-focus/) on September 9, 2026. The trade outlet's headline called it a "Goldilocks" dose. The paper, titled ["Observational real-world analysis of dose-dependent effects of Mitragyna speciosa (Korth.) on focus and energy,"](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2026.1838148) was led by C. Miyabe Shields, with co-authors from the Network of Applied Pharmacognosy, the British Columbia Institute of Technology, and the Scientific Association for Botanical Education and Research, per the article.

## How the study was run

The researchers collected data through health and wellness software from MoreBetter Ltd., using text message and email surveys with structured questionnaires over a two-week period, according to NutraIngredients. Participants were recruited through social media, podcasts, and email lists. The article did not report how many people took part, and the authors described the work as a small-scale investigation.

The product used was Vivazen, made by Levo Naturals, per the article. Funding came from the Canada Research Chairs Program and the Scientific Association for Botanical Education and Research. Meghan DiMuzio, a vice president at Levo Naturals, told the outlet the study "reflects Vivazen's broader commitment to efficacy and integrity."

## What participants reported

In the 20 mg group, participants also reported a decrease in average pain and perceived pain, and headache was the most common side effect, logged in fewer than five percent of sessions, according to the article. Participants who consumed 20 to 40 mg of mitragynine equivalent reported a higher rate of side effects, with 24 percent describing fatigue, sedation, and appetite changes.

The researchers wrote that low doses "typically engage in non-opioid pathways associated with psychostimulant-like effect, and higher doses associated with the opioid receptor activity of analgesia and sedation," NutraIngredients reported. Paula Brown, director of applied research for natural health and food products at the British Columbia Institute of Technology, said the study "confirmed that the serving size is an important consideration to achieve the positive benefits of natural kratom," and that participants "only needed to take one serving of the Vivazen ingredient, as directed."

Thomas Brendler, an adjunct assistant professor at the University of Illinois Chicago's Retzky College of Pharmacy, argued for the method itself. "Highly meaningful efficacy and safety data can be amassed outside the established scope of clinical trial designs with superior capture of demographic intricacies," Brendler said, per the outlet. Outcomes were self-reported through the surveys, and the article did not describe a control group or placebo comparison.

## The authors on 7-OH

The paper takes a position on 7-hydroxymitragynine that tracks the distinction regulators have been drawing. "Although initially thought to be present in kratom leaf, it is now recognized that 7-HMG is an oxidative byproduct of mitragynine, observed in post-harvest kratom leaf at trace levels," the authors wrote, according to NutraIngredients. They added that concentrated 7-HMG products "have proliferated the U.S. marketplace and in contrast to traditional kratom preparations are reported to have significantly different dose effects," and that such products "are often mislabeled as kratom, may contain other unidentified constituents not found in kratom leaf and pose a significant risk to public health." An [analysis reported in August found that about 85 percent of tested kratom-derived products did not match their labels][study-finds-85-percent-kratom-products-mislabeled], including undisclosed 7-OH.

The article placed the study against the DEA's stated intent to schedule 7-OH and related substances, noting that [three synthetic compounds entered Schedule I on an emergency basis in late August][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. The [public comment period on the separate proposed 7-OH concentration threshold][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10], which HHS extended by 15 days, closes September 10. NutraIngredients cited an estimate of 10 to 16 million kratom consumers in the United States.
