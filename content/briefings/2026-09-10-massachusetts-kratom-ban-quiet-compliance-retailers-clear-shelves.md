Retailers across Massachusetts have pulled kratom from their shelves with little resistance in the two weeks since the state's emergency order took effect, local health and inspection officials told the State House News Service in a report [published by WBUR](https://www.wbur.org/news/2026/09/10/kratom-ban-massachusetts-compliance-public-health) on September 10, 2026. The order, which the Healey administration unveiled August 13, 2026, and which [took effect August 28, 2026][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect], places all forms of kratom in state Schedule I. It covers natural leaf products as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts found in raw leaf.

## What local inspectors are seeing

Worcester Inspectional Services Commissioner Nicholas Antanavica told the news service his department estimated that about half of the city's retailers knew about the ban and half did not when inspectors began visits. "We haven't had any real pushback or confrontation when asking to have the products removed," Antanavica said. Follow-up visits are expected, and a retailer found still selling kratom would get a ticket, then a referral to the licensing commission, then confiscation, according to the report.

Westfield Director of Public Health Debra Mulvenna described no immediate pushback after informing retailers, per the outlet. One vendor asked questions and then removed the products. Boston Public Health Commissioner Bisola Ojikutu said the city notified retailers and that inspectors are monitoring compliance during tobacco control checks. Ojikutu said residents who need help should call 311 and get connected with the city's Providing Access to Addictions Treatment, Hope and Support program.

Revere, which [held a hearing on its own ban in June][revere-massachusetts-kratom-ban-hearing], adopted a Board of Health ordinance in late June with a September 1, 2026 implementation date and fines of up to $300, according to the report. Revere Chief of Health and Human Services Lauren Buck said inspectors visited 16 stores at the end of last week and found 15 in compliance. The one exception had products behind the checkout counter, removed them, and received a warning. The report followed [Springfield's mailing of more than 500 notices][springfield-500-notices-kratom-ban-no-confiscation-license-revocation] under the same order.

The New England Convenience Store and Energy Marketers Association told the news service the order "is not having a big impact on members," describing kratom as a fringe product for its stores.

## The retailer lawsuit

Four retailers sued Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein and the Department of Public Health on August 21, 2026, according to the report: Jay's Smoke Shop in Salem and stores in North Billerica, Quincy, and Bellingham. They argued that Goldstein lacked the authority to issue the order, failed to satisfy state requirements for doing so, and applied it too broadly. DPH spokesperson Ann Scales told the outlet the department was served last Friday and plans to respond. No hearing has been scheduled.

Jignesh Patel, who owns Jay's Smoke Shop, said in an August 31, 2026 court filing that the store had sold kratom since 2023 and that kratom accounted for about $67,000 in sales in 2025 and roughly 20 percent of the store's gross margin, per the news service. Patel said the shop carried dried leaf, powdered leaf, capsules, and teas, and did not carry concentrated 7-OH or synthetic derivatives. He estimated that about $10,000 in inventory at wholesale cost became "unsellable, unreturnable, and, as I understand it, unlawful for us even to possess in the ordinary course" on August 28, 2026, a loss he said exceeds 10 percent of the store's net income for its last full fiscal year. "I do not believe Jay's Smoke Shop can survive two months without kratom sales," Patel wrote.

Under the department's guidance, retailers with leftover stock can transfer it to wholesalers or distributors, hand it over to law enforcement officials willing to take it, or place it in secure storage subject to inspection by DPH, local public health officials, and law enforcement, according to the report.

## The state's framing

Governor Maura Healey and Goldstein have described kratom as an unregulated product that is sometimes combined with more potent or synthetic substances, per the outlet. "Some of the synthetic kratom products are hard to spot on the shelves and have serious side effects," Goldstein said. The report cited more than 100 kratom-related deaths in the state since 2020. It did not specify how many of those deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom, a distinction the state's order does not draw.

The order took effect three days before International Overdose Awareness Day, when more than 20,000 purple flags were planted on Boston Common, the news service noted, and as the state reported opioid-related overdose deaths declining for a third consecutive year.
