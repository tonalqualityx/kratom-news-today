A key committee of the Ulster County Legislature in New York declined to move forward with a proposed ban on the sale of concentrated and synthetic kratom, saying it would wait to see what the state and federal governments do, the [Daily Freeman reported](https://www.dailyfreeman.com/2026/09/08/ulster-county-delays-ban-on-concentrated-synthetic-kratom/) on September 8, 2026. The proposed local law targets concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf, and would not reach natural leaf kratom.

The request to postpone a vote on setting a public hearing came from County Executive Jen Metzger's office, according to the paper. "It's a very fast-moving legal landscape at the moment," Deputy County Executive James Amenta told the legislature's Health, Human Services and Rights Committee at its September 3 meeting.

## The county executive's argument

Amenta told the committee that since Legislator William Murray introduced the resolution to set a hearing, the state Legislature adopted a bill banning the sale of synthetic and concentrated forms of kratom, the Daily Freeman reported. That bill, which [passed both chambers unanimously in June][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban], is awaiting Governor Kathy Hochul's signature. The county's proposed local law "almost exactly mirrors the state legislation," Amenta said, per the paper.

Amenta also pointed to the federal government. He told the committee that on August 26 the Drug Enforcement Administration temporarily listed synthetic 7-OH and three related substances in Schedule I, banning the sale or distribution of products containing them, and that the DEA did not list natural kratom leaf, according to the Daily Freeman. The DEA's order as announced by the Justice Department [placed three synthetic kratom-related compounds in Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]: mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16. The agency's separate proposal to schedule 7-OH itself above a concentration threshold has not been finalized, and [its public comment period closes September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10].

The federal action makes selling concentrated or synthetic kratom products a criminal offense, Amenta said, while the county's proposed law would instead assign the county Health Department to ensure compliance and levy administrative penalties, the paper reported. "The Health Department doesn't currently have any function in this ... illegal drug enforcement space," Amenta said, according to the Daily Freeman. Giving the department those duties would require creating an entirely new unit within it, he added.

## The sponsor's objection

Murray, a New Paltz Democrat and the bill's author, was one of two committee members who voted against the delay, the Daily Freeman reported. He said there was no guarantee Hochul would sign the state bill or that the federal government would make its temporary listing permanent. A public hearing would open a "dialogue" with the community about kratom and the issues surrounding it, Murray said, per the paper.

The Daily Freeman reported that concentrated and synthetic kratom is sold in gas stations and smoke shops as an energy booster, and that the Food and Drug Administration has said the risks of using kratom include liver toxicity, seizures, substance use disorder, and, in some instances, death.

## Local death figures

Ulster County Health Director Eve Walter has said there has been one death in 2026 from kratom and another in which kratom was one of a number of drugs in the person's system, according to the Daily Freeman. In 2025 there was one death in which kratom was among many drugs present, the paper reported. The reporting does not specify whether the products involved in those deaths were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

## Where Ulster sits among New York counties

Ulster's pause runs against the direction of its neighbors. Rensselaer County's legislature [voted 15-2 on September 8 to ban all kratom sales][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2], rejecting a proposal from two legislators to separate natural leaf from synthetics. Dutchess County [banned concentrated and synthetic kratom products in August][dutchess-county-bans-concentrated-synthetic-kratom-products] with a law similar in scope to the Ulster proposal. The Daily Freeman's report did not indicate when, or whether, the Ulster committee would take the hearing resolution up again.
