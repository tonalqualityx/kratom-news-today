A Blythe, California ordinance banning the sale and distribution of kratom products to anyone under 21 took effect on Thursday, September 10, 2026, [City News Service reported](https://mynewsla.com/riverside/2026/09/10/blythe-ordinance-banning-kratom-products-for-youth-takes-effect-thursday-2/) via MyNewsLA. The Blythe City Council approved the ordinance on August 11, 2026, according to the report.

## What the ordinance covers

The measure prohibits selling or distributing kratom products to people under 21, [KESQ reported](https://kesq.com/news/2026/09/09/blythe-ordinance-banning-kratom-products-for-youth-takes-effect-thursday/) in its version of the City News Service story on September 9. Adults 21 and older with valid identification may still purchase kratom in the city. The ordinance is an age restriction on sales, and neither report described any limit on adult possession or use.

A person convicted of selling kratom products to a minor faces a fine of up to $1,000, per City News Service. Neither report included a council vote count or comments from city officials.

## Following the county's lead

The Blythe prohibition mirrors a Riverside County ordinance adopted in October 2025 with the same age restriction, City News Service reported.

The reports noted that kratom contains the alkaloids mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). City News Service reported that some synthesized products contain alkaloids that produce stronger effects than natural kratom, and KESQ reported that the Food and Drug Administration has warned that products containing synthesized 7-OH, which the agency describes as having opioid-like effects, may cause addiction, serious harm, or overdose. Per City News Service, the FDA has not approved any products containing 7-OH as an added ingredient or at enhanced levels, issued warning letters to companies marketing such products in July 2025, and recommended that the Drug Enforcement Administration add them to the controlled substances list. Those synthesized and enhanced 7-OH products, as the reports describe them, are a category apart from the 7-OH that the reports say occurs in kratom itself.

## California's broader picture

The Blythe ordinance arrives as the state has stepped up its own enforcement. Governor Gavin Newsom's office [reported on September 2, 2026 that state enforcement had removed nearly 15,300 illegal hemp, kratom, and 7-OH products][california-statewide-enforcement-removes-kratom-7oh-products] from store shelves, as KNT reported. Neither City News Service report referenced the state enforcement campaign in connection with the Blythe ordinance.
