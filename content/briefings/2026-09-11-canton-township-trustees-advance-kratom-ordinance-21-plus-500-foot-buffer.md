Canton Township trustees advanced a local kratom ordinance on September 8, 2026, less than two weeks after authorities seized hundreds of kratom and concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from a township tobacco shop, [MLive reported](https://www.mlive.com/news/detroit/2026/09/canton-moves-to-regulate-kratom-sales-after-hundreds-of-products-seized.html) on September 10. The ordinance would restrict purchases to adults 21 and older and would single out synthetic, enhanced, and high-potency products, according to the report. Those product categories are distinct from natural leaf kratom, which MLive described as containing naturally occurring alkaloids including mitragynine and trace amounts of 7-OH.

## What the ordinance would do

MLive reported that the ordinance under consideration would:

- Restrict kratom purchases to individuals 21 and older, with valid identification required at the point of sale.
- Prohibit kratom sales and displays within 500 feet of schools, childcare facilities, parks, libraries, and recreational centers.
- Address synthetic, enhanced, and high-potency kratom products as a category separate from traditional leaf products.

The coverage reviewed by KNT did not include a vote count on the September 8 action or a date for a final adoption vote. The measure is not in effect.

## The seizure that preceded it

The ordinance follows an August 28 joint inspection by Canton police, the Michigan Department of Agriculture and Rural Development, and Michigan State Police that [turned up hundreds of kratom and concentrated 7-OH products][canton-michigan-inspection-seizes-kratom-7oh-products] along with hundreds of THC items allegedly sold illegally, as KNT reported earlier this month. The state agriculture department seized the products and the case was referred to the Wayne County prosecutor. MLive's September 10 report cited the seizure of hundreds of kratom products as the backdrop for the trustees' action.

The earlier reporting on the seizure did not break down how many of the seized items were natural leaf kratom versus concentrated 7-OH preparations. MLive's description of the ordinance's "enhanced" and "high-potency" provisions did not specify whether they would apply to natural leaf kratom.

## Where Michigan stands

Michigan has no statewide kratom statute in effect. A bill that would ban kratom production and sales [passed the Michigan House in March and remains pending in the state Senate][warren-michigan-kratom-ban-north-dakota-special-session], as KNT has reported. In the absence of state law, Canton joins Warren among Michigan municipalities that have moved on local kratom measures this year.

At the federal level, the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary Schedule I order for [three synthetic kratom-related compounds][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] took effect August 26, while the agency's separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold has not been finalized. Neither federal action covers natural leaf kratom, according to the DEA's published orders as KNT has reported.
