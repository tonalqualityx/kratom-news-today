According to the [Delaware General Assembly's bill record](https://legis.delaware.gov/BillDetail/143524), Governor Matt Meyer signed House Substitute 1 for House Bill 332 on September 8, 2026, setting a statewide minimum age of 21 for kratom purchases. The record lists the law as effective the same day. [Delaware Public Media reported](https://www.delawarepublic.org/politics-government/2026-09-10/delaware-to-get-a-state-title-ix-coordinator-implement-new-rules-on-kratom) on September 10 that people under 21 can no longer buy kratom products in the state, describing kratom as an herbal supplement sold in smoke shops.

## What the law does

The General Assembly's synopsis of the signed substitute describes two provisions. It prohibits selling, providing, or marketing kratom or a kratom product to anyone under 21, with a violation classified as a class B misdemeanor. It also requires the Division of Alcohol and Tobacco Enforcement and the Department of Health and Social Services to deliver a report on kratom testing capabilities and potential licensing requirements to the governor and General Assembly by January 1, 2027.

Delaware Public Media characterized that second provision as an evaluation of whether the state has the capacity to test kratom products and license the retailers that sell them. According to the General Assembly's synopsis, the report is a study directive; the synopsis does not describe the law as creating a licensing program.

State Senator Marie Pinkney, a New Castle Democrat and the bill's Senate prime sponsor, told Delaware Public Media that "there are people who use it in its purity" and that the legislation was written with community members in mind "who have found solace in this." Representative Melanie Ross Levin was the House sponsor, per the General Assembly record.

## A narrower bill than the one that passed the House

The signed version is narrower than the bill KNT reported on in June. At that point, [HB 332 as passed by the House][delaware-house-passes-kratom-regulation-bill] had been described as banning synthetic kratom products, restricting sales to adults 21 and older, and requiring accurate labeling of alkaloid content, with the bill headed to a Senate committee that was also weighing a competing outright ban. The General Assembly's synopsis of House Substitute 1, the version signed on September 8, mentions only the age limit and the January report. Delaware Public Media's coverage did not mention synthetic-product or labeling provisions, nor the competing Senate ban bill.

The law as signed sets an age-21 purchase floor while deferring questions about product testing, synthetic alkaloids, and retailer licensing to the January report and the next legislative session.
