Montana's Law and Justice Interim Committee voted 8-1 on September 1, 2026 to table a draft Kratom Consumer Protection Act, with several members saying the bill did not go far enough, the [Daily Montanan reported](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/09/01/legislators-eye-reining-in-wild-west-kratom-industry/). The report was republished by the [Northern Plains Independent](https://www.northernplainsindependent.com/2026/09/10/state-legislators-eye-reining-in-kratom-industry/?destination=community-news) on September 10. Montana has no statewide kratom law, and the committee's action leaves the question to be taken up again before the 2027 session.

## What the tabled bill would have done

The draft would have banned synthesized kratom, added labeling requirements for kratom products, and allowed the state to regulate and test the products and sell them to people 18 and older, according to the Daily Montanan.

The 18-year purchase age drew the sharpest objection. "I don't think it's strong enough," Senator Vince Ricci, a Livingston Republican, said, noting that the age limit for tobacco, cannabis, and alcohol in Montana is 21, per the paper.

## A ban proposal is moving in a second committee

Senator Ellie Boldman, a Missoula Democrat, told the paper she supported a proposal to ban kratom that has advanced in the Economic Affairs Interim Committee, which she described as a bipartisan effort. "I voted to ban Kratom because 'gas station heroin' has no business being sold at gas stations in Montana," Boldman said, according to the Daily Montanan. The paper reported that the Economic Affairs committee passed a separate bill that would designate kratom's chemical compounds as controlled substances. Boldman referenced deaths among young men in explaining her position; the reporting does not specify whether those cases involved concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products or natural leaf kratom.

Representative Steven Kelly, a Flathead Republican, described the current state of kratom regulation in Montana as the "Wild West," per the paper.

## Testimony on both sides

Dr. Lauren Wilson, a former president of the Montana chapter of the American Academy of Pediatrics, told the committee about a case in which a newborn required morphine after the mother used kratom during pregnancy, the Daily Montanan reported. The paper's account does not specify whether the product involved was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom. Drenda Niemann of the Association of Montana Public Health Officials cited concerns about addiction, withdrawal, and unknown drug interactions.

Casey Hugs testified that kratom helped her late husband move away from heroin at a time when treatment was not accessible to him, per the paper. Her testimony was the report's principal account of a harm-reduction perspective before the committee.

The Daily Montanan reported that kratom is banned in eight states and regulated in roughly 30 others, and cited a federal report, which the paper did not further identify, finding that kratom-related poisonings rose about 1,200 percent over 10 years, along with a 2023 Tampa Bay Times investigation that counted hundreds of deaths in Florida. The paper's report did not distinguish between natural leaf and concentrated 7-OH products in those figures.

## The regional context

Montana's committees are working through the same regulate-versus-ban split that played out next door this month. North Dakota's governor [signed a synthetic kratom ban on September 8 after the House rejected a total ban][north-dakota-armstrong-signs-hb-1628-synthetic-kratom-ban-house-rejects-total-ban], as KNT reported. In Montana, the Daily Montanan reported general agreement among lawmakers that the state needs kratom regulation in place by the 2027 legislative session, without a consensus on which model to adopt.
