The Downey City Council voted 4-0 on Tuesday, September 8, 2026 to direct staff to draft a local ordinance restricting synthetic and high-concentration 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) kratom products while leaving traditional kratom leaf outside the restrictions, [Los Cerritos Community News reported](https://www.loscerritosnews.net/2026/09/10/downey-council-directs-staff-to-draft-rules-on-laughing-gas-and-kratom/) on September 10. Councilmember Mario Trujillo was absent. The same motion directed staff to draft a second ordinance limiting recreational retail sales of nitrous oxide, according to the outlet.

## Timeline and cost

Community Development Director Irma Huitron told the council that drafting could begin in November and that the ordinances could return to the council around April 2027, Los Cerritos Community News reported. The work is estimated to cost $15,000 from the General Fund reserve, per the outlet.

The council had first asked staff in June to examine stopping sales at smoke shops, according to the report. Huitron said limiting the regulations to tobacco retailers would miss other businesses that sell the products, since neither nitrous oxide nor kratom is a tobacco product and both may be available through other types of retail establishments, according to the outlet.

Huitron said concentrated 7-OH products raise separate health concerns because potency and labeling can vary and the products have not been approved by the Food and Drug Administration, according to Los Cerritos Community News. The outlet described traditional kratom leaf as containing naturally occurring compounds in relatively small amounts, while newer extracts, gummies, and liquids can contain much higher concentrations of 7-OH.

## Where council members landed

Mayor Claudia Frometa said she did not favor a blanket ban on every form of either product, according to the report. On kratom, she said she favored targeting synthetic and highly concentrated products while allowing traditional kratom products to remain available. "My opinion would be targeted regulation, leaving for medical purposes, but really banning the synthetics and, for the concentration, establishing that 2% threshold," Frometa said, as quoted by the outlet. She also said purchasers should be at least 21.

Mayor Pro Tem Horacio Ortiz said he agreed with the mayor on regulating synthetic 7-OH kratom products, per the report. Councilmember Dorothy Pemberton said she has seen traditional products help some people manage pain without opioids and that manufactured products with high 7-OH concentrations should be restricted, according to the outlet. Council members then clarified that the proposed ordinance would target synthetic and highly concentrated products rather than traditional kratom leaf, Los Cerritos Community News reported.

Councilmember Hector Sosa asked whether existing state and county regulations were sufficient. Huitron responded that a city ordinance would give Downey additional enforcement "teeth," particularly while proposed state legislation addressing both products remains unresolved, per the outlet. [California's AB 1088][california-kratom-sales-bill-north-carolina-licensing], which KNT reported on in May, would add kratom and 7-OH products to state food and drug law with alkaloid concentration limits and a 21-and-over sales floor.

Police Chief Scott Loughner told the council that nitrous oxide has been the larger problem for Downey police, with 26 possession arrests over the previous 18 months, and that the department has had few dealings with kratom, according to the report.

## What staff will draft

Under the council's direction, staff will prepare a targeted kratom ordinance rather than a ban on traditional kratom leaf, Los Cerritos Community News reported. The proposal is expected to prohibit synthetic 7-OH products and products exceeding a concentration threshold, with Frometa suggesting a two percent limit modeled after regulations adopted elsewhere, according to the outlet. The report does not specify how the threshold would be measured.

The two percent figure matches the standard adopted in July by [Fountain Valley in Orange County][fountain-valley-restricts-high-7oh-kratom-products], which prohibited kratom products whose alkaloid fraction contains more than two percent 7-OH and set a 21-and-over age limit. Downey, in Los Angeles County, joins a series of Southern California cities and counties acting locally while AB 1088 remains pending; [Blythe's under-21 sales ban took effect September 10][blythe-california-under-21-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect], mirroring a Riverside County rule. The nitrous oxide ordinance will restrict recreational retail sales, including large canisters, flavored products, and inhalation devices, while preserving exemptions for medical, dental, veterinary, pharmacy, food-service, automotive, manufacturing, industrial, and wholesale uses, per the outlet.
