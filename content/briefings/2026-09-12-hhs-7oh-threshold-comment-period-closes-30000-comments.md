The public comment period on the Department of Health and Human Services' request for information on a proposed scheduling threshold for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) closed at 11:59 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, September 10, 2026, according to the [docket record on Regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov/document/HHS-OASH-2026-0232-0001). As of the morning of September 12, the [docket](https://www.regulations.gov/docket/HHS-OASH-2026-0232) listed 30,346 posted comments, with the most recent entries carrying a September 11 posting date. The federal action at issue targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations above a proposed threshold, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf.

## What closed, and what the count covers

The docket, HHS-OASH-2026-0232, belongs to the Office of the Assistant Secretary for Health (OASH). According to the request for information posted July 6, OASH opened the docket to solicit input on a proposed threshold for 7-OH scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act, and comments submitted to it "will be provided by the Secretary for Health and Human Services for consideration by the Attorney General." Regulations.gov classifies the docket as nonrulemaking. The companion DEA docket for the agency's July proposed rule, DEA-2026-1190, lists no comments on Regulations.gov; the HHS docket served as the public comment avenue, as KNT reported when [the original comment window closed July 31][7oh-comment-window-closes-dea-order-august-5].

The 30,346 figure is the number of comments Regulations.gov had posted to the docket as of September 12. A [September 10 post on the newsroom of Rize Recovery](https://www.rizerecovery.com/newsroom/kratom-7-oh-federal-comment-period-closes-2026), an addiction treatment provider, said more than 32,000 public comments had been submitted before the deadline. KNT could not independently confirm the higher figure. The Regulations.gov docket page reports a posted count and does not report a separate submitted total, so the two numbers are not directly comparable. By comparison, [the docket had drawn more than 24,000 comments as of late July][7oh-retailers-discount-inventory-ahead-august-deadline], before the extension.

The extension itself was [published in the Federal Register on August 26](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17409/hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified-threshold-in-schedule-i-extension-of-comment-period), reopening the window through September 10, as [KNT reported at the time][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10].

## The pending DEA action

The comment period concerned the threshold in the Drug Enforcement Administration's July 6 notice of intent, which [proposed placing 7-OH in Schedule I on a temporary basis when it is present above specified concentrations][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule]. According to the [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13580/schedules-of-controlled-substance-temporary-placement-of-7-hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified), the proposal covers products containing more than 0.050 percent 7-OH by dry weight or more than 1 milligram per finished unit. Natural kratom leaf, which contains 7-OH in trace amounts, falls below those levels according to the DEA's own description of the threshold-based approach.

A Federal Register search on September 12 returned no DEA document on 7-OH published since the August 26 extension notice, and no such document appeared on the Federal Register's public inspection list that morning. The DEA's separate temporary order placing [mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] was published August 26 and is not affected by the 7-OH comment period.

## Where advocates stand

The major kratom trade groups have not opposed the threshold approach as such. When the original comment window closed July 31, the American Kratom Association urged policymakers to target chemically manipulated 7-OH products rather than natural kratom leaf, while Students for Sensible Drug Policy reported organizing thousands of opposition comments, as KNT reported at the time. In late August, the Global Kratom Coalition applauded the DEA's temporary order on the three synthetic compounds and noted that the 7-OH piece of the same initiative had not been finalized, with the comment period running through September 10. Those positions separate the trade associations from the retailers and manufacturers of concentrated 7-OH products, who discounted inventory ahead of the original August 5 scheduling date and whose products the DEA proposal names. Rize Recovery's post, per its headline, framed the comment count as public input on "kratom's fate"; the OASH docket title and the DEA notice both describe the action as limited to 7-OH above a specified threshold.
