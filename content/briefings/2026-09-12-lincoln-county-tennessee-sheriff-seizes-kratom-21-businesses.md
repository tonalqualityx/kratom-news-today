The Lincoln County Sheriff's Office announced on Thursday, September 10, 2026 that it had seized kratom from 21 businesses throughout the county, totaling tens of thousands of dollars in merchandise, [WAFF reported](https://www.waff.com/2026/09/11/kratom-seized-21-lincoln-county-businesses/) on September 11. The station's report does not specify whether the seized products were concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom. The distinction separates trace-level 7-OH in raw leaf from the high-potency formulations at the center of federal scheduling action, though [Tennessee's statewide prohibition, which took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], reaches both.

## What the sheriff's office said

Deputy Chief Patrick Murdock told WAFF that the investigation started shortly after the ban went into effect. According to the station, Murdock said the sheriff's office had recently handled one overdose and one near overdose it attributed to kratom, and that enforcing the new law was important to the office. Murdock described products displayed at the counter in many of the stores, telling WAFF the businesses "were blatantly selling the drug."

Murdock said some businesses were selling mislabeled products, including "Cat's Claw" tablets whose labels listed Uncaria tomentosa extract, WAFF reported. He said the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI) tested the product and it came back positive for kratom. The "Cat's Claw" label also appeared in [a Greenbrier, Tennessee raid earlier this month][greenbrier-tennessee-raid-seizes-350-packages-7oh-fentanyl-analogue], where police told local media that some products containing kratom alkaloids and fentanyl analogues may be marketed under that name. WAFF's Lincoln County report does not mention fentanyl or any substance other than kratom.

Selling kratom in Tennessee is a Class C felony, according to the station. Murdock said narcotics officers are working with the district attorney's office to determine whether charges will be filed against the businesses. WAFF's report names no business and no individual, and it does not describe any arrests.

Murdock also told WAFF that kratom "does kill people" and is "very addictive," that some people refer to it as "gas station heroin," and that he had been told by people who used kratom that stopping was "a horrible experience." Those characterizations are the deputy chief's; WAFF did not cite toxicology findings or medical sources in its report.

## What retailers said

A WAFF reporter visited four smoke shops in Lincoln County on Friday, September 11, and employees at the stores said their kratom had been seized the day before, according to the station. One employee said deputies "took everything we had" and that kratom had been a big part of the store's business. The same employee, whose store sits near the state line, told WAFF that the state's THC and kratom bans that took effect July 1 had eliminated roughly 80 percent of the shop's business.

## Tennessee enforcement since July 1

The Lincoln County action is the third local Tennessee seizure KNT has covered since the ban took effect, and the first described as a county-wide sweep. In July, [the Van Buren County Sheriff's Office seized 120 units of products described as synthetic 7-OH from a liquor store on State Route 111][van-buren-county-tennessee-7oh-seizure], and earlier this month Greenbrier police seized more than 350 packages in a raid tied to a drug and money-laundering investigation. WAFF's Lincoln County report describes a county-wide sweep of retail inventory rather than a single-location raid, with the charging decision still pending at the district attorney's office.
