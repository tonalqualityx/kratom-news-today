A physician at Allegheny Health Network's Wexford hospital told [CBS Pittsburgh](https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/gas-station-heroin-kratom-withdrawal/) that the hospital has seen an increase in patients admitted with kratom withdrawal over the past month, according to a report the station published on September 10, 2026. The report does not specify whether the patients had used concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural kratom leaf, or natural leaf products. CBS refers throughout to "kratom" and to products sold at gas stations and vape shops.

## What the physician described

Dr. Christopher Eicher of AHN Wexford told CBS that the hospital normally sees one or two patients a month with kratom withdrawal, but that in the past month it has had "multiple patients on our ICU, ICU stepdown wards and the telemetry ward." He listed the problems bringing patients to the intensive care unit as respiratory depression, cardiac instability, gastrointestinal symptoms, electrolyte abnormalities, and long hospital stays, per the station. Eicher said the patients span "all genders, ages and socioeconomic statuses."

Eicher offered his own explanation for the timing. "I think it kind of coincides around the buzz with the DEA talking about legislation to kind of crack down on it," he told CBS, which characterized that as a possible link between the publicity and more purchases. He described patients as "self-medicating" for pain and said that "at lower doses, the kratom is an upper" while "higher doses" were "causing a lot of problems that are leading them to the ICU." Those characterizations are Eicher's; the station did not cite toxicology results or hospital data beyond his account. Eicher called kratom a "formula for disaster" and said, "It's unbelievable how easily they can get it."

CBS also reported that two men in Allegheny County have died from complications of kratom. That matches the [Pittsburgh Post-Gazette's August 20 report that the county had documented a second kratom-associated death][allegheny-county-kratom-deaths-pennsylvania-regulation]; neither outlet's reporting specifies what type of product was involved in those deaths.

The station's headline uses the term "gas station heroin." The CBS report itself is about kratom specifically and does not mention any other substance.

## The Pennsylvania backdrop

CBS reported that kratom "is not heavily regulated in Pennsylvania" and that there is "absolutely no regulation on kratom" in the state, describing that as the reason buyers do not know what is in a product or how much. As KNT reported on September 9, the [state Department of Health has said it is "reviewing the situation and considering all options"][pennsylvania-health-department-reviewing-7oh-request-lawmakers-push-regulation] after two district attorneys asked it to emergency-schedule 7-OH and other kratom derivatives, and lawmakers are sponsoring bills that would ban synthetic kratom products and high-potency extracts while allowing natural leaf sales to adults 21 and older. An earlier Post-Gazette investigation [ranked Allegheny County second in the Northeast for kratom-related emergency calls][pennsylvania-kratom-ems-hotspot-legislation].

The federal action Eicher referred to is the Drug Enforcement Administration's proposed temporary placement of concentrated 7-OH in Schedule I, which targets products above a threshold concentration and does not reach natural leaf; the [Department of Health and Human Services' comment period on that threshold closed September 10][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments], and no DEA order had been published as of September 12.

Eicher told CBS that patients in uncontrolled pain or a mental health crisis should seek out their medical providers and that "you can't go through any withdrawal at home." The station reported that health leaders are urging education on kratom while legislation is discussed at the state and federal levels.
