The Tupelo Police Department began enforcing the city's ban on the sale of kratom and kratom products at 10 a.m. on Saturday, September 12, 2026, [WTVA reported](https://www.wtva.com/2026/09/11/kratom-ban-take-effect-soon/) on September 11. The ordinance is a full prohibition: according to [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/09/tupelo-police-set-sept-12-deadline-to-enforce-long-delayed-kratom-ban/), it bans kratom and kratom products within city limits, including their use, sale, distribution, and public display. That scope reaches natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and none of the available reporting draws a distinction between the two.

## The department's notice

The department issued a reminder on Thursday, September 10 that enforcement would begin Saturday morning, WTVA reported, and said businesses selling kratom "should prepare to comply." The station quoted the department's statement: "The City has allowed the legal process to run its course, and businesses have been permitted to continue selling these products during that time. We are providing businesses with 48 hours notice and an opportunity to come into compliance before enforcement begins. Our goal is voluntary compliance with the ordinance." [Darkhorse Press](https://darkhorsepressnow.com/news/2026-09-10/tupelo-police-to-begin-enforcing-kratom-ban-sept-12/) and Hoodline attributed the statement to Police Chief John Quaka.

Hoodline reported that the ordinance provides for confiscation of substances found after enactment. WTVA's report does not describe penalties, and none of the coverage describes what enforcement will look like beyond the voluntary-compliance goal.

## Seven months between vote and enforcement

The Tupelo City Council adopted the ordinance on February 3, 2026, with the members present voting unanimously, according to Hoodline, and the measure was set to take effect 30 days after passage. Enforcement stalled when a local retailer challenged it. The [Daily Journal reported on February 18](https://www.djournal.com/news/local/tupelo-storeowner-appeals-councils-kratom-ban/article_20ece226-1175-412a-a515-b69ab0e2577b.html) that store owner Mohamed Gamiel, represented by attorney Bradley S. Clanton, filed an appeal in Lee County Circuit Court, where the case was assigned to Judge Paul Funderburk. Per the Daily Journal, Gamiel's filing argued the ordinance was "arbitrary and not supported by substantial evidence," that the city had offered no citations beyond "self-serving statements," and that the ban violated his constitutional rights. The paper reported that the council had cited Mayo Clinic, DEA, and FDA materials in support of the measure, and that City Attorney Ben Logan had not yet responded to the appeal at the time.

Hoodline reported, citing the Daily Journal, that the court dismissed the case in August 2026 after the plaintiff failed to submit required procedural paperwork, and that the shop owner had until September 17 to appeal. KNT has not reviewed the dismissal order. Whether an appeal was filed before enforcement began is not addressed in the September coverage.

## State law and the local-ban carve-out

Hoodline reported that Mississippi's statewide kratom law, House Bill 1077, was signed in April 2025 and took effect that July, setting a minimum purchase age of 21, capping 7-OH content at one percent, adding a five percent tax on kratom sales, and explicitly preserving municipal authority to enact total local bans. Tupelo's ordinance relies on that preserved authority, per Hoodline's account. The WTVA and Darkhorse Press reports do not discuss the state law.

Other jurisdictions where full bans took effect this summer have reported different enforcement experiences. In Massachusetts, WBUR reported that [inspectors found most retailers had cleared shelves without confrontation][massachusetts-kratom-ban-quiet-compliance-retailers-clear-shelves] in the days after the state's emergency order took effect. In Tennessee, WAFF reported that the [Lincoln County Sheriff's Office seized kratom from 21 businesses][lincoln-county-tennessee-sheriff-seizes-kratom-21-businesses] more than two months after that state's ban began. The Tupelo department's stated goal, per WTVA, is voluntary compliance; the coverage does not say what happens if businesses do not comply. In New York, [Dutchess County's executive signed a narrower measure this week][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh] that bans concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while leaving natural leaf legal, News 12 reported.
