The Burlington, Massachusetts Board of Health voted unanimously on Tuesday to ban the sale, manufacture, and distribution of kratom in the town, [Burlington Buzz reported](https://www.burlington.buzz/local-government/burlington-board-of-health-adopts-local-ban-on-kratom/) on September 11, 2026. The regulation covers kratom products generally; the report does not say whether the products inspectors found in local stores were natural leaf kratom or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, and the local rule does not draw that line.

According to the outlet, the board set a $1,000 fine for each violation, followed by suspension or revocation of a business's license for multiple violations within a 36-month period. The penalty structure was shaped during the meeting with guidance from Cheryl Sbarra of the Massachusetts Association of Health Boards, the report said. The regulation takes effect after it is advertised once more in local media, per Burlington Buzz, which did not give a date.

## A local rule that stays in place after the state order expires

Burlington's ban sits on top of a statewide restriction that is already in force. Governor Maura Healey's emergency order [placing all forms of kratom into Schedule I under state law][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] for up to one year [took effect August 28][massachusetts-boards-of-health-prepare-kratom-enforcement], and it covers natural leaf kratom as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products. Burlington Buzz reported that the town's local regulation will remain in place after that order expires.

The board had been working on the question before the state acted, according to the outlet. A subcommittee reviewed regulations from other Massachusetts towns and model language from the Massachusetts Association of Health Boards, weighing a full ban against a sales restriction to adults 21 and older. By July the subcommittee had drafted a full ban and planned to bring it to the board in September; the state's emergency order landed in the interim. The local regulation, developed from the state association's model, applies only to kratom products, per the report.

## What the board and residents said

Ed Weiner, a longtime board member and former chair, told the meeting that "there is no regulation, no large-scale clinical studies" establishing the parameters under which kratom can be used safely, according to Burlington Buzz. Weiner cited research he said he had read in the National Library of Medicine, said kratom comes in natural and synthetic forms, and put the number of U.S. users at 14 million to 16 million, per the outlet. He told the board that the Food and Drug Administration had issued warnings about kratom as early as 2017 and that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention had reported 91 deaths linked to kratom in 2019, and he said many products on the market have been found to contain compounds not listed on the label while lacking compounds that are. The report attributes those figures to Weiner and does not independently cite the FDA or CDC documents.

"Some say that kratom may have some usefulness," Weiner said, "however there's no regulation and poor understanding." Chair Gayle DaMore said the substance "is unregulated and often marketed as harmless or a dietary supplement or mood booster, and with no regulation there's no true way to know what's in it," according to the outlet.

Holly Trouville, whose son Tyrell died in 2024, told the board that mitragynine toxicity was the only cause of death listed on his death certificate and that he died after drinking a kratom seltzer marketed as free of sugar, alcohol, and caffeine, per Burlington Buzz. She urged the board to seek evidence from medical professionals on the substance's efficacy claims. The report does not say whether the seltzer was a natural leaf product or a concentrated 7-OH preparation.

Burlington Buzz summarized the opposing view in a single sentence: kratom advocates say it has many uses, including addiction recovery and pain management, and argue that a ban could push some users toward other illicit substances. The report does not quote any advocate or retailer from the meeting.

## Enforcement starts with the stores already on the list

Health Department Director Susan Lumenello said department representatives had visited gas stations and other establishments in Burlington and found "six or seven" retailers selling kratom, generally in powder form, according to the outlet. She said the department would return to those businesses to confirm the products are off the shelves, and that other retailers may be selling kratom without the department's knowledge. "We would depend on the public to bring it to our attention," she said, per Burlington Buzz.

The reliance on return visits rather than confiscation matches what other Massachusetts municipalities have described. Springfield's health commissioner told New England Public Media that the governor's office had [instructed the city not to confiscate products from shelves][springfield-500-notices-kratom-ban-no-confiscation-license-revocation] after the city sent more than 500 notices, and that councilors there are weighing a "trigger" ordinance to impose a citywide ban when the state order lapses, the same gap Burlington Buzz says the town's rule will cover. Officials in Worcester, Westfield, Boston, and Revere told the State House News Service that retailers have [removed kratom products with little resistance][massachusetts-kratom-ban-quiet-compliance-retailers-clear-shelves] since August 28.

The statewide order is still being contested. [Four retailers sued the Department of Public Health][massachusetts-retailers-sue-emergency-kratom-ban] in Suffolk County Superior Court to block it, arguing the public health commissioner exceeded his authority, and as of WBUR's September 10 report the department had been served and no hearing had been scheduled. Burlington Buzz's report does not mention the litigation. Burlington joins a line of Massachusetts municipalities that moved on kratom before or alongside the state, including Revere, whose board of health [held a hearing on a local sales ban in June][revere-massachusetts-kratom-ban-hearing] at which 14 of 15 written comments opposed the measure.
