The Czech government will take up a package of tighter kratom rules on Monday, September 14, 2026, [Expats.cz reported](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/czech-news-in-brief-for-september-13-2026-sunday-top-morning-headlines) on September 13 in a morning news roundup sourced to the Czech News Agency (ČTK). According to the report, the proposal would raise the minimum purchase age for kratom from 18 to 21 and ban online sales outright.

## What the package contains

Beyond the age limit and the online ban, Expats.cz reported that the proposal would:

- Introduce an excise tax on kratom products.
- Raise the value-added tax on kratom from 12 percent to 21 percent.
- Tighten packaging, advertising, and sales rules.
- Raise fines to CZK 20 million.
- Allow municipalities to restrict licensed stores.

The roundup does not name the ministry sponsoring the proposal, does not describe the current licensing regime the new rules would modify, and does not give a timeline for parliamentary consideration or an effective date. It also does not say whether the rules would treat natural leaf kratom differently from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, a distinction that has shaped regulation in the United States.

## Who is pushing back

Business groups and addiction experts have warned that a ban on online sales could push customers toward the illegal market, according to Expats.cz. The report does not name the groups or experts, and it does not quote a government response to that concern. That is the only opposition the roundup records; it does not say whether the age increase, the tax measures, or the higher fines drew similar objections.

## The enforcement sweep behind the proposal

The government discussion follows a nationwide inspection campaign KNT covered in August. Expats.cz [reported on August 20](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/czech-news-in-brief-for-august-20-2026-thursday-top-morning-headlines) that authorities had [inspected about 1,000 stores selling kratom and similar substances and found violations at roughly 63 percent of them][czech-inspection-sweep-kratom-store-violations], with 373 cases classified as criminal offenses, nearly half a million products seized, and 25 online stores blocked. Interior Minister Lubomír Metnar said at the time that the sweep exposed gaps that let sellers skirt existing rules, per the outlet, and the findings prompted 26 proposed legislative changes. The August report said the results would inform a proposed law known as "lex kratom," expected to take effect in January.

The September 13 roundup does not say whether Monday's package is that same lex kratom proposal or a separate measure, and it does not connect the new tax and age provisions to the 26 changes the Interior Ministry floated in August.

## A second Central European government moves the same week

The Czech discussion lands two days after Slovakia's Ministry of Health [proposed adding mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine to the country's Group I psychotropic substances list][slovakia-health-ministry-proposes-group-one-scheduling-mitragynine-7oh-january-2027], with a proposed effective date of January 1, 2027, according to the Slovak daily Pravda. The two approaches differ in kind: Slovakia's proposal would schedule the alkaloids themselves, reaching natural leaf products, while the Czech package as described would keep kratom on sale to adults 21 and older under a licensed, taxed, and advertising-restricted regime.

The age threshold in the Czech proposal matches a line several U.S. jurisdictions have drawn this month. Delaware's governor [signed a bill on September 11 setting a minimum purchase age of 21][delaware-governor-signs-hb-332-kratom-age-21] for kratom products, and Blythe, California's under-21 sales ban took effect the same day, as KNT reported.
