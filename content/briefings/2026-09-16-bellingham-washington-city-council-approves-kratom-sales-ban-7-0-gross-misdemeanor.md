The Bellingham City Council voted 7-0 on Monday evening to approve an emergency ordinance prohibiting the preparation, distribution, and sale of kratom products inside city limits, [Bellingham Metro News reported](https://bellinghammetronews.com/news/breaking/bellingham-city-council-kratom-ban-7-oh/) on September 14, 2026. The council's discussion centered on the growing availability of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, according to the outlet, but the ordinance reaches well past them. It covers traditional products made from the kratom plant, kratom extracts, and synthesized kratom material, including synthetic mitragynine and synthetic 7-OH, in powders, capsules, pills, beverages, liquids, and other edible forms.

Violations can be treated as a gross misdemeanor, which [My Bellingham Now reported](https://mybellinghamnow.com/news/297792-bellingham-moves-forward-with-ban-on-kratom-sales/) on September 15 carries up to a year in jail or a fine of up to $5,000. Both outlets reported that the ordinance does not make possession or personal use illegal, and My Bellingham Now added that purchase is not banned either. Councilmembers acknowledged during the meeting that residents may still obtain kratom online or outside the city, per Bellingham Metro News.

## The two reports differ on whether the vote was final

Bellingham Metro News described Monday's action as approval of an emergency ordinance and said it would follow up on when the restrictions take effect and how enforcement will work. My Bellingham Now, which called the measure a "partial ban," reported that the ordinance will return for final approval at a future council meeting. Neither report gave an effective date. Bellingham Metro News noted that Cascadia Daily News first reported the proposal the previous week, and that the ordinance was only a proposal days before Monday's vote.

## Lilliquist: the city cannot tell leaf from concentrate

Councilmember Michael Lilliquist said the city is not well equipped to regulate a drug like kratom and raised the difficulty of distinguishing traditional leaf-based products from highly concentrated or synthetic ones, according to Bellingham Metro News. He called the ordinance a "band-aid" and criticized the absence of state and federal rules, per My Bellingham Now.

"Ideally, we'd have a system for testing when you have a natural leaf product and when you have highly-concentrated synthetic mitragynine. We don't have that system," Lilliquist said at the meeting, according to My Bellingham Now. "There's no way that anyone who wants to enforce this could go into a store and know. They could guess, they could be told, it could say on the package, but there's no guarantee that any of that is true, because there's no regulation."

Lilliquist described an emergency in which people are "dying and are being hurt" by some forms and uses of the products, but did not identify specific Bellingham deaths in those remarks, Bellingham Metro News reported. He voted for the ordinance while calling for state and federal officials to take a larger role, and other councilmembers voiced similar concerns about the city's limited ability to determine what a product contains, per the outlet. Several said they would prefer state or federal regulation to a patchwork of city ordinances.

Community members who spoke during public comment raised the need to distinguish whole-leaf kratom from the more potent concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products, according to My Bellingham Now. The trace 7-OH present in natural kratom leaf is a different category from the concentrated and lab-made preparations that have drawn federal attention, and Bellingham's ordinance as described by both outlets does not draw that line.

## Washington cities keep moving while the state does not

Bellingham joins Spokane, Spokane Valley, and Mount Vernon among Washington cities that have adopted kratom regulations in 2026, according to Bellingham Metro News. Mount Vernon's council [voted unanimously last month to ban sales and distribution of all kratom products][mount-vernon-city-council-bans-kratom-sales], and My Bellingham Now reported that Bellingham's action follows that measure. Bellingham Metro News reported separately on September 14 that Lynden is exploring a possible ban of its own. The pattern of [local governments acting in the absence of a state framework][washington-local-governments-fill-kratom-regulatory-vacuum] has been building in Washington since the spring.

A statewide 95 percent excise tax on kratom and related products was floated in the legislature last session but never advanced past its first committee hearing, My Bellingham Now reported. The outlet also cited Cascadia Daily News reporting that local clinicians have seen an increase in patients dependent on the product.

At the federal level, My Bellingham Now pointed to the DEA's emergency scheduling last month of three 7-OH-related substances, which [placed three synthetic kratom-derived compounds in Schedule I for two years][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. Bellingham Metro News reported that city officials have cited public health concerns about kratom and concentrated 7-OH, including misuse and dependence, and that officials pointed to products sold at convenience stores and smoke shops with labeling they believe does not communicate risk. The outlet, whose reporter previously worked in the local convenience store trade, said kratom can be a significant source of sales for some retailers. Across the state line, Coeur d'Alene, Idaho took a different route the following night, [adopting a permit-and-threshold ordinance rather than a ban][coeur-dalene-idaho-council-kratom-ordinance-21-plus-permit-2-percent-7oh-cap].
