The Wayne County Medical Examiner's Office in Michigan listed "alcohol and mitragynine toxicity" as the cause of death for adult film performer Christian Styles, the office told the [New York Daily News](https://www.nydailynews.com/2026/09/15/mix-alcohol-kratom-killed-adult-entertainer-christian-styles/) by email on Tuesday, September 15, 2026. Styles, whose given name was Zachary Christian Jaghab, was 29 when his parents found him dead on August 20, according to [Us Weekly](https://www.usmagazine.com/celebrity-news/news/adult-film-star-christian-styles-cause-of-death-revealed/), which obtained the medical examiner's report the same day and reported that the manner of death was ruled accidental.

Neither report identifies the kratom product involved. Us Weekly reported that "Kratom" and the prescription antidepressant Lexapro were found at the scene, but the outlets do not say whether the product was natural leaf, a concentrated extract, or a 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation. The medical examiner's finding names mitragynine, the primary alkaloid in kratom leaf, and not 7-OH. Mitragynine is present in natural leaf products as well as in extracts, so the toxicology finding on its own does not settle which type of product was used.

## What the medical examiner's report shows

Styles had a blood alcohol level of 0.168 when he died, with mitragynine and many of its breakdown products in his blood, according to the report obtained by Us Weekly. The Daily News, citing TMZ's account of the medical examiner's findings, reported that caffeine and "numerous other drugs" were also in his system. The autopsy report indicated a 13-year history of drug use including past heavy use of heroin and methamphetamine, prior overdoses, and drug-related encounters with law enforcement, per the Daily News, citing TMZ. Us Weekly reported that the report described a "bender of various substances for a prolonged time" before his death, that his parents had been checking on him hourly, and that authorities suspected an overdose.

A GoFundMe page quoted by Us Weekly described the family as from Canton, the Wayne County community where township inspectors [seized kratom and 7-OH products from retailers earlier this month][canton-michigan-inspection-seizes-kratom-7oh-products]. Neither report connects Styles' death to any specific retailer or product source.

## How the two outlets framed kratom

The Daily News led with the phrase "gas station heroin" and then noted that the label mainly applies to tianeptine, an antidepressant not approved in the United States, with kratom falling under the same umbrella because of shared characteristics. The paper described mitragynine as an alkaloid that occurs in trace amounts in kratom leaves and wrote that products in the U.S. are often processed to increase the concentration of mitragynine and its derivatives, adding that health officials have long warned of potentially harmful side effects. It cited Yale Medicine for the range of forms in which kratom is sold, including concentrated liquid extracts, and noted the existence of synthetic versions.

Us Weekly described mitragynine as the active ingredient in kratom, citing the Mayo Clinic, and reported that the Food and Drug Administration has warned people against using the product because of its potential for harm and risk of addiction. Neither outlet quoted a toxicologist, the medical examiner beyond the cause-of-death finding, or any kratom industry or advocacy source.
