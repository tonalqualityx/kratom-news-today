The Coeur d'Alene City Council unanimously approved an ordinance on Tuesday that requires kratom sellers inside city limits to obtain a permit, forbids sales to anyone under 21, and sets chemical limits on the products that can be sold, the [Coeur d'Alene Press reported](https://cdapress.com/news/2026/sep/16/city-regulates-kratom-products/) on September 16, 2026. Until the vote, according to the Press, retailers in the city could legally sell kratom to buyers under 21.

The ordinance prohibits the possession, production, or sale of any kratom product containing more than 2 percent total 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) alkaloids, and separately bars any single serving that exceeds 1 milligram of 7-OH, per the Press. Those two numbers are the dividing line the city drew between leaf-derived products and the concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations that have become the focus of state and federal action. The Press described the concern as manufacturers offering a synthesized version of kratom above 2 percent 7-OH, while natural kratom contains only small traces of the alkaloid.

Gina Rogers, co-founder of Inland Botanicals, a Hayden herbal supplier, told the council that natural kratom does not reach a 7-OH level above 2 percent, according to the Press. "That's pretty normal and reasonable," Rogers said of the cap. "I don't think that there should be anything higher than that."

## What the council debated before the vote

Council members spent time on the mechanics of the ordinance before passing it, the Press reported, including whether kratom products should carry labeling, whether confection products such as gummies should be prohibited, and whether the purchase age should be 21 or 18.

"I do believe that time is of the essence," Councilmember Dan English said, per the Press. "There's a real awareness of this issue in the community, and I do think this is a good starting point."

Resident Allie Mathes, who told the council she uses natural kratom for health issues, spoke in favor during public comment. "With that, I absolutely support regulation of kratom," Mathes said, according to the Press.

The council asked Police Chief Greg Yeager whether minors have been misusing kratom. "I've already had minors in this city shoplift kratom," Yeager said, per the Press, adding that school resource officers have confiscated kratom from students.

City Attorney Randy Adams said the council still needs to decide the license fee for businesses that want to sell kratom, the Press reported. Violators of the ordinance could face civil or criminal misdemeanor penalties, and sellers could lose their license after multiple offenses, according to the outlet.

## A permit system in a county whose coroner wants a ban

The Coeur d'Alene approach differs from what Kootenai County Coroner Dr. Duke Johnson has been asking for. Johnson has visited local governments to oppose kratom and call for bans, the Press reported, and has said kratom use is driving an influx of people with dependence into drug rehabilitation facilities in the county. In June, Johnson [urged county commissioners to ban all kratom sales][idaho-coroner-seeks-kratom-ban-alabama-seizes-products], arguing that concentration limits and age restrictions had not reduced hospitalizations or deaths elsewhere. Coeur d'Alene's ordinance adopts exactly those two tools.

Among nearby governments, Kellogg is the only one in the region to ban kratom outright, and Spokane, across the state line, has banned sale and distribution, according to the Press. Idaho Falls [adopted its own ban earlier this year][idaho-falls-kratom-ban-botanic-tonics-wrongful-death]. On Monday night, the Bellingham, Washington City Council [voted 7-0 for an emergency ordinance banning kratom sales][bellingham-washington-city-council-approves-kratom-sales-ban-7-0-gross-misdemeanor], with councilmembers there saying the city had no way to test whether a product was natural leaf or a synthetic concentrate. Coeur d'Alene's ordinance answers that problem with a numeric threshold; the Press report does not say how the city plans to verify compliance with it.
