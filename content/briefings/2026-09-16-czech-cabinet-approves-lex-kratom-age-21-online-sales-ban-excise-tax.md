The Czech cabinet approved the amendment to the country's addictive substances law known as lex kratom on Monday, September 14, 2026, Health Minister Adam Vojtěch announced on X, [Expats.cz reported](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/czechia-approves-tighter-kratom-rules-online-sales-to-be-banned) the same day. The approval follows the government's [scheduled September 14 discussion of the proposal][czech-government-weighs-kratom-age-21-online-sales-ban-excise-tax] and moves the bill to parliament, which must pass it before the rules can take effect next year, according to the outlet.

The bill raises the minimum purchase age from 18 to 21 and bans sales through e-shops, [Deník N reported](https://denikn.cz/minuta/2183904/), citing the Czech News Agency (ČTK). "Kratom is not a harmless product, and I certainly do not want it to become a normal part of young people's lives," Vojtěch wrote on X, per Deník N. Kratom has been legally sold to adults 18 and older through licensed shops since November 2025 as a lower-risk psychomodulatory substance, and only brick-and-mortar retailers have been allowed to run online stores, according to the outlets.

## What the bill contains

[Zdravotnický deník reported](https://www.zdravotnickydenik.cz/2026/09/precenovana-prohibice-exkoordinator-voboril-tepe-vladni-lex-kratom-posili-cerny-trh/), citing ČTK, that the measure prepared by the health and finance ministries would:

- Confine sales to licensed physical stores and ban online and cross-border sales. Municipalities could decide whether to allow such shops in their territory and where.
- Raise the annual shop fee from CZK 20,000 to CZK 30,000, restrict storefront signage to "Psychomodulatory substances" in letters no taller than 30 centimeters with the company name at the entrance in letters no taller than three centimeters, and require covered windows and entrances.
- Set a personal-use limit of 50 grams or 10 milliliters.
- Raise VAT from 12 to 21 percent and add an excise tax of CZK 2, 10, or 20 per gram or milliliter depending on concentration. The ministries project CZK 400 million a year in VAT and CZK 800 million in excise revenue.
- Require a tax stamp on packaging, impose hygiene procedures on distributors, sellers, and carriers, expand the powers of customs and public health officers, provide for seizure and destruction, and raise fines to as much as CZK 20 million.

The same bill bans enriching cannabis by spraying or other methods, bars imports and exports of enriched material, and prohibits using CBD or THCA as raw material for other substances, per Zdravotnický deník. Expats.cz reported that the CBD and technical cannabis provisions have drawn their own criticism from addiction experts and businesses.

The Health Ministry's supporting materials argue the current rules do not protect young people adequately, saying kratom use is widespread among secondary school students, often combined with other substances, and has led to intoxications requiring medical intervention, according to Deník N and Zdravotnický deník. The ministry did not give a case count, both outlets noted. The government previewed the bill in early July, and Zdravotnický deník reported that after the comment procedure it added further proposals from an interministerial group following the police operation KORUND without consulting experts.

## Experts back the tax and the age limit, oppose the online ban

The Chamber of Commerce and addiction specialists oppose the e-shop ban, arguing demand will shift from the legal market to the illegal one, Deník N reported. Former national drug coordinator Jindřich Vobořil, speaking for the Think Tank for Rational Addiction Policy, said blanket bans were inserted "at the last minute and without a comment procedure" and would work against the logic of health protection, according to Zdravotnický deník. He called the result "more like prohibition" that would push demand "to an illegal market that no one controls and creates space for organized crime."

The think tank supports the excise tax, the VAT increase, the quality, hygiene, and packaging requirements, advertising limits, the move to age 21, and the cannabis enrichment ban, per Zdravotnický deník. Its objection is to the online sales ban specifically: remote sales verify age at the time of ordering and again at delivery, which the group described as stricter than an in-store check. It argued that demand would move to unlicensed cross-border sellers and the darknet, health risks would rise, and the state would lose both oversight and tax revenue, according to the outlet. The group recommended reworking the bill with the ministry's drug policy experts, keeping regulated online sales with strict age verification, and directing part of the tax proceeds to prevention and treatment.

Prime Minister Andrej Babiš answered the criticism directly. "We are not stupid, we monitor the black market too," Babiš said, per Deník N, adding that police, customs, and the anti-drug unit "will fight hard against this substance, which should never have been here."

The cabinet's action comes weeks after a [nationwide inspection sweep found violations at kratom stores][czech-inspection-sweep-kratom-store-violations], and days after neighboring Slovakia's health ministry [proposed placing mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine on its Group I psychotropic substances list][slovakia-health-ministry-proposes-group-one-scheduling-mitragynine-7oh-january-2027] from January 2027. The Czech bill, as described in the reports, keeps kratom in its regulated psychomodulatory category rather than scheduling it.
