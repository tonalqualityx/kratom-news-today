Lee County, Mississippi's ban on kratom takes effect on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, [WTVA reported](https://www.wtva.com/2026/09/15/lee-countys-kratom-ban-go-into-effect-this-week/) on September 15. The Board of Supervisors unanimously approved the ban in February, according to the station, but an appeal filed to stop it kept the measure from taking effect. Board attorney Gary Carnathan told supervisors on Tuesday morning that the appeal had been dismissed in court, clearing the way for enforcement, per WTVA.

Businesses in the county that sell kratom have until Friday to remove it from their shelves, the station reported. WTVA's report does not describe the ordinance's scope, whether it distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, or the penalties for selling after the deadline.

## The county follows its cities

Tupelo, the county seat, already has a kratom ban in place, and Guntown and Saltillo have adopted bans of their own, according to WTVA. Tupelo's ordinance was also held up by a store owner's appeal before its police department [began enforcing it on September 12][tupelo-mississippi-police-begin-enforcing-kratom-ban-september-12] with 48 hours' notice to businesses; that ordinance covers all kratom products, including natural leaf, and the city said its goal was voluntary compliance. WTVA's report on the county ban does not say whether the county appeal was the same case or a separate one.

Carnathan said he expects other municipalities in Lee County to follow suit now that the appeal has been dismissed, per the station. WTVA's report includes a description of kratom's effects that it attributes to the Drug Enforcement Administration, characterizing the leaves as producing stimulant and sedative effects with a risk of dependence; the station does not cite a specific DEA document. The report does not quote any retailer or say how many businesses in the county carry the products.
