More than 3,000 additional comments were posted to the Federal Register during the 15-day extension of the public comment period on the federal plan to set a legal threshold for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), [Pain News Network reported](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/9/15/thousands-of-public-comments-support-keeping-7-oh-legal) on September 15, 2026. Almost all of them oppose the DEA's proposed limit of 1 milligram per tablet, gummy, or shot, arguing there is no evidence to support it, according to PNN's Pat Anson. The outlet put the total at nearly 36,000 comments since the Department of Health and Human Services opened its request for information in early July.

Under the DEA's July proposal, products containing more than 1 milligram of 7-OH, or more than 0.05 percent by weight or volume, would be classified as Schedule I controlled substances if the order is finalized as written, PNN reported. That threshold targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations; the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf sits far below it.

## How the count compares with the docket record

PNN's figure is higher than what the docket showed when the comment period closed. [As of the morning of September 12, Regulations.gov listed 30,346 posted comments][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments], while a treatment provider's September 10 post put submissions above 32,000. PNN does not say how it arrived at nearly 36,000 or whether its count includes submissions that had not yet been posted. HHS [extended the deadline to September 10][hhs-extends-7oh-comment-period-september-10] in late August after the original window closed.

## What the commenters wrote

The comments PNN quotes come from individuals rather than trade groups or manufacturers. Several identified themselves as pain patients who use 7-OH because they cannot obtain prescription opioids, and said 1 milligram is far below their daily use, according to the outlet. A licensed practical nurse, April Sikes, wrote that she takes 15 milligrams twice a day and asked regulators not to "take another tool away from people who are simply trying to function, work, and live with dignity," per PNN. Other commenters quoted by the outlet described daily amounts between 20 and 50 milligrams per dose.

Some commenters supported regulation short of scheduling. Chris O'Donoghue wrote that "no concentration or quantity of 7-OH has been identified as an imminent hazard" and said he supports age limits, lab testing, and licensed retail to get the products out of gas stations, according to PNN. William Baldwin wrote that 7-OH "most certainly must be regulated in some way" and "absolutely must not be allowed to be sold at gas stations and head-shops," proposing a license comparable to a tobacco or liquor license and strict quality control among suppliers, per the outlet. Collin Lomelino argued that a ban would drive people toward fentanyl and other black-market opioids, PNN reported.

Commenter Joseph Garnella pointed to animal data, asking how humans could die from oral 7-OH if researchers "couldn't kill mice" with it, and asserted that the only related deaths involved multiple drugs and alcohol, according to PNN. The outlet did not independently verify that claim.

## PNN's framing of the evidence

PNN, a nonprofit pain-advocacy news service, wrote that no clinical trials or toxicological studies have shown 7-OH to be toxic or unsafe in humans, and that no studies have established what a safe threshold would be. The outlet cited the FDA's 2025 scientific assessment of 7-OH as acknowledging "little information" on the alkaloid's safety profile, and said the existing rodent studies found 7-OH to be a potent analgesic several times stronger than morphine that did not produce the same respiratory depression when taken orally. PNN also wrote that states, cities, and counties that have banned 7-OH have relied mainly on anecdotal reports of poison control calls, overdoses, and hospitalizations. Those characterizations are PNN's; the FDA's July 2025 recommendation and the DEA's proposal rest on the agencies' own reading of the same literature.

PNN acknowledged that it is unclear what effect the comments will have. The DEA reports to Attorney General Todd Blanche, not to HHS, the outlet noted, and could either amend the threshold to allow more than 1 milligram or implement the order at any time as currently written. As of September 12, no DEA temporary scheduling order for concentrated 7-OH had appeared in the Federal Register. PNN's report does not describe where the kratom trade associations, which have taken positions on concentrated 7-OH distinct from those of the consumers it quotes, stand on the extension-period comments.
