The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation is warning that tablet packs sold as "Cat's Claw," a South American vine that is legal to buy and consume in Tennessee, are turning up with kratom inside, [NewsChannel 9 reported](https://newschannel9.com/news/local/tbi-warns-mislabeled-cats-claw-products-may-contain-illegal-kratom-in-tennessee) on September 14, 2026. A TBI photo caption carried by both NewsChannel 9 and [FOX 17 Nashville](https://fox17.com/news/local/whats-cats-claw-tennessee-officials-warn-of-mislabeled-products-containing-kratom) identifies the substance found in some of the products as MGM-15, which the agency described as made from kratom.

MGM-15 is a synthetic compound derived from kratom alkaloids, and it is one of the three substances, along with MGM-16 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, that the DEA [placed in federal Schedule I for two years effective August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. It is distinct from natural kratom leaf and from 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The two Tennessee reports use "kratom" and "MGM-15" interchangeably when describing the tablets, and neither says whether the crime lab found natural leaf alkaloids, the synthetic compound, or both across the cases it has examined.

## Eighteen submissions since the ban took effect

TBI Agent Mark Delaney said the agency has seen 18 instances since July in which Cat's Claw-labeled tablet packs laced with kratom were submitted to the TBI crime lab, including some products that did not list kratom as an ingredient, according to NewsChannel 9. Tennessee's statewide kratom ban [took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], and Delaney said the substance is showing up again on store shelves, including in tablet form mixed with Cat's Claw, per the outlet.

"It poses a high risk of overdose, whether fatal and or non-fatal," Delaney said, according to NewsChannel 9. "I'd like to reiterate that cat's claw itself is legal."

The agency is responding with public awareness alerts. "We're making public safety and health awareness bulletins to make sure that the consumers actually know what they're trying to consume," Delaney said, per the outlet. FOX 17 reported that the TBI is urging store owners to double-check what they order and stock, since selling mislabeled products exposes both the store and its customers, and is telling consumers not to trust a product on the strength of its packaging.

Thad Oliver, a clinician with the Chattanooga treatment provider CADAS, told NewsChannel 9 he has worked with many people who have experienced kratom overdose or withdrawal and warned that the mislabeled tablets can be dangerous. "If you don't know what you're putting in your body, you can overdose from it," Oliver said. The TBI pointed people seeking help with substance use to the Tennessee Redline, per both outlets.

## Where the warning fits in Tennessee's enforcement picture

Matthew Davenport's Law makes it a crime to possess, manufacture, sell, or distribute kratom anywhere in Tennessee, FOX 17 reported. The TBI warning follows a run of local enforcement actions since the ban took effect: a [Greenbrier raid that seized 350 packages of 7-OH products and a fentanyl analogue][greenbrier-tennessee-raid-seizes-350-packages-7oh-fentanyl-analogue] earlier this month, and the [Lincoln County sheriff's seizure of kratom from 21 businesses][lincoln-county-tennessee-sheriff-seizes-kratom-21-businesses] last week. The Cat's Claw cases differ from those in one respect the TBI emphasized: the packaging does not say kratom at all.

Neither report names the brands, manufacturers, or retailers involved, says where the 18 submissions originated, or describes any arrests or charges tied to the tablets. FOX 17 stated that kratom "has ties to several deadly overdoses" in the state without citing a source for that figure, and NewsChannel 9 did not report a comparable claim.
