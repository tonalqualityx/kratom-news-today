The Town of Castle Rock, Colorado, outlawed the retail sale of kratom products on Tuesday, September 15, by a 6-1 vote of the Town Council, the [Denver Gazette reported](https://www.denvergazette.com/2026/09/16/castle-rock-outlaws-kratom-after-community-pushback-2/) on September 16, 2026. The measure prohibits retail sale only; it does not prohibit possession or use, according to the paper's account by Nicholas Fogleman. The vote came after residents spoke against the ban, citing benefits they said they had experienced and what they described as government overreach.

The kratom provision is part of a broader update to the town's municipal criminal code, first adopted in 1968, the Gazette reported. Town staff said many provisions in that code were obsolete or needed revision, and the kratom prohibition drew the most public scrutiny of any item in the package, per the paper. The Gazette's report did not give an effective date for the ordinance.

## What residents told the council

Multiple residents opposed the ban, and many urged the council to distinguish natural leaf kratom from synthetic derivatives in the way state and federal governments have, according to the Gazette. Speakers argued that regulators had already addressed the higher-risk synthetic products: the Drug Enforcement Administration [placed three kratom-derived synthetic compounds in Schedule I in August][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], and Colorado in 2025 outlawed products containing high-potency synthesized kratom alkaloids and products with 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) levels above 2 percent, the paper reported.

"It is reprehensible that a city or town would try to control a person's private opportunity to make their own choices about how they manage their pain," one speaker said, per the Gazette. Other speakers described chronic pain, opioid addiction, and anxiety they said had been helped by access to natural kratom products, and some called kratom a "Plan B" to opioids for people in recovery, arguing that a prohibition could push some residents toward alcohol, according to the paper. Others framed the ban as inconsistent with limited-government principles, saying adults do not need a "morality police," the Gazette reported.

## How the council split

Some council members were hesitant about a blanket ban, the Gazette reported, asking about a carveout for natural kratom products or whether sales could instead be limited to specialty stores better able to manage minors' access. Councilmember Kevin Bracken, who cast the only dissenting vote, cited harm to existing businesses and adults' access to the benefits described in public comment, and said he would support a ban on synthetic derivatives rather than a blanket prohibition. "Most of the bad outcomes point to the synthetic," Bracken said, per the paper.

Councilmember Max Brooks supported the ban, citing public health and community safety, and compared it to Castle Rock's longstanding prohibition on cannabis sales, noting that residents remain free to possess and use kratom in town, according to the Gazette. Brooks said kratom was inconsistent with the town's character and predicted little impact on local businesses, the paper reported.

Castle Rock Police Chief Jack Cauley told the council there had been no significant issues involving adult users, though he raised concerns about youth access and noted that sales to minors are already illegal, per the Gazette.

## The health framing in the report

The Gazette cited the Food and Drug Administration's warning that kratom consumption can increase the risk of addiction, liver toxicity, and other health risks, and cited the American Psychiatric Association's concern about highly concentrated synthetic products that contain high levels of 7-OH and similar high-potency synthetic compounds. Those concentrated products are distinct from the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf, a distinction the residents who spoke against the ban asked the council to write into the ordinance, according to the paper. The Gazette's account does not indicate that the ordinance as passed makes that distinction.

Castle Rock's vote follows the Steamboat Pilot's reporting earlier this month on [two Yampa Valley deaths tied to concentrated 7-OH tablets][yampa-valley-deaths-concentrated-7oh-tablets-colorado-shops] that were still available by request at a Colorado shop. The Gazette's report on the Castle Rock meeting did not reference those deaths.
