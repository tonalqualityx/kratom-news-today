Kinsale Insurance Co. has asked a federal judge in San Diego to declare that it never had a duty to defend Hydroxie LLC, a maker of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) tablets, or the company's two members against a wrongful-death suit in North Carolina, and to order the defendants to repay the legal fees Kinsale has spent defending them, according to a [complaint filed September 11, 2026](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.casd.870599/gov.uscourts.casd.870599.1.0.pdf) in the U.S. District Court for the Southern District of California. [Law360 reported](https://www.law360.com/articles/2524866/insurer-wants-back-kratom-co-wrongful-death-suit-atty-fees) the filing on September 16.

Hydroxie sells concentrated 7-OH tablets, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH found in natural kratom leaf, according to the Drug Enforcement Administration's July Federal Register notice on 7-OH, which [named Hydroxie among the branded 7-OH tablet products][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action] it cited as examples of the category. Separately, Kinsale's complaint references a [June 25, 2025 FDA warning letter](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/warning-letters/hydroxie-llc-709661-06252025) to the company that, according to a preservation letter from the plaintiff's counsel quoted in the complaint, described Hydroxie's 7-OH products as adulterated and reported to have opioid-like effects. The underlying wrongful-death complaint, as Kinsale summarizes it, refers to both "Kratom and 7-OH" products bought at Charlotte-area smoke shops and does not, in that summary, separate which products came from which manufacturer.

## The underlying suit

According to Kinsale's summary, Kristin Mills filed suit on or around July 10, 2026, in Mecklenburg County, North Carolina, Superior Court (Case No. 26CV038244-590) as personal representative of the estate of her husband, James Patrick Mills, who she alleges died at age 36 on July 11, 2024, at the couple's home in Lancaster, South Carolina. The underlying complaint alleges that Mills "died of a massive overdose of Kratom and 7-OH" sold to him at vape shops in Charlotte, that he spent more than $5,600 on 7-OH products in the 15 days before his death, and that a North Carolina Medical Examiner's Office toxicology test found "lethal level of mitragynin and deadly additive 7-hydroxymitragynine" in his blood, per Kinsale's complaint.

The Mills suit names a group of smoke shop operators, including entities doing business as High Life Smoke shops and Vapor Smoke Shop, and a group of manufacturers that includes Thang Botanicals Inc., doing business as 7ΩHMZ, along with Hydroxie, Shaw, and Israel, according to Kinsale's complaint. It asserts claims for wrongful death, negligence and gross negligence, fraud, and product liability, and seeks compensatory and punitive damages. Kinsale states in its filing that it recites the allegations as background "without opining on whether the allegations are true."

Kinsale says it agreed to defend Hydroxie, Shaw, and Israel under a written reservation of rights dated August 28, 2026.

## The policy and the exclusions

The complaint describes a claims-made Life Sciences General Liability policy issued to Hydroxie, KAVAHH LLC, and Ricco Management Company Inc. for the period May 7, 2025 to May 7, 2026, with limits of $1 million per claim and $2 million in the aggregate, a $25,000 per-claim deductible, and a retroactive date of March 1, 2024 for Hydroxie products.

Kinsale's first claim rests on an endorsement it calls the Kratom Limitation, a policy form titled "LIMITATION - KRATOM" that adds a "Kratom, Opioids, and Narcotics" exclusion, according to the complaint. As quoted in the filing, the exclusion removes coverage for any claim "arising out of, related to, or in any way involving any actual or alleged" abuse, misuse, overuse, unlawful distribution, or "addictive use" of any opioid, narcotic, "kratom derived product, derivative of kratom," or controlled substance under federal or state schedules, and extends to failure-to-warn allegations. The exclusion carves out bodily injury caused by a "defect in manufacturing" of the insured's product, per the complaint. Kinsale argues that because the Mills suit arises from alleged addiction to kratom and 7-OH and alleges inadequate warnings, the exclusion is "a complete bar to coverage."

The second claim invokes a Health Hazard exclusion in the same endorsement, which the complaint quotes as excluding any "organ failure or disruption in the function of any organ as a result of any use of, consumption of, or exposure to" the insured's product. Kinsale's third claim seeks a declaration that it has no duty to indemnify, and its fourth and fifth claims seek reimbursement of defense fees and costs paid from August 28, 2026 forward, under a declaratory theory and, alternatively, in quasi-contract. The complaint states that Kinsale "is informed and believes" the defendants dispute that the exclusions apply.

## Procedural posture

The case, No. 3:26-cv-05265, is assigned to U.S. District Judge Benjamin J. Cheeks and referred to Magistrate Judge David D. Leshner, and is brought under diversity jurisdiction, according to the [CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/74782632/kinsale-insurance-company-v-hydroxie-llc/). Kinsale is represented by Matthew J. Hafey and Patricia A. Daza-Luu of Nicolaides Fink Thorpe Michaelides Sullivan LLP in Los Angeles. The docket shows a summons issued on September 11 and no response from the defendants as of September 17, and Law360's report did not include comment from Hydroxie or its members.

The filing lands while 7-OH's federal status remains unsettled. The DEA's proposed threshold-based scheduling of 7-OH [drew nearly 36,000 public comments][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments] before its docket closed September 10, and the agency's [August order][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] placed three related synthetic compounds, though not 7-OH itself, in Schedule I. The exclusion Kinsale quotes lists "kratom derived product" and "derivative of kratom" separately from substances on federal or state controlled-substance schedules.
