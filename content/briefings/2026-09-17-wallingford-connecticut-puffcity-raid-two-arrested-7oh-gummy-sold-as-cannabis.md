Two employees of PuffCity Smoke Shop in Wallingford, Connecticut, have been arrested on controlled-substance charges after police said a product sold at the shop, which the buyer believed to be a cannabis gummy, contained the kratom alkaloid 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and a related compound, 7-hydroxymitragynine pseudoindoxyl, the [Hartford Courant reported](https://www.courant.com/2026/09/16/ct-vape-shop-employees-accused-of-selling-controlled-substances-that-led-to-severe-reaction-for-customer/) on September 16, 2026. Both substances are Schedule I controlled substances under Connecticut law, Lt. Stephen S. Jaques Jr. of the Wallingford Police Department told the paper. Gummies containing 7-OH as an ingredient are concentrated or synthetic preparations, distinct from the trace amounts of 7-OH found in natural kratom leaf, according to the Drugs.com description the Courant cited; the Courant's report does not say whether the shop also sold natural leaf kratom.

## How the investigation started

The case began in July, when police responded to a welfare check involving a person who had suffered a "severe adverse reaction" after consuming a product allegedly bought at the shop, according to Jaques, as quoted by the Courant. The buyer reportedly believed the product was a cannabis gummy, the paper reported. Testing found the product contained 7-OH and 7-hydroxymitragynine pseudoindoxyl, Jaques said. The Courant's account does not describe the person's condition after the reaction, or say whether the product was labeled as cannabis or the buyer assumed it was.

Police obtained multiple search and seizure warrants for the shop and a vehicle tied to the investigation after finding evidence that 7-OH products were being sold there, Jaques said, and raided the shop last Friday with the department's Crime Suppression Unit, Patrol Division, and a K-9 unit, per the Courant. Investigators found "additional quantities" of the prohibited products and about $14,000 believed to have come from the sales, Jaques said.

## The charges

The two employees arrested were identified as Oguzhan Goktas, 51, of Branford, and Baris Alanc, 27, of Wallingford, according to the Courant. Each faces charges of illegal possession of a controlled substance and possession of a controlled substance with intent to sell, and bond for each was set at $15,000, the paper reported. Goktas is scheduled to appear in Meriden Superior Court on September 24, with Alanc expected the following day, per the Courant. Police said the investigation is continuing.

The Courant characterized 7-OH by citing Drugs.com, which the paper said describes the alkaloid as occurring naturally in trace amounts in kratom but as dangerous at enhanced levels or as an added ingredient, and as a "novel potent opioid" sold in gummies and drink shots at smoke shops, gas stations, and corner stores without FDA approval.

## Where Connecticut's law stands

Connecticut added kratom and 7-OH to its controlled-substance schedule earlier this year, and [retailers began pulling products from shelves in June][georgia-kratom-law-connecticut-schedule-one], as KNT reported at the time.

The compound the Courant identifies as 7-hydroxymitragynine pseudoindoxyl is described in the report only by that name. The Drug Enforcement Administration's August order [placed a compound it calls mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in federal Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], along with MGM-15 and MGM-16; the Courant's report does not address the federal order or say whether the substance found in Wallingford falls under it. 7-OH itself remains unscheduled at the federal level while a separate threshold proposal is pending.
