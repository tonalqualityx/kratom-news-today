Marion County, Florida commissioners voted unanimously on Monday, September 14, 2026 to adopt zoning rules that keep new standalone smoke, vape, and kratom shops at least 1,500 feet from schools, licensed daycares, places of worship, and public parks, and at least 2,500 feet from one another, [352today](https://352today.com/news/257752-marion-county-approves-1500-foot-buffer-for-smoke-vape-and-kratom-shops/) reported on September 15. The ordinance creates Section 4.3.30 of the county's Land Development Code and applies only in unincorporated Marion County, according to the report. The county framed it as a rule about where such shops may open, and explicitly not a ban on the products they sell.

The rule reaches shops where tobacco, e-cigarettes, vapes, or kratom are the primary use, 352today reported. Kratom is defined by reference to Section 500.92 of the Florida Statutes, the state's kratom consumer protection provision, according to the outlet. Grocery stores and convenience stores are exempt when the products are incidental to their principal business, and shops already operating become pre-existing nonconforming uses, which under the report's description allows them to continue at their current locations. The report did not give an effective date.

## What commissioners and the public said

Commissioner Matt McClain asked staff during the hearing, "Why did we strike vape shops, and are e-cigarettes going to encompass what we're trying to do?" according to 352today, a question about whether the ordinance's definitions capture the retail category commissioners intended. Commission Chair Carl Zalak replied that the definition could be revisited later if it becomes an issue, the outlet reported. The only public comment 352today described came from resident David Tillman, who questioned the convenience store exemption and said some such stores "carry just products of hedonism altogether."

The final version is broader than the draft that first surfaced this summer. The county's Land Development Regulation Commission, a volunteer advisory body, took up a proposal on July 1 that would have set a 1,000-foot separation from schools and public parks for new smoke shops, with a special use permit available for those that could not meet it, [352today](https://352today.com/news/257752-marion-county-land-development-regulation-commission-to-discuss-smoke-shops-food-trucks-at-wednesday-meeting/) reported at the time. The adopted rule extends the distance to 1,500 feet, adds daycares and places of worship, and adds the 2,500-foot spacing between shops.

## A zoning rule in place of a ban

The buffer arrives a month after the same board declined to pursue a product ban. On August 18, commissioners [paused drafting a local kratom ordinance][naperville-council-debates-kratom-retail-ban] to await the Drug Enforcement Administration's scheduling decision on synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), which occurs only in trace amounts in natural leaf, and to align with a parallel City of Ocala effort, [Ocala-News.com](https://www.ocala-news.com/2026/08/18/marion-county-pauses-local-kratom-ban-for-federal-dea-ruling-alignment-with-city/) reported. Commissioner Kathy Bryant said at that meeting that the board's interest lay in pausing and following up on the DEA ruling, and the discussion cited the lack of local testing capacity to enforce a synthetic-only ban under Section 500.92, according to the outlet. McClain said then that he was "not interested in banning any plant or leaf," called synthetic derivatives concerning but hard to enforce against, and raised the idea of regulating smoke and vape shop proximity to schools and churches through the land development code, per Ocala-News.com. The September 14 ordinance is that idea enacted.

Marion County's approach places it among local governments that have reached for land-use tools rather than sales prohibitions. [Canton Township, Michigan trustees advanced an ordinance last week][canton-township-trustees-advance-kratom-ordinance-21-plus-500-foot-buffer] pairing a 21-and-over sales rule with a 500-foot buffer from schools. Elsewhere this week, [the Montgomery County, Pennsylvania district attorney took the opposite route][montgomery-county-pennsylvania-da-steele-notices-retailers-cease-concentrated-7oh-designer-drug], serving notices that treat concentrated 7-OH products as designer drugs under existing state law. 352today did not report any comment from kratom retailers, trade groups, or the City of Ocala on the Marion County vote.
