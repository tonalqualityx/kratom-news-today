Montgomery County District Attorney Kevin R. Steele's office served notices on retailers throughout the suburban Philadelphia county on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, stating that concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products qualify as designer drugs under Pennsylvania law, [Daily Voice](https://dailyvoice.com/article/da-kevin-steele-warns-pa-retailers-to-stop-selling-7-oh/) reported on September 17. The notice tells retailers: "You should immediately cease sale, offer for sale, or other distribution of concentrated 7-OH products," according to the outlet. The products at issue are concentrated and semisynthetic 7-OH preparations, which the office distinguished from natural kratom leaf: 7-OH occurs naturally in very small amounts in kratom, and manufacturers use a semisynthetic process to create concentrated products with opioid-like effects, Daily Voice reported, summarizing the office's description.

The office announced the notices on Thursday, September 17, according to [Patch](https://patch.com/pennsylvania/norristown/montco-kratom-sellers-urged-remove-products-shelves-da), which reported that the notice informs retailers that concentrated 7-OH products "qualify as a designer drug and are prohibited under Pennsylvania Law now." Neither outlet reported how many retailers received notices or named any store.

## What the notices cover and what the office says happens next

The products are typically sold at smoke shops, gas stations, and convenience stores as tablets, gummies, vapes, and small drinks resembling energy shots, Daily Voice reported. Some carry branding that references opioids, including "Opiates," "Dope," and "Perks," according to the outlet. Patch quoted the office as saying that "many concentrated 7-OH products do not indicate their similarities to other opioids," which the office said could leave users unaware of dependence risk.

Knowing or intentional sale or distribution of a designer drug carries a maximum penalty of 15 years in prison and a $250,000 fine, Daily Voice reported. The office also warned that a retailer that continues to sell the products after receiving the notice could have that conduct used as evidence that it knew what the products were and how they act, according to the outlet. Neither report described any seizures, charges, or a compliance deadline, and neither cited a court ruling applying Pennsylvania's designer drug provision to 7-OH.

The two outlets frame the products' current legal status differently. Patch wrote that, despite the request from officials, "it remains legal for concentrated 7-OH products to be sold as tablets, gummies, vapes, and small energy drinks," and that the DEA's July announcement had so far resulted in only some 7-OH derivatives being scheduled. Daily Voice reported that the DEA announced its intent to temporarily schedule certain 7-OH products in July and that many concentrated products remained unscheduled as of September 17, while carrying the district attorney's position that the products are already prohibited under the state designer drug law. The federal piece both outlets reference is the DEA's [August 26 Schedule I order covering mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]; the separate order on concentrated 7-OH itself has not been published.

## The Pennsylvania backdrop

Steele's notices follow a run of county-level pressure on the state. [The Bucks and Chester County district attorneys sent letters on August 31][bucks-chester-das-urge-pennsylvania-emergency-7oh-scheduling] asking Health Secretary Dr. Debra Bogen to use emergency powers to schedule 7-OH and related derivatives, arguing that the federal gap left local police unable to act without a matching state statute. [The Department of Health told the Pennsylvania Capital-Star on September 8 that it was "reviewing the situation and considering all options,"][pennsylvania-health-department-reviewing-7oh-request-lawmakers-push-regulation] as lawmakers pushed bills to ban synthetic kratom products and the American Kratom Association, which supports restrictions on concentrated 7-OH, vowed to "step it up" in the state. Daily Voice reported that the department issued an advisory on August 19 warning of withdrawal risks associated with the products.

Steele's approach differs from his neighbors' in relying on an existing statute rather than asking for a new scheduling action. Attorney General Dave Sunday had said in August that [his office was prepared to enforce the DEA's temporary scheduling][pennsylvania-7oh-enforcement-san-diego-smoke-shop-lawsuit] once it took effect. Neither Daily Voice nor Patch reported a response from retailers, from any trade group, or from the attorney general's office to the Montgomery County notices.

Elsewhere this week, [Marion County, Florida commissioners adopted a zoning buffer for smoke, vape, and kratom shops][marion-county-florida-1500-foot-buffer-smoke-vape-kratom-shops-not-a-ban], and [the Nashville medical examiner attributed producer Tay Keith's death in part to 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl][tay-keith-death-mitragynine-7oh-pseudoindoxyl-ketamine-toxicity-nashville-medical-examiner] without identifying the product involved.
