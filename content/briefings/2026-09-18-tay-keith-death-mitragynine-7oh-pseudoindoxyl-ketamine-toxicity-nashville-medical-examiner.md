The Office of the Medical Examiner in Nashville, Tennessee listed "mitragynine, 7-hydroxy mitragynine, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and ketamine toxicity" as the cause of death for Grammy-nominated producer Tay Keith and ruled the manner an accident, according to an autopsy report obtained by [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/tay-keith-grammy-nominated-record-producer-died-drug/story?id=136531117) and reported on September 17, 2026. Keith, whose given name was BryTavious Lakeith Chambers, was 29 when officers conducting a welfare check found him dead in his Nashville apartment on June 18, 2026, ABC News reported. [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/tay-keith-cause-of-death-revealed-1235628490/), which said a source at the medical examiner's office confirmed the finding, quoted the report as citing "combined" toxicity of the four substances.

The toxicology finding names two kratom alkaloids beyond mitragynine, and both carry regulatory weight. 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) occurs in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf and is the compound at the center of the Drug Enforcement Administration's pending scheduling action on concentrated and synthetic preparations. Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl is one of the three compounds the DEA [placed in Schedule I on August 26, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], which the Justice Department described as synthetic, 7-OH-related substances manufactured into opioid products. None of the reports identify what product Keith used, and none say whether it was natural leaf kratom, a concentrated extract, or a synthetic preparation. [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/09/17/tay-keith-cause-of-death/), which first reported the finding, wrote that it remained unclear whether he was using kratom intentionally or came into contact with it another way. The presence of the two additional alkaloids on a toxicology report does not, on its own, settle that question: the American Kratom Association has [argued that there is no reliable way to measure pseudoindoxyl levels in botanical material][aka-warns-pseudoindoxyl-scheduling-could-reach-natural-leaf], and the reports do not say whether the medical examiner drew any conclusion about the product's origin.

## What the report and the coverage say

The autopsy also noted the presence of nicotine and ethanol and mild coronary artery disease, none of which were identified as contributing to the death, according to Rolling Stone. TMZ reported that Keith was found "surrounded by drug paraphernalia." [Complex](https://www.complex.com/music/a/tracewilliamcowen/tay-keith-cause-death-autopsy-ketamine), citing TMZ, reported that Metropolitan Nashville Police had said at the time that no foul play was suspected and that the death was unclassified pending the autopsy.

The four outlets diverge in how much of the finding they carry. ABC News and Rolling Stone reproduce the full four-substance cause of death, including 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl. TMZ and Complex describe the cause as the toxic effects of ketamine and mitragynine, which Complex called "a main component of kratom." Complex also noted that kratom "is the subject of bans in multiple states despite being legal federally." ABC News described mitragynine as a naturally occurring indole alkaloid in Mitragyna speciosa that is "frequently regarded" as behaving like an opioid with milder withdrawal symptoms. None of the outlets quoted a toxicologist, a kratom researcher, or an industry or advocacy source.

## Timing relative to Tennessee and federal action

Keith died on June 18, 2026, 13 days before [Tennessee's statewide kratom ban took effect on July 1, 2026][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one] under Matthew Davenport's Law, and more than two months before the DEA's August 26, 2026 order on mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16. Neither the medical examiner's finding nor the coverage connects his death to either measure, and the reports do not say where the product came from.

The finding lands in a Tennessee news cycle already focused on kratom alkaloid deaths. [Knox County's forensic center reported 18 kratom-involved deaths so far this year][knox-county-tennessee-18-kratom-involved-deaths-2026-five-sole-cause-synthetics-treatment-surge], which its director attributed to synthetic compounds in mislabeled cat's claw products rather than leaf. Keith's death is a separate case handled by a different office, and the Nashville medical examiner has not, in the coverage available, characterized the source of the alkaloids.

Keith is the second entertainment figure this week whose death a medical examiner attributed in part to mitragynine. [The Wayne County, Michigan medical examiner listed "alcohol and mitragynine toxicity" for adult film performer Christian Styles][christian-styles-death-alcohol-mitragynine-toxicity-wayne-county-medical-examiner], and in that case, as here, the reporting did not identify the kratom product involved. Separately, [a Pennsylvania district attorney served notices this week telling retailers to stop selling concentrated 7-OH products][montgomery-county-pennsylvania-da-steele-notices-retailers-cease-concentrated-7oh-designer-drug].

## Who Tay Keith was

Keith was nominated for the best rap song Grammy for "Sicko Mode" in 2019 and "Rich Flex" in 2024, and produced for Beyoncé, Cardi B, Drake, and Travis Scott, according to ABC News, which also reported that he was the first in his family to graduate from college, at Middle Tennessee State University. His family, in a June 21, 2026 statement released through his label Drumatized and quoted by ABC News and Complex, called him "a visionary producer, songwriter, entrepreneur, philanthropist, and cultural force whose work helped define the sound of a generation." No family or representative statement responding to the autopsy finding appears in any of the reports.
