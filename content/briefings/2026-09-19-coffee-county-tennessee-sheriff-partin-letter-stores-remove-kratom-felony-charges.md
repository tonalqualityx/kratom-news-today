Coffee County Sheriff Chad Partin has sent a letter to convenience store owners in the Manchester, Tennessee area warning them to remove kratom and kratom-related products from their shelves or risk felony charges, [WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/09/18/tennessee-sheriff-warning-stores-remove-kratom-products-or-face-felony-charges-heres-what-know/) reported on September 18, 2026. According to the station, the letter states that Tennessee law prohibits the possession, manufacture, and sale of kratom and products containing kratom derivatives, naming mitragynine, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and compounds extracted from the Mitragyna speciosa leaf.

The letter's scope reaches natural leaf kratom, concentrated 7-OH extracts, and synthetic 7-OH preparations alike. Tennessee's statewide prohibition, which [took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], covers the plant and its alkaloids rather than singling out concentrated or synthetic products, so the distinction between trace 7-OH in raw leaf and the far higher concentrations in tablets and shots does not change a product's status under the letter as WSMV described it. The station's report does not describe any particular product or brand found in Coffee County stores, and it does not say whether deputies have seized inventory or filed charges against any retailer.

## Penalties and next steps described in the letter

Selling kratom is a Class C felony carrying a three-to-six-year prison sentence, and selling it to a juvenile is a Class B felony carrying eight to 12 years, according to the letter as reported by WSMV. The sheriff's office said it will enforce the law and may obtain products from stores for testing or investigation, the station reported, and retailers are being urged to review their inventory and remove products containing kratom-related compounds immediately.

Partin wrote that store owners cannot rely only on a product's packaging to determine whether it contains prohibited substances. "If you don't know then you shouldn't be selling it, get it gone," he wrote, according to WSMV.

## The cat's claw problem behind the packaging warning

The packaging language tracks a warning the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation issued earlier this week. TBI said some products marketed as "Cat's Claw," a South American vine that is legal in Tennessee, may contain MGM-15, a synthetic kratom-derived compound, and that labels may not fully disclose a product's contents, WSMV reported. KNT reported on September 16 that TBI's crime lab had [received 18 such submissions since July][tennessee-tbi-warns-cats-claw-tablets-contain-mgm-15-kratom-18-lab-submissions], some of which did not list kratom as an ingredient. MGM-15 is one of three synthetic kratom compounds the Drug Enforcement Administration [placed in federal Schedule I on August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds].

Coffee County is the latest Tennessee county sheriff's office to move against retail kratom since the ban took effect. The Lincoln County Sheriff's Office announced on September 10 that it had [seized kratom from 21 businesses][lincoln-county-tennessee-sheriff-seizes-kratom-21-businesses], including mislabeled cat's claw tablets that TBI testing found to contain kratom, with charging decisions left to the district attorney. In Knox County, the Regional Forensic Center [reported 18 kratom-involved deaths so far in 2026][knox-county-tennessee-18-kratom-involved-deaths-2026-five-sole-cause-synthetics-treatment-surge], and its director said the deaths trace to synthetic compounds sold in mislabeled cat's claw supplements rather than to natural leaf. Partin's letter, as WSMV described it, does not draw that distinction; it treats any product containing the named compounds as prohibited.

WSMV also tied the renewed attention to the death of Grammy-nominated producer Tay Keith, whose death the Nashville medical examiner [attributed to mitragynine, 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, and ketamine toxicity][tay-keith-death-mitragynine-7oh-pseudoindoxyl-ketamine-toxicity-nashville-medical-examiner] and ruled accidental. Reports on that finding did not identify the product involved.

The Coffee County warning arrived the same day an Oklahoma lawmaker [announced a bill to place 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, and MGM-15 in that state's Schedule I][oklahoma-rep-tim-turner-schedule-one-bill-7oh-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-ocme-deaths], citing state medical examiner data.
