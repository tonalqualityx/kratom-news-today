Oklahoma Rep. Tim Turner, R-Kinta, announced on Friday, September 18, 2026 that he intends to file legislation in the 61st Oklahoma Legislature placing three kratom-derived compounds under Schedule I of the Oklahoma Uniform Controlled Dangerous Substances Act, according to a [release from the Oklahoma House of Representatives](https://www.okhouse.gov/posts/news-20260918_1). The release names 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, and dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, which it identifies as MGM-15, and describes them as "dangerous kratom alkaloids and concentrated or synthetically enhanced compounds." Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15 are synthetic kratom-derived compounds, and the release pairs 7-OH with the words "concentrated or synthetically enhanced"; 7-OH also occurs naturally in raw kratom leaf in trace amounts, a distinction the release does not address directly.

The release is headlined "Turner to Pursue Legislation to Ban Kratom," while its text describes a bill aimed at specific alkaloids and enhanced compounds. It does not say how the bill would treat natural leaf kratom products, which contain small amounts of 7-OH, or how it would interact with the state's existing Kratom Consumer Protection Act, under which the [Oklahoma Bureau of Narcotics and State Department of Health warned retailers in July][oklahoma-warns-retailers-liability-kratom-alkaloid-limits] about liability for products exceeding the law's alkaloid limits. No bill text has been filed; the release says the introduction deadline for the first session is January 14, 2027 and that the session opens February 1.

## The medical examiner figures

The release attributes its death figures to the Oklahoma Office of the Chief Medical Examiner (OCME). From January through June 2026, 38 deaths were reported involving one of the identified compounds alone, and another 73 deaths involved at least one of the compounds in combination with another intoxicating drug or substance, according to the release. OCME has reported a significant rise in deaths associated with mitragynine alkaloids, both alone and combined with alcohol or other medications, and the release says the increase began in the second half of 2025 and has escalated in recent months.

The release lists 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, and MGM-15 as OCME's "compounds of concern," and says the agency describes them as capable of producing potent opioid-like effects that raise the likelihood of severe respiratory depression when taken alone or with sedating substances. The release does not state whether the products involved in those deaths were concentrated 7-OH preparations, synthetic compounds, or natural leaf kratom, and it does not break the 38 deaths down by compound. OCME cited ease of access, unregulated content, promotion as dietary supplements, and vague package labeling as factors contributing to misconceptions about product safety, according to the release.

KNT's [July briefing on the retailer warning][oklahoma-warns-retailers-liability-kratom-alkaloid-limits], citing KTUL, reported that the state medical examiner's office had attributed more than 50 deaths to kratom products since January 2025, a figure covering a different time window and not directly comparable to the new numbers.

## What law enforcement and prosecutors said

Oklahoma Bureau of Narcotics Director Donnie Anderson said in the release that current state law limits his agency's ability to investigate suppliers. "Synthetically enhanced Kratom products are contributing to an alarming rise in overdoses and deaths in our state," Anderson said. "But, because these products are not listed as Controlled Dangerous Substances in Oklahoma, we currently do not have jurisdiction to investigate and shut down the suppliers." Anderson said he looked forward to working with Turner and the Legislature on "strict laws" that would give his agency enforcement authority.

District 27 District Attorney Jack Thorp framed the issue around product standards rather than scheduling. "When kratom, particularly in combination with common medications like Benadryl, contributes to a death, we should take notice," Thorp said in the release. "Reasonable legislation ensuring these products are safe, properly tested, and clearly labeled can help prevent future tragedies."

Turner, described in the release as a former sheriff and longtime narcotics officer, said he is particularly concerned that kratom-derived products and highly concentrated compounds are "readily available on the counters of convenience stores and other retail locations across Oklahoma." "Our youth are more important than a dollar," Turner said. "There is no amount of tax revenue, retail sales or industry profit that is worth the life of an Oklahoma child." He also said he wants lawmakers to examine ways of holding manufacturers and distributors accountable, including "both criminal and civil consequences where the facts and law support it," when evidence shows their products contributed to deaths or serious injuries. The release says Turner is working with law enforcement officials and prosecutors to develop the bill and intends to involve fellow legislators, medical professionals, and affected families.

## How the proposal lines up with federal action

Two of the three compounds Turner named are already in federal Schedule I. The Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary order that [took effect August 26 covers mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] and, according to the Justice Department's announcement, does not reach natural kratom leaf or unaltered leaf products. The separate federal action on concentrated 7-OH itself, for which the Department of Health and Human Services solicited comment on a scheduling threshold, [drew more than 30,000 comments before the docket closed September 10][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments], and no DEA order on 7-OH has been published. A state Schedule I listing would give the Oklahoma Bureau of Narcotics jurisdiction the federal order alone does not, according to Anderson's statement.

The release does not include a response from kratom industry or advocacy organizations. The American Kratom Association [endorsed the DEA's scheduling of concentrated 7-OH products in July][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action], calling them opioids rather than kratom, and the Global Kratom Coalition applauded the August order, while both groups oppose restrictions on natural leaf. KNT did not find a public statement from either organization on the announcement in the reporting reviewed for this briefing.

Turner's announcement came the same day a [Tennessee sheriff warned retailers in his county to remove kratom products or face felony charges][coffee-county-tennessee-sheriff-partin-letter-stores-remove-kratom-felony-charges] and a New York county committee [advanced a resolution to ban all kratom sales][saratoga-county-new-york-committee-advances-total-kratom-ban-board-of-supervisors].
