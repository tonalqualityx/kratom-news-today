Saratoga County could become the next New York county to ban kratom after a resolution prohibiting the sale of all forms of the plant, synthetic and naturally derived alike, passed unanimously at a September 1 meeting of the county's Public Safety Committee, the [Times Union](https://www.timesunion.com/health/article/saratoga-county-weighs-kratom-ban-22438168.php) reported on September 18, 2026. The resolution now goes before the full county Board of Supervisors. The paper did not report when the board will take it up, what penalties it would carry, or whether it would exempt any product category.

The Saratoga measure would go further than the state bill now awaiting Governor Kathy Hochul's signature. That bill, which the [Legislature passed in June][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban], bans synthetic and concentrated kratom products and leaves natural leaf on the market, according to the Times Union, which reported that the American Kratom Association applauded it. The county resolution would cover natural leaf as well, putting Saratoga in the total-ban camp alongside several neighbors.

## Where the county patchwork stands

The Rensselaer County Legislature passed a near-total ban on the sale or distribution of kratom last week, and Albany County issued an emergency public health order banning the drug that took effect this month, the Times Union reported. KNT covered the [Rensselaer vote, which passed 15-2][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2] over objections from two legislators who wanted natural leaf separated from synthetics, and the [Albany County order][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect]. Nassau, Washington, and Oneida counties have total bans, while Dutchess and Suffolk counties banned synthetic and concentrated versions, according to the paper. Oneida's ban [was signed in August and has already produced seizures in Utica][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures]; Dutchess County Executive Sue Serino [signed a partial ban this month][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh] after the legislature rejected her proposal to cover natural leaf, and Suffolk's law [exempts natural loose leaf][suffolk-county-kratom-ban-vote-exempts-natural-loose-leaf]. Ulster County is weighing a synthetic ban, the Times Union noted, though its health committee [declined to schedule a hearing on September 3][ulster-county-committee-delays-synthetic-kratom-ban-vote] to await state and federal action.

Cari Scribner, a Ballston Spa resident, told the Times Union she approached her home county after watching Rensselaer and Albany act. Scribner has pushed for a statewide total ban since her son Nick, 27, died in November 2024; the paper reported he asphyxiated in his sleep after ingesting a large amount of kratom. "I feel concerned that it's come to this level where I believe a statewide ban is needed," she said, adding that the county-by-county approach is "turning New York into a patchwork quilt." The Times Union report does not specify whether the product involved in her son's death was a concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom, though Scribner told the paper it was not synthetic kratom.

## The disagreement over what a synthetic-only ban would do

The Times Union's report sets out a direct disagreement between Scribner and David Carlucci, a former state senator who now lobbies for the American Kratom Association. Carlucci told the paper the state bill proposes a 2% threshold for 7-OH in kratom. "That's where it really starts; those higher concentrations, which are not natural, can be very dangerous," he said. Carlucci argued a total ban would create an underground market that would be more dangerous, and said he was encouraged that lawmakers may be considering a synthetic ban rather than a total one. "I think the lawmakers have been willing to roll up their sleeves and learn more about this," he said.

Scribner said a synthetic-only ban would lead to a spike in "natural" or "raw leaf" use. Carlucci responded that it would be virtually impossible to consume enough leaf kratom to equal a dose of 7-OH. Scribner countered that the shops that sold her son the product that caused his death would be forced to clear their shelves under a total ban. "That would be an enormous relief," she said. The paper does not resolve the question, and neither does the sourcing available to KNT.

The positions track a split that has surfaced in several New York county debates this summer. The AKA and other kratom advocacy groups have supported restrictions on concentrated and synthetic 7-OH, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH found in natural leaf, while opposing bans that reach the leaf itself. The Times Union also noted that the Drug Enforcement Administration has temporarily listed three 7-OH-related substances in Schedule I; that order, which [took effect August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], covers mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 and does not reach natural kratom leaf. New York separately bans kratom sales to anyone under 21 and requires warning labels, the paper reported.

The Saratoga resolution advanced the same week an Oklahoma legislator [announced plans to place 7-OH and two synthetic kratom compounds in that state's Schedule I][oklahoma-rep-tim-turner-schedule-one-bill-7oh-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-ocme-deaths].
