Oneida County health inspectors, working with the New York State Office of Cannabis Management and the county sheriff's office, seized 184 kratom products and 1,950 flavored vapor products from Brooklyn Exotics at 401 Black River Boulevard N. in Rome on September 16, 2026, [WKTV reported](https://www.wktv.com/news/crime/kratom-cannabis-and-flavored-vapes-seized-from-rome-store/article_ab877599-fbc6-48a6-8194-2cf61fc589a6.html) on September 18. Inspectors also seized an unspecified quantity of cannabis and one unlabeled prepackaged product containing an unidentified substance, according to the station. None of the reports specifies whether the kratom products were concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom. The distinction matters less in Oneida County than in neighboring jurisdictions, because the [countywide sales ban County Executive Anthony J. Picente Jr. signed in August][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures] covers natural leaf as well as 7-OH products.

[CNY Central reported](https://cnycentral.com/news/local/large-quantity-of-kratom-cannabis-flavored-vapes-seized-from-oneida-county-shop) that the Office of Cannabis Management padlocked the store after the inspection. Neither CNY Central nor WKTV mentioned criminal charges or civil penalties, and the reports do not state which authority the kratom seizure rests on. The county's local law, [passed unanimously by the Board of Legislators on August 12][oneida-county-unanimous-countywide-kratom-sales-ban], carries a $500 civil penalty for a first violation and up to $1,000 per confiscated item for repeat violations.

## Eight inspections since July

Picente's office said the Rome inspection was the eighth enforcement action the county has carried out since July, and that the cumulative haul now exceeds 4,600 flavored vapor products and 400 kratom products, along with synthetic cannabis, K2, and 7-OH products, per WKTV. The station listed seven prior inspections between July 9 and September 3. "This latest inspection in Rome demonstrates that these enforcement efforts are ongoing and that we will continue working to protect the health and safety of our residents," Picente said in the statement WKTV quoted.

The [Utica Observer-Dispatch](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/200-kratom-products-2-000-172310482.html), in a report syndicated by Yahoo News, quoted a second line from Picente: "Together with our state and local partners, we continue to take action when businesses are found to be selling products that violate the law." The paper listed the Oneida County District Attorney's Office, the New York State Department of Taxation and Finance, local police departments, the U.S. Marshals Service, and the FBI Joint Terrorism Task Force among agencies that have participated in the county's enforcement effort.

One detail in the new reports differs from the county's own August account. CNY Central and the Observer-Dispatch both describe the August 27 seizure at UT One Corporation in Utica as 30 packets of 7-OH. The county's August 27 release, which KNT [covered at the time][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures], described the same items as 30 packages of kratom products without identifying them as 7-OH. The county has not published a lab result or other explanation for the change in description.

## Local enforcement across New York

Oneida County's campaign is running alongside a wave of county-level action across upstate New York. The [Saratoga County Public Safety Committee advanced a resolution][saratoga-county-new-york-committee-advances-total-kratom-ban-board-of-supervisors] this week to ban all kratom sales, natural and synthetic, while a statewide bill limited to synthetic and concentrated products awaits Governor Kathy Hochul's signature. On the West Coast, [San Luis Obispo County, California committed opioid settlement funds][san-luis-obispo-county-167000-opioid-settlement-funds-kratom-addiction-coordinator-local-enforcement-authority] to a kratom program and moved to give its health department local enforcement authority, another sign that counties are building their own enforcement machinery while state and federal rules remain unsettled.
