San Luis Obispo County has committed $167,000 in opioid settlement money to a kratom program, [Mustang News reported](https://mustangnews.net/slocounty-kratom-addiction-funding/) on September 18, 2026. According to the Cal Poly student newspaper, the county's governing board approved the spending at its September 1 meeting, and the money will pay the salary of an additional program coordinator at the County of San Luis Obispo Public Health Department, along with services and supplies. The coordinator's assignment is reducing addiction to kratom, which the paper said has been attributed to nine overdose deaths in the county over the past five years. The paper identified the approving body as the county's "Board of Advisors"; San Luis Obispo County is governed by a Board of Supervisors, and the paper did not report a vote count.

A second item followed a day later. With an agenda item passed on September 2, the Public Health Department "will have the power to create regulations that give the county authority to enforce the state's ban on kratom," Mustang News reported. Jennifer Miller, the department's prevention and community health division manager, described the gap the ordinance is meant to close: "The retail landscape is very wide, and so that makes it quite difficult to enforce without a local ordinance that explicitly gives authority to a specific county entity."

## The state ban the county intends to enforce

The state-level prohibition the county intends to enforce is not a statute. As the paper put it, the California Department of Public Health "considers selling kratom or 7-OH products illegal in California by labeling them as 'adulterated.'" That adulteration position underpins the [statewide enforcement campaign Governor Gavin Newsom's office described on September 2][california-statewide-enforcement-removes-kratom-7oh-products], which reported nearly 15,300 illegal hemp, kratom, and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products removed from shelves, and the [state's earlier claim to be leading the national 7-OH crackdown][california-claims-enforcement-lead-7oh-crackdown]. As in those state announcements, the Mustang News report treats kratom and 7-OH as a single prohibited category and does not distinguish natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations, which carry the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in raw leaf.

Miller did draw one line between the two. She told the paper that some forms of 7-OH are "13 times more potent than morphine," a figure that echoes potency comparisons regulators and researchers have applied to concentrated and synthetic 7-OH, not to leaf kratom. She also described the dependence pattern the county is seeing: "They thought that they were using kratom as an energy supplement, a natural alternative to an energy drink, and then ended up with a dependence." Drug treatment centers in the county have recorded 40 percent more intakes for kratom and 7-OH withdrawal, according to Miller.

## Campus and retail

The paper tied the county's move to student use. About one percent of Cal Poly students reported using kratom in each year from 2023 to 2025, according to the Healthy Minds Survey conducted by the university's Campus Health and Wellbeing office, compared with a national college figure of 0.38 percent that the paper cited. Diego Abeloos, the office's communications director, said education initiatives would continue.

Two local retailers gave the paper different pictures of compliance. The manager of Wild Side Smoke Shop on Santa Rosa Street said the store regularly consults lawyers and meets with law enforcement about what it can and cannot sell. A representative of Smoke Arts on Foothill Boulevard said they were unsure whether kratom was sold in the store at all.

The county's approach, funding a public health position and then writing its own enforcement rules, is a variation on the local-ordinance route other California cities have taken. [Downey directed staff to draft a 7-OH and kratom ordinance][downey-california-council-directs-staff-draft-7oh-kratom-ordinance] earlier this month. Across the country, [Oneida County, New York reported its eighth kratom enforcement inspection since July][oneida-county-rome-brooklyn-exotics-inspection-184-kratom-products-seized-400-total-eight-actions] this week, under a local law that county passed rather than waiting on state rules.
