Fulton County commissioners backed a resolution on Thursday, September 17, 2026, to ban synthetic kratom in Georgia, the [Sandy Springs Record reported](https://sandyspringsrecord.com/articles/fulton-county-backs-kratom-ban-affecting-sandy-springs-mu77qgme) the following day. District 2 Commissioner Bob Ellis, who represents Sandy Springs, sponsored the measure. The resolution is aimed at synthetic kratom-derived products, a category separate from natural leaf kratom, and it also calls for natural kratom products to be kept behind pharmacy counters, Ellis said in a post on X on September 18, according to the paper.

"When altered, this widely available herb can pose serious addiction risks for our youth," Ellis wrote, per the Record. "It's easy to find at gas stations, convenience stores & online." His district covers Sandy Springs, Milton, Mountain Park, and parts of Alpharetta and Roswell.

## What the resolution does is unresolved

The exact vote count has not been published, the Record reported. Ellis wrote that his "fellow Commissioners" supported the measure, but official minutes from the September 17 meeting were not available as of September 18. The paper said it remains unclear whether the resolution carries the force of law within Fulton County or serves as a formal request to the Georgia General Assembly. If it is directed at the state legislature, the next opportunity for action would be the 2027 session, according to the Record. No public statements from other commissioners or from Sandy Springs retailers appeared in public records as of September 18, the paper reported.

The Record placed the resolution in the context of federal action. The Drug Enforcement Administration [placed three synthetic kratom-related compounds in Schedule I on August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]: mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16. The DEA's separate proposal to schedule products containing 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) above a concentration threshold, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH in natural kratom leaf, has not been finalized, and its [public comment period closed September 10][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments].

## Georgia already has potency limits and a study committee

Georgia set per-serving limits on 7-OH in 2025, and an [investigation by The Current published in August][georgia-kratom-investigation-7oh-limits-testing-gap] reported that the state has no dedicated regulatory body or funding to test products against those limits, with one product measuring 24 times the one milligram per-serving cap. The Georgia House's Blue-Ribbon Study Committee on Youth Exposure to Kratom and Retail-Available Substances [heard testimony in Brunswick on August 21][georgia-brunswick-kratom-hearing-west-chester-ordinance], where presenters split between banning kratom outright and moving unadulterated products into healthcare channels. The Record's report does not mention the study committee or the state's existing 7-OH limits, and it does not say whether Ellis's pharmacy-counter proposal draws on the hearing testimony.

## Ulster County, New York revisits its own concentrated-kratom ban

In New York, Ulster County lawmakers are again considering a proposed local law that would ban highly concentrated and synthetic forms of kratom, [Mid-Hudson News reported](https://midhudsonnews.com/2026/09/21/ulster-county-lawmakers-consider-ban-on-concentrated-kratom-products/) on September 21. Legislator William Murray, a Democrat, is the sponsor. "Though selling to persons under age 21 is unlawful in New York State, kratom is readily available to the public in convenience stores and gas stations and easily falls into the hands of our youth via online and illicit sales," Murray said, according to the outlet. Murray also said restrictions adopted in neighboring Dutchess and Sullivan counties could shift sales into Ulster County. County health officials cited concerns about dependence and withdrawal and about the growing availability of concentrated and synthetic products, Mid-Hudson News reported.

The report does not give a hearing or vote date. A legislative committee [declined on September 3 to schedule a public hearing on Murray's bill][ulster-county-committee-delays-synthetic-kratom-ban-vote] at the county executive's request, citing pending state and federal action, and Murray voted against that delay. Dutchess County Executive Sue Serino [signed a similar concentrated and synthetic 7-OH ban on September 12][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh].

Both county measures land in the same week that [addiction clinics told STAT they are seeing a rising share of patients dependent on kratom and concentrated 7-OH products][stat-addiction-clinics-rising-kratom-7oh-withdrawal-boulder-care-14-percent-new-patients], with one clinician theorizing that access restrictions may be driving some of that treatment demand.
