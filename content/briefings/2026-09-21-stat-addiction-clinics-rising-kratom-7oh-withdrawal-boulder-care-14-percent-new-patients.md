Addiction treatment providers in several states are seeing more patients dependent on kratom products and on concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, [STAT reported](https://www.statnews.com/2026/09/21/kratom-withdrawal-addiction-centers-report-surge/) on September 21, 2026. The products most of those patients describe are newer, high-potency preparations with concentrated 7-OH, according to the report, a category distinct from the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf. STAT's reporting does not specify whether the products involved were concentrated extracts or lab-synthesized 7-OH.

At Boulder Care, a telehealth addiction clinic, patients who reported using kratom products made up 3% of new patients in September 2025, STAT reported. That share reached 8% in July 2026 and 14% in August. "That really is quite an explosion," Ayesha Appa, the company's head of medical affairs, told the outlet. Most of those patients reported using products with highly concentrated 7-OH rather than traditional leaf powder, according to STAT, which described 7-OH as acting directly on the brain's opioid receptors and producing stronger, more opioid-like effects than mitragynine, the leaf's dominant alkaloid.

## 10 to 20 admissions a week in Massachusetts

Recovery Centers of America saw no more than about one patient a week admitted for kratom use at its two Massachusetts inpatient clinics last year, and the withdrawal was mild enough to require only "comfort meds," medical director Myles Jen Kin told STAT. The same two clinics now see 10 to 20 people a week who are either fully dependent on kratom or 7-OH or for whom those products are part of a larger substance use problem, Jen Kin said, adding that the severity of withdrawal appears to be rising along with the volume. "We're basically treating it as if it's fentanyl or oxycodone or heroin," he said.

Phil Smyth, executive director of Milestone Treatment Center, which runs clinics in New Jersey, Virginia, and Texas, told STAT he has seen more patients who need medical help to detox from 7-OH products, many of whom started taking them for pain. "The common story I get from a lot of these clients is that they didn't know really what it was," Smyth said. Steve Klein, a physician at Caron Treatment Centers in Pennsylvania, said use tends to start out benign, with patients seeing products sold over the counter and assuming they cannot be very harmful, before some newer users go down what he called a "potency rabbit hole." Klein also said he has seen patients in remission from opioid use disorder try kratom and then spiral quickly, and that by the time they reach residential care they are typically using high doses of 7-OH, according to STAT.

## Appa ties the increase to loss of access

The Food and Drug Administration recently flagged 7-OH products as an emerging opioid threat, and the Drug Enforcement Administration [proposed placing products above a 7-OH threshold in Schedule I][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], STAT noted. That proposal has not been finalized, though the [public comment period on the threshold closed September 10][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments], and numerous states have enacted their own restrictions. Appa theorized to STAT that laws revoking access to the products may be pushing people into withdrawal. "I think we're really seeing and feeling what happens when people lose access and need to seek care in unprecedented numbers," she said. Jen Kin said clinicians noticed the same trend before local bans took effect, according to the report.

The observation matches what clinicians in other states have described in recent weeks. A physician at Allegheny Health Network's Wexford hospital [told CBS Pittsburgh on September 10][ahn-wexford-pittsburgh-hospital-uptick-kratom-withdrawal-icu] that the hospital had treated multiple kratom withdrawal patients in intensive care in the past month and that he believed the increase coincided with publicity around the DEA's proposed scheduling action. In Tennessee, where a statewide kratom ban took effect July 1, treatment centers [reported more patients seeking help for kratom and 7-OH withdrawal][knox-county-tennessee-18-kratom-involved-deaths-2026-five-sole-cause-synthetics-treatment-surge], with one medical director putting those admissions at four for every one fentanyl admission.

The regulatory pressure is not uniformly opposed by kratom advocates. The American Kratom Association [endorsed the DEA's proposed 7-OH scheduling action in July][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action], drawing a line between natural leaf products and concentrated 7-OH preparations. STAT's report does not quote industry or advocacy organizations.

## What the treatment data can and cannot show

STAT cited federal survey data showing the number of Americans who had tried kratom in their lifetime rose from about four million in 2019 to five million in 2023, and said reports to U.S. poison centers about kratom exposure rose from 258 to 3,434 over the past decade, with a sharp increase in 2025. The outlet also reported that 79% of kratom-related deaths reported to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention over the last decade involved other substances, mainly other opioids, and wrote that kratom does not appear to be acutely lethal in the way fentanyl is.

STAT wrote that the research it reviewed has generally characterized kratom withdrawal as mild, without naming specific studies, while users, clinicians, and advocates told the outlet the experience can be as taxing as opioid withdrawal. STAT reported that early case reports have described patients whose kratom use disorder was managed with medications for opioid use disorder, including buprenorphine alone and buprenorphine combined with naloxone, and that methadone has also been described as having potential in that role. One such case report, [published in the Journal of the American Pharmacists Association in August][case-report-documents-opioid-like-7oh-withdrawal], documented opioid-like withdrawal in a man who had been using roughly 360 milligrams of 7-OH a day. Nearly a third of Boulder Care's new patients starting buprenorphine last month reported currently using kratom or 7-OH, STAT reported.

Katherine Hill, an epidemiology Ph.D. candidate at the Yale School of Public Health who has studied kratom, cautioned STAT that testimony from treatment centers may reflect sampling bias, since people who discontinue kratom or 7-OH on their own without much difficulty would never show up at a clinic.

STAT also reported that 7-OH is often marketed as kratom, and that products of all types have been found to contain compounds not listed on the packaging along with additives such as caffeine, kava, or CBD. The reporting comes as county governments continue to act on the same product category, including a [Fulton County, Georgia resolution backing a statewide synthetic kratom ban][fulton-county-georgia-commissioners-back-resolution-synthetic-kratom-ban-natural-kratom-behind-pharmacy-counters] that commissioners approved last week.
