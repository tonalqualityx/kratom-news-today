The Bucks County, Pennsylvania Board of Commissioners passed a resolution at its meeting on Wednesday, September 16, 2026, calling on Pennsylvania Secretary of Health Dr. Debra Bogen to use her emergency powers to designate kratom as a Schedule I controlled substance under state law, [LevittownNow.com reported](https://levittownnow.com/2026/09/21/county-commissioners-pass-resolution-to-restrict-kratom/) on September 21. The outlet did not report a vote count. As described in the report, the resolution asks the state to schedule kratom itself rather than only concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and the report does not specify whether the deaths the commissioners cited involved concentrated 7-OH preparations, which are distinct from the trace 7-OH in natural kratom leaf, or natural leaf kratom.

"We have residents who have died from using kratom. We have residents who have completed suicide by using kratom, and it's in our community, and we'd like to put a stop to it," Commissioner Chairperson Diane Ellis-Marseglia said, according to LevittownNow.com. The report does not cite a county death count or a source for the deaths.

Commissioner Gene DiGirolamo called kratom a dangerous and addictive drug, particularly for young people, and said the county was asking for a Schedule I classification because in his view the substance has no legitimate use, according to the outlet. He said the goal was to save lives. Commissioner Bob Harvie said many people and businesses will do whatever it takes to make money, per the report.

## A broader request than the district attorneys made

The resolution follows a chain of local requests to Harrisburg. Bucks County District Attorney Joe Khan and Chester County District Attorney Christopher de Barrena-Sarobe [sent letters on August 31 urging Bogen to emergency-schedule 7-OH and related kratom derivatives][bucks-chester-das-urge-pennsylvania-emergency-7oh-scheduling], citing the Drug Enforcement Administration's August 26 order and precedents in Massachusetts, North Dakota, and Florida. That request was framed around concentrated 7-OH and its derivatives. The commissioners' resolution, as LevittownNow.com described it, names kratom as a whole.

The Pennsylvania Department of Health [told the Pennsylvania Capital-Star](https://penncapital-star.com/health-care/health-experts-lawmakers-push-for-pa-kratom-regulation-proponents-warn-theyll-fight-a-ban/) on September 8, 2026, that it was "reviewing the situation and considering all options" in response to the district attorneys' request, a position [covered here at the time][pennsylvania-health-department-reviewing-7oh-request-lawmakers-push-regulation]. The same report cited state data showing 17 overdose deaths in 2026 in which kratom was a contributing factor. Lawmakers including Rep. James Prokopiak and Sen. Tracy Pennycuick are sponsoring bills that would ban synthetic kratom products and high-potency extracts while allowing natural leaf sales to adults 21 and older, the Capital-Star reported. The American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow told the Capital-Star the group supports regulation and opposes an outright ban. Neither the association nor the Department of Health is quoted in the LevittownNow.com report on the commissioners' resolution.

LevittownNow.com reported that the DEA temporarily placed three kratom-related compounds in Schedule I in August, and that the federal order omitted 7-OH. The DEA's order [covers mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], three synthetic compounds, and does not reach natural leaf kratom. The agency's separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold has not been finalized.

## Retail enforcement in the county

Khan's office is investigating a Buckingham Township smoke shop for allegedly selling kratom products to minors, LevittownNow.com reported, after an undercover 17-year-old purchased kratom-infused chocolate bars there in July. The district attorney's office [obtained an emergency preliminary injunction on September 2 against Zaza Vape & Cigar, Inc. in Buckingham Township][bucks-county-da-injunction-zaza-vape-kratom-minors], halting sales of kratom and tobacco products to minors at the shop. The LevittownNow.com report does not name the shop under investigation, and the office's earlier announcements did not specify whether the kratom products involved were concentrated 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

Khan and Chester County District Attorney Christopher de Barrena-Sarobe recently said there is an expanding supply of potent products sold at retail, often packaged as vapes, gummies, and candy bars without age limits, LevittownNow.com reported. In neighboring Montgomery County, District Attorney Kevin Steele [sent notices to retailers this month telling them to stop selling concentrated 7-OH products][montgomery-county-pennsylvania-da-steele-notices-retailers-cease-concentrated-7oh-designer-drug], which his office said may qualify as a designer drug under state law.

Rachael Neff, the county's human services director, told the commissioners that the county needs multiple approaches to the ways substances are reaching its schools and communities, per LevittownNow.com. Jennifer King of the Council of Southeast Philadelphia and Diane Rosati of the Bucks County Drug and Alcohol Commission attended in support of the measure, the outlet reported. The report contains no response from the Department of Health to the resolution.

The same day the report was published, Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser [announced a $152,000 settlement](https://coag.gov/press-releases/colorado-smoke-shop-owners-to-pay-152000-for-selling-illegal-kratom-products/) with the owners of a 15-store smoke shop chain over kratom products that exceeded that state's 7-OH potency limit, according to his office, an enforcement model built on a concentration threshold rather than scheduling. That settlement is [covered in a separate briefing today][colorado-attorney-general-weiser-152000-settlement-dnb-distro-million-smoke-n-vape-7oh-limit-labeling].
