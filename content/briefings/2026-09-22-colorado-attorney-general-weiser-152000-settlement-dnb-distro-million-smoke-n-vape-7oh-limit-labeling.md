Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser announced a $152,000 settlement on September 21, 2026, with Sahansila Karki and Gokul Tiwari, resolving allegations that their chain of smoke shops sold kratom products above the state's legal limit for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), [according to the attorney general's office](https://coag.gov/press-releases/colorado-smoke-shop-owners-to-pay-152000-for-selling-illegal-kratom-products/). The release said the limit concerns concentrated 7-OH products, which it described as manufactured at levels above the trace amounts that occur naturally in kratom leaf, and the office's investigation also found the pair failed to ensure the products met state labeling and packaging requirements.

"Like other legally mandated products, we keep a careful eye on the sale of kratom," Weiser said in the release. "If a wrongdoer tries to dodge the law and sell an addictive product that failed to comply with legal limits and labeling requirements, we will hold them and their businesses accountable."

## Who was investigated

Karki and Tiwari founded and own the wholesale distribution company DNB Distro and 15 brick-and-mortar smoke shops, most of them operating under the Million Smoke N Vape brand, according to the attorney general's office. The shops have sold convenience store products, smoking paraphernalia, tobacco, and kratom products since 2020, the release said. The release does not name the kratom products or brands involved, and it does not say what 7-OH concentrations investigators measured or how many products were tested.

The office drew its own line between the alkaloid as it occurs in the plant and the products it says the case concerned. "While the alkaloid substance 7-OH naturally occurs in the kratom plant, companies manufacture concentrated forms of 7-OH products that interact with the same brain receptors as opioids, which can be extremely potent and addicting," the release said.

## What the owners agreed to

Under the settlement, Karki and Tiwari agreed to comply with the Colorado Consumer Protection Act and the Daniel Bregger Act, according to the attorney general's office. They also agreed to test any kratom product they prepare, distribute, advertise, sell, or offer for sale in Colorado for compliance with local, state, and federal law, and to document every kratom purchase from a third party with invoices and receipts. They will pay $152,000 to the state. If they violate the settlement's terms, they owe an additional $500,000, the release said.

The office described the agreement as its latest action under the Daniel Bregger Act. [Colorado Politics reported](https://www.coloradopolitics.com/2026/09/21/kratom-sellers-colorado-reach-settlement/) on September 21, 2026, that the settlement does not include an admission by Karki and Tiwari that they violated the state's kratom statutes.

## The Bregger Act's 2% cap

The Daniel Bregger Act, passed in 2025, prohibits kratom products in which 7-OH exceeds 2% of total alkaloids, according to Colorado Politics. The law is named for Daniel Bregger, who died of a kratom-related overdose in 2021, the outlet reported. Colorado Politics also reported that a 2022 law, Senate Bill 120, set a minimum purchase age of 21 and required manufacturer identification and ingredient labeling on kratom products. The report does not say whether Bregger's death involved a concentrated 7-OH product or natural leaf kratom.

[CBS Colorado's report](https://www.cbsnews.com/colorado/news/settlement-reached-colorado-vape-shops-accused-illegal-products/) on the settlement matched the release on the $152,000 payment, the $500,000 penalty provision, and the 15-shop footprint, and it quoted the office's description of concentrated 7-OH products. Neither CBS Colorado nor the attorney general's release states the numeric 7-OH limit. The 2% figure comes from Colorado Politics.

## Colorado's enforcement track and the federal backdrop

The settlement follows other kratom developments in Colorado this month. The Castle Rock Town Council [voted six to one on September 15, 2026, to outlaw retail kratom sales][castle-rock-colorado-council-votes-6-1-outlaw-retail-kratom-sales-possession-legal] while leaving possession legal, with the lone dissenter saying he would back a ban limited to synthetic products. The Steamboat Pilot reported on September 7, 2026, that [two men in the Yampa Valley died after taking concentrated 7-OH tablets][yampa-valley-deaths-concentrated-7oh-tablets-colorado-shops] of 60 mg and 80 mg, and that similar tablets remained available by request at a Steamboat shop.

At the federal level, the Drug Enforcement Administration [placed three synthetic kratom-related compounds in Schedule I on August 26, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]: mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16. The agency's separate proposal to schedule products containing 7-OH above a concentration threshold has not been finalized, and its [public comment period closed September 10, 2026][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments]. The Bregger Act is a state statute, as Colorado Politics described it, and the attorney general's release does not reference the federal process.

Mac Haddow, the American Kratom Association's senior fellow on public policy, argued in a [September 21, 2026, opinion column in The Washington Times](https://www.washingtontimes.com/news/2026/sep/21/7-oh-needs-science-based-regulation-not-regulatory-panic/) that federal 7-OH policy should rest on evidence-based concentration thresholds separating the trace 7-OH in natural leaf from concentrated and synthetic products. He criticized the prospect of placing 7-OH in Schedule I, according to the column, and noted that the Department of Health and Human Services reopened the comment period on the proposed threshold through September 10, 2026, while it continued seeking scientific evidence on where the line should sit. The column does not address the Colorado settlement.

In Pennsylvania, meanwhile, [Bucks County commissioners passed a resolution asking the state health secretary to emergency-schedule kratom itself][bucks-county-pennsylvania-commissioners-resolution-health-secretary-bogen-emergency-schedule-one-kratom], a broader request than Colorado's potency-threshold approach.
