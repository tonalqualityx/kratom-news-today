Two University of Mississippi students were found dead on Monday, September 21, 2026, in separate incidents, one on the Oxford campus and one in the city of Oxford, and packaged kratom sold from a retail store was found during both investigations, Lafayette County Metro Narcotics said in a statement [reported by NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/ole-miss-student-deaths-kratom-rcna599305) on September 22. The unit said it has "no confirmed information indicating that kratom or any other substance was a contributing factor in either death." None of the reports reviewed by KNT specify whether the products were natural leaf kratom or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, which are distinct from the trace 7-OH found in natural leaf, and no toxicology findings have been released.

The unit said it has no information that the two deaths are linked, according to NBC News. "Out of an abundance of caution, we encourage everyone to avoid kratom and any other medication or substance that was not prescribed to you or obtained from a trusted, regulated source," the statement said, per the outlet. The unit also asked the public "to avoid speculation regarding the circumstances or causes of either death while these investigations remain ongoing," [Newsweek reported](https://www.newsweek.com/ole-miss-deaths-students-lsu-investigation-drugs-mississippi-12475383).

## Where the students were found

One student was found unresponsive at Stewart Hall on campus around 1:30 p.m. Monday and received CPR, and the second was found in the city of Oxford, according to Newsweek. [CNN reported](https://kvia.com/cnn-national/2026/09/22/police-investigating-deaths-of-two-students-at-the-university-of-mississippi/) that one student was found in a residential area on campus and the other in a residential area off campus, and that the deaths followed Saturday's Ole Miss football game against LSU. Authorities have not released the students' names, ages, or genders, per CNN. Autopsies were "in the process of being scheduled," CNN reported, and the Lafayette County Coroner's Office is involved alongside the Oxford Police Department and local, state, and federal partners.

Capt. Alex Fauver, commander of the Metro Narcotics unit, told NBC News that kratom is banned in Oxford under local ordinance but can be bought in surrounding counties. Mississippi's local patchwork has been in motion this month: [Lee County's countywide ban took effect on September 16 after a court appeal was dismissed][lee-county-mississippi-kratom-ban-takes-effect-september-16-appeal-dismissed], and [Tupelo police began enforcing that city's ban on September 12][tupelo-mississippi-police-begin-enforcing-kratom-ban-september-12]. The NBC News report does not say which county or retailer the packaged products came from.

## The university's response

Vice Chancellor for Student Affairs Shawnboda Mead said in a statement that the university was heartbroken over the loss of two students and extended condolences to their families and loved ones, according to CNN. The university also said a considerable amount of unverified information was circulating on social media and in the community, and it urged the campus not to speculate before official confirmation, Newsweek reported. University police are not aware of any additional fatalities, per the same report. Newsweek reported that free Narcan is available through vending machines on campus and at Oxford's downtown parking garage.

[Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ole-miss-students-found-dead-packaged-kratom-recovered-both-sites-officials) that the university confirmed both of the deceased were enrolled students and that authorities have not confirmed whether the deaths are connected.

## What the coverage says about kratom, and where it differs

The national reports differ in how they describe the federal status of kratom and its alkaloids. Newsweek reported that the Trump administration temporarily scheduled 7-hydroxymitragynine, which it described as a naturally occurring kratom compound, as a Schedule I substance. The Drug Enforcement Administration's August order [placed three synthetic compounds, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, in Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], and the agency's separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold has not been finalized. NBC News reported that the DEA lists kratom as a "drug and chemical of concern," and cited the Mayo Clinic's position that kratom is unsafe and its note that deaths linked to kratom "also involved other drugs or substances."

Oliver Grundmann, a clinical professor of medicinal chemistry at the University of Florida who studies kratom, told CNN that kratom carries an addiction risk because of how it interacts with the brain. Newsweek described kratom as producing stimulant effects at low doses and sedating effects at higher doses, and noted that the FDA has not approved it for any use.

None of the four reports names the products, the retailer, or the form the kratom took, and none reports a toxicology finding. In an earlier case this month, the medical examiner's finding arrived weeks after the death: the Wayne County, Michigan medical examiner [attributed an August 20 death to alcohol and mitragynine toxicity][christian-styles-death-alcohol-mitragynine-toxicity-wayne-county-medical-examiner], a finding reported about four weeks later. The Metro Narcotics statement, as quoted by NBC News, describes the products as an investigative finding and stops short of any causal claim.
