Rensselaer County Executive Steve McLaughlin said he will take several days to consider public comments before announcing whether he will sign or veto Local Law T-4 of 2026, the countywide ban on kratom sales, [CBS6 Albany reported](https://cbs6albany.com/news/local/rensselaer-county-kratom-ban-now-awaits-county-executives-decision) on September 24, 2026. "I'll make a decision on what I think is the best move for the people of Rensselaer County," McLaughlin said, according to the station.

The decision follows the [county legislature's 15-2 vote on September 8 to ban all kratom sales][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2], a vote in which the two dissenting legislators argued the law should have separated natural leaf kratom from synthetic and concentrated products. CBS6's report does not give a deadline for the executive's decision or an effective date for the law if he signs it.

## What Local Law T-4 would do

The law would prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom in the county, and it defines kratom broadly, CBS6 reported: the Mitragyna speciosa plant, products containing mitragynine, products derived from the plant's leaves, and synthetic derivatives, including 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). That definition means the ban would reach natural leaf products, which contain 7-OH only in trace amounts, along with concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations that contain the alkaloid at far higher levels. The station's account draws that line as a matter of the law's scope; it does not report a separate treatment for the two categories.

Two exemptions survive in the law as passed, according to CBS6: products approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), and kratom dispensed by a licensed pharmacist under a physician's prescription.

Penalties would be civil in the first instance, the station reported. A first violation would carry a fine of up to $500 per item confiscated, with subsequent violations carrying up to $1,000 per item. Willful violations could be prosecuted criminally under New York Public Health Law, according to CBS6. The county health department and law enforcement would enforce the law and would be authorized to inspect retailers and vehicles, the station reported.

## What McLaughlin heard

Cari Scribner, one of the mothers who spoke in favor of the ban at the legislature's September 2 hearing, returned to urge the executive to sign. "Please spare other families our pain by signing the ban on all forms of kratom," she said, according to CBS6. The station's report does not specify whether the products involved in the deaths the mothers described were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

Legislator Bruce Patire, the Hoosick Republican who sponsored the bill, argued for the need for FDA standards and said regulation prevents a haphazard approach, CBS6 reported.

Speakers against the law made the case McLaughlin will have to weigh against that testimony. David Bencero, who told the hearing he uses kratom, said the plant had changed his life. "That botanical safety really saved my life," Bencero said, according to the station, adding that it had given him the footing to work full time, build a career, buy a home, and pay taxes in the county. Tracey Hudson, who owns a kratom shop and had criticized the legislature's vote earlier this month, asked McLaughlin to veto the law so the county could consider it further, CBS6 reported.

Legislator Ken Zalewski, the District 1 Democrat who voted against the ban on September 8, repeated his objection, telling the hearing he felt the legislation went too far, per CBS6.

Robert Durkin, identified by the station as a former FDA regulator, framed the problem as one of product identity, saying that much of what is sold under the kratom name is not kratom at all, according to CBS6. The report does not say which side of the ban Durkin was speaking for.

## Where the county sits in New York's map

If McLaughlin signs, Rensselaer would join neighboring [Albany County, where an emergency public health order banning kratom sales took effect September 1][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect], and the growing list of New York counties that have moved beyond the state's under-21 sales restriction and labeling requirements on their own. [Saratoga County's public safety committee advanced a total ban to its board of supervisors last week][saratoga-county-new-york-committee-advances-total-kratom-ban-board-of-supervisors]. Other counties have gone narrower: [Dutchess County Executive Sue Serino signed a partial ban aimed at concentrated 7-OH products][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh], and [Ulster County's legislative committee delayed a vote on a synthetic-only ban][ulster-county-committee-delays-synthetic-kratom-ban-vote] earlier this month.

The choice between those two models, a total ban or one confined to synthetic and concentrated products, is the same split that divided the Rensselaer legislature on September 8. McLaughlin's decision will determine which version the county adopts, and CBS6 reported only that it will come in the coming days.
