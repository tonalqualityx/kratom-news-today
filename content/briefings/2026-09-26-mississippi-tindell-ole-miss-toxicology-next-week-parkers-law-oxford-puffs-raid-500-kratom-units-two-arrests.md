Mississippi Department of Public Safety Commissioner Sean Tindell said toxicology reports on the two University of Mississippi students found dead in Oxford this week are "expected to be completed as early as next week," [Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ole-miss-toxicology-reports-expected-soon-results-may-trigger-anti-fentanyl-law-official-says) on September 25, 2026. Governor Tate Reeves has pledged state resources to expedite results that typically take weeks, according to the report.

Fox News and NBC News have both covered the deaths in connection with kratom because packaged kratom was found at both scenes, and Lafayette County Metro Narcotics has said it has no confirmed information that kratom or any other substance contributed to either death, [as KNT reported on September 23][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link]. None of the reporting reviewed for this briefing specifies whether the packaged kratom at the scenes, or the kratom seized in a store raid this week, was natural leaf kratom or a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product, a category that the Mississippi State Department of Health's warning this week and the Drug Enforcement Administration's scheduling actions treat as distinct from the trace 7-OH present in raw leaf, [as KNT reported on September 25][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning].

## Parker's Law and the possibility of charges

Tindell told Fox News Digital that authorities have not uncovered any indications of foul play but have not ruled it out either, according to the report. He pointed to Parker's Law, a Mississippi statute enacted in 2022 that allows criminal charges when an unlawful delivery of fentanyl results in a person's death. "If the investigation establishes those circumstances, law enforcement will work with prosecutors to pursue the appropriate charges," Tindell said, per Fox News.

The statute as Fox News described it is specific to fentanyl. The report does not say that fentanyl has been detected in either case, and the toxicology results that would answer that question were not available as of September 25. South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson's advisory earlier this week described the deaths as overdoses and warned about kratom products laced with fentanyl or other synthetic opioids, while the Mississippi investigators handling the cases have not characterized the cause of death, [as KNT reported on September 25][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning].

## The Puffs raid and the arrests

Metro Narcotics agents raided Puffs Vape, Tobacco and Beer Store on Heritage Drive in Oxford on Thursday, September 24, 2026, [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/mississippi-police-seize-kratom-make-arrests-deaths-ole-miss-students-rcna599889). Agents confiscated "more than 500 units of kratom, along with an assortment of THC cartridges, gummies, and other suspected narcotics," according to an Oxford police statement quoted by NBC News. Oxford police booked Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, and Islam Mohamed, 18, both local residents, on drug offenses, and officials said additional charges may be forthcoming as the investigation continues, the network reported.

The store's owner, Amer Alowdi, 40, was indicted on narcotics trafficking charges in 2022 and fled the country before he could be prosecuted, according to both NBC News and Fox News. Both outlets reported that possession, distribution, and sale of kratom have been prohibited in Oxford under a city ordinance since 2019, and Fox News noted that kratom remains legal in many jurisdictions.

NBC News reported that it "wasn't immediately clear Friday whether these arrests have any connection to the Hamilton and Strang deaths." Fox News, which reported the raid alongside Tindell's comments, likewise did not report a confirmed link between the store and the students. The Oxford police statement quoted by NBC News describes the seized kratom by unit count and does not describe the products' alkaloid content or labeling, so the reporting does not establish whether the units were natural leaf products or concentrated 7-OH tablets, gummies, or shots of the kind the Mississippi State Department of Health warned about this week.

## The students

The students were identified as Aidan Hamilton, an 18-year-old freshman from Englewood, Colorado, and Robert Strang, a 20-year-old junior from Atlanta, according to NBC News. They were found dead in separate locations on Monday, September 21, 2026, and the cases are being investigated separately, as KNT reported earlier this week.

[Reason](https://reason.com/2026/09/25/the-toxicology-report-on-2-dead-students-isnt-back-but-the-kratom-panic-is-already-here/), in a September 25 piece, also reported the seizure of more than 500 kratom units and the two arrests, and argued that the presence of kratom at or near a death scene does not establish that it caused the death. Reason's piece is commentary, and its characterization of the surrounding coverage as a "panic" is the publication's own. The American Kratom Association held a press conference on September 25 to make a similar argument about premature attribution, as KNT reported; a recap of what was said at that event was not available in the reporting reviewed for this briefing.
