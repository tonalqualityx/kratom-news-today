Physicians at Wills Eye Hospital and Thomas Jefferson University in Philadelphia have published a case report describing a 70-year-old man who used about 6.8 grams of kratom daily for four years and developed visual snow-like symptoms along with structural changes in both retinas, [according to the abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42461119/) in Retinal Cases & Brief Reports. The report, whose listed authors include Lily C. Farrell and Jose S. Pulido, was published online on July 16, 2026, and drew wider attention after [Medical Daily reported on it](https://www.medicaldaily.com/kratom-visual-snow-retina-changes-case-report-479112) on September 25, 2026.

The authors characterized what they found as a possible association, and wrote that a single case cannot establish cause and effect, according to Medical Daily. Neither the abstract nor Medical Daily's account specifies what form of kratom the patient used. The daily amount is given by weight, and the reports do not say whether it was natural leaf powder, an extract, or a concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product, a distinction the Drug Enforcement Administration drew in July when it moved to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold while stating that botanical kratom with naturally occurring trace 7-OH was not the target, [as KNT reported at the time][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products].

## What the patient presented with

The man came in with worsening night vision, which the abstract calls nyctalopia, and a visual snow-like phenomenon that Medical Daily described as "flickering dots across the entire visual field" resembling television static. Optical coherence tomography showed disruption of the ellipsoid zone in both eyes, a band on the scan that reflects the health of the photoreceptors, along with changes in the peripheral retinal pigment epithelium, according to the abstract. Fundus autofluorescence, an imaging test that maps pigment-related signal across the retina, showed reduced signal in the macula, the abstract states.

Other tests were unremarkable, the abstract states: indocyanine green and fluorescein angiography, which image retinal blood flow, and electroretinography, which measures the retina's electrical response to light. Medical Daily reported that a brain MRI was also unremarkable. The physicians noted skin hyperpigmentation that was otherwise unexplained, and a urine test was positive for mitragynine, the principal kratom alkaloid, according to the abstract.

## What the authors concluded

The abstract states that the case raises the possibility that chronic kratom use may be associated with visual snow-like symptoms, ellipsoid zone disruption, and retinal pigment epithelium changes. The authors recommended that clinicians consider multimodal retinal imaging in chronic kratom users, especially those with dermatologic signs, and said more research is needed, according to Medical Daily. The recommendation is the authors' clinical suggestion to other physicians; the report does not make population-level claims about the frequency of such findings among people who use kratom.

Medical Daily noted that a single case report cannot establish that kratom caused the condition. Case reports of this kind form a small part of the kratom clinical literature, which [a systematic review in the Journal of Medical Toxicology surveyed this month][journal-medical-toxicology-systematic-review-kratom-pulmonary-toxicity-24-reports-five-categories] for pulmonary findings, working from 24 published reports, as KNT reported. That review did not address ocular findings, and the Wills Eye report does not cite prior retinal cases in the abstract available on PubMed.
