The American Kratom Association is asking Lafayette County Sheriff Joey East to identify the products that investigators publicly described as "packaged kratom" after two University of Mississippi students were found dead in Oxford, and to have those products tested independently of the toxicology being run as part of the autopsies, [according to a release the association distributed](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/american-kratom-association-calls-immediate-172100388.html) through ACCESS Newswire on September 27, 2026.

The request follows the association's September 23 condolence statement and a September 25 press conference on the media coverage, [as KNT reported][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning]. Lafayette County Metro Narcotics said early in the investigation that it had no confirmed information that kratom or any other substance contributed to either death, [as KNT reported on September 23][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link], and none of the reporting reviewed to date has established whether the packaged products were natural leaf kratom or a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation.

## What the association is asking for

The release lists five requests. The association wants immediate product identification, including brand names, manufacturers or distributors, dosage forms, labeled ingredients, and available lot numbers, along with packaging photographs. It wants direct laboratory testing of the recovered contents to identify the relevant alkaloids, synthetic derivatives, and any adulterants. It asks that the lab findings be compared with the labeling claims, followed by an investigation of any evidence of deliberate adulteration or deceptive labeling. It asks for clarification about cychlorphine, specifically whether testing capable of detecting that synthetic opioid has been requested, whether results are pending, and whether any verified evidence establishes its presence in either case. And it asks for a public update that separates confirmed findings from unresolved questions, including whether any evidence shows the products were consumed or contributed to either death.

The release also asks the sheriff to clarify whether investigators recovered product contents, empty packaging, or both. If some details cannot be disclosed while the investigation is open, the association asks authorities to say so and release the rest.

Cychlorphine entered the public record through South Carolina Attorney General Alan Wilson's September 24 advisory, which said student messages mentioned the substance although law enforcement had not confirmed its presence, [as KNT reported][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning]. The September 27 release does not say where the association's information about cychlorphine came from.

## The distinction the association wants drawn

"A package labeled 'kratom' does not establish what was inside it, whether it was consumed, or whether it contributed to a death," Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy, said in the release. "Once authorities publicly identified those packages, explaining those limitations and establishing their contents became critically important."

The association wants investigators to determine whether the products contained natural kratom leaf, concentrated formulations, chemically enhanced 7-OH, synthetic derivatives, or other undisclosed substances. The release says the Food and Drug Administration, the Department of Health and Human Services, and the Drug Enforcement Administration have differentiated natural kratom leaf, which contains naturally occurring trace amounts of 7-OH, from concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and related synthetic compounds. That is the line the DEA drew when it [placed mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] in August, and the line in its separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold, which [had not been published as a final order][dea-7oh-temporary-scheduling-order-still-unpublished] as of KNT's last check.

The release cites a 2016 paper by Lydecker and colleagues that reported substantially elevated 7-OH concentrations in commercial kratom products and concluded the findings strongly suggested adulteration. The association says that research supports testing product contents rather than relying on labels, and adds that it "does not establish what was present in these cases."

The association also draws a line between the two kinds of testing it is discussing. Testing recovered material can establish what the product contained, the release says, while determining whether a substance contributed to a death requires interpreting post-mortem toxicology alongside the full medical and investigative record.

"We are not asking investigators to blame or rule out any substance prematurely," Haddow said. "It remains possible that neither natural kratom nor synthetic 7-OH contributed to these deaths. We are asking for precise information, comprehensive testing, and a clear explanation of what the evidence supports."

## Where the investigation stands

Mississippi Department of Public Safety Commissioner Sean Tindell said on September 25 that toxicology results could be complete as early as the week of September 28, [as KNT reported on September 26][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests]. The association's release does not reference Tindell's timeline, and it does not mention the more than 500 units of kratom that Metro Narcotics seized from an Oxford vape store on September 24, which police have not publicly connected to the deaths.

The release is addressed to the sheriff, not to the state agencies handling the toxicology. No response from East or the Lafayette County Sheriff's Office was reported as of September 28, and the release does not say whether the association sent the request as a formal letter or only as the public statement.

The American Kratom Association is a 501(c)(4) advocacy organization that, according to its own description, supports access to natural leaf kratom products and supports enforcement against synthetic, semi-synthetic, concentrated, and chemically manipulated products marketed as kratom. Its request is an advocacy position, and the questions it raises about the recovered products remain open until the sheriff's office or the state releases results.
