The "packaged kratom" that Lafayette County Metro Narcotics said it found at both University of Mississippi death scenes was a manufactured tablet product labeled "Smax" Pseudo-DHM, and preliminary laboratory testing found 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) in some of the pills, according to a Metro Narcotics affidavit described by [WAPT](https://www.wapt.com/article/oxford-ole-miss-kratom-sales-affidavit/73934083) on September 29, 2026. The affidavit says the lab could not test for several other compounds listed on the packaging, including MGM-15, and that investigators arranged further testing, WAPT reported. The affidavit describes the pills as believed to contain kratom or a synthetic kratom analogue, according to WAPT. The American Kratom Association said in a September 29 release that the Department of Health and Human Services has stated MGM-15 does not occur naturally in the kratom plant and has identified it as a synthetic derivative or chemically manipulated form of 7-OH, and that natural kratom leaf contains only trace levels of 7-OH. The reporting does not say whether the 7-OH the lab detected was that natural trace or a concentrated or synthetic preparation.

The affidavit does not establish that the pills caused either death, WAPT reported. The medical examiner has not determined a cause of death for either student, and toxicology results remain pending, according to [FOX13 Memphis](https://www.fox13memphis.com/news/investigators-find-same-tablets-in-rooms-of-two-ole-miss-students-who-died-records-show/article_f1da9c40-3127-4106-8091-8bee96a91e64.html) and [WSMV](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/09/28/raided-oxford-store-was-where-ole-miss-student-purchased-kratom-before-death-records-show/). WSMV reported that coroner records indicate the deaths are consistent with drug overdose, and FOX13 wrote that investigators believe drugs are responsible, per the court records.

When the students were found on September 21, Metro Narcotics [said only that packaged retail kratom was present at both scenes][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link] and that it had no confirmed information linking it to the deaths, as Kratom News Today (KNT) reported. The American Kratom Association, which [asked Sheriff Joey East on September 27][american-kratom-association-asks-lafayette-county-sheriff-joey-east-identify-independently-test-packaged-kratom-ole-miss-cychlorphine] to name the products and have them independently tested, said on September 29 that students should have been given the product name from the start.

## What the affidavit says

The affidavit was filed in support of a criminal complaint and names Yahya Mohamed Mosleh and his son, Islam Yahya Mosleh, stating there is probable cause to believe they possessed a controlled substance or analogue with intent to distribute, WAPT reported. WSMV identified the two as Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, and Islam Mohamed, 18, and said they were charged with possession of Schedule I controlled substances with intent to distribute. The two were booked after Metro Narcotics [raided Puffs Vape, Tobacco and Beer Store on Heritage Drive on September 24][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests], as KNT reported.

According to WAPT's account of the affidavit, investigators found packages labeled "Smax" near Aidan Hamilton, who was found unresponsive in a University of Mississippi dorm room on September 21, and near Robert Strang, who was found unresponsive at an Oxford residence later that day. The products were believed to contain kratom or a synthetic kratom analogue, the affidavit says. [WMC Action News 5](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/09/29/court-documents-detail-hours-before-ole-miss-students-suspected-overdose-deaths/) reported that Hamilton was 18 and Strang was 20, that the pills were found on Hamilton's dresser in his Stewart Hall room, and that a Smax packet in similar packaging was found on Strang's nightstand at his home on Van Buren Avenue.

Hamilton's bank records showed purchases at Puffs on September 9, 14, and 20, and Strang's records showed purchases at Express Mart No. 7 in Yalobusha County on September 9 and 11, WAPT reported. WSMV's account differs slightly, describing two Hamilton purchases at Puffs, on September 9 and 14. Action News 5 placed the Express Mart on Highway 315 and reported that undercover officers bought the product there the day after Strang's death.

A confidential informant bought kratom-based pills from Yahya Mosleh at Puffs on September 24, and during the search that day agents detained both men and seized more than 500 units of kratom, suspected controlled substances, and $1,799, according to WAPT. FOX13 and Action News 5 both reported a line from the records in which the clerk told the informant "to be careful taking the pills because people were dying from them."

Action News 5 also reported other details from the two scenes described in the court documents. Strang's roommates said he used nitrous oxide daily, investigators found 20 to 30 nitrous oxide cans at the residence, and he had taken a Xanax bar around 4 p.m. the day before he was found, according to the court documents as described by the station. Hamilton's roommate told investigators he smoked marijuana heavily from pens and pipes, Action News 5 reported. A Florida lab is fast-tracking testing of the Smax products and toxicology reports could arrive as soon as this week, the station said.

## Where the testing stands

The preliminary result reported in the affidavit, 7-OH in some pills, is the only laboratory finding described in the reports KNT reviewed. WAPT also reported that the lab could not test for several compounds listed on the packaging, including MGM-15. The AKA's release said the Drug Enforcement Administration has placed MGM-15 and MGM-16 in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act; the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation described MGM-15 the same way on September 16, 2026, when it [warned that tablets sold as "Cat's Claw" contained the compound][tennessee-tbi-warns-cats-claw-tablets-contain-mgm-15-kratom-18-lab-submissions], as KNT reported.

None of the reports describe what "Pseudo-DHM" refers to, list the full ingredients printed on the packaging, or say whether the 7-OH detected was quantified. Mississippi Department of Public Safety Commissioner Sean Tindell told Action News 5 the state wants to test the products to make sure "it's not being laced with any other chemicals, or there's no other harmful chemicals in that product." Tindell said the legislature will likely consider next year whether "we need a statewide ban on kratom," according to the station. Action News 5 and WLBT both reported that Oxford and Lafayette County have local ordinances banning kratom, and Fox News reported that Oxford's has been in place since 2019.

## The AKA: 'the product was not kratom at all'

The American Kratom Association [said on September 29](https://www.reflector.com/news/national/american-kratom-association-calls-for-public-safety-clarity-following-reports-that-products-in-ole-miss/article_069de09a-041b-594c-9a77-5185d833f146.html) that law-enforcement records reported by WMC and WSMV identify the products purchased by both students as "Smax" Pseudo-DHM tablets, and that publicly available marketing for the product describes it as a high-potency MGM-15 product. The release, distributed through EZ Newswire, said the DEA has placed MGM-15 and MGM-16 in Schedule I and that the Department of Justice describes them as highly potent opioid compounds presenting an imminent hazard to public safety.

"If law enforcement knew the specific product involved, Ole Miss students should have been warned what product they needed to be concerned about," said Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy. "Instead, the public was broadly warned about 'kratom' when the product was not kratom at all." Haddow called the delay "a public-safety problem" and said a student with a package of the tablets at home would not necessarily have understood that a warning about "kratom" was directed at that product.

Haddow also said the sheriff's office's initial characterization led national and local media to report the deaths as involving kratom without knowing the products' formulation. "The media relied upon what law enforcement told them," he said. The association had made the same argument at its ["Reporting Before the Results" press conference][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning] on September 25, as KNT reported.

The release stopped short of attributing the deaths to the tablets. "We criticized premature conclusions when natural kratom was being blamed, and we are not going to commit the same error now," Haddow said. "Identification of these products is enormously important, but toxicology still has to determine causation." "If the evidence ultimately establishes that MGM-15, another manufactured opioid, natural kratom, another illicit substance, or some combination contributed to either death, then the public should be told exactly that," Haddow said.

The AKA's position that a Smax tablet "was not kratom at all" is the association's characterization. The affidavit, as WAPT described it, calls the pills "kratom-based" and says they were believed to contain kratom or a synthetic kratom analogue, and the Smax marketing description comes from the AKA's reading of the product's own advertising. The association said it supports age restrictions, accurate labeling, contaminant testing, good manufacturing practices, and enforcement against synthetic, concentrated, or chemically manipulated products marketed as kratom.

## Mississippi physicians and lawmakers

Physicians and legislators quoted by [WLBT](https://www.wlbt.com/2026/09/29/kratom-debate-doctors-lawmakers-push-more-regulation/) on September 28 argued for tighter controls in Mississippi ahead of the toxicology results. Dr. Jennifer Bryan, a family physician who sits on the American Medical Association's Council on Science and Public Health, told the station, "We don't have to speculate about these two deaths at Ole Miss to recognize" the public health issue, and said, "natural does not mean safe. It's not synonymous." Bryan supports federal regulation and an over-the-counter ban, WLBT reported.

State Rep. Lee Yancey, a former chair of the House Drug Policy Committee, told WLBT that sellers "find ways to work around the law" and compared enforcement to "playing whack-a-mole," and said he wants safeguards such as pre-market testing or Schedule II classification. WLBT reported that Mississippi's legislature last year passed a law that, as the station described it, banned synthetic kratom, restricted natural kratom sales to adults 21 and older, and required products to be kept behind the counter. The station said numerous cities and counties, including Oxford and Lafayette County, have adopted broader local bans.

Whether the Smax tablets fell within the state synthetic kratom ban WLBT described, and whether the charges WSMV described as possession of Schedule I controlled substances rest on MGM-15, 7-OH, or another substance, is not addressed in any of the reports KNT reviewed. Tindell [told Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ole-miss-toxicology-reports-expected-soon-results-may-trigger-anti-fentanyl-law-official-says) last week that toxicology could be complete as early as this week and that charges under Parker's Law, which Fox News described as a 2022 statute covering fentanyl deliveries that result in death, are possible if the investigation supports them, as KNT reported.
