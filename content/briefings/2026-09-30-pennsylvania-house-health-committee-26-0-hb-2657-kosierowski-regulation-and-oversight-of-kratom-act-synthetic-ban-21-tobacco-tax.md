The Pennsylvania House Health Committee voted 26-0 on September 29, 2026 to report House Bill 2657, the Regulation and Oversight of Kratom Act, which would ban "synthetic kratom products" defined in part by 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) above 0.5 percent of total alkaloid content, and the full House gave it first consideration the same day, according to the [General Assembly's bill record](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/2025/hb2657). The bill text treats those products, which include concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations, as a separate category from natural leaf products, which it would continue to allow under a registration system. The [Pennsylvania Capital-Star reported](https://penncapital-star.com/government-politics/pa-house-panel-advances-bill-to-regulate-kratom/) on September 30 that the committee advanced the bill unanimously and described it as allowing registered retailers to sell natural kratom leaf to adults 21 and older, subject to packaging, labeling, tobacco-tax, and no-medical-claims requirements.

The bill separates natural leaf products from what it calls "synthetic kratom products," and it defines that category by alkaloid thresholds as well as by the presence of synthetic compounds. Under the [bill text as introduced](https://www.palegis.us/legislation/bills/text/HTM/2025/0/HB2657/PN3668) at Printer's Number 3668, a product is synthetic if the 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) in its alkaloid fraction exceeds 0.5 percent of total alkaloid composition, if it contains more than 10 parts per million of mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, if it contains synthetically derived mitragynine, 7-OH, or other kratom compounds, or if it contains kratom without qualifying as a natural product. The bill text does not say how the 0.5 percent figure was chosen, and the Capital-Star did not report any discussion of it at the committee meeting.

## Who is behind it and where it stands

The prime sponsor is Rep. Bridget Kosierowski, a Democrat from the 114th District in Lackawanna County, and the bill lists 30 co-sponsors, according to the General Assembly record. It was referred to the Health Committee on June 22, 2026. The record shows the committee reported it "as committed," meaning without amendment, followed by first consideration, a motion to lay it on the table, and a motion removing it from the table, all on September 29.

The bill's title, per the record, is "An Act providing for the regulation and sale of kratom products; imposing a tobacco products tax on natural kratom products; imposing duties on the Department of Health; and imposing penalties." House Democrats [began drafting the measure in June][pennsylvania-kratom-ems-hotspot-legislation] under the name Kratom Standards, Labeling, and Consumer Protection Act, as reported at the time.

According to the bill record, HB 2657 has received first consideration only and has not passed either chamber. The Capital-Star's [September 8 report](https://penncapital-star.com/health-care/health-experts-lawmakers-push-for-pa-kratom-regulation-proponents-warn-theyll-fight-a-ban/), covered [here][pennsylvania-health-department-reviewing-7oh-request-lawmakers-push-regulation], identified Rep. James Prokopiak and Sen. Tracy Pennycuick as sponsors of separate bills that would ban synthetic kratom products and high-potency extracts while allowing leaf sales to adults;

## What the bill would require

The bill text lays out a registration-and-standards regime for natural products alongside the synthetic prohibition:

- Processors and retailers would have to register with the Department of Health before operating and renew annually, with fees capped at $100.
- Sales would be limited to people 21 and older, and retailers would have to use transaction scan devices to verify age.
- Labels would have to carry the processor's name and address, a Food and Drug Administration disclaimer, a statement verifying the product's natural form, and a warning to keep the product out of the reach of minors.
- Products would have to be sold in resealable, child-resistant packaging.
- Products could not make medical claims or be promoted for medical use.
- The state's tobacco products tax would apply to natural kratom products at the first sale to a Pennsylvania retailer.

Manufacturing, delivering, selling, or possessing with intent to sell a synthetic kratom product would be prohibited, with exceptions for controlled substance registrants and licensed practitioners. A violation of that section would be a misdemeanor punishable by a fine of up to $15,000, up to five years in prison, or both, according to the bill text. Violations of the natural-product requirements would carry a civil penalty of up to $10,000 per offense. Most of the act would take effect immediately on enactment, with the registration and civil-penalty sections effective in 90 days.

## Other 7-OH thresholds in use

Colorado's Daniel Bregger Act caps 7-OH at 2 percent of total alkaloid content, according to the [Colorado attorney general's announcement](https://coag.gov/press-releases/colorado-smoke-shop-owners-to-pay-152000-for-selling-illegal-kratom-products/) of a [$152,000 settlement with a 15-store smoke shop chain][colorado-attorney-general-weiser-152000-settlement-dnb-distro-million-smoke-n-vape-7oh-limit-labeling] this month. The Drug Enforcement Administration's pending federal proposal, as [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/dea-moves-place-some-strong-kratom-related-products-under-strict-federal-drug-2026-07-01/) when the agency announced it in July, uses a different measure, [0.050 percent 7-OH by dry weight for plant material and 1 milligram per unit for finished products][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products]. The measures differ, and none of the sources for this briefing compares them. 
## Other Pennsylvania action on kratom

Separately, several Pennsylvania officials have called for state action in recent weeks. The Bucks County commissioners, [LevittownNow.com reported](https://levittownnow.com/2026/09/21/county-commissioners-pass-resolution-to-restrict-kratom/), [asked Health Secretary Debra Bogen in mid-September to use emergency powers to place kratom in Schedule I][bucks-county-pennsylvania-commissioners-resolution-health-secretary-bogen-emergency-schedule-one-kratom], and Montgomery County District Attorney Kevin Steele [sent retailers notices to stop selling concentrated 7-OH products][montgomery-county-pennsylvania-da-steele-notices-retailers-cease-concentrated-7oh-designer-drug] he characterized as designer drugs. The Department of Health told the Capital-Star in the September 8 report that it was reviewing the scheduling requests and considering all options, and the same report cited state data showing 17 overdose deaths this year with kratom as a contributing factor. Neither that report nor the Bucks County resolution specifies whether the deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

The American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow told the Capital-Star in the September 8 report that the association supports regulation, opposes an outright ban, and planned to increase its lobbying in the state. The association has separately said it supports the DEA's action against synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products, a position it stated when it [endorsed the federal proposal in July][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action]; its position on HB 2657's 0.5 percent definition was not reported. Neither the bill record nor the Capital-Star's September 30 report, as reflected in the coverage available for this briefing, includes a statement from the association on the committee vote itself, and no testimony from retailers or manufacturers at the committee meeting was reported.
