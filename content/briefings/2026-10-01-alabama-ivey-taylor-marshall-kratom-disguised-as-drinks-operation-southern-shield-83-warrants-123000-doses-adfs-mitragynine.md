Alabama Gov. Kay Ivey, Alabama Law Enforcement Agency (ALEA) Secretary Hal Taylor, and Attorney General Steve Marshall warned the public on September 30, 2026, that kratom products are still being sold at gas stations and vape shops packaged as drinks, energy shots, and "feel-good" supplements, according to a [release from the governor's office](https://governor.alabama.gov/newsroom/2026/09/governor-ivey-urges-public-awareness-of-potential-dangers-associated-with-kratom-products/). The release describes kratom as a controlled substance under Alabama Code and says the Alabama Department of Forensic Sciences confirmed mitragynine, the primary alkaloid in kratom leaf, in products it tested. It does not say whether any of the products contained concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and none of the coverage reviewed by KNT reports a 7-OH finding from the state lab.

"Kratom is illegal in Alabama, but unfortunately, it is not that plain and simple," Ivey said in the release. "Our law enforcement folks continue identifying stores putting this illegal product on their shelves disguised as harmless drinks. Y'all, please just be aware: Just because it is sold on a gas station shelf, does not always mean it is safe."

## Operation Southern Shield's updated count

The governor's office said Operation Southern Shield, which ALEA launched in May, has produced 83 search warrants and the seizure of nearly 123,000 doses, with several hundred more bottles removed from retailers since the operation began. When the attorney general's office and ALEA first announced results in June, [as KNT reported at the time][alabama-operation-southern-shield-kratom-seizures], the figure given was nearly 64,000 bottles. The September 30 release counts doses rather than bottles, and it does not reconcile the two numbers or say how much of the new total reflects seizures after June. [Alabama Public Radio](https://www.apr.org/news/2026-10-01/two-ole-miss-student-deaths-put-the-stimulant-kratom-back-in-the-headlines) reported the June operation as more than 60,000 products seized.

[ABC 33/40 reported](https://abc3340.com/news/local/alabama-officials-warn-public-about-illegal-kratom-products-gov-kay-ivey-and-the-alabama-law-enforcement-agency-september-2026) that the investigations began in 2025 after citizen complaints prompted ALEA and the attorney general's office to make undercover purchases, with ADFS testing confirming mitragynine in some of what they bought. The station said ALEA has since worked with the attorney general's office, the Alabama Alcoholic Beverage Control Board, ADFS, local police, and district attorneys' offices on complaints involving suspected kratom products.

Alabama Public Radio's Chris Ahlf reported that the state placed kratom in Schedule I in 2016, that the attorney general's office sent its first cease-and-desist letter over a product called "Bougie Bliss" in September 2025 and a second on March 26, 2026, and that seven or eight new brands appeared on shelves after the initial enforcement. Paul Hayes of ALEA's State Bureau of Investigation told APR that tips from citizens remain the agency's best source of information on where the products are being sold.

## What Taylor and Marshall said

Taylor, the ALEA secretary, framed the warning around where the products are sold. "Something being sold over the counter at a gas station or vape shop should not automatically be considered safe," he said in the release. "Education and awareness are critical as we continue working with our partners to identify these products and remove illegal substances from Alabama communities."

Marshall directed his statement at retailers. "Alabama is not open for business to anyone selling these dangerous and illegal substances, however they're labeled or packaged," he said, according to the release. "Retailers had every opportunity to comply, and those who ignored the law will now face serious consequences. Anyone who continues to sell these products will face the full force of the law, including seizure of assets and felony prosecution."

[AL.com's Carol Robinson reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/alabama-officials-warn-people-feel-213142587.html) that Marshall also said kratom can be addictive, that unregulated products do not carry accurate ingredient information, and that combining the products with alcohol produces unpredictable effects. Those characterizations are the attorney general's; the release does not cite a study or case data behind them.

## Labeling, and who officials say is buying

The release says the products are marketed as beverages, shots, or "feel-good" items promising relaxation, energy, or improved mood, and that they often appear without clear kratom labeling. [WBRC's Taylor Pollock reported](https://www.wbrc.com/2026/09/30/yall-please-just-be-aware-gov-ivey-warning-alabamians-be-aware-illegal-kratom-found-unlabeled-products/) that officials expressed particular concern about teenagers and college students, writing that "because packaging often promotes relaxation, energy, or focus without clearly disclosing that the contents are controlled and potentially addictive, consumers may have no idea what they are ingesting."

WBRC also described 7-OH as "a highly addictive byproduct of Kratom." That characterization is the station's, and it is not attached to any product the state tested. Jessica Pescatore, clinical director of the Alabama Poison Information Center, told APR that "kratom" functions as an umbrella term for a plant species and that callers describe escalating use over time; APR did not report the center's call volume.

The public is asked to report suspected kratom products to local law enforcement or through the attorney general's website, according to the release. None of the coverage names the retailers served with the 83 warrants, says how many have been charged, or lists the brands seized beyond the earlier "Bougie Bliss" letter.

## The out-of-state references

WBRC noted that the warning came days after two University of Mississippi students were found dead in Oxford, and AL.com reported that packaged kratom bought at retail stores was found in both of those investigations with no confirmed link to the deaths. Mississippi's attorney general said this week she will push for a statewide ban, [as KNT reports today][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc]. The Alabama release itself does not mention Mississippi, and the Oxford investigation centers on a tablet product that Lafayette County investigators describe as a manufactured, kratom-related compound rather than the beverages Alabama officials describe.

WBRC also pointed to Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes's lawsuit against Botanic Tonics, maker of the Feel Free kratom-and-kava drink, [covered by KNT on September 24][arizona-attorney-general-mayes-sues-botanic-tonics-feel-free-consumer-fraud-act-first-in-nation]. The Alabama release does not name Feel Free or any other brand among the products seized.
