Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch said her office will push for a statewide ban on kratom "in all forms" when the Legislature convenes next session, [NewsNation's Rob Taub reported](https://www.newsnationnow.com/us-news/mid-south/mississippi-ag-pushing-kratom-ban-students-deaths/) on October 1, 2026. The remarks, made on the network's "Katie Pavlich Tonight," came as the investigation into the September 21 deaths of University of Mississippi students Aidan Hamilton, 18, and Robert Strang, 20, remains centered on packages of "Smax" Pseudo-DHM tablets found near both students. Investigators describe the tablets as a manufactured, kratom-related product whose packaging lists MGM-15, one of three compounds the narcotics unit says the Drug Enforcement Administration placed in Schedule I in August, and [a Lafayette County affidavit reported last week][lafayette-county-affidavit-smax-pseudo-dhm-tablets-both-ole-miss-scenes-7oh-preliminary-mgm-15-untested-aka-students-should-have-been-warned] said preliminary testing found 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) in some pills. Whether that 7-OH was the trace amount natural to leaf or a concentrated or synthetic form has not been reported, cause of death has not been determined, and toxicology is pending.

"We have supported in the past banning that in our state," Fitch said, according to NewsNation. "We'll certainly be promoting legislation as well this next session to ban it in all forms as well." She added: "It's unregulated. And again, I think it's really significant for us to determine all those side effects and know that it's putting Mississippians in harm's way." NewsNation did not report Fitch drawing a distinction between natural leaf kratom and synthetic or concentrated products, and the segment as described did not address the Smax tablets by name.

The same day Fitch's comments aired, the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit asked state lawmakers for something narrower, the American Kratom Association (AKA) held a press conference in Oxford accusing local police of misleading the public, and the Global Kratom Coalition (GKC) sided with the narcotics unit's approach over a general ban.

## What Metro Narcotics asked the Legislature for

In a statement released September 30 through the Oxford Police Department, Metro Narcotics said packaging "associated with kratom-related products" was found at both death scenes and prompted a warning "out of an abundance of caution," [according to the text published by The Local Voice](https://www.thelocalvoice.net/oxford/lafayette-country-metro-narcotics-issues-law-enforcement-update). "That warning was not a declaration that kratom or any specific substance caused either death," the unit said. "It was a proactive effort to inform the public of a potential danger while investigators worked to determine the facts."

The statement said agents made a controlled purchase of a product marketed as SMAX and that investigators are waiting on the Mississippi State Medical Examiner's Office for autopsy and toxicology findings. It also set out the federal landscape as the unit sees it: the DEA placed mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I on August 26, 2026, and that action "does not impose a general federal Schedule I classification on traditional botanical kratom."

"We are calling on the Mississippi Legislature to address synthetic kratom-related products with the urgency this public safety issue demands," the unit said, [SuperTalk Mississippi's Caleb Salers reported](https://www.supertalk.fm/lafayette-county-officials-call-on-lawmakers-to-address-emerging-drug-threat-across-mississippi/). It asked for "immediate legislative hearings, a thorough examination of the state's controlled substance laws, and clear answers about how manufacturers, distributors, and retailers can be held accountable when their actions put lives at risk." The statement closed: "Families deserve answers. Communities deserve protection. Law enforcement deserves clear, enforceable laws that keep pace with emerging drug threats." SuperTalk noted that Gov. Tate Reeves has not called a special session.

The narcotics unit's request and the attorney general's are different in scope. Metro Narcotics asked for action on "synthetic kratom-related products" and described botanical kratom as outside the DEA's order. Fitch, as quoted by NewsNation, described a ban "in all forms."

## The AKA press conference

The AKA's Mac Haddow, the organization's senior fellow on public policy, spoke at the Lafayette County Sheriff Building on the morning of September 30, [according to the AKA's media advisory](https://www.thelocalvoice.net/oxford/american-kratom-association-to-hold-press-conference-in-oxford-mississippi-wednesday-september-30-at-10-am-cst/). "The police chief and the sheriff absolutely misled everybody when they issued an investigative report that was incomplete and called it a kratom package product when it was not," Haddow said, [WMC Action News 5 reported](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/09/30/it-gave-me-my-life-back-advocates-condemn-misinformation-about-kratom-speak-ole-miss-student-deaths/). He called Smax "a synthetically engineered opioid, not kratom at all," said "the media was misled," and asked law enforcement to correct the record.

[The Oxford Eagle reported](https://oxfordeagle.com/2026/09/30/kratom-advocate-challenges-statements-following-deaths/) Haddow saying the AKA "wanted to correct the false claim that was provided in a pre-investigation report" and that the product was "a federally controlled synthetic opioid and not traditional botanical kratom." The Eagle ran the narcotics unit's response alongside Haddow's remarks and noted that cause of death is undetermined for both students.

The AKA's advisory said the Smax tablets are marketed as high-potency MGM-15, which it said the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services has confirmed does not occur naturally in the kratom plant. It also restated the AKA's position that a product should be called kratom only when its alkaloid profile holds at least 100 parts mitragynine to one part combined 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl. That ratio is the association's own benchmark, and no federal or Mississippi standard adopts it. The AKA had previously asked Sheriff Joey East to identify and independently test the products, [as KNT reported on September 28][american-kratom-association-asks-lafayette-county-sheriff-joey-east-identify-independently-test-packaged-kratom-ole-miss-cychlorphine].

Jennifer Gillis, a kratom consumer from Tennessee, told the press conference that natural kratom products are "a completely different breed than any tablets synthesized in a lab" and called for synthetic products to be pulled from shelves nationwide, according to WMC. The station reported that Hamilton bought the tablets twice at Puffs Vape, Tobacco and Beer Store in Oxford and that Strang bought them at a store in Yalobusha County, that Puffs has closed permanently, and that Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, and Islam Mohamed, 18, each face three counts of possession of Schedule I controlled substances with intent to distribute. NewsNation reported a third person sought in connection with the sales remains at large; no other outlet reviewed by KNT reported a third suspect.

## The Global Kratom Coalition's position

The GKC said it supports Metro Narcotics' distinction between natural kratom leaf and manufactured synthetic alkaloid products such as Smax, in a [September 30 release](https://www.eagletribune.com/region/global-kratom-coalition-supports-lafayette-county-metro-narcotics-call-to-target-concentrated-synthetic-opioids/article_e8ed1878-0b81-543a-9135-af2c2a250edb.html) carried by The Eagle-Tribune. Executive Director Matthew Lowe said the coalition backs legislative hearings aimed at specific synthetic compounds, including 7-OH, and urged Mississippi lawmakers to strengthen state law by targeting the synthetic drugs the DEA has already scheduled rather than imposing what the release called a broad ban on natural kratom that would fail to address the products at issue. The release does not mention Fitch by name, and it predates NewsNation's report of her comments.

Both national kratom advocacy groups are on record this week opposing synthetic and concentrated products and supporting enforcement against them. The GKC release argues against a broad natural-leaf ban, and neither group had responded to Fitch's remarks in the coverage reviewed by KNT.

## Where the sources differ on Mississippi's current law

NewsNation reported that it is illegal to sell kratom to anyone under 21 in Mississippi, that the drug "isn't illegal statewide," and that Oxford adopted its own ban years ago. [WMC reported on October 1](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/10/01/still-shelves-kratom-sales-under-scrutiny-following-overdose-deaths/) that the state's 2025 law both raised the purchase age to 21 and banned synthetic kratom extracts, that Oxford's ban dates to 2019, and that Lafayette County's took effect in 2021. Fitch's description of kratom as "unregulated" sits alongside those accounts of a 2025 statute. NewsNation's report does not address the statute.

State Rep. Lee Yancey, who told WLBT last week that he wants pre-market testing or Schedule II treatment, put the question to WMC this way: "Why should we allow our gas stations to be places where unregulated drugs are sold to college kids? And high school kids?" Dr. Lucas Trautman of Oxford Treatment Center told the station that new synthetic opioids are appearing in the United States at a rate of about one every other month.

WMC's October 1 report also said the Smax products contained pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and 7-OH. That goes further than the affidavit KNT reviewed last week, which said preliminary testing found 7-OH in some pills and that the lab could not test for MGM-15. WMC did not say whether new test results have come back. Public Safety Commissioner Sean Tindell said on September 25 that toxicology could be complete as early as this week, [as KNT reported][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests]; as of October 1, none of the outlets cited here reported results.

Alabama officials issued their own kratom warning on September 30, citing the Oxford deaths, [as KNT reports separately today][alabama-ivey-taylor-marshall-kratom-disguised-as-drinks-operation-southern-shield-83-warrants-123000-doses-adfs-mitragynine].
