A Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) affidavit dated September 25 used bank records to connect University of Mississippi students Aidan Hamilton, 18, and Robert Strang, 20, to two stores before investigators recovered similar suspected kratom-related products at both death scenes, [Fox News' Bradford Betz reported](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ole-miss-vape-store-clerks-jailed-facing-federal-drug-charges) on October 1, 2026. The products at issue are "Smax" Pseudo-DHM tablets, a manufactured product whose label lists the synthetic compounds MGM-15 and pseudoindoxyl along with 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), according to court documents cited by Fox and WMC Action News 5. Whether the 7-OH in the tablets is a concentrated or synthetic form, as opposed to the trace amount found in natural leaf, has not been reported. Cause of death for both students has not been determined, and toxicology was still pending as of the affidavit date, Fox reported.

The new reporting adds three things to the record KNT has built since [the deaths were first reported September 23][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests]: a federal affidavit, a day-by-day purchase timeline from the court documents, and the first public comments from the owner of the Water Valley store the DEA searched.

## The affidavit and the charges

Bank records linked Hamilton to purchases at Puffs Vape Tobacco & Beer in Oxford and Strang to Express Mart #7 in Water Valley, in Yalobusha County, according to the DEA affidavit as described by Fox. Authorities also describe undercover purchases and the seizure of more than 500 suspected kratom-related products from the Oxford store. "Yahya Mohamed Mosleh and Islam Yahya Mosleh are each charged with possession with intent to distribute a controlled substance, the documents show," Fox reported.

Two points in Fox's account do not line up with earlier coverage. First, Fox's headline describes the two as "facing federal drug charges," and the story attributes its facts to a DEA affidavit, while WMC reported last week that the two men face three state counts each of possession of a Schedule I controlled substance with intent to distribute, [as KNT reported][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc]. Fox's article text does not name a federal court or a U.S. Attorney's office, and KNT could not confirm from the coverage whether federal charges have been filed in addition to the state charges. Second, Fox identifies the second defendant as Islam Yahya Mosleh; WMC and other outlets have reported the name as Islam Mohamed, 18. A [separate Fox article by Adam Sabes](https://www.foxnews.com/us/ole-miss-students-died-near-drug-listing-newly-illegal-ingredient-ex-prosecutor-says-selling-unlawful.amp) the same day used the name Islam Mohamed and reported that both men were held on $100,000 bonds.

Sabes' article quoted Neama Rahmani, a former federal prosecutor, on the legal exposure created by the DEA's August 26 order. "It would be unlawful for anyone, whether it's a drug dealer or a gas station convenience store owner, to sell MGM-15 after it was categorized as Schedule I," Rahmani said. "Now, to be prosecuted, the Department of Justice would have to prove intent." He added that "there's no doubt that Modus and any company that distributed this drug can face civil, even criminal, liability." Fox identified Modus as the brand behind Smax, with offices in Florida and California. Modus has not commented in any coverage reviewed by KNT. Fox's reporting describes the DEA order as covering mitragynine pseudoindoxyl; the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit's September 30 statement listed the order as covering mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, and said it did not reach botanical kratom.

Sabes also reported that court documents said Hamilton "could have died due to a Pulmonary Edema" and that Strang was found "cold to the touch." Neither is a cause-of-death finding.

## What the Water Valley store owner said

Ibrahim Aydah, who owns Express Mart #7, told [Fox News' Adam Sabes](https://noticias.foxnews.com/us/pills-found-near-dead-ole-miss-student-didnt-come-gas-station-feds-raid-store-owner-says) that DEA agents arrived at his store on a Friday in late September with what he estimated at 15 unmarked cars. "I was home and stopped by. I opened the office," he said. "They searched everything. They took it all." The agents seized all of the store's kratom products, including items whose labels listed pseudoindoxyl, according to Fox, and described the search as part of an ongoing investigation.

Aydah disputed that the Smax package shown in news coverage came from his store. "The package that I see in the news was not from my store," he said. "The Smax costs 200. I never sell 200. And it carries different ingredients than mine." He said he wished the federal government had given notice that the products were unlawful, according to Fox. "Yes, they took all my stuff. I lost everything, and it's the law," he said. And: "I'm a businessman. I'm not a drug dealer." Fox did not report any charge against Aydah. His account that the Smax product differs from what he stocked sits against the DEA affidavit's statement, as described by Fox, that bank records placed Strang at his store and that similar products were found at the death scene. The coverage does not resolve the difference.

Water Valley sits outside the Oxford and Lafayette County local bans, and kratom sales to adults 21 and over are legal there under Mississippi's 2025 Kratom Consumer Protection Act, [WATN noted](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/da-mulroy-warns-sale-purchase-165148317.html) in a report on Tennessee enforcement that [KNT covers separately today][shelby-county-da-mulroy-west-tennessee-task-force-novelty-king-memphis-30000-packages-synthetic-kratom-klaz-cats-claw-tipton-county-4700-pills]. The 2025 law also banned synthetic kratom extracts, according to WMC.

## The court-document timeline

[WMC's Joyce Peterson and Lydian Coombs](https://www.kltv.com/2026/10/01/court-documents-detail-hours-leading-up-2-ole-miss-students-suspected-overdose-deaths/) published a timeline drawn from the court documents on October 1. Hamilton made purchases at Puffs on September 9, September 14, and September 20 and was found dead in his Stewart Hall dorm room on September 21. Strang visited Express Mart #7 on September 9 and September 11, took a Xanax bar at about 4 p.m. on September 20, was last seen in his kitchen around midnight, and was found in his bedroom at 3:30 p.m. on September 22, according to the station's reading of the documents. Investigators also recovered 20 to 30 nitrous oxide canisters at Strang's residence and marijuana from pipes and vape pens belonging to Hamilton, WMC reported, and THC cartridges, gummies, and pre-rolls at the Puffs store.

WMC reported that agents described the Smax product as having "significantly higher potency compared to natural leaf products," and that the label lists pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and 7-hydroxymitragynine. [The affidavit KNT reviewed last week][lafayette-county-affidavit-smax-pseudo-dhm-tablets-both-ole-miss-scenes-7oh-preliminary-mgm-15-untested-aka-students-should-have-been-warned] said preliminary testing found 7-OH in some pills and that the lab could not test for MGM-15. WMC's October 1 account, like its September 30 report, does not say whether new laboratory results have come back. "Toxicology tests are incomplete, and the autopsies on both young men are still pending," the station reported, adding that results were expected as soon as this week.

The presence of Xanax and nitrous oxide in the timeline is new to public reporting on Strang's death. No outlet has reported what role, if any, those substances played, and the medical examiner has not issued findings.

## The AKA's letter to Fitch

The American Kratom Association (AKA) [asked Attorney General Lynn Fitch on October 1](https://finance.yahoo.com/healthcare/articles/american-kratom-association-urges-mississippi-035200996.html) to "amend her call for a statewide ban on all kratom and instead support legislation specifically prohibiting dangerous synthetic and chemically manipulated opioid products," a day after Fitch said on NewsNation that her office would push to ban kratom "in all forms." The release names mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 as the compounds it wants targeted and describes Smax as a chemically manipulated product.

"We strongly support the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit's call for immediate legislative action against these dangerous synthetic opioid products," said Mac Haddow, the AKA's senior fellow on public policy, according to the release. "Mississippi should separate the natural botanical from the synthetic opioids." The release says the AKA sent Fitch a letter and a recording of its September 30 Oxford press conference. Fitch's office had not responded publicly in coverage reviewed by KNT. The AKA and the Global Kratom Coalition both support banning the synthetic compounds named in the DEA order while opposing a ban on natural leaf, a position that is now set against the attorney general's.
