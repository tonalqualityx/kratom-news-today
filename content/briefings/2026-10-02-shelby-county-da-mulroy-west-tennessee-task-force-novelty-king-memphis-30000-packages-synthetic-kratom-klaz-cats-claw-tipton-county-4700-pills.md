Shelby County District Attorney Steve Mulroy and the West Tennessee Violent Crime and Drug Task Force seized nearly 30,000 packages of kratom products from Novelty King, a wholesaler at 3090 Broad Street in Memphis, and from a second location in Collierville, [the district attorney's office announced](https://www.scdag.com/news-releases/da-steve-mulroy-west-tn-violent-crime-and-drug-task-force-announce-major-kratom-enforcement-operation) on October 1, 2026. The products were marketed under names including "KLAZ," "Botanical Extract," and "Cat's Claw," according to the release. Task force director Johnie Carter described the seized inventory as chemically manipulated synthetic kratom rather than leaf, though the release does not report laboratory results identifying the alkaloids involved, and the Tennessee statute it cites prohibits natural leaf kratom as well.

The operation is the largest single kratom seizure reported in Tennessee since [Matthew Davenport's Law took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], based on the enforcement actions KNT has tracked. A Tipton County seizure of more than 4,700 pills from five businesses, reported by WMC Action News 5 the same week, and a new NBC News report on poison-center calls tied to products sold as "cat's claw" round out a week in which the "cat's claw" label kept surfacing in enforcement and public-health reporting.

## What the Shelby County office said

"Tennessee law has changed, and retailers need to understand what that means," Mulroy said in the release. "We are particularly concerned about synthetic and chemically manipulated kratom products being sold in gas stations, vape shops and convenience stores." The release lists the participating agencies as the West Tennessee Violent Crime and Drug Task Force, the 25th and 26th Judicial District drug task forces, the Tennessee Dangerous Drugs Task Force, the Tennessee Alcoholic Beverage Commission, the Drug Enforcement Administration's Memphis office, and the Tennessee Counterdrug Task Force.

Carter was more pointed. "I want to be clear that what we are dealing with is synthetic kratom," he said, according to the release. "It has been chemically manipulated, it is accompanied by fake lab tests, and it is deadly." [WATN reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/da-mulroy-warns-sale-purchase-165148317.html) that Carter added a warning to retailers: "If you have it, destroy it, because if you sell it and are caught, you are going to jail." Mulroy told the station that "stores selling products prohibited under Tennessee law are now on notice." WATN described the haul as tens of thousands of packages, enough to require a semi-truck to remove.

The release sets out the penalty structure under Matthew Davenport's Law, Public Chapter 950: knowing possession is a Class A misdemeanor, knowing manufacture, delivery, sale, or possession with intent to sell is a Class C felony, and sales or deliveries to minors are a Class B felony. The release did not name anyone arrested or charged in connection with the Novelty King seizure, and it did not say whether the products were tested before the release was issued. Carter's reference to "fake lab tests" indicates the products were accompanied by certificates of analysis that officials dispute, but the release does not describe what those documents claimed.

The release's framing and the statute's reach differ in one respect worth noting. Officials emphasized that the targets were chemically manipulated products. The law, as [The Tennessean described it when it took effect][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one], prohibits kratom in all forms, natural and synthetic. A wholesaler holding natural leaf products in Tennessee would face the same statute.

## The Tipton County seizure

In Tipton County, directly north of Shelby County, deputies seized more than 4,700 suspected kratom pills from five businesses on September 28 and 29, [WMC Action News 5's Jacob Gallant reported](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/09/30/deputies-seize-thousands-suspected-kratom-pills-tipton-co-businesses/). The investigation began when a person was found unresponsive and given Narcan, and deputies traced the packages found at the scene back to local businesses, according to the station. The primary location was Gilt Edge Express at Holly Grove and Munford Gilt Edge roads. The pills ranged in labeled strength from 100 milligrams to 3,000 milligrams, WMC reported.

"The safety of the citizens of Tipton County will always be our first priority," Sheriff Shannon Beasley said, according to WMC. "When a call of an overdose leads our Investigators to a product being sold from a local business, we are going to follow that investigation wherever the evidence takes us." The station's report does not identify what the pills contained, does not describe them as synthetic or natural, and does not report arrests or charges. Whether the pills were a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product, a manufactured derivative such as MGM-15, or leaf-based kratom is not stated in the coverage.

The Shelby and Tipton operations follow a string of county-level actions since July: [Lincoln County's seizure from 21 businesses][lincoln-county-tennessee-sheriff-seizes-kratom-21-businesses] in September, in which Tennessee Bureau of Investigation testing found kratom in tablets labeled "Cat's Claw," and the [TBI's September warning][tennessee-tbi-warns-cats-claw-tablets-contain-mgm-15-kratom-18-lab-submissions] that "cat's claw" tablets submitted to its crime lab contained MGM-15, a compound the DEA placed in Schedule I in August.

## "Cat's claw" beyond Tennessee

The "Cat's Claw" marketing name on the Novelty King inventory matches a pattern poison centers in other states have described. [NBC News' Claretta Bellamy reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/health/health-news/kratom-related-compounds-supplements-cats-claw-rcna600754) on October 1 that calls began reaching the Utah Poison Control Center in March from people who had taken a supplement sold as cat's claw and were reporting symptoms that resembled an opioid overdose. Cat's claw, Uncaria tomentosa, is a South American vine unrelated to Mitragyna speciosa, and NBC characterized the supplement as usually harmless on its own. The portion of the NBC report available to KNT did not include case counts or laboratory results, and KNT could not confirm which compounds Utah investigators identified.

[Virginia's Blue Ridge Poison Center reported a similar pattern in September][virginia-blue-ridge-poison-center-unlabeled-kratom-cats-claw-products], with director Chris Holstege describing unlabeled kratom turning up in cat's claw products as a "repetitive problem." The Tennessee, Virginia, and Utah accounts each involve products whose labels did not disclose kratom alkaloids, which is a different compliance problem from the one Carter described in Memphis, where products were sold as kratom with documentation officials called fake.

Tennessee's enforcement posture also figured in coverage of the Ole Miss investigation across the state line. WATN noted that kratom remains legal statewide in Mississippi for adults 21 and over under that state's 2025 Kratom Consumer Protection Act, while Lafayette County and the city of Oxford have local bans and Water Valley, where one of the two students bought tablets, does not. [KNT reports separately today][ole-miss-dea-affidavit-mosleh-charges-express-mart-water-valley-owner-aydah-they-took-it-all-wmc-timeline-rahmani-aka-letter-fitch] on the DEA's search of the Water Valley store.
