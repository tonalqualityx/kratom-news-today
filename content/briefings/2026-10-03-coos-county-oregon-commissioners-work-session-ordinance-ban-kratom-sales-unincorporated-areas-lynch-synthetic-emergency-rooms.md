Coos County, Oregon is weighing an ordinance that would ban kratom sales in the county's unincorporated areas, and the county's public health director told [KCBY's Sadie Rison, in a report published October 1, 2026](https://kcby.com/news/local/coos-co-health-officials-weigh-potential-ban-on-kratom-sales), that the products driving the proposal are synthetic, high-potency 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, which he distinguished from the original botanical plant. The [county's calendar](https://www.co.coos.or.us/calendar) lists a Board of Commissioners work session at 1:30 p.m. on September 30 on "Proposed Ordinances to Ban Kratom & Regulate Nitrous Oxide Sales." No ordinance number or vote date appears in the listing or in KCBY's report.

## What the county is considering

The proposal under discussion would ban kratom sales in unincorporated Coos County, KCBY reported. A companion ordinance would tighten rules on nitrous oxide sales. KCBY reported that a county ordinance would reach only unincorporated areas and that cities within the county would have to adopt their own regulations for a ban to apply inside their limits.

Dr. Timothy Lynch, public health director at Coos Health & Wellness, told KCBY the county has been working on the proposal for six to eight months.

## The health director's stated target

Lynch's comments to KCBY focused on products that have been chemically altered. "What they're doing is they're taking the original botanical plant and they're synthesizing that plant," he said. "We are seeing in our emergency rooms where people are using the synthetic mostly."

The Drug Enforcement Administration drew a related line in its July proposal to temporarily schedule 7-OH products, which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered when it was published][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products]. According to [Reuters' report on that proposal](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/dea-moves-place-some-strong-kratom-related-products-under-strict-federal-drug-2026-07-01/), the agency set a potency threshold aimed at concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and said natural leaf kratom, in which it described 7-OH as present only in trace amounts, falls outside the rule. KCBY's report did not include counts of emergency room visits or identify specific products, and it did not say whether the draft ordinance's definition of kratom would distinguish natural leaf from the synthesized products Lynch named or would cover both.

KCBY's report did not include comment from kratom trade groups. The American Kratom Association's position on the broader question is on record: in July the association [endorsed the DEA's scheduling of concentrated 7-OH products][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action], calling them "opioids, not kratom," while saying it opposes restrictions on natural leaf. Whether the association or any other group has weighed in on the Coos County proposal specifically was not reported.

## What comes next

KCBY reported that the commissioners would need to act on the proposal and that individual cities would determine their own rules. The report did not give a date for a first reading or a public hearing.
