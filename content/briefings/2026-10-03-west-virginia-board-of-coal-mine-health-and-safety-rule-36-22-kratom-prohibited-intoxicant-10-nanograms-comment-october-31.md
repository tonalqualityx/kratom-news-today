West Virginia's coal mine safety regulators have opened a public comment period on a proposed rule change that would name kratom as a prohibited intoxicant at the state's mines, [WV News editor Charles Young reported on October 2, 2026](https://www.wvnews.com/news/wvnews/kratom-could-be-banned-at-west-virginia-coal-mines-under-proposed-rule/article_611549ed-8f13-4660-b5e2-4b2050783768.html). The West Virginia Board of Coal Mine Health and Safety filed the proposed amendments to Rule 36-22 on September 30, according to the report and the [pending-rule record at the West Virginia Secretary of State's office](https://apps.sos.wv.gov/adlaw/csr/ruleview.aspx?document=18613). WV News described the rule as governing acts that endanger mine security and setting protocols for searches for intoxicants and matches.

The proposal would impose a workplace restriction separate from the state's consumer market, WV News reported. The paper noted that West Virginia has permitted regulated kratom sales to adults 21 and older since 2023.

## What the amendment would do

Under the proposed changes, kratom would be added to the definition of prohibited intoxicants, WV News reported. The paper said the proposed definition describes kratom as a psychoactive substance derived from the leaves of the Mitragyna speciosa tree, "including concentrated or synthetically manipulated products," and that miners would be prohibited from carrying the substance into a mine or working under its influence.

The proposed guidelines set a testing standard, according to WV News: a positive urine test above 10 nanograms per milliliter would constitute "reasonable cause" for a mine operator to deny an individual entry or remove them from a mine work area. The report said miners denied entry or removed under the provision would be entitled to a timely medical examination arranged and funded by the operator, with participation rights for designated miner representatives. The report did not say which alkaloid the threshold measures. The proposed text and the accompanying memo, posted by the state mine safety office as scanned PDFs, could not be read for this briefing.

The Secretary of State's record lists the rule as legislative exempt, filed under sections 22A-6-4 and 22A-6-5 of the state code, with a comment deadline of October 31, 2026. WV News reported that written comments can be submitted to John F. Willems at the West Virginia Attorney General's Office.

## The agency's stated reason

The Office of Miners' Health, Safety and Training said in an [October 1 industry notice](https://minesafety.wv.gov/laws-regulations-industry-notices/industry-notices/) that kratom use can produce stimulant and opioid effects, along with potential psychotic symptoms such as hallucinations and confusion, posing a significant safety hazard in underground and surface mining environments, according to WV News. The notice is posted alongside the proposed amendment on the office's industry notices page.

The proposed definition, as WV News described it, places concentrated and synthetically manipulated products in a single category with leaf kratom. The Drug Enforcement Administration took a different approach in its July proposal to temporarily schedule 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered when it was published][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products]. According to [Reuters' report on that proposal](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/dea-moves-place-some-strong-kratom-related-products-under-strict-federal-drug-2026-07-01/), the agency set a potency threshold aimed at concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and said natural leaf kratom, in which it described 7-OH as present only in trace amounts, falls outside the rule. The American Kratom Association, which supported West Virginia's 2023 law, [endorsed the DEA's 7-OH scheduling action in July][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action] while stating that it opposes restrictions on natural leaf, KNT reported at the time. The WV News report does not indicate whether the Board considered that distinction, and neither the report nor the Secretary of State's record cites incident data from West Virginia mines.

## West Virginia's 2023 kratom law

West Virginia's existing kratom framework dates to Senate Bill 220, the Industrial Hemp Development Act, which included the Select Plant-Derived Regulation Act: Kratom and took effect June 9, 2023, WV News reported. The report said that law made West Virginia the eighth state at the time to enact kratom-specific regulations, created permits for businesses that manufacture, process, distribute, or sell kratom, and authorized the state Department of Agriculture to set sampling, testing, manufacturing, and labeling requirements, including a warning to keep products away from children and to consult a physician before use if pregnant or taking medication.

The 2023 law was supported by the American Kratom Association, which said at the time that regulation would let consumers access registered and tested products, according to WV News. Then-Gov. Jim Justice said the law would give the state the ability to monitor and regulate products that had been sold without age restrictions, the paper reported. In a September 30 STAT piece KNT covered this week, Shatterproof's Leah Kaufman described [Utah and West Virginia as having the best-regulated kratom marketplaces in the country][stat-gaffney-experts-agree-kratom-7oh-need-regulation-split-on-how-herring-make-it-illegal-dasgupta-threshold-volkow-mitragynine-haddow].

Workplace drug-testing policy has come up in KNT's coverage once before this year. In July, Tennessee's Bureau of Workers' Compensation said in a legislative update that the [state's kratom prohibition extends to workplace drug-testing policies][tennessee-workers-comp-kratom-drug-testing].
