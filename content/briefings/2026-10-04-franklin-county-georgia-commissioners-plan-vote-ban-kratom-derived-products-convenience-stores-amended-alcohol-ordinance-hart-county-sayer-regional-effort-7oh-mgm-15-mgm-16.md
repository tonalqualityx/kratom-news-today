Franklin County, Georgia's Board of Commissioners is preparing to vote on a ban on kratom-derived products in convenience stores, [WLHR's MJ Kneiser reported on October 1, 2026](https://921wlhr.com/franklin-county-boc-working-to-ban-the-sale-of-kratom-derived-products/). The products the board has in view are concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and the synthetic compounds MGM-15 and MGM-16, according to the report, which described them as sold in gummies, pills, liquid shots, edibles, and injectable form. WLHR did not say whether the draft would reach natural leaf kratom, in which 7-OH occurs only in trace amounts.

## What the board agreed to

At a work session on Tuesday, September 29, commissioners agreed to take the matter up again at their Monday meeting and directed County Attorney Ken Jarrard to draft an amended alcohol ordinance for review and a potential vote, WLHR reported. The station's account did not explain why the alcohol ordinance was chosen as the vehicle.

Commissioner Corey Pulliam proposed following the approach taken by neighboring Hart County, according to WLHR. Commissioner Josh Smith supported the ban and cited his experience as an emergency medical technician. Commission Chair Courtney Long and Smith have consulted with Hart County Commission Chair Marshall Sayer, who WLHR said is leading a regional effort to ban kratom-derived products across Northeast Georgia. The report did not list the other counties involved or say how far Hart County's own measure has progressed.

## The Attorney General's statement WLHR cited

WLHR attributed its description of the products' risks to Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr, who it said has stated that the Food and Drug Administration determined "concentrated forms of 7-OH are 13 times more potent than morphine" and that the products may cause seizures, substance use disorder, withdrawal, overdose, and death. The FDA made that potency comparison in July when it recommended scheduling concentrated 7-OH products, and the Drug Enforcement Administration [proposed a potency-threshold rule][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products] in response, as Kratom News Today (KNT) reported at the time; the proposal remains unfinalized. In that July announcement, as KNT reported, the FDA said its concern was concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and that natural leaf kratom was outside its target.

WLHR reported that the U.S. Department of Justice placed MGM-15 and MGM-16 under emergency federal Schedule I control in August but that the compounds remain legally sold in Georgia. The DEA's August 26 order, [as KNT reported][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], covered mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 for two years. The DEA said in that order that manufacture and distribution of the three compounds became federal offenses on its effective date, as KNT reported. WLHR did not elaborate on its statement that the compounds are "still legally sold in Georgia," which appears to refer to the absence of a matching state or local prohibition.

## Where Franklin County fits in Georgia

The county's move comes as Georgia localities and legislators have been working on the same question from different directions. In September, [Fulton County commissioners backed a resolution][fulton-county-georgia-commissioners-back-resolution-synthetic-kratom-ban-natural-kratom-behind-pharmacy-counters] calling for a state ban on synthetic kratom while keeping natural kratom behind pharmacy counters, and the [Georgia House study committee on kratom held a hearing in Athens][georgia-house-study-committee-kratom-hearing-university-of-georgia-athens-townsend-enforcement] focused on enforcement. Georgia regulates leaf kratom statewide under its Kratom Consumer Protection Act rather than banning it, as KNT has reported in its study committee coverage.

WLHR's report did not include comment from kratom retailers or trade groups. The American Kratom Association's stated position, as reported elsewhere this week, is that MGM-15, MGM-16, and concentrated 7-OH products are synthetic opioids that should be enforced against while natural leaf remains legal, which places the association closer to the Franklin County draft's apparent scope than to a broad ban; whether the association has engaged with Franklin or Hart County was not reported.

WLHR reported that the board expects to take the measure up at its Monday meeting, October 5.
