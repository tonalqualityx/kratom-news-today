Oxford, Mississippi police revived a 22-year-old found unresponsive at a Sweetgum Lane residence early on October 1, 2026 and said the person had "synthetic kratom products" in their possession, according to [WJTV's Kaitlin Howell](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/oxford-police-respond-overdose-involving-151527347.html) and the [Mississippi News Group's Jon Myers](https://msnewsgroup.com/crime/oxford-police-investigate-suspected-overdose-involving-synthetic-kratom-products-2/). The department described the products only as synthetic and manufactured, did not name a brand or a compound, and said there is "currently no indication the products were purchased in the Oxford area." The reports do not say whether the products were concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, one of the manufactured compounds the Drug Enforcement Administration scheduled in August, or something else, and nothing in either report ties the incident to natural leaf kratom, in which 7-OH occurs only in trace amounts.

The call came ten days after University of Mississippi students Aidan Hamilton and Robert Strang [were found dead at separate locations][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link] with packaged "Smax" tablets nearby. Police told both outlets the Sweetgum Lane case is unrelated to those death investigations.

## What police said about the October 1 call

Officers responded around 5 a.m. Thursday and found the 22-year-old unresponsive, the Mississippi News Group reported. Emergency medical personnel revived the person, who was alert and oriented while being transported by ambulance for further care and is expected to recover, according to the department's account as carried by WJTV. Police said the person is not a university student.

The Oxford Police Department is working the case with the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit, both outlets reported. In its statement, the department said that "synthetic and manufactured kratom products can contain powerful and unpredictable substances and pose serious, potentially deadly dangers," per the Mississippi News Group, and it urged anyone who witnesses a suspected overdose to call 911.

WJTV's report recalled that the narcotics unit's September warning after the student deaths said "kratom packaging was discovered at both locations" and was issued "out of an abundance of caution," language the unit has since [described as not a declaration that kratom or any specific substance caused either death][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc].

## The AP's account of the DEA affidavit

The Associated Press's Sudhin Thanawala reported on October 2 that federal prosecutors have charged Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, and his son Islam Yahya Mosleh, 18, each with one count of possession with intent to distribute a controlled substance, in a [story carried by PBS NewsHour](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/federal-prosecutors-charge-shop-clerk-and-son-with-selling-kratom-thought-to-be-tied-to-ole-miss-death). Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported the charges on October 2][ole-miss-dea-affidavit-mosleh-charges-express-mart-water-valley-owner-aydah-they-took-it-all-wmc-timeline-rahmani-aka-letter-fitch] from Fox News and WMC accounts that disagreed on whether the charges were state or federal; the AP describes them as federal and names the defendants' attorneys as Scott Davis and A.E. Harlow Jr. Fox News had identified the younger defendant as Islam Mohamed.

According to the affidavit of DEA Special Agent Michael Dassance as described by the AP, bank records showed Hamilton made multiple purchases in September at a vape, tobacco, and beer store in Oxford. On September 24, a confidential informant bought kratom-based pills from the elder Mosleh, who, the affidavit says, advised the buyer to "be careful taking the pills because people were dying from them." The affidavit also says Mosleh concealed kratom pills when other customers entered the store, the AP reported. A version of the AP story [carried by Hawaii News Now](https://www.hawaiinewsnow.com/2026/10/03/shop-clerk-son-charged-with-selling-illegal-kratom-amid-probe-into-ole-miss-student-deaths/) says the informant asked whether the store sold "7-OH kratom-based pills" and that police seized about 500 units of kratom and kratom-like products in a search of the store, a figure that matches [the Puffs raid KNT covered on September 26][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests].

The AP reported that Smax products found in Hamilton's room contained one of the three kratom-related compounds the DEA [placed in Schedule I on August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], that Smax packages were found in both students' rooms, and that Strang bought from a different store. The AP did not identify which of the three compounds was found. The [Lafayette County affidavit KNT reported on September 29][lafayette-county-affidavit-smax-pseudo-dhm-tablets-both-ole-miss-scenes-7oh-preliminary-mgm-15-untested-aka-students-should-have-been-warned] said the Smax Pseudo-DHM packaging listed MGM-15 and pseudoindoxyl, that preliminary testing found 7-OH in some pills, and that the lab could not test for MGM-15. None of the reporting to date says whether the 7-OH detected was the trace amount natural to leaf or a concentrated or synthetic form.

## What remains open

Toxicology reports for both students are being expedited, the AP reported, and "it is still not clear whether those substances contributed to either death," although federal prosecutors said both deaths "appear consistent with drug overdoses." Lafayette County Metro Narcotics told the AP the investigation remains active and additional charges are possible.

The American Kratom Association's position on the Oxford products is on record from earlier in the week: its senior fellow Mac Haddow has called the Smax tablets a federally controlled synthetic opioid rather than kratom, and the association has asked Attorney General Lynn Fitch to narrow her proposed ban to synthetic and chemically manipulated products, as KNT reported. Neither the association nor any other trade group had commented on the Sweetgum Lane case in the reporting reviewed.
