The Virginia Poison Center has received more kratom-related reports in 2026 than it did in all of 2025, its director, Dr. John Downs, told [WWBT's Oliver Sabo in a report published October 2, 2026](https://www.12onyourside.com/2026/10/03/ole-miss-deaths-spotlight-concerns-over-synthetic-kratom-products/). Downs, whose center is based at Virginia Commonwealth University, framed the concern around synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which WWBT described as concentrated versions of a compound that occurs naturally in kratom leaf only in minimal amounts. The station did not publish the center's case counts for either year, and the report does not say how many of the reports involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom.

## What Downs told the station

Downs told WWBT that synthetic 7-OH products are substantially more potent than leaf kratom. "You're placing people at greater risk of having toxicity like you would have from morphine or heroin or fentanyl," he said. He also addressed the products' regulatory status: "This isn't something that's gone through like a review process, even for an over-the-counter type medication."

On dependence, Downs described escalation among people who have contacted the center. "I've heard folks describe, hey, I was using initially just a tab a day and then it went to dozens of tabs a day," he said, according to WWBT.

The station's report follows a September warning from the Blue Ridge Poison Center at the University of Virginia about [unlabeled kratom and "cat's claw" products][virginia-blue-ridge-poison-center-unlabeled-kratom-cats-claw-products], which Kratom News Today (KNT) covered on September 20. The Blue Ridge center is separate from the Richmond-based Virginia Poison Center, and WWBT's report concerns only the latter's figures.

## Virginia's July restrictions

WWBT reported that products predominantly containing 7-OH have been restricted in Virginia since July. The restriction comes from the state law that [took effect July 1][virginia-kratom-restrictions-take-effect-july-one], which, as KNT reported at the time, limits kratom sales to adults 21 and over, requires labeling and behind-the-counter or locked-display storage, bans synthetic and semisynthetic kratom compounds, and caps 7-OH content in kratom products. Natural leaf kratom remains legal for adults in the state under that framework, WWBT reported.

## The AKA's framing of the Ole Miss product

WWBT's report was pegged to the deaths of two University of Mississippi students, near whom investigators found "Smax" Pseudo-DHM tablets whose packaging listed the compounds MGM-15 and pseudoindoxyl. Mac Haddow, senior fellow at the American Kratom Association, told the station: "What that product is is a federally controlled, as of the third week in August, synthetic opioid that is illegal federally." The Drug Enforcement Administration [placed mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I on August 26][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] on an emergency basis.

The association's stated position, which Haddow has repeated through the week, is that manufactured compounds of that kind should be enforced against as synthetic opioids while natural leaf kratom stays legal, and the association [endorsed the DEA's 7-OH scheduling proposal in July][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action] on those terms, as KNT reported. WWBT quoted Downs and Haddow in the same report; both described the manufactured products as far more potent than leaf, and Haddow's added point was that they should not be called kratom. In Oxford, police said on October 1 that a 22-year-old was revived after a suspected overdose with synthetic kratom products in their possession, a case [KNT reports separately today][oxford-police-22-year-old-revived-sweetgum-lane-synthetic-kratom-not-purchased-locally-unrelated-ole-miss-deaths-ap-affidavit-mosleh-informant-people-were-dying].

Local governments elsewhere are moving on the same category: [Franklin County, Georgia commissioners plan a vote on banning kratom-derived products from convenience stores][franklin-county-georgia-commissioners-plan-vote-ban-kratom-derived-products-convenience-stores-amended-alcohol-ordinance-hart-county-sayer-regional-effort-7oh-mgm-15-mgm-16], KNT also reports today.
