Utah health officials warned on October 5, 2026 that four deaths in the state have been linked to tablets sold as "cat's claw" that laboratory testing found contain mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) and dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine (MGM-15), two lab-made compounds derived from kratom alkaloids that are distinct from natural kratom leaf and from the trace 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) that occurs in it, [KSL reported](https://www.ksl.com/article/51632638/utah-health-dept-issues-warning-after-4-deaths-linked-to-certain-herbal-supplements). The warning came from the Utah Department of Health and Human Services, the Utah Poison Control Center, and the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food, according to KSL, and it named products sold under the "Buzzers" brand and labeled as cat's claw or Uncaria tomentosa, a South American vine that is a different plant from kratom.

Dr. Michael Moss, medical director of the Utah Poison Control Center, told [FOX 13](https://www.fox13now.com/news/local-news/salt-lake-city/health-authorities-warn-against-kratom-related-products-after-four-deaths) that "we've confirmed that there are deaths caused by these products." Megan Broekemeier, a forensic epidemiologist with the Department of Health and Human Services, described the toll as four deaths over four months, per the same report. None of the coverage available for this briefing identifies the counties where the deaths occurred or gives the dates of the individual cases.

## The same products as the July warning

The October warning follows a [July 30 alert][utah-death-mislabeled-synthetic-kratom-tablets] from the Department of Agriculture and Food and the Poison Control Center that tied one death and several severe health incidents, including overdoses, to Buzzers-brand cat's claw tablets. That [July release](https://ag.utah.gov/news/illegal-kratom-product-sold-in-utah-county-linked-to-death-in-utah/) described the same two compounds as "opioid-acting compounds derived from the kratom plant" and as Schedule I drugs under Utah law. A separate [FOX 13 report](https://www.fox13now.com/news/local-news/4-new-utah-deaths-linked-to-illegal-cats-claw-kratom-products) by Jeff Tavss characterized the October count as four new deaths, two months after the earlier warning about the same products. Whether the four deaths include the one reported in July or come in addition to it is not stated consistently across the coverage; KSL, KUTV, and ABC4 refer to four deaths without specifying.

According to [KUTV](https://kutv.com/news/local/health-officials-issue-urgent-warning-after-four-utah-deaths-tied-to-herbal-supplements), the agencies said the products have been sold at smoke shops, gas stations, and online, and that "the labels often claim the product is legal and free of kratom, yet warn that it is for 'adults only/21+' or produces strong psychoactive effects." Moss told FOX 13 that a label carrying a warning that the product may be addictive "should be a big red flag." KSL reported that the agencies asked the public to report suspected products to the Department of Agriculture and Food by email.

## What officials said about the compounds

The agencies described MGPI and MGM-15 as chemicals that act as opioids and carry risks of addiction, dangerous withdrawal, overdose, and death, according to FOX 13 and KUTV. KSL and [ABC4](https://www.abc4.com/news/health/health-warning-cats-claw-kratom-chemicals/) reported that officials said naloxone is effective for reversing an overdose involving these substances. MGM-15 is a chemical name for dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, a derivative of 7-OH; both it and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl are manufactured rather than extracted from leaf, a distinction the Drug Enforcement Administration drew when it [placed both compounds, along with MGM-16, in federal Schedule I][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] on a temporary basis effective August 26, 2026. The Utah agencies' warning, as reported by KSL and ABC4, noted that federal classification.

The agencies also restated Utah's position on what may be sold. "Under Utah law, only pure, natural leaf kratom products may be legally sold," the warning said, per KUTV, and "any product containing kratom extracts or semi-synthetic chemicals is illegal." KSL quoted the agencies as adding that "although these substances are illegal to sell in Utah, consumers could find them in some retail stores." The Utah agencies' framing separates the natural leaf product from the synthetic compounds at issue; nothing in the coverage attributes any of the four deaths to natural leaf kratom.

## A pattern seen in other states

Utah is not the only state to find these compounds in products labeled as cat's claw. The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation [warned in September][tennessee-tbi-warns-cats-claw-tablets-contain-mgm-15-kratom-18-lab-submissions] that tablet packs sold as cat's claw were turning up in crime lab submissions with MGM-15 in them. The American Kratom Association, for its part, has [argued since August][aka-warns-pseudoindoxyl-scheduling-could-reach-natural-leaf] that the federal scheduling of mitragynine pseudoindoxyl could reach natural leaf if the compound is present in trace amounts, a position it has pressed in federal court; the association's reaction to the Utah warning was not reported in the coverage available for this briefing.

The Utah warning landed the same day the Pennsylvania House [passed a bill][pennsylvania-house-202-0-hb-2657-kosierowski-kratom-bill-amended-0-05-percent-7oh-dry-weight-no-extracts-12-percent-tax-kratom-regulatory-fund-haddow-restraint-senate-seven-days] whose synthetic-product definition would cover products containing mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above 10 parts per million or any synthetically derived kratom compound, while allowing registered sales of natural leaf to adults.
