The Anchorage Assembly's Public Safety Committee reviewed a draft ordinance on October 7, 2026 that would create misdemeanor penalties for selling or recreationally using concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products and other substances marketed as analogues of kratom, while leaving kratom in its "unaltered form" outside the ban, [Zachariah Hughes reported for the Anchorage Daily News](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/anchorage/2026/10/07/anchorage-assembly-to-consider-ban-on-kratom-and-related-products/). According to the Daily News, kratom leaf contains small levels of mitragynine and 7-OH, and the products drawing the Assembly's attention are over-the-counter substances containing increasingly high levels of 7-OH, a category distinct from natural leaf. The sponsor is Assembly member Janice Park, who represents Midtown, and the Daily News reported that formal introduction to the full Assembly is expected on October 20, 2026.

## How the draft is written

The draft defines the covered products by their marketing. According to the Daily News, it targets "packaging materials that market substances as analogues of kratom or 7-OH," an approach Park said draws on the template Anchorage used a decade ago against the synthetic cannabinoid products sold as K2 and Spice. The Daily News reported that the ordinance would not sanction use of kratom in its unaltered form and would not apply to use under a prescription from a licensed physician.

Under the draft, recreational use, possession, or sale would be a Class B misdemeanor, which the Daily News compared to disorderly conduct or shoplifting of goods worth less than $50 under municipal code. Sales to minors would be a Class A misdemeanor carrying stiffer penalties, per the report. The Daily News described the sales channels at issue as gas stations, convenience stores, and smoke and vape shops, and noted that the products are also sold online.

## What the Health Department told the committee

Katharine Stodgell, director of the Anchorage Health Department's public health division, told the committee that the products on local shelves have changed. "We didn't have the same concentration of 7-OH that we have now," Stodgell said, per the Daily News. "The product has become a lot stronger." According to data the department cited at the meeting, substances containing rising levels of 7-OH are being sold over the counter in the municipality with no regulation or quality control, the Daily News reported. The department's presentation described liver disease and seizures as hazards associated with long-term use, per the report. The Daily News did not specify the source of that characterization or state whether the hazards the department cited relate to concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products or to natural leaf kratom, and Kratom News Today (KNT) has not reviewed the department's presentation materials.

Stodgell also acknowledged what the municipality does not know. The Daily News reported that Anchorage lacks detailed local tracking of kratom use, emergency room visits, poison control calls, or product testing, and that no local counts of hospitalizations or deaths were presented. The Daily News report does not describe any Alaska statute governing kratom or 7-OH sales.

Park framed the measure around access and treatment. "I'm just very much interested in seeing that other people don't go down that road and have that experience," she said, per the Daily News. She told the committee that people "can delay getting treatment because they have rapid access to something that can at least stave off their withdrawal for a short period of time," according to the report.

## A second attempt in Anchorage

The draft is the Assembly's second run at kratom penalties in 18 months. In April 2025, Assembly member Randy Sulte sponsored AO 2025-50, which would have added recreational possession of nitrous oxide and kratom to the municipality's list of misdemeanor drugs, [Alaska's News Source reported](https://www.alaskasnewssource.com/2025/04/09/anchorage-assembly-discusses-new-illegal-drugs-tables-gun-legislation) at the time. [Must Read Alaska](https://mustreadalaska.com/anchorage-assembly-may-make-recreational-use-of-laughing-gas-poppers-kratom-illegal/) reported that the Sulte ordinance also covered amyl and butyl nitrite and carried the same Class B and Class A structure. That measure did not draw a distinction between natural leaf and concentrated or synthetic products, based on the two 2025 reports, and its final disposition is not described in the coverage available for this briefing. The Daily News did not report whether the Park draft has co-sponsors or how committee members responded.

## Other local and federal action

Other local governments have moved on kratom products in recent weeks. Coos County, Oregon commissioners [held a work session][coos-county-oregon-commissioners-work-session-ordinance-ban-kratom-sales-unincorporated-areas-lynch-synthetic-emergency-rooms] on September 30, 2026 on an ordinance to ban kratom sales in unincorporated areas, with the county's public health director pointing to synthesized products in emergency rooms, and Franklin County, Georgia commissioners [moved toward a vote][franklin-county-georgia-commissioners-plan-vote-ban-kratom-derived-products-convenience-stores-amended-alcohol-ordinance-hart-county-sayer-regional-effort-7oh-mgm-15-mgm-16] on removing kratom-derived products from convenience stores. At the federal level, the Daily News referenced the Justice Department's August emergency scheduling action, which the newspaper described as covering three concentrated 7-OH derivatives. The Justice Department's [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) identified the compounds as mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 and described them as kratom-related compounds manufactured and concentrated into opioid products, as KNT [reported at the time][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. The Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) separate [July notice of intent](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13580/schedules-of-controlled-substance-temporary-placement-of-7-hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified) proposes to schedule products containing more than 0.05% 7-OH by dry weight, per the notice's text, and a search of the Federal Register's document listings on October 8, 2026 showed no final order on that proposal.

No industry or advocacy group response to the Anchorage draft appeared in the coverage available to KNT as of October 8, 2026. The Daily News did not report a hearing date or the steps that would follow the October 20 introduction.
