Three Republican senators asked the Drug Enforcement Administration and the Food and Drug Administration on October 7, 2026 to finish the pending emergency scheduling of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and to go further by considering federal control of the whole kratom plant, according to a [letter posted by the office of Sen. Chuck Grassley](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/download/grassley-lee-collins-to-dea-fda_-7-oh/). Grassley, the Iowa Republican who chairs the Senate Judiciary Committee, signed the letter with Sen. Mike Lee of Utah and Sen. Susan Collins of Maine. It is addressed to DEA Administrator Terrance Cole and to Kyle Diamantas, whom the letter identifies as acting commissioner of the FDA, and it asks for a written reply by October 15.

"It is imperative that the final emergency scheduling order for 7-OH be expeditiously promulgated," the senators wrote. The order they refer to is the DEA's [July notice of intent](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13580/schedules-of-controlled-substance-temporary-placement-of-7-hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified) to temporarily place products containing more than 0.05% 7-OH by dry weight, or more than one milligram per unit, in Schedule I, which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered in July][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products]. According to that notice, the proposal targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations and does not cover the trace 7-OH that occurs in natural kratom leaf. The [comment period on that proposal closed September 10][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments], and the letter itself notes that the final temporary order has not been published.

## What the letter asks for

The letter opens by welcoming the DEA's 7-OH proposal and applauding the agency's [August order][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], [announced by the Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) on August 25, temporarily placing mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, three lab-made compounds derived from kratom alkaloids, in Schedule I. It then asks for broader action. "We encourage DEA, in consultation with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA), to consider scheduling the entire kratom plant, including all levels of 7-OH (not simply the threshold limit as stated in the Notice of Intent) and all other pharmacologically active alkaloids present in the plant," the senators wrote.

The letter closes with two formal requests. The first asks the DEA, in consultation with the FDA, "to initiate proceedings to permanently schedule all harmful substances found in kratom, including 7-OH, MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl." The second asks the FDA "to conduct the necessary scientific and medical evaluations of these substances and provide a permanent scheduling recommendation to the Drug Enforcement Administration for the full kratom plant, its derivatives, and all levels of the semi-synthetic analogues like mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 that are being marketed as natural products." The letter copies Russell Vought, director of the Office of Management and Budget.

The [press release](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-lee-collins-call-on-trump-administration-to-issue-emergency-scheduling-order-for-7-oh-initiate-permanent-scheduling-proceedings-for-kratom) that accompanied the letter summarized the asks as three items: immediately issue the emergency scheduling order for 7-OH, permanently place "all harmful substances found in kratom" in Schedule I, and "develop a permanent scheduling recommendation for the full kratom plant and its derivatives." Bloomberg Government's Skye Witley [reported the letter](https://news.bgov.com/health-law-and-business/gop-senators-urge-trump-to-expand-synthetic-kratom-crackdown) the same day under a headline describing it as a push to expand the synthetic kratom crackdown. KNT could not review the full Bloomberg Government article, which sits behind a paywall. [Ottumwa Radio](https://ottumwaradio.com/2026/10/grassley-lee-collins-call-on-trump-administration-to-issue-emergency-scheduling-order-for-7-oh-initiate-permanent-scheduling-proceedings-for-kratom/) in Iowa republished the release.

## The threshold the senators want removed

The senators' objection to the DEA's July design is explicit. "Even after the final publication of the temporary order scheduling 7-OH, 7-OH less than 0.05% (weight/weight, weight/volume, or volume/volume) or less than 1 mg (in the article) will remain easily accessible to the American public," the letter states. The DEA's July notice described the threshold as the line separating concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products from leaf, and it did not propose scheduling mitragynine or natural leaf.

The letter also describes the three compounds the DEA scheduled in August as a reason for broader action. "Kratom-derived concentrates, other alkaloids, and semi-synthetic analogues like mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 are making kratom-labeled products more dangerous than 7-OH with an increased abuse liability," the senators wrote, adding that "these semi-synthetic substances may be found in kratom-labeled products unbeknownst to the user." The letter cites the DEA's own August order for the statement that the three analogues are "pharmacologically similar to and more potent than, schedule II opioids like morphine."

Identifying themselves as supporters of the HALT Fentanyl Act, the senators wrote that they are "alarmed to see that an opioid known to be approximately 14 times more potent than morphine (when given orally) is currently not only legal, but entirely unregulated." The footnote attached to that sentence cites a [2014 paper in the Journal of Pharmacology and Experimental Therapeutics](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.113.208108) by Kenjiro Matsumoto and colleagues, whose title describes MGM-16 as a mitragynine derivative studied in mice. The accompanying press release states that kratom "is known to be approximately 14 times more potent than morphine when given orally."

## The numbers the letter relies on

The letter draws on three data sources. Citing a May 15, 2026 [report from the National Drug Early Warning System (NDEWS)](https://ndews.org/wordpress/files/2026/05/05.15.26_NDEWS-2026-7oh.pdf), the senators wrote that 3,672 suspected nonfatal overdose encounters with emergency medical services involving 7-OH, mitragynine, or kratom were recorded across 38 states from January 1, 2023 to April 30, 2026, with 2,007 of those in Southern states, 736 in Western states, 526 in the Midwest, and 403 in the Northeast. KNT has not independently reviewed the NDEWS report; the figures are as the letter presents them.

Citing a [2026 Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report article](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7511a1.htm) by Eleanor Blair Towers and colleagues at the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), the letter states that poison control centers received 14,449 kratom exposure reports between 2015 and 2025, that the 3,434 reports in 2025 were a record high, and that the 2025 figure represents an increase of about 1,200% over 2015. KNT [covered that CDC report][cdc-kratom-poison-center-calls-surge] when it was published. The letter also cites an [Iowa Department of Public Safety drug control strategy document](https://dps.iowa.gov/media/3134/download?inline) for the statement that the Iowa Poison Control Center logged 24 kratom exposure calls in 2023 and 2024 and 53 in 2025, an increase the senators put at roughly 120%.

The letter does not cite a death count. The NDEWS and poison center figures, as the letter presents them, are not broken out by natural leaf kratom versus concentrated 7-OH or synthetic analogue products. The senators' framing treats the plant and its derivatives together: "We worry the availability of these products is providing consumers with a false sense of security because they are readily available and marketed as 'natural.'"

## Where this sits against the advocacy positions

The request to schedule the whole plant separates the senators from the major kratom trade groups, which have supported federal action against concentrated and synthetic 7-OH while opposing control of natural leaf. The American Kratom Association (AKA) [endorsed the DEA's July 7-OH proposal][aka-endorses-dea-7oh-scheduling-action], with its senior fellow Mac Haddow calling concentrated 7-OH products "opioids, not kratom" in a [Pain News Network op-ed](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/7/6/banning-7-oh-opioids-will-save-lives), and in August the association [filed a federal lawsuit][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order] arguing that the DEA's three-compound order could sweep up natural kratom products unless clarified to exclude them, [Bloomberg Law reported](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/health-law-and-business/kratom-group-warns-government-ban-threatens-market-in-lawsuit). Global Kratom Coalition executive director Matthew Lowe told [Stateline](https://stateline.org/2026/10/05/kratom-7-oh-laws-diverge-amid-the-spread-of-potent-synthetic-products/) this month that ["we support what the feds are doing"][stateline-kratom-7oh-laws-diverge-36-states-rockefeller-14-ban-13-potency-limits-lowe-gkc-supports-feds-haddow-overblown-smith-hart], referring to the DEA's actions against synthetic products. Neither organization had published a response to the October 7 letter as of this briefing, based on the coverage available to KNT.

The letter is the second Senate letter this year that KNT has covered asking for scheduling beyond 7-OH. In May, Sens. Pete Ricketts and Richard Blumenthal led a [bipartisan letter][senators-push-full-kratom-scheduling-states-crack-down] urging the FDA to pursue scheduling for the whole kratom leaf. On the legislative side, Sen. Bernie Moreno's [S. 5383][moreno-bill-permanent-scheduling-synthetic-7oh] and Rep. Lance Gooden's [House version of the End Gas Station Heroin Act][gooden-house-bill-hr-10272-connors-end-gas-station-heroin-act-aka-endorsement] would permanently schedule synthetic 7-OH and the kratom-derived analogues while preserving specified naturally occurring concentrations, and the AKA announced its endorsement of the Gooden bill on September 26, per KNT's earlier coverage. The Grassley, Lee, and Collins letter does not mention either bill.

Grassley had posted on X on October 2 that "all Kratom is deadly" and that the administration "needs 2 put out final 7OH rule NOW," according to The Hill's Joseph Choi, and the letter restates the demand for the final order in more formal terms. Neither the DEA nor the FDA had publicly responded to the letter as of October 8, based on the coverage available to KNT, and the Federal Register showed no final temporary scheduling order for 7-OH as of the same date.
