Seminole County Sheriff Dennis Lemma is urging the Florida Legislature to outlaw kratom, [WKMG investigative reporter Troy Campbell reported](https://www.clickorlando.com/news/local/2026/10/09/kratom-faces-renewed-scrutiny-as-florida-law-enforcement-pushes-for-restrictions/) on October 9, 2026. The Orlando station's report is anchored by the death of Kristie Dijak, a 43-year-old from Williston, whose partner, Edward Valykeo, told the station her cause of death was a kratom 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) overdose. 7-OH occurs only in trace amounts in natural kratom leaf and is sold separately in concentrated tablets and shots. The WKMG report does not name the product Dijak used, does not say whether a medical examiner confirmed the cause of death, and does not specify whether a concentrated 7-OH product or natural leaf kratom was involved.

The request is notable because Florida has already acted on 7-OH. Attorney General James Uthmeier issued an emergency rule on August 15, 2025 prohibiting the sale and manufacture of 7-OH and adding it to the state's list of most dangerous drugs, [the News Service of Florida's Dara Kam reported](https://www.orlandoweekly.com/news/florida-ag-asks-judge-to-toss-out-challenge-to-kratom-7-oh-ban/) in Orlando Weekly. WKMG's report states that 7-OH is outlawed in Florida and that state law permits adults 21 and older to buy kratom products. Lemma's proposal, as the station described it, would reach the leaf products that the current rule leaves legal.

## What Lemma and the physician said

Lemma's argument, as quoted by WKMG, was about the limits of the assumption that retail shelves are safe by default. "The idea that surely the government and society would not allow something to sit on the shelf if it was going to do us harm, I think has been tested over and over again to be absolutely not true," he said. The station reported that Lemma is particularly concerned about high-dose forms of kratom and about the substance being combined with other drugs.

Dr. Charles Smith, a rehabilitation medicine physician interviewed by the station, used the phrase "gas station heroin" to describe the products and said, "This isn't bubble gum off the shelves." The station did not indicate whether Smith was referring to 7-OH products specifically or to kratom generally.

Valykeo described the effect of Dijak's death on the couple's 12-year-old daughter. "Seeing my daughter struggle with this. It just tears me up daily," he told WKMG.

## What the report does not include

The story carries one source for the central medical claim, the family, and WKMG did not report a medical examiner finding, toxicology results, or the identity of the product. The station also did not cite statistics on kratom-related deaths in Florida. Lemma's call follows [warnings last week from three Panhandle sheriffs][northwest-florida-sheriffs-aden-tifft-simmons-nine-kratom-related-overdose-deaths-district-1-medical-examiner-fdle-7oh-field-test], who cited a District 1 Medical Examiner count of nine kratom-related overdose deaths this year and pointed to concentrated gummies and oils with high 7-OH levels, as KNT reported on September 30. Uthmeier has [publicized the state's 7-OH enforcement][florida-ag-touts-7oh-enforcement-at-tampa-press-conference] since the emergency rule took effect.

No kratom advocacy organization or industry operator is quoted in the WKMG report. The distinction matters for reading Lemma's request: the American Kratom Association has supported federal action against concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while opposing bans on natural leaf kratom, a position its senior fellow Mac Haddow restated in [Stateline's survey of state laws][stateline-kratom-7oh-laws-diverge-36-states-rockefeller-14-ban-13-potency-limits-lowe-gkc-supports-feds-haddow-overblown-smith-hart] that KNT covered on October 5. A statewide ban of the kind Lemma described would reach leaf kratom. The station's report does not say whether Lemma has proposed bill language, identified a legislative sponsor, or set a timeline.
