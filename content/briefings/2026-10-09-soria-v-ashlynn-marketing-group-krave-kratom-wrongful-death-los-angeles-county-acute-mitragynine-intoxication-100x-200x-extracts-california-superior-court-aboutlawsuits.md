Mary Soria has sued Ashlynn Marketing Group Inc., the Santee, California company doing business as Krave Kratom, over the death of her son Martin Soria, [AboutLawsuits senior editor Michael Adams reported](https://www.aboutlawsuits.com/kratom-lawsuit/kratom-overdose-lawsuit-krave-products-fatal-mitragynine-intoxication/) on October 8, 2026. The complaint, filed September 30 in California Superior Court, says Martin Soria died in Los Angeles County on May 30, 2025 from acute mitragynine intoxication, according to the report. Mitragynine is the primary alkaloid in kratom leaf. The complaint also alleges the products contained dangerous and undisclosed levels of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and the products named include 100X and 200X extracts, which are concentrated preparations rather than plain leaf. The reporting does not say whether any of the products contained 7-OH added or concentrated beyond what extraction of leaf yields, and the listed cause of death is mitragynine rather than 7-OH.

The suit lands five months after the state of California went to court against the same company. The California Department of Public Health and Attorney General Rob Bonta filed a complaint in San Diego County Superior Court on May 1 [accusing Ashlynn of manufacturing and shipping kratom products in defiance of a state embargo][california-sues-kratom-manufacturer-litigation-risk-grows], as KNT reported at the time.

## What the complaint alleges

According to AboutLawsuits, Soria brings the case individually and as successor in interest to her son, who the complaint says regularly used several Krave products: KRX Extra Strength Gold, Krave 100X and 200X Kratom Extracts, Krave Botanics Maeng Da Kratom Extract, and Krave Kratom Extract capsules. The complaint alleges the company failed to warn about addictive potential, did not adequately disclose mitragynine and 7-OH levels, gave insufficient warnings about respiratory depression, sedation, and overdose risk, lacked ingredient transparency, and provided inadequate use instructions, per the report. It also alleges the products were marketed as "natural" and "botanical."

The report quotes one passage from the complaint directly: "KRAVE products executives knew that the Krave products' formula contained ingredients that were dangerous and that individually and in combination could cause acute mitragynine intoxication and addiction, like MARTIN SORIA suffered."

The causes of action are negligence, failure to warn, manufacturing and design defects, breach of implied warranty, and violations of California's unfair competition law, according to AboutLawsuits. Soria seeks wrongful-death and survival damages, funeral and burial expenses, punitive damages, corrective notices, and stronger warnings. The report does not name plaintiff's counsel or give a case number. These are allegations in a complaint, and the report does not indicate that the company has responded in court. AboutLawsuits spells the defendant "Ashlyn Marketing Group"; the state's filings use "Ashlynn."

## The state's parallel case

The state's complaint describes the same company from a regulatory angle. CDPH inspectors visited Ashlynn's Santee facility in May 2025, found kratom powders, capsules, liquids, and chewable tablets being manufactured and held for sale, and issued an embargo, [according to the department's May 1 release](https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/OPA/Pages/NR26-016.aspx). Follow-up visits in October 2025 and April 2026 found evidence that embargoed products had been moved, tampered with, and repackaged, and that manufacturing continued, CDPH said. "The firm's owner continues to manufacture kratom products and ships orders weekly," the release states.

CDPH's position, stated in the release, is that food and dietary supplements containing kratom or 7-OH are adulterated under the state's Sherman Food, Drug and Cosmetic Act and federal law, and are therefore illegal to manufacture or sell for consumption in California. The state's complaint seeks an order halting manufacture, condemnation and destruction of the embargoed inventory, civil penalties of up to $1,000 per day per violation, and enforcement costs. "7-OH and kratom-derived products have been associated with addiction, serious health harms, overdose and death," CDPH Director Dr. Erica Pan said in the release. The department said it has seized more than $5 million in kratom and 7-OH products statewide.

Martin Soria's death in May 2025 falls in the same month as the state's first inspection of the Santee facility. Neither the AboutLawsuits report nor the CDPH release connects the two events, and KNT has not reviewed the Soria complaint.

## Where this fits

The Soria case is the second kratom product-liability filing to surface this week. On October 7, KNT reported that a federal judge in Manhattan [declined to dismiss a consumer suit against Mitra-9 Brands][mitra-9-brands-southern-district-new-york-swain-denies-motion-dismiss-addiction-false-advertising-suit-bursor-fisher-vasquez-adexchanger-transparency] over alleged failure to warn about addiction. The Soria complaint pleads a similar failure-to-warn theory in the wrongful-death context, under California law, against a company already defending a state enforcement action.
