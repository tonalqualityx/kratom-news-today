The Washington Poison Center is warning the public about counterfeit "Cat's Claw" herbal supplements that it says are laced with highly concentrated chemicals related to kratom and are causing opioid overdoses and severe withdrawal, according to [a safety alert posted on the center's website](https://www.wapc.org/insights/safety-alert-fake-cats-claw/). The products at issue are mislabeled tablets and supplements that investigations found to contain undisclosed 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), the alert says. Those are concentrated or lab-made compounds, distinct from the trace 7-OH present in natural kratom leaf, and the alert does not describe any case involving natural leaf kratom.

The alert was issued Tuesday, October 6, [the Tri-City Herald's Annette Cary reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/wa-poison-center-links-overdoses-210442940.html), and the center's page shows a last-updated date of October 7. Washington becomes the latest state poison center to put out a cat's claw warning, following [Utah's four confirmed deaths][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products], a [Tennessee Bureau of Investigation bulletin][tennessee-tbi-warns-cats-claw-tablets-contain-mgm-15-kratom-18-lab-submissions], and a [Virginia poison center notice][virginia-blue-ridge-poison-center-unlabeled-kratom-cats-claw-products] over the past month.

## What the center says it is seeing

"Poison centers across the United States, including in Washington state, are treating sick patients following their use of fake cat's claw products," the alert states. The center did not publish a count of Washington cases, and the Tri-City Herald's report does not give one either. The newspaper described Washington residents as overdosing and becoming severely ill after taking what was labeled as a legal supplement for arthritis or cancer symptoms. The alert cites the Utah Poison Control Center's report of four confirmed deaths involving cat's claw as the fatal cases to date.

Authentic cat's claw, Uncaria tomentosa or Uncaria guianensis, is a legal herbal supplement that "is not psychoactive and does not cause opioid overdose or withdrawal symptoms" on its own, according to the alert. The center said recent investigations into products labeled as cat's claw found undisclosed 7-OH, MGM-15, MGPI, "and other potent compounds derived from kratom," sold in vape shops, gas stations, convenience stores, and online. The Tri-City Herald's account also listed MGM-16 among the suspected adulterants.

## How the alert describes the compounds

The center drew the distinction between natural and manufactured alkaloids in its own words. "While 7-OH naturally occurs in small amounts in kratom, it can be manufactured in a highly concentrated form," the alert says. "MGM-15 and MGPI are semi-synthetic compounds created in labs." The center noted that the Drug Enforcement Administration designated MGM-15 and MGPI as Schedule I controlled substances as of August 2026, "making their sale and possession illegal." The DEA's separate proposal to schedule 7-OH above a concentration threshold has not been finalized, and the alert does not describe 7-OH itself as scheduled.

According to the center, all three compounds act on the same brain receptors as morphine, heroin, and fentanyl, and in the adulterated products "these compounds are added at dangerous, highly concentrated levels." The alert says taking too much can slow or stop breathing and that regular use can lead to physical dependence and severe withdrawal.

## Brand names, and what the center does not know

The alert includes photographs the center said are examples of products from stores in Spokane. It added a caveat: "as of October 7, only Buzzers and Homiez brands of cat's claw have been implicated via investigations in other states to contain kratom alkaloids. We do not know which brands in Washington are linked to patient symptoms." The Buzzers name is the same one Utah officials tied to that state's deaths, per the earlier KNT coverage linked above.

The Spokane detail intersects with local law. The city [banned kratom sales effective April 8][washington-local-governments-fill-kratom-regulatory-vacuum] under an ordinance the council passed unanimously, as KNT reported in May, and [Bellingham followed with its own ban in September][bellingham-washington-city-council-approves-kratom-sales-ban-7-0-gross-misdemeanor]. Products labeled as cat's claw rather than kratom would not announce themselves as covered by a kratom ordinance, and the alert does not say whether the Spokane products pictured were tested or whether any Washington enforcement agency has acted on them.

## Guidance to clinicians

The center's recommendations are directed at the public and at health care providers. It advised stopping use of cat's claw products purchased from vape shops, gas stations, convenience stores, and online retailers, keeping suspected products away from children, and watching anyone who may have used an adulterated product for extreme sleepiness or slowed breathing, with 911 and naloxone if the person cannot be woken.

For clinicians, the center said adulterated products should be considered in patients presenting with unexplained opioid toxicity or severe withdrawal, and that providers should ask patients specifically about cat's claw use. "Traditional urine drug screening will not identify these products, but some laboratories are equipped for specialized testing," the alert says. The center asked that suspected cases be reported to its helpline at 1-800-222-1222.
