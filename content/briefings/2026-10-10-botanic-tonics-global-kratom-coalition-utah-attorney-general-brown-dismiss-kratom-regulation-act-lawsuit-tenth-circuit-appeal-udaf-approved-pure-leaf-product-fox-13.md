The federal challenge to Utah's Kratom Regulation Act is over. Attorneys for Botanic Tonics LLC, the Global Kratom Coalition, and the Utah Attorney General's Office agreed late in September to dismiss both the lawsuit in district court and the appeal pending before the Tenth U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals, according to court records reviewed by [Fox 13 News](https://www.fox13now.com/news/politics/lawsuit-challenging-utahs-ban-on-kratom-products-is-dropped) reporter Ben Winslow, who published the story on October 9, 2026. The Act restricts kratom sales in Utah to pure leaf products and [took effect May 6][utah-kratom-act-takes-effect-manufacturers-reformulate] after a federal judge denied the plaintiffs' request for an injunction.

"The Parties have resolved this matter, have agreed that this appeal should be dismissed, have agreed to dismiss the underlying case in the district court, and have agreed that the Parties will each bear their own attorney fees, costs and expenses," Botanic Tonics attorney Ronald Price wrote in the motion, as quoted by Fox 13.

## Why the company dropped the case

A spokesperson for Attorney General Derek Brown's office gave Fox 13 the state's account of the sequence: "Botanic Tonics recently submitted a new pure leaf kratom product to the Utah Department of Agriculture for review. The department reviewed and approved the product. After the product was approved, Botanic Tonics dismissed its lawsuit." Botanic Tonics did not respond to a message Fox 13 sent through its website seeking comment. The Oklahoma-based company had argued that limiting Utah sales to pure leaf kratom would cost it millions of dollars, [Utah Public Radio](https://www.upr.org/utah-news/2026-10-09/daily-news-kratom-ban-lawsuit-zion-rockfall-road-closure-hogle-zoo-savanna) reported in its October 9 news roundup, and its [renewed motion in June][utah-kratom-law-court-challenge-beverage-ban] contended the law's requirements made it impossible to manufacture kratom beverages.

Fox 13 reported in August that the suit had been put on hold while the state and the company held settlement talks. The outlet did not report the terms beyond the fee arrangement in the motion, and neither Fox 13 nor Utah Public Radio identified the approved product by name. Botanic Tonics is the maker of the Feel Free line, which is the subject of a [consumer fraud suit Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes filed in September][arizona-attorney-general-mayes-sues-botanic-tonics-feel-free-consumer-fraud-act-first-in-nation], per KNT's earlier coverage.

## What stays in place, and what may come next

The dismissal "means Utah's ban on most products except 'pure leaf kratom' will remain in place," Fox 13 reported. Sen. Mike McKell, the Spanish Fork Republican who sponsored the law, "has suggested he may pursue an all-out ban on kratom in the upcoming legislative session," according to the outlet, which noted that the 2026 session featured competing bills to ban or restrict kratom and that residents testified on both sides. Utah Public Radio wrote that supporters of the law point to kratom's addictive potential and to [recent deaths tied to mislabeled synthetic kratom products][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products]. Those deaths, as Utah health officials described them this month, involved tablets sold as "cat's claw" that testing found contained mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15, concentrated or synthetic compounds distinct from natural leaf kratom, and the Kratom Regulation Act litigation did not concern those products.
