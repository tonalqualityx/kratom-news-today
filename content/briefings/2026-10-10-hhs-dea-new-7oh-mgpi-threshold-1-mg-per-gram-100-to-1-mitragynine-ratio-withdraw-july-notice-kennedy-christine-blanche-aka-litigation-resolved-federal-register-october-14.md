The federal threshold that separates scheduled 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from the rest of the kratom market is changing. The Justice Department [announced on October 9, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) is issuing two new notices of intent based on a new recommendation from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). Under the HHS recommendation, as the department describes it, 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) would be controlled in Schedule I when either substance alone, or both combined, exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the mass of mitragynine in a product divided by the mass of 7-OH, MGPI, or both is less than 100. Both notices appeared on the Federal Register's public inspection desk on October 9 and are scheduled for publication on October 14.

"Dangerous and addictive opioids do not belong behind convenience store counters," Attorney General Todd Blanche said in the Justice Department release. HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. framed the recommendation differently in the [HHS release](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html): "We are drawing a clear line between natural kratom and dangerous, enhanced opioid products. We listened to the public, followed the science, and developed a clear recommended threshold for temporary scheduling that targets concentrated 7-OH and MGPI without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I."

## What the new threshold says

The [7-OH and MGPI notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine), filed under Docket No. DEA-1570, quotes the threshold from a letter dated October 8, 2026 from HHS Assistant Secretary for Health Admiral Brian Christine, M.D. The two substances would be controlled "when either substance alone, or their combined amount if both present, is: (A) Above a specified threshold concentration, that is, greater than 1 mg per gram (for solid powders, encapsulated material, or other solid forms) or greater than 1 mg per milliliter (for liquid preparations), OR (B) The mass of mitragynine present divided by the mass of [7-hydroxymitragynine], or [mitragynine pseudoindoxyl], or the combined amount of [7-hydroxymitragynine] and [mitragynine pseudoindoxyl] if both present, is less than 100."

The ratio test is new. The DEA's [July notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/06/2026-13580/schedules-of-controlled-substance-temporary-placement-of-7-hydroxymitragynine-above-a-specified), which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered when it was announced][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], proposed scheduling 7-OH above 0.05% by dry weight, and the DEA describes that earlier figure in the new notice as "a different threshold provided by the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) in July of 2025" and as a "lower threshold." By KNT's arithmetic, 1 milligram per gram is 0.1% by weight, double the July concentration figure. The threshold language quoted in the new notice contains no per-unit milligram cap of the kind the July notice carried. HHS said the ratio approach "reflects the alkaloid composition of natural kratom products and provides a way to identify products intentionally enhanced or concentrated with 7-OH or MGPI."

The notice states that the DEA is withdrawing the July notice of intent "in a separate document contained in this issue of the Federal Register." As of October 10, the Federal Register's public inspection list showed the two notices of intent and no separate withdrawal document from the DEA; KNT will track whether it appears in the October 14 issue. The notice's body refers to the July notice as published July 7, 2026, while its footnote cites the document at 91 FR 40917 with a July 6 date.

## No finding of safety below the line

The DEA attaches a caveat to the new design in the notice's summary: "While the new threshold recommended by HHS would permit certain products to remain in the marketplace without being subject to schedule I controls, neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels." Later in the document the agency writes that "individuals should not infer from this notice of intent that DEA views 7-hydroxymitragynine or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl below this threshold as safe for consumption" and that the DEA "still views kratom and its two major psychoactive ingredients, mitragynine and 7-hydroxymtragynine, as drugs of concern."

The notice gives the reason for a threshold at all: "because both 7-hydroxymitragynine and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl have supposedly been found in trace amounts in some botanical kratom products." The DEA's background section states that in its natural botanical form 7-OH "makes up less than two percent of the total alkaloid content or occurs in trace amount" in Mitragyna speciosa, that it can be synthesized from mitragynine "through a one-step chemical reaction," and that commercial extracts and synthetic formulations "contain a significantly higher concentration of 7-hydroxymitragynine than what is found in botanical M. speciosa."

HHS's release summarizes the Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) scientific review as finding 7-OH "substantially more potent than morphine in preclinical studies, with available data indicating approximately two to nine times greater potency depending on the study," and as identifying "evidence supporting a meaningful risk of opioid-like physical dependence and withdrawal from MGPI." The department also said that "some evidence reviewed by FDA suggests MGPI may also occur at trace levels in some natural or kratom-derived products," and that "the science concerning the presence of MGPI continues to develop," per the Justice Department release.

## What happens to the August order

The DEA's [August 26 order][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], [announced by the Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) on August 25, placed MGPI, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I with no concentration threshold. The new notice says the DEA "intends to withdraw the temporary order placing mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and replace it with a new temporary order," and that the August order on MGPI "remains in effect until either it expires or the new temporary order is published and the previous order is withdrawn, whichever occurs first."

MGM-15 and MGM-16, the two lab-made derivatives, are handled in a [second notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20942/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-mgm-15-and-mgm-16-in-schedule-i), Docket No. DEA-1644, that the DEA says it is publishing "only for the technical and procedural purpose of distinguishing MGM-15 and MGM-16 from mitragynine pseudoindoxyl." According to that notice, HHS's letter "related only to mitragynine pseudoindoxyl" and "did not recommend imposing any threshold for either MGM-15 or MGM-16," so the new order for those two compounds "will maintain the temporary placement of these substances in schedule I of the CSA without change." The two notices give different dates for the HHS letter: the MGM-15 and MGM-16 notice says October 6, 2026, while the 7-OH and MGPI notice and the Justice Department release both say October 8.

Under 21 U.S.C. 811(h), the statute both notices cite, a temporary scheduling order cannot issue until 30 days after the notice of intent is published, which puts the earliest date for the new orders on or after November 13 if publication proceeds on October 14 as scheduled. A temporary order lasts two years and can be extended one year, according to the notices. The DEA says it considered the public comments submitted to HHS before issuing the new 7-OH and MGPI notice; the HHS docket [closed September 10 with more than 30,000 comments][hhs-7oh-threshold-comment-period-closes-30000-comments].

## The AKA says its lawsuit is over

The American Kratom Association (AKA), which [sued the DEA in federal court in August][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order] over the MGPI order after [warning that it could reach natural leaf][aka-warns-pseudoindoxyl-scheduling-could-reach-natural-leaf], said in an [October 9 statement](https://www.americankratom.org/news/dea-and-department-of-justice-scheduling-of-7-oh-and-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl) that it "welcomes the resolution of its litigation challenging the potential unintended consequences of the federal scheduling action involving mitragynine pseudoindoxyl." The statement does not name the court, the case, or the terms of the resolution. The case, American Kratom Association v. United States Drug Enforcement Administration, was filed August 26 in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia as No. 1:26-cv-02997, according to CourtListener's docket listing; KNT could not independently confirm the docket's disposition as of this briefing.

"This is an important victory for science, sound public policy, and the millions of Americans who rely on responsibly manufactured natural kratom products," AKA senior fellow for public policy Mac Haddow said in the statement. The association said it "has consistently supported appropriate federal action against products containing chemically manipulated or synthetically enhanced concentrations of 7-OH and related opioid compounds," and urged states to adopt "appropriate concentration thresholds, objective laboratory testing standards, and clear protections for natural kratom products" rather than "sweeping prohibitions." HHS's release acknowledged the same dispute, saying that after the August order "questions have emerged about how scheduling could affect natural botanical kratom products potentially containing trace amounts of MGPI" and that the new recommendation "responds to those concerns."

The Global Kratom Coalition, which applauded the August order, had not posted a response to the new notices on its news page as of October 10, based on KNT's review. The new approach also runs against the request three Republican senators sent the DEA and FDA two days earlier. Sens. Chuck Grassley, Mike Lee, and Susan Collins asked the agencies in an [October 7 letter][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply] to finalize the July order quickly and to "consider scheduling the entire kratom plant, including all levels of 7-OH (not simply the threshold limit as stated in the Notice of Intent)," according to the [letter posted by Grassley's office](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/download/grassley-lee-collins-to-dea-fda_-7-oh/). The senators had requested a written reply by October 15. Neither the HHS nor the Justice Department release mentions the letter.

The MGPI threshold arrives as state health officials in Utah and Washington have linked deaths and poisonings to tablets sold as "cat's claw" that laboratory testing found contained MGPI and MGM-15, per KNT's [coverage this week][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products]. Those products, as the officials described them, are concentrated or synthetic preparations distinct from natural leaf kratom, and the HHS threshold, as the DEA notice describes it, applies the same concentration and ratio tests to any product regardless of how it is labeled.
