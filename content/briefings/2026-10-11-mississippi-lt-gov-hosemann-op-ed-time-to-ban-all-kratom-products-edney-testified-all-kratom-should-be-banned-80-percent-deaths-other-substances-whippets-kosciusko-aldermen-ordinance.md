Mississippi Lt. Gov. Delbert Hosemann, the presiding officer of the state Senate, called for a statewide ban on all kratom products in [an op-ed published October 8, 2026 in the Magnolia Tribune](https://magnoliatribune.com/2026/10/08/hosemann-it-is-time-for-mississippi-to-ban-all-kratom-products/). "When we know a substance carries serious risks, we have a responsibility to act," Hosemann wrote. His call arrives one week after Attorney General Lynn Fitch [said she would promote legislation to ban kratom "in all forms"][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc] in the next session, and it places the official who controls the Senate's committee assignments and calendar on the side of a full ban rather than the synthetic-only approach that the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit and the Global Kratom Coalition have urged.

## What Hosemann wrote

The op-ed opens with the [September deaths of two University of Mississippi students][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link], which Hosemann said have brought renewed attention to kratom, while noting that the causes of death remain under investigation. Packages of "Smax" Pseudo-DHM tablets, a manufactured product whose labeling lists the lab-made compound MGM-15 and in which [preliminary testing found 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH)][ole-miss-dea-affidavit-mosleh-charges-express-mart-water-valley-owner-aydah-they-took-it-all-wmc-timeline-rahmani-aka-letter-fitch], were found near both students, according to a Lafayette County affidavit KNT reported earlier this month; whether that 7-OH was a concentrated preparation or the trace amount natural to leaf has not been reported, and toxicology remains pending.

Hosemann distinguished between the two categories of product under Mississippi law. Synthetic kratom sales are already banned in the state, he wrote, while non-synthetic, plant-based kratom is subject to state regulation and is "outlawed entirely by some Mississippi counties and cities, similarly to how alcohol is regulated." The Legislature's 2025 law raised the purchase age to 21 and banned synthetic kratom extracts, as KNT has previously reported. Hosemann wrote that products sold at gas stations may be mislabeled or contain undisclosed ingredients, and he associated synthetic kratom with "respiratory depression, memory loss, addiction, and depression."

On the question of whether a ban should reach plant-based products, Hosemann wrote that "Dr. Dan Edney, Mississippi's State Health Officer, testified last Thursday that all kratom should be banned," and said he agrees. The op-ed does not identify the hearing at which Edney testified, and KNT found no separate published account of the testimony; the date Hosemann references would be October 1. Edney told the House Drug Policy Committee in early 2025 that he favored placing kratom in Schedule III under state law, a step he said would remove it from stores while imposing relatively small penalties for possession, [the Star-Herald reported](https://www.starherald.net/house-drug-policy-committee-takes-action-restrict-kratom-mississippi-67a245771ff34) at the time.

Hosemann also cited Centers for Disease Control and Prevention data that "nearly 80 percent of all kratom-associated deaths involve other substances," and wrote that the Mississippi Bureau of Narcotics has identified nitrous oxide, sold as "whippets," as a substance frequently combined with kratom. The op-ed closes with an appeal to young people to refuse peer pressure and a call for comprehensive state-level prohibition. Hosemann is serving his second term as the state's 33rd lieutenant governor and previously served as secretary of state.

## Where the advocacy groups and local law enforcement stand

The full-ban position taken by Hosemann and Fitch differs from the approach the investigating agency itself has asked for. The Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit [asked the Legislature on September 30 for "immediate legislative hearings" on "synthetic kratom-related products"][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc] and said its initial public warning "was not a declaration that kratom or any specific substance caused either death." The Global Kratom Coalition said it supports the narcotics unit's focus on specific synthetic compounds rather than a broad ban on natural leaf, and the American Kratom Association [sent Fitch a letter on October 1](https://fortune.com/press-releases/aka-urges-mississippi-ag-fitch-amend-kratom-ban-recommendation-2026-10-01/) urging her to amend her ban call and instead support legislation targeting "synthetic and chemically manipulated opioid products," according to the association's release. The AKA's Mac Haddow has called the Smax tablets "a synthetically engineered opioid, not kratom at all."

Both advocacy groups support the federal government's targeting of lab-made compounds and concentrated 7-OH, including the Drug Enforcement Administration's [October 9 threshold notice][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14], which exempts natural leaf. Neither organization had published a response to Hosemann's op-ed as of this briefing, based on the coverage available to KNT.

## Kosciusko weighs a local ban

Local action is continuing alongside the state debate. The Kosciusko Board of Aldermen is reviewing an ordinance that would prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom within city limits, with a vote expected at an upcoming meeting, [WLBT reported](https://www.wlox.com/2026/10/08/kosciusko-leaders-consider-ordinance-banning-kratom-sales/) on October 7, citing reporting by Breezy News. Mayor Tim Kyle said he wants to ensure residents live in a safe community, according to the station, which described the proposal as a response to the Ole Miss deaths and the scrutiny that followed. The WLBT report did not say whether the Kosciusko ordinance would distinguish synthetic products from plant-based kratom. Oxford banned kratom in 2019 and Lafayette County's ban took effect in 2021, as KNT reported in its coverage of the Fitch announcement.

The Legislature does not convene in regular session until January. Hosemann's op-ed does not say whether he will introduce or sponsor a bill, and any ban would require passage in both chambers and the governor's signature.
