Tablets containing mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) or MGM-15, two lab-made compounds related to kratom alkaloids that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) [placed in Schedule I by emergency order in August][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], were on sale in eight of 52 New York City stores visited by New York Post reporters over a two-week period, [the Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/10/10/us-news/illegal-kratom-products-sold-in-nyc-smoke-shops-investigation/) on October 10, 2026. According to the Post, each of those products also contained 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) in a concentrated, manufactured form, a preparation distinct from the trace 7-OH that occurs naturally in kratom leaf, and products built around concentrated 7-OH were available at 34 of the stores the reporters visited. The investigation, by Adry Torres, Sonya Gugliara, Rich Calder, and Shane Galvin, covered smoke shops, convenience stores, and dispensaries across the boroughs. The Post did not report independent laboratory testing of the products; its findings rest on product labeling and on what store employees told reporters.

## What the reporters found

The MGPI products on sale included 1,000-milligram packs of a Kama brand product labeled "7-Hydroxy+Psuedoindyl," which the Post said was promoted at New City Smoke Shop at Park Place and Church Street in Lower Manhattan. An employee at East Village Smoke Shop on First Avenue, selling the same product, told the reporter it was "hard to find" because "it's about to be banned," according to the Post. The same store sold Rush brand tablets that the Post said contained 7-OH, MGPI, and MGM-15. In Astoria, a store called Ditmars Organic Convenience sold Kama products and pseudoindoxyl-containing products from a brand called Opia, and an employee showed the selection on an iPad rather than displaying it openly, the Post reported.

A worker at New City Smoke Shop told the Post that customers choose the MGPI-containing Kama product because it is stronger than other varieties, and that a health inspector had warned the store a few months earlier that the tablets would soon be illegal. He said he did not know the current legal status of any of the substances, a position the Post said was shared by employees at several other stores. A worker at East Village Smoke Shop said the Rush tablets were going to become illegal soon, which the Post noted was already the case. An employee who gave his name as Rifa told the paper that customers "say that it makes them feel stronger, stay awake, focused," while a worker at an Astoria shop described customers appearing weak after taking the tablets.

The Post described the ingredients as similar to those of the "Smax Pseudo-DHM" tablets found near two University of Mississippi students who died in September, which [officials there said contained MGPI, MGM-15, and 7-OH][ole-miss-dea-affidavit-mosleh-charges-express-mart-water-valley-owner-aydah-they-took-it-all-wmc-timeline-rahmani-aka-letter-fitch]. Two Mississippi retailers charged in that case face up to 20 years in prison, the Post noted, and the paper also pointed to [Utah's warning linking four deaths to similar tablets][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products]. The DEA did not return the Post's requests for comment.

## The potency claims and who made them

The Post characterized 7-OH as "13 times stronger than morphine" and said MGPI is 31 times stronger than 7-OH, citing the National Library of Medicine for the latter figure. Those characterizations are the Post's, drawing on the sources it named, and the paper described kratom's effects by reference to the Mayo Clinic, which it said describes small amounts as stimulant-like and larger amounts as sedating. The Post's framing treated the Kama, Opia, and Rush tablets as "synthetic kratom," and its reporting did not distinguish concentrated 7-OH, which can be extracted or converted from leaf material, from the fully lab-made MGPI and MGM-15 that the DEA's order covers. The Justice Department's own announcement of the August order [described the three scheduled compounds as substances that do not occur naturally in the kratom plant](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), and the DEA said on October 9 that it is [reissuing the MGM-15 and MGM-16 order without change][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] while replacing the MGPI portion with a threshold-based notice, [according to the department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid).

The Post reported that the Justice Department's Friday announcement means anyone caught selling 7-OH could face up to 20 years in prison once the scheduling takes effect. The DEA's October 9 notice applies to 7-OH and MGPI above a 1 milligram per gram threshold or below a 100 to 1 mitragynine ratio, and neither the department's announcement nor the notice describes natural leaf kratom as covered, as KNT reported this week.

## The New York legal picture

Products made from botanical kratom remain legal at the federal level, the Post noted, and 7-OH is legal in New York for now. A bill sponsored by Assemblyman Phil Steck, a Schenectady Democrat, that would ban synthetic kratom products statewide [passed both chambers in June][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban] and remains under review by Gov. Kathy Hochul, the Post reported. Steck told the paper he expects a signature early next year and criticized Hochul's earlier kratom warning-label requirement as "watered down" by industry lobbying. Hochul spokesperson Nicolette Simmonds defended the governor's record on kratom, according to the Post. Long Island Republicans want a total statewide ban on all kratom, the paper said, and [Albany][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect], [Oneida][oneida-county-kratom-ban-signed-utica-enforcement-seizures], and Nassau counties have already enacted local bans.

The New York State Health Department told the Post it recorded 100 overdoses linked to kratom from 2023 to 2024 and that kratom contributed to 45 overdose deaths from 2024 to 2025, with a spokesperson noting that other substances were involved in some of those cases. The department's figures, as the Post reported them, do not separate natural leaf products from concentrated 7-OH or lab-made compounds. The Post also cited Centers for Disease Control and Prevention data that 1,221 drug overdose deaths between December 2024 and December 2025 involved kratom, and said products made from botanical kratom are banned in nine states.

## The advocates the Post quoted

The people quoted in the investigation favored broader restrictions than the pending synthetic-only bill. Wendy Chamberlain, founder of Kratom Danger Awareness, whose son Joseph died in 2020 in upstate New York from what the Post described as an accidental kratom overdose, told the paper: "You can't have one without the other. If you get rid of the entire plant, then you're not going to have the synthetic or semi-synthetic." Jennifer Brandt, a Virginia-based pharmacist, told the Post that the synthetic products produce "your predictable opioid-like toxicities" and said leaf kratom also carries risks of withdrawal, seizures, cardiac abnormalities, and liver failure. Rich Marianos, a former Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives official who founded the Tobacco Law Enforcement Network, said of smoke shops: "We are licensing these places [and they're] like crack houses." Greg Kiesow, founder of Kratom Anonymous, told the Post he was 18 years sober before developing a dependence on 7-OH products after starting with leaf powder. Dana Knox of South Carolina, whose 26-year-old son Micah died in June from what she described as a synthetic kratom overdose, asked why the products are sold over the counter.

The Post did not quote the American Kratom Association, the Global Kratom Coalition, or any retailer trade group in the investigation. Both advocacy organizations have supported federal scheduling of MGPI, MGM-15, MGM-16, and concentrated 7-OH while opposing restrictions on natural leaf, positions KNT has reported in its coverage of the [DEA's threshold notice][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. A separate Post story published the same day on the industry's lobbying spending, including the American Kratom Association's response, is [covered in a companion briefing][new-york-post-kratom-industry-lobbying-aka-1-million-opensecrets-botanic-tonics-ross-1-1-million-kennedy-pacs-rnc-stop-gas-station-heroin-checkmate-750000-steck-haddow-seat-at-the-table].
