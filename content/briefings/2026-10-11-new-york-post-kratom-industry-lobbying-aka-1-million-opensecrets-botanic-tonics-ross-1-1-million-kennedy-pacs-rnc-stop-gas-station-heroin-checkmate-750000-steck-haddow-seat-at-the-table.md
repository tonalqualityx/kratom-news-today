The kratom industry has spent millions of dollars on political action committees, candidate campaigns, and lobbyists to fight blanket bans and to persuade lawmakers to target synthetic and concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products instead, [the New York Post's Rich Calder reported](https://nypost.com/2026/10/10/us-news/kratom-industrys-multimillion-lobbying-effort-to-ban-synthetic-kratom-while-pushing-natural-version/) on October 10, 2026, citing lobbying and campaign finance records. The story ran the same day as the Post's [investigation of New York City smoke shops][new-york-post-investigation-52-nyc-smoke-shops-eight-sold-mgpi-mgm-15-tablets-kama-opia-rush-7oh-legal-new-york-34-stores-steck-hochul-synthetic-ban-review] and one day after the Department of Justice announced [threshold-based scheduling for 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] that leaves natural leaf outside the order.

## The figures the Post reported

The American Kratom Association (AKA) has spent more than $1 million on lobbyists over the past decade, including $443,000 in 2026 alone, according to OpenSecrets data cited by the Post. The association's lobbyists include Mac Haddow, a former Reagan administration official who is also the AKA's senior fellow on public policy, and former New York state Sen. David Carlucci, a Rockland County Democrat, the paper reported.

Botanic Tonics, the Oklahoma-based maker of the Feel Free kava and kratom beverage founded by Jerry Ross, has "reportedly spearheaded a multi-million dollar lobbying effort" built on private meetings with senior federal officials to promote natural leaf kratom, the Post wrote. Citing earlier New York Times reporting, the Post said that effort included $1.1 million to PACs affiliated with Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. and $443,000 to the Republican National Committee, spending the Times connected to a private meeting between Ross and Vice President JD Vance about potential crackdowns on 7-OH and other synthetic kratom alternatives while protecting natural products. Ross allies also formed a company called "Stop Gas Station Heroin" to promote natural kratom as the safer option, and that entity paid Checkmate Government Relations, a lobbying firm owned by Kennedy ally Ches McDowell, at least $750,000, according to records the Post cited. Ross and Botanic Tonics were already the subject of [Senator Ron Wyden's August inquiry][wyden-inquiry-kennedy-kratom-vape-policy] into donations and policy outcomes, and the company faces a [consumer fraud suit by Arizona's attorney general][arizona-attorney-general-mayes-sues-botanic-tonics-feel-free-consumer-fraud-act-first-in-nation].

Politico's Influence newsletter covered related ground on October 9 under the headline ["The kratom millionaires quietly boosting the GOP,"](https://www.politico.com/newsletters/politico-influence/2026/10/09/the-kratom-millionaires-quietly-boosting-the-gop-01115136) reporting on political giving by kratom industry figures to Republican candidates and policy efforts. The newsletter also noted that Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton, the Republican nominee for U.S. Senate, has pursued retailers selling products with elevated 7-OH under the state's 2023 Kratom Consumer Health and Safety Protection Act, which permits adult sales of kratom while prohibiting high-7-OH products. KNT was unable to access the full Politico item; the characterization here is limited to what the newsletter's headline and summary describe.

## 'Effective' in New York, and the AKA's reply

The lobbying campaign to protect natural leaf while cracking down on synthetic products was "effective" in New York, Assemblyman Phil Steck told the Post. Steck, a Schenectady Democrat, sponsored the bill to ban synthetic kratom products statewide that [passed both chambers in June][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban] and is still awaiting action from Gov. Kathy Hochul. He told the Post that, if he had his way, the state would also ban natural or "distilled" kratom products because of what he called "dangerous side effects," even if the health risks are less serious than those linked to synthetic products, and that legislative leaders and Hochul's office backed away from that broader approach.

Haddow told the Post that the money spent by natural kratom supporters on lobbying and contributions "is to get a seat at the table" to ensure "decision makers are being fair," and said the competing synthetic kratom industry does the same thing. "It's not a perfect system … but in order to have access to these [government] offices we have to retain lobbyists," he said. Haddow also told the paper that the country's 24 million kratom consumers primarily use the products for energy, anxiety and depression, chronic pain, and opioid addiction; those are his characterizations of consumer motivations, and the Food and Drug Administration has not approved kratom for any use.

The Post's framing of the two lobbying camps matches the split KNT has tracked in federal policy: the AKA and the Global Kratom Coalition have supported scheduling of lab-made compounds and concentrated 7-OH while opposing control of natural leaf, and three Republican senators [asked the DEA last week to consider scheduling the entire plant][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply]. Representatives for HHS declined to comment to the Post on the industry's lobbying. Kennedy, in the department's [October 9 statement](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) on the new thresholds, said: "We are drawing a clear line between natural kratom and dangerous, enhanced opioid products." The Post did not report a response from Botanic Tonics, Ross, Checkmate Government Relations, or the Vance or Kennedy offices.
