The Arlington, Washington City Council is scheduled to take up a draft ordinance on October 12, 2026 that would prohibit the sale, distribution, and advertising of all kratom products within city limits, [according to the council's agenda bill](https://www.arlingtonwa.gov/DocumentCenter/View/17467/ATTACHMENT-B). The October 12 session is listed as a workshop for discussion only. The agenda bill says the recommended motion at the council's October 19 meeting will be "to approve the ordinance prohibiting the sale, advertisement and distribution of Kratom and authorize the mayor to sign."

The draft is a full-category ban. It covers natural leaf products along with concentrated extracts and synthetic compounds, and it does not distinguish between them.

## What the draft ordinance would do

The draft would create a new Chapter 6.12 of the Arlington Municipal Code. Its definition of "kratom product" reaches "any kratom analogue, food product, food ingredient, dietary ingredient, dietary supplement, or beverage that contains any part of the plant Mitragyna speciosa and its leaves, or any derivative that contains any part of the leaf of the plant, or any extract, synthetic alkaloid, or synthetically derived compounds of such plant or its leaf containing mitragynine or 7-hydroxymitragynine," in "any powder, capsule, pill, beverage, or other ingestible form," according to the text.

Under the draft, no person or business "may knowingly sell, distribute, advertise for sale or distribution, or permit to be sold, any kratom product in the City of Arlington." "Advertise" is defined to include in-store displays, signs, billboards, vehicle ads, print, websites, social media, and broadcast. "Distribute" covers giving products away as well as selling them.

Each violation would be a class 1 civil infraction under RCW 7.80 with a $250 fine, and the draft states that each separate sale, advertisement, or distribution counts as an independent violation, with each 24-hour period of a continuing violation treated as a separate offense. A retailer found in violation could also have its city business license revoked or denied, according to the text. The ordinance would take effect five days after its title is published in the city's official newspaper.

Personal possession and use would not be prohibited, [KIRO 7 reported](https://www.kiro7.com/news/local/arlington-city-council-consider-ban-kratom-sales-advertising/CNPQSPKZGVFKNK2MT6J6EVNJFI/) on October 10. The agenda bill lists the city administration, under City Administrator Paul Ellis, as the originating department and shows no budget request. The draft carries the signature block of City Attorney Oskar E. Rey for approval as to form.

## The city's stated rationale

Mayor Don Vanney said "Arlington is committed to protecting our young people and our community from harmful and potentially addictive substances," [the Lynnwood Times reported](https://lynnwoodtimes.com/2026/10/10/kratom/), citing a city statement. The city's materials describe effects "ranging from nausea, dizziness, and drowsiness to seizures, liver toxicity, dependence, and withdrawal," and say risks increase with concentrated products or combined use with alcohol, medications, or other drugs, according to both outlets. Neither outlet reported a source for those statements beyond the city, and the agenda bill cites no local poison-center or hospital data.

The draft's recitals state that the Drug Enforcement Administration has listed kratom as a "Drug of Chemical Concern," that the Food and Drug Administration has not approved any drug product containing kratom or its "two main alkaloids, mitragynine and 7-OH," and that the FDA "has determined that 7-hydroxymitragynine ('7-OH'), a naturally occurring alkaloid in the kratom plant, is a dangerous substance." That framing differs from the federal government's own current approach. The DEA and the Department of Health and Human Services [proposed on October 9][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] to control 7-OH only above 1 milligram per gram, a threshold designed to leave the trace amounts in natural leaf outside Schedule I. The Arlington draft makes no such distinction.

The recitals also acknowledge the preemption question. They note that under RCW 69.50.611, Washington cities may enact only controlled-substance ordinances consistent with the state Controlled Substances Act, and they state that Washington "has not enacted any laws or regulations governing the sale, advertisement of, distribution or use of kratom or its derivatives." The draft includes a severability clause covering any portion preempted by state or federal law.

## Washington's city-by-city pattern

Arlington, in Snohomish County north of Everett, would join a growing list of Washington municipalities acting in the absence of a state law. [Bellingham's council approved a sales ban 7 to 0][bellingham-washington-city-council-approves-kratom-sales-ban-7-0-gross-misdemeanor] in September, making violations a gross misdemeanor, and [Mount Vernon banned sales][mount-vernon-city-council-bans-kratom-sales] in August. [Quincy set an October hearing][quincy-washington-council-sets-october-hearing-kratom-ban-grant-county-health-district] on its own ban. Arlington's choice of a $250 civil infraction and license revocation is a lighter enforcement model than Bellingham's criminal penalty.

The local push comes as the Washington Poison Center [issued a safety alert on October 9][washington-poison-center-safety-alert-fake-cats-claw-buzzers-homiez-7oh-mgm-15-mgpi-spokane-stores-utah-four-deaths-tri-city-herald] about tablets sold as "cat's claw" in Spokane-area stores that contained the synthetic compounds MGM-15 and MGPI. Those products are manufactured tablets, distinct from natural leaf kratom, and Arlington's draft would cover both categories equally.

Neither outlet reported comment from Arlington retailers or from the American Kratom Association or Global Kratom Coalition, both of which have supported local and state restrictions on synthetic and concentrated products while opposing bans on natural leaf. The council's October 19 agenda had not been posted as of this briefing.
