Toxicology found the same three substances in the blood of both University of Mississippi students who died September 21: mitragynine, the primary alkaloid in kratom leaf, and two lab-made compounds, MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), that the Drug Enforcement Administration placed in Schedule I on August 26, according to federal court documents [reported by Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs) on October 10, 2026. The filing links the substances to Smax Mango Breeze MGM-15 tablets, a manufactured product that is distinct from natural leaf kratom, and states that "there is also probable cause to believe these drugs caused" the two deaths in Oxford, Fox reported. 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) is not among the substances the filing lists in the students' blood, according to Fox's account.

The court-document findings are the first reported toxicology results in a case that KNT has followed since [the students were found dead on September 21][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link] with packaged kratom-related products nearby. They arrive as a defense attorney has given the court a different account of how the two men died.

## What the filing says

Aidan Hamilton, 18, and Robert Strang, 20, "had the same three substances present" in their bloodstreams, the documents say, per Fox News. The filing states that "the Government proved that there was probable cause that the laboratory-tested drugs taken by the two overdose victims were the same type of drugs that were seized from the Moslehs during a court-authorized search warrant," Fox reported. The Smax tablets were priced at $39.99 at Puffs Vape, Tobacco and Beer Store in Oxford, where both defendants worked, according to the report.

The two defendants are Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, and his son Islam Mohamed Mosleh, 18, who face federal drug charges, Fox reported. [A DEA affidavit KNT reported on October 2][ole-miss-dea-affidavit-mosleh-charges-express-mart-water-valley-owner-aydah-they-took-it-all-wmc-timeline-rahmani-aka-letter-fitch] used bank records to tie Hamilton to purchases at Puffs on September 9, 14, and 20. The store's owner, Amer Alowdi, was previously indicted by a state grand jury and is believed to be operating remotely from Egypt, according to Fox.

Fox's report does not say when the full toxicology report will be released. A state official told Fox News Digital in September that the families would be notified of the results first.

## What the defense attorney told the court

At an October 9 detention hearing at the federal courthouse in Oxford, one of the defense attorneys said one student "died from a combined use of various substances, including cough syrup," and that the other "died from inhalation asphyxiation," [WMC Action News 5 reported](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/10/09/cause-death-ole-miss-students-revealed-court/). The station did not report which student the attorney was describing in each instance, and the attorney's statements have not been confirmed by a medical examiner's finding in the coverage available to KNT.

The government's filing, as described by Fox, asserts probable cause that the Smax tablets caused two overdose deaths. The defense attorney's account, as reported by WMC, points to a combination of substances in one death and to asphyxiation in the other. Neither outlet reported an official cause-of-death determination, and the Mississippi State Medical Examiner's Office has not publicly released one.

Prosecutors told the court the defendants "pose a serious danger to this community," WMC reported. Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit agent Caleb Easter testified, and the station reported that an informant had asked whether the store sold "7-OH kratom-based pills" and that a clerk said it did. That exchange is the only reference to 7-OH in either report; whether the Smax tablets contain concentrated 7-OH, in addition to the MGM-15 listed on their label, has not been established in the reporting.

The judge ordered Yahya Mosleh held, finding him "a flight risk with ties to another country" and noting that he had been in communication with Alowdi, WMC reported. Islam Mosleh was released on a $20,000 unsecured bond with a curfew and location monitoring, according to both outlets.

## Where this sits in the broader MGM-15 and MGPI picture

The compounds named in the filing are the same ones Utah officials [linked to four deaths over four months][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] in tablets sold as "cat's claw," and the same ones the DEA scheduled in August. The Department of Health and Human Services and the DEA [proposed a 1 milligram per gram threshold for 7-OH and MGPI on October 9][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] that would leave natural leaf outside federal control.

In Mississippi, the deaths have become the reference point for a statewide ban debate. Lt. Gov. Delbert Hosemann [called for a ban on all kratom products][mississippi-lt-gov-hosemann-op-ed-time-to-ban-all-kratom-products-edney-testified-all-kratom-should-be-banned-80-percent-deaths-other-substances-whippets-kosciusko-aldermen-ordinance] in an October 8 op-ed, while the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit, the American Kratom Association, and the Global Kratom Coalition have each asked lawmakers to target synthetic and chemically manipulated products rather than leaf. The toxicology findings reported by Fox name mitragynine, the leaf alkaloid, alongside two synthetic compounds in a single manufactured tablet. Neither the AKA nor the GKC had published a response to the filing as of this briefing, based on the coverage available to KNT.
