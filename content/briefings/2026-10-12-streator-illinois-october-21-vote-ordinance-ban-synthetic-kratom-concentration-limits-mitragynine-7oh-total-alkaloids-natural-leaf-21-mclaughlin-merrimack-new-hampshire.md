The Streator, Illinois City Council is scheduled to vote October 21, 2026 on an ordinance that would ban synthetic kratom products within city limits while leaving natural leaf kratom legal for adults 21 and older, [Shaw Local reported](https://www.shawlocal.com/illinois-valley/2026/10/11/streator-moves-to-ban-synthetic-kratom-in-city-limits/) on October 11. "The ordinance primarily will prohibit the sale and distribution of synthetic kratom products and other synthetic alternative drugs, establish concentration limits for mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine, which is what you see with kratom, and total kratom alkaloids," City Manager Yates McLaughlin said at a committee-of-the-whole meeting, according to the outlet.

The approach is narrower than the one [OSF Healthcare nurses asked the council for in August][streator-nurses-urge-city-council-kratom-ban], when they requested a ban on kratom sales generally while presenting data that concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products were 13 times more likely than regular kratom to cause severe outcomes. Concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts naturally present in kratom leaf, and the Streator draft, as McLaughlin described it, is built around that distinction.

## What the ordinance would require

Beyond the synthetic ban and alkaloid limits, the ordinance would require specific labeling and product information on kratom products and would require retailers to maintain certificates of analysis for the kratom they sell, Shaw Local reported. Sales would be restricted to people 21 and older. Enforcement would run through the police department and code enforcement, according to the outlet.

Shaw Local did not report the numerical concentration limits for mitragynine, 7-OH, or total alkaloids, and did not report penalty amounts. KNT could not locate the ordinance text on the city's website as of this briefing. The limits matter: Pennsylvania's House [set a 0.05 percent 7-OH dry-weight ceiling][pennsylvania-house-202-0-hb-2657-kosierowski-kratom-bill-amended-0-05-percent-7oh-dry-weight-no-extracts-12-percent-tax-kratom-regulatory-fund-haddow-restraint-senate-seven-days] in its bill this month, and the federal government's October 9 proposal [uses 1 milligram per gram][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] as the line between natural leaf and a controlled product. The limits Streator adopts would determine which extract products, if any, remain legal to sell in the city.

Council member David Reed said federal action from the Food and Drug Administration could be two years away, Shaw Local reported, quoting him as saying "it might be two years before they get to it."

## The nurses' August request

The OSF Healthcare nurses who addressed the council in August described patients arriving at the emergency room with withdrawal symptoms including anxiety, rapid heart rate, agitation, nausea, vomiting, and tremors, some requiring prolonged hospitalization, Shaw Local reported. Nearby Seneca, Ottawa, and Marseilles already have kratom bans in place, according to the outlet. [Naperville's council voted to ban kratom sales][naperville-city-council-votes-ban-kratom-sales] in September.

Statewide, an Illinois treatment provider [reported a threefold increase in patients seeking treatment for 7-OH][brightside-recovery-illinois-threefold-increase-7oh-patients-nine-in-ten-kushner-atteberry-outpacing-fentanyl-idph-fatalities-declined-wttw] this month, while the Illinois Department of Public Health said kratom and 7-OH fatalities have declined over five years, WTTW reported.

Shaw Local did not report comment from Streator retailers or from kratom advocacy organizations. The American Kratom Association and Global Kratom Coalition have generally supported local measures that ban synthetic products and set alkaloid limits while preserving natural leaf sales, which is the structure Streator is considering.

## Merrimack, New Hampshire weighs a similar split

The Merrimack, New Hampshire Town Council is considering an ordinance that would ban most kratom products locally while allowing natural leaf forms for adults 21 and older, [Patch reported](https://patch.com/new-hampshire/merrimack/patch-am-how-proposed-kratom-ban-could-change-merrimack-store-nodx-20261012) on October 12, summarizing a Union Leader article. According to Patch, a councilor who also serves as a state representative and a Merrimack police officer outlined addiction and public health concerns, and the proposal follows the failure of a statewide kratom bill in the Legislature this year. Patch's summary did not include meeting dates, penalties, or the ordinance's definition of the products it would ban, and the Union Leader article was not accessible to KNT.
