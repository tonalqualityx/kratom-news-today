The American Kratom Association will hold a press conference on the U.S. Capitol grounds at 11 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, October 13, 2026 and a congressional briefing in Room 2168 of the Rayburn House Office Building at 9 a.m. on Wednesday, October 14, according to a [press release](https://apnews.com/press-release/ez-newswire/press-release-e533edd7a35b818910e3c733bb6f86ba) the association distributed through EZ Newswire on October 12. Both events respond to the [October 9 Justice Department announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) that the Drug Enforcement Administration is adopting Department of Health and Human Services recommendations to place 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) in Schedule I only when a product exceeds 1 milligram per gram or per milliliter, or when its mitragynine content falls below a 100-to-1 ratio to those compounds, an action [KNT covered when it was announced][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14].

The release says the association will use the events to "address recent media mischaracterizations of botanical kratom," particularly in coverage of the University of Mississippi investigations, to "explain how federal action now clearly differentiates botanical kratom from synthetic and chemically manipulated products," and to address what it calls "the danger of inappropriately labeling synthetics as 'kratom' rather than distinguishing them as HHS, DOJ, and DEA now do." Both events will feature the association's policy leadership, scientific experts, and "Americans directly harmed by synthetic products falsely marketed as kratom," according to the release. The association announced [a virtual press conference on September 25][american-kratom-association-reporting-before-the-results-press-conference-ole-miss-south-carolina-wilson-mississippi-health-warning] making a similar argument about the Ole Miss coverage before toxicology results were available. A federal court filing has since reported that [toxicology found mitragynine along with MGM-15 and MGPI][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] in both students, with MGM-15 and MGPI being synthetic compounds already in federal Schedule I.

## What the briefing will cover

The Wednesday briefing, which the release says is open to members of Congress, congressional staff, and media, will address the October 9 announcement and the decision to accept HHS's concentration and ratio thresholds, "recent media misinformation about kratom stemming from the University of Mississippi investigations," testimony from people the association describes as impacted by synthetics posing as kratom, and "policy recommendations for Congress and the states to protect consumers without banning natural kratom," according to the release. The association said the events would stream live on its YouTube channel and asked media and congressional offices to register through its website. The release carries a disclaimer identifying it as a paid press release and the association as a 501(c)(4) advocacy organization, and lists Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy, as the media contact.

The release frames the federal action as separating "natural botanical kratom from chemically manipulated 7-OH and MGPI products." The DOJ announcement describes the threshold in terms of concentration, not production method: scheduling would attach when 7-OH or MGPI exceeds 1 milligram per gram, and the announcement does not condition that on whether the alkaloid was synthesized or concentrated from leaf. The DEA notice states that neither HHS nor DEA "has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels," as KNT reported October 10. Natural kratom leaf contains 7-OH only in trace amounts; concentrated extracts and synthetic preparations contain it at far higher levels.

## The timing

The association's briefing falls one day before the October 15 deadline that [Senators Chuck Grassley, Mike Lee, and Susan Collins set][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply] in an October 8 letter asking the DEA and FDA to finalize the 7-OH emergency order and to pursue permanent Schedule I placement for the entire kratom plant, a position the association opposes. It also comes two days after [the New York Post reported][new-york-post-kratom-industry-lobbying-aka-1-million-opensecrets-botanic-tonics-ross-1-1-million-kennedy-pacs-rnc-stop-gas-station-heroin-checkmate-750000-steck-haddow-seat-at-the-table] on the association's federal lobbying spending, in which Haddow said the spending was "to get a seat at the table."

The association's stated position, per the release's boilerplate, is to protect access to natural kratom leaf products while supporting age restrictions, labeling, contaminant testing, good manufacturing practices, and "strong enforcement against adulterated, mislabeled, synthetic, semi-synthetic, concentrated, fortified, or chemically manipulated products falsely marketed as kratom." That stance puts the association on the same side as the federal threshold action on concentrated 7-OH and against the whole-plant bans that some state and local officials continue to propose, including [the outright ban former Oklahoma Rep. Doug Cox urged][oklahoma-house-interim-study-pogemiller-grego-ocme-cliburn-mitragynine-44-death-certificates-42-deaths-top-five-overdose-drugs-webb-healthy-minds-natural-plant-material-cox-outlaw-all-marti-stoner-more-harm-than-good] at a House interim study on October 12.
