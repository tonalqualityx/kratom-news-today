The Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center at Wayne State University recorded 347 cases involving kratom, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), or other similar synthetics from 2021 through 2025, and 87 of those came in 2025, an 81 percent increase from 2024, Varun Vohra, a clinical toxicologist and the center's senior director, told [Axios Detroit](https://www.axios.com/local/detroit/2026/10/13/kratom-ban-regulation-poison-cases) in a report published October 13, 2026. Vohra said 7-OH exposures were associated with more severe withdrawal symptoms and greater intensive care needs than other kratom exposures, according to Axios.

The 347-case figure, as Axios describes it, combines natural leaf kratom, concentrated 7-OH products, and other synthetic derivatives in a single count. Concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts naturally present in kratom leaf, and Vohra's comparison of 7-OH exposures with "other kratom exposures" is the only point in the report where the two are separated. Axios did not report how many of the 347 cases, or the 87 in 2025, involved 7-OH products specifically.

Vohra told Axios he favors regulation over prohibition. "People are going to procure these items in some shape or form or another," he said. "We think [the regulation] should be based on a science-informed framework, so working alongside lawmakers, policymakers for more clear, consistent regulation as opposed to a full-on, blanket ban." Axios reported that spokespeople for the University of Michigan and Michigan State University said their data do not indicate significant use on campus but that both schools are watching for it. The report ties the renewed Michigan debate to [the deaths of two University of Mississippi students][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] last month, where a federal filing said toxicology found mitragynine and the Schedule I compounds MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl.

## Two bills, two approaches

Michigan has no statewide kratom statute in effect. House Bill 5537, introduced by Rep. Cam Cavitt, R-Cheboygan, would amend the Michigan Penal Code to prohibit the production and sale of kratom, and it passed the House 56 to 48 with immediate effect on March 18, according to the [Michigan Legislature's bill record](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Bills/Bill?ObjectName=2026-HB-5537). The Senate referred it to the Committee on Government Operations on March 24, and the record shows no action since. A [Michigan House Republicans release](https://gophouse.org/posts/rep-cavitt-celebrates-house-vote-to-ban-kratom-products) at the time described the bill as prohibiting "the growing, synthesizing, selling, or sale of kratom in Michigan" and quoted Cavitt saying "what we are experiencing with kratom is a crisis" and that "our children are being poisoned by products that they can easily purchase at gas stations." Axios characterized the bill as a ban on kratom and its "highly potent, synthetic derivatives like" 7-OH.

Senate Bill 1181, introduced by Sen. John Cherry on September 22 and referred to the Senate Regulatory Affairs Committee, would create a new act to regulate the distribution, sale, and manufacturing of kratom products, according to the [Legislature's record](https://www.legislature.mi.gov/Bills/Bill?ObjectName=2026-SB-1181). Axios reported that the bill would require warnings and dosage information on products and ban highly potent derivatives. KNT has not reviewed the bill text, and the record lists no committee hearing.

The split between the two chambers' bills matches the one [KNT has tracked at the municipal level][canton-township-trustees-advance-kratom-ordinance-21-plus-500-foot-buffer] in Michigan this year, where Canton Township advanced a 21-and-over ordinance with a 500-foot buffer in September after [a joint state and local inspection seized hundreds of kratom and concentrated 7-OH products][canton-michigan-inspection-seizes-kratom-7oh-products] from a Canton tobacco shop, and where [Warren's council took up a local sales ban in July][warren-michigan-kratom-ban-north-dakota-special-session].

## Detroit's council pauses

The Detroit City Council on Tuesday, October 6 voted to send a resolution supporting HB 5537 back to its Public Health and Safety Standing Committee after a long public comment period in which callers urged the council to vote it down, [BridgeDetroit reported](https://www.bridgedetroit.com/detroit-council-wants-to-know-more-before-supporting-statewide-kratom-ban-proposal/) on October 7. The committee was to reconvene in two weeks, and council members said they wanted to learn more about SB 1181 and about the differences between natural and synthetic kratom before moving the resolution, according to the outlet.

BridgeDetroit reported that pro-kratom commenters were nearly unanimous in backing natural leaf kratom over synthetic variants such as 7-OH, and in supporting measures like age limits and testing; many pointed to SB 1181. Council Member Angela Whitfield-Calloway, who authored the resolution, said she was becoming "more informed and educated" and was open to learning about regulation versus a blanket ban once she reviewed SB 1181, while also saying "we cannot continue to wait on the state" and faulting commenters for not calling about youth deaths and gun violence, according to the outlet. Council President Pro Tem Coleman A. Young II asked the council's Legislative Policy Division to analyze any connection between marijuana tax revenue and kratom use and questioned whether a ban would be enforced as a health matter or through incarceration. District 5 Council Member Renata Miller said she had received an "overwhelming" volume of email, spoke in favor of regulation and "a pause" on the resolution, and noted that the council is the lowest rung in Michigan's kratom lawmaking, BridgeDetroit reported.

The "natural versus synthetic" framing the Detroit commenters used, and the derivatives ban in Cherry's bill, run along the same line the federal government drew on October 9, when [the DEA adopted an HHS recommendation][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] to schedule 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl only above a concentration threshold while leaving natural leaf outside the action. Oklahoma lawmakers [heard the same ban-or-regulate argument][oklahoma-house-interim-study-pogemiller-grego-ocme-cliburn-mitragynine-44-death-certificates-42-deaths-top-five-overdose-drugs-webb-healthy-minds-natural-plant-material-cox-outlaw-all-marti-stoner-more-harm-than-good] at an interim study on October 12. The Legislature's website lists both chambers as adjourned until October 14.
