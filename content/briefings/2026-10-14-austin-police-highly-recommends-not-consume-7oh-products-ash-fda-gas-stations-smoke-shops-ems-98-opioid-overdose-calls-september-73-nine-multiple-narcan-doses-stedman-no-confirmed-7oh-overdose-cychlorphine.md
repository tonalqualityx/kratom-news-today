The Austin Police Department (APD) is warning residents away from concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, the tablets, gummies, drink mixes, and shots sold at gas stations and smoke shops that contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts found in natural kratom leaf. "APD highly recommends that citizens not consume products labeled as containing 7-OH," public information officer Clara Ash said, according to an [October 13, 2026 report](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/austin-police-warn-dangerous-unregulated-194148986.html) by Faith Bugenhagen of the Austin American-Statesman. The warning concerns products marketed around 7-OH rather than natural leaf kratom. The Statesman notes that 7-OH occurs naturally in the kratom plant and that the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has warned against it as an added ingredient or at enhanced levels.

Ash echoed the FDA's position that, because the products are unregulated, consumers cannot know how much of the substance a given product contains, per the report. She listed the symptoms the department associates with a 7-OH overdose as nausea, vomiting, agitation, confusion, rapid heart rate, high blood pressure, trouble breathing, sleepiness or unconsciousness, seizures, and respiratory depression. The FDA has not approved any product containing 7-OH and states that distributing or selling such products is illegal, the Statesman reported.

## No confirmed 7-OH overdose in Austin

The department's warning is not tied to a confirmed case. Ash said officers have found people in possession of products labeled as 7-OH but that APD has not confirmed any overdose linked to the products, according to the Statesman. The report names no stores and describes no local enforcement action.

The warning arrived alongside overdose figures from Austin-Travis County EMS that the agency could not attribute to any one substance. Capt. Christa Stedman said EMS responded to 98 opioid overdose calls last month and that nine of those patients needed more than two doses of Narcan, the naloxone nasal spray, the Statesman reported. In September 2025, by Stedman's account, the agency answered 73 opioid overdose calls, and two patients needed more than two doses. Stedman said one or two doses are typical and that the rise in multi-dose cases is notable, per the report.

## Cychlorphine not confirmed either

The Statesman's report also covers cychlorphine, a synthetic opioid in what the paper describes as the "orphine" family, which has been found mixed into marijuana, cocaine, and other drugs across the United States and linked to overdoses elsewhere. Social media accounts connected to the University of Texas at Austin posted an advisory about cychlorphine late last month urging students to learn about Narcan, the paper reported. Ash said Austin Forensic Science has not confirmed cychlorphine in its casework, and Stedman said EMS could not confirm whether cychlorphine or another substance is behind the increase in multi-dose cases. The report does not state that 7-OH products are responsible for any of the September calls.

The Austin warning lands the same day the Drug Enforcement Administration's notice of intent to schedule 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above a concentration threshold [published in the Federal Register][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline], a step Kratom News Today [covered when it was announced][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. Under that threshold as the DEA describes it, products with more than 1 milligram of 7-OH per gram or per milliliter, or with less than 100 times as much mitragynine as 7-OH, would be treated as Schedule I substances once a temporary order issues, which the statute permits no earlier than 30 days after publication. Texas law separately bars kratom products containing synthetic alkaloids or 7-OH above 2 percent of total alkaloid content, according to a [state-law summary](https://www.recordinglaw.com/us-laws/kratom-laws/texas-kratom-laws/) published by Recording Law on October 11, though the Statesman's report does not address whether any of the products APD has encountered violate that limit.
