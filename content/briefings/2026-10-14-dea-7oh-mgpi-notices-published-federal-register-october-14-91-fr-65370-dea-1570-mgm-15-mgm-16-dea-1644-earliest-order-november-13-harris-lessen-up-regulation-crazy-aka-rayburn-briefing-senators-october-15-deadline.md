The Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) threshold-based notice of intent for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) was published in the Federal Register on October 14, 2026, five days after the Justice Department [announced the new approach][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. The [notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine), filed under Docket No. DEA-1570, begins at 91 FR 65370 in the proposed rules section of the day's issue. Under the threshold it describes, which the DEA attributes to an October 8 letter from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), the two substances would be placed in Schedule I when either one, or both combined, exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the mass of mitragynine in a product divided by the mass of 7-OH, MGPI, or both is less than 100.

The threshold is written around concentration rather than origin. The DEA's notice states that in its natural botanical form 7-OH makes up less than two percent of the total alkaloid content of the leaf or occurs in trace amounts, that both 7-OH and MGPI "have supposedly been found in trace amounts in some botanical kratom products," and that commercial extracts and synthetic formulations contain significantly higher concentrations of 7-OH than botanical Mitragyna speciosa. A product's treatment under the published threshold would turn on its measured alkaloid content.

## What publication changes

The DEA announced the two notices on October 9 and posted them for public inspection the same day, so the text was already public. What changed on October 14 is the clock. Under 21 U.S.C. 811(h), the statute both notices cite, a temporary scheduling order cannot be issued until at least 30 days after the notice of intent is published in the Federal Register, which puts the earliest date for a new order on or after November 13. A temporary order lasts two years and can be extended for one year, according to the notice.

The DEA's August 26 order placing MGPI in Schedule I without a threshold, which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered when it took effect][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds], stays in force in the meantime. The published notice states that the August MGPI order "remains in effect until either it expires or the new temporary order is published and the previous order is withdrawn, whichever occurs first." The notice also repeats the caveat that appeared in the public inspection version: "While the new threshold recommended by HHS would permit certain products to remain in the marketplace without being subject to schedule I controls, neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels."

A [second notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20942/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-mgm-15-and-mgm-16-in-schedule-i), Docket No. DEA-1644, published the same day. According to its summary, the DEA intends to issue a new temporary order for MGM-15 and MGM-16, the two lab-made derivatives, that "will maintain the temporary placement of these substances in schedule I of the CSA without change." The DEA describes that notice as procedural, separating the two compounds from MGPI now that HHS has recommended a threshold for MGPI alone.

## The withdrawal document

KNT reported on October 10 that the 7-OH and MGPI notice refers to the withdrawal of the DEA's [July notice of intent][dea-temporary-schedule-one-high-7oh-products], which used a 0.05 percent threshold, "in a separate document contained in this issue of the Federal Register," and said it would check whether that document appeared. Based on KNT's review of the [Federal Register's listing of DEA documents](https://www.federalregister.gov/api/v1/documents.json?conditions%5Bpublication_date%5D%5Bis%5D=2026-10-14&conditions%5Bagencies%5D%5B%5D=drug-enforcement-administration) for October 14, the issue contains three DEA documents: the 7-OH and MGPI notice, the MGM-15 and MGM-16 notice, and an unrelated registration decision involving a nurse practitioner. No standalone withdrawal document is listed. The published 7-OH and MGPI notice does state in its own text, after citing the July document at 91 FR 40917, that "DEA is withdrawing that notice of intent." KNT could not determine from the published documents whether a separate withdrawal notice will follow or whether the statement inside the new notice is intended to serve that purpose.

## Harris says the approach does not go far enough

The published thresholds drew criticism from a House Republican who wants broader action. Rep. Andy Harris of Maryland told Tony Perkins on the "Washington Watch" program last week that HHS's approach treats kratom as a nutritional supplement, [American Family News reported](https://afn.net/medical-health/2026/10/13/house-republican-sounds-alarm-on-hhs-treatment-of-kratom-warns-of-china-vape-products) on October 13. "They're going to lessen up the regulation on that. This is crazy," Harris said, according to the report. He also said, "We are ruining young people's lives with Kratom and its derivatives," and that "there's nothing nutritional about Kratom," describing kratom and hemp as "pharmacologic agents" that the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) should regulate as such.

Harris said he hoped the senators' letter would prompt the administration to reconsider and that Congress would act if it did not, per the report. The American Family News article also states that kratom-related reports and cases have risen 1,200 percent since 2015, that 14 states have banned or scheduled kratom, and that 23 states have age restrictions, without naming a source for any of the three figures. The article pairs its kratom coverage with Harris's separate call for the Senate to pass House measures against illicit nicotine vapes imported from China.

The letter Harris referred to is the one Sens. Chuck Grassley, Mike Lee, and Susan Collins [sent the DEA and FDA on October 7][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply], which asked the agencies to "consider scheduling the entire kratom plant, including all levels of 7-OH (not simply the threshold limit as stated in the Notice of Intent)," according to the [letter posted by Grassley's office](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/download/grassley-lee-collins-to-dea-fda_-7-oh/). The senators requested a written reply by October 15, which is tomorrow. The published notice does not address the senators' request.

## The AKA briefs Congress the same day

The American Kratom Association (AKA), which [applauded the thresholds on October 9](https://www.americankratom.org/news/dea-and-department-of-justice-scheduling-of-7-oh-and-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl) and said its lawsuit over the August MGPI order had been resolved, scheduled a press conference on the U.S. Capitol grounds for October 13 and a congressional briefing in the Rayburn House Office Building for the morning of October 14, according to its [October 12 release](https://www.eznewswire.com/newsroom/aka-hosts-us-capitol-press-conference-kratom-vs-synthetic-7-oh), which KNT [covered on October 13][american-kratom-association-capitol-press-conference-october-13-rayburn-congressional-briefing-october-14-doj-dea-october-9-thresholds-media-mischaracterizations-ole-miss-synthetics-posing-as-kratom]. The release said the events would explain how the federal action "differentiates botanical kratom from synthetic and chemically manipulated products" and address what the association called media mischaracterizations of botanical kratom in coverage of the University of Mississippi student deaths. The association's senior fellow on public policy, Mac Haddow, is listed as the media contact. As of publication, KNT had not located independent reporting on what was said at the October 13 press conference.

The split on display this week is between two camps that both describe themselves as targeting concentrated 7-OH. The AKA has backed the threshold model and called the October 9 action "an important victory for science," while Harris and the three senators want the entire plant scheduled. The DEA's notice addresses neither position directly beyond the threshold itself and its statement that the agency "still views kratom and its two major psychoactive ingredients, mitragynine and 7-hydroxymtragynine, as drugs of concern," a line KNT quoted from the notice on October 10.

The MGM-15 and MGPI compounds covered by the August order are the same substances a [federal court filing identified][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] in the toxicology of both University of Mississippi students who died September 21, tied to Smax Mango Breeze tablets, a manufactured product distinct from natural leaf kratom, according to Fox News Digital's report on that filing.
