Castle Rock's retail kratom ban is now law. The Town Council voted 6-1 on Tuesday, October 6, 2026 to give final approval to an ordinance prohibiting the retail sale of kratom products for human consumption within the town, [Denver7 reported](https://www.denver7.com/news/local-news/in-your-community/dougco/castle-rock-passes-ordinance-banning-sale-of-kratom). The measure [passed first reading on September 15][castle-rock-colorado-council-votes-6-1-outlaw-retail-kratom-sales-possession-legal] by the same margin. "The ban will officially go into effect in November," Denver7's Tyler Melito wrote, and it applies only to sales, not to possession or use. The [Castle Rock News-Press](https://www.castlerocknewspress.net/news/article_ae1bb305-b165-4c6c-971b-2e53d8807d6b.html) reported that selling kratom in town could bring a fine of up to $2,650 and up to one year in jail, and that the ordinance cites public health, safety, and welfare as its basis and describes itself as a "blanket prohibition" on retail sales following warnings from medical authorities and federal agencies.

The ordinance replaces a 2019 code amendment that barred sales to minors but allowed sales to adults. Councilmember Max Brooks, who backed the ban, said the town probably could have enacted a full prohibition in 2019 but synthetic forms of kratom were not yet prevalent, according to Denver7. "We're making decisions based on the evolution of this product right now, and doing so to ensure that we're keeping our citizens safe," Brooks said. "If you're putting natural kratom in tea, that's not where these bad actors are making their money."

## What changed between the readings

The two Castle Rock votes bracketed the September 21 deaths of two University of Mississippi students in Oxford, one of them 18-year-old freshman Aidan Hamilton of Englewood, Colorado, in the Denver metro area. Denver7 reported that toxicology in court filings against the father and son charged in the deaths showed both students had the same three substances in their systems, all found in Smax Mango Breeze pills, and that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) says those pills "do not contain standard, raw kratom" and instead contain highly concentrated isolated kratom compounds. The [federal filing, as reported by Fox News Digital][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond], listed mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, the latter two being lab-made compounds the DEA placed in Schedule I in August. The tablets are a manufactured product distinct from natural leaf kratom, a distinction Denver7 said opponents of the ordinance raised, calling the pills "very different than what some people use regarding kratom."

Brooks invoked the death in the final debate. "We've got data and there's a dead kid coming out of Englewood, Colorado," he said, according to the News-Press. Mayor Pro Tem Laura Cavey said, "But at least it's not here and it's not on our shelves." Brooks also told Denver7 the measure "originated from constituents, this originated from moms, dads in Castle Rock that were worried about what their kids were able to access," that "there were some pretty sad stories of residents right here in Castle Rock of kids that had fallen ill from using kratom," and that "one vendor here in Castle Rock went so far as to call it gas station heroin." He said some stores that used to sell kratom have already taken it off their shelves.

Resident Dana Bonham, speaking in support at the October 6 meeting, said, "A teenager at a checkout counter isn't reading lab reports, and neither are most parents. A clear line protects our kids better than a blurry one." Another supporter, unnamed in the News-Press account, said the products "sit on the counters of gas stations, smoke shops, often in packaging that appeals to young people," and that "this ordinance does not punish anyone for personal use."

## The dissent and the opposition

Councilmember Kevin Bracken cast the only no vote at both readings. "We have allowed kratom sales within the town of Castle Rock for all these years and now we have businesses that are selling kratom, and I'm supportive of the natural leaf, not the synthetic," Bracken said, according to Denver7. "I just don't feel it's right to change the deal on the business owners." At first reading in September, Bracken had said he would back a ban limited to synthetic products.

Opponents made the same leaf-versus-synthetic argument through a month of emails, written comments, and testimony, the News-Press reported. A resident who described herself as a toxicologist and pharmacist told the council that "there is no oversight on the amount of synthetic kratom in these substances," that natural kratom is a safe herbal supplement, and that the concern is products with high levels of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), which she described as a potent opioid compound found in natural kratom. Natural leaf contains 7-OH only in trace amounts; the concentrated and synthetic preparations at issue in enforcement actions contain it at far higher levels. A representative of the American Kratom Association also argued that traditional kratom differs from synthetic versions, per the News-Press, which did not name the representative. Alaina Newhouse, whose family owns Goodfellas Smoke Shop in Castle Rock, wrote in an emailed comment that kratom is a large part of the store's inventory and that "banning kratom in Castle Rock will have dangerous consequences, forcing people in our community to outsource ways to handle their pain," according to Denver7. An unnamed opponent at the meeting said, "Kratom is not a drug. Kratom is not an opiate. Kratom is not a synthetic substance."

Castle Rock joins Parker, Greenwood Village, and Monument among Colorado municipalities that prohibit kratom sales, the News-Press reported. At the state level, the Daniel Bregger Act passed in 2025 requires labeling and dosing information, bans synthetic and semi-synthetic alkaloids, caps 7-OH content, requires products to be kept behind a counter, and bars sales to anyone under 21, per the News-Press. The Colorado attorney general's office [settled with two distributors under that law][colorado-attorney-general-weiser-152000-settlement-dnb-distro-million-smoke-n-vape-7oh-limit-labeling] in September.

## A coroner's count

Separately, El Paso County Coroner and Chief Medical Examiner Dr. Emily Russell-Kinsley told [KOAA News5](https://www.koaa.com/news/local-news/colorado-mother-warns-of-kratom-dangers-after-19-year-old-sons-death) that 41 deaths in Colorado in 2025 were associated with kratom or mitragynine products, in a report on the May 31 death of 19-year-old Masen Campbell of Severance. KOAA reported that Campbell's autopsy found kratom toxicity as the cause of death and that 7-OH was found in his system; his mother, Ashley Campbell, said a friend gave him half of a pink 40 milligram pill whose label said to take 10 milligrams, and that the combination with alcohol killed him. The station did not name the product or say whether it was a concentrated 7-OH preparation. Russell-Kinsley said 7-OH in such products is manufactured and present in higher amounts than occurs naturally, that pure kratom or mitragynine carries a low overdose risk in her assessment, and that products with added alkaloids like 7-OH are more dangerous and difficult to dose. "That's actually much stronger than morphine," she said of 7-OH. Her description tracks the [Yampa Valley deaths KNT covered in September][yampa-valley-deaths-concentrated-7oh-tablets-colorado-shops], where concentrated 7-OH tablets of 60 and 80 milligrams were involved.

The Castle Rock vote was one of three state or local moves this week tied to the Oxford deaths. Georgia's attorney general [called for a default ban on all kratom-derived products][georgia-attorney-general-carr-ajc-op-ed-default-ban-all-kratom-derived-products-approved-list-contraband-seizure-poison-center-lopez-315-calls-four-deaths-2026-wsb-synthetic-20-to-30-times-more-potent], and Mississippi's state health officer [said he will seek Schedule III classification in 2027][mississippi-state-health-officer-edney-2027-session-schedule-iii-kratom-fda-eliminate-from-private-stores-penalties-on-sellers-hosemann-full-ban-yancey-statewide-prohibition-clarion-ledger].
