Mississippi's top public health official has put a specific proposal on the table for the 2027 session, and it stops short of the full ban the state's lieutenant governor and attorney general have called for. State Health Officer Dr. Daniel Edney told the [Clarion Ledger](https://www.clarionledger.com/story/news/politics/2026/10/14/will-ms-legislature-ban-kratom-after-ole-miss-student-deaths/92246433007/) after the Board of Health's quarterly meeting on October 14, 2026 that kratom regulation is one of his most important goals for next year, and that he wants the Legislature to classify kratom as a Schedule III controlled substance and place it under Food and Drug Administration (FDA) oversight, state government reporter Bea Anhuci wrote. Edney said that approach would "eliminate it from those private stores," impose heavy penalties on sellers, and avoid punishing consumers harshly.

"My main position, and the Board of Health agrees, at the very least," Edney said, is "can we all agree to get this stuff out of the gas stations and vape stores?" He told the paper that he and his colleagues, as addiction physicians, deal with the drug and its "nasty detox" daily. "I have grave concerns today of what's in even the plant packages," he said. "No one knows what you get." He added that "we cannot say the kratom supply in the state of Mississippi is safe."

## Schedule III as the ceiling

Edney described Schedule III as the strongest classification he would support. "I think everybody agrees it should not be any stronger than schedule III," he said, and the FDA should decide whether any legitimate use exists. The paper reported that he compared the structure to Mississippi's medical cannabis program, which it said brings in $15 million to $20 million in profit annually, while Edney said he would not go that far for kratom. "I wouldn't advocate people having to get kratom cards and all that but, you know, at least make it safe," he said.

The position is consistent with what Edney told the House Drug Policy Committee in early 2025, when he said he favored Schedule III under state law because it would remove kratom from stores while imposing relatively small penalties for possession, as Kratom News Today (KNT) [noted last week][mississippi-lt-gov-hosemann-op-ed-time-to-ban-all-kratom-products-edney-testified-all-kratom-should-be-banned-80-percent-deaths-other-substances-whippets-kosciusko-aldermen-ordinance]. It sits differently from how Lt. Gov. Delbert Hosemann characterized Edney's more recent testimony. In his [op-ed calling for a complete ban](https://magnoliatribune.com/2026/10/08/hosemann-it-is-time-for-mississippi-to-ban-all-kratom-products/), which ran in the Magnolia Tribune on October 8 and, according to the Clarion Ledger, in that paper on October 13, Hosemann wrote that "Dr. Dan Edney, Mississippi's State Health Officer, testified last Thursday that all kratom should be banned," and said he shares that view. The Clarion Ledger described Hosemann's call for Mississippi to join nine other states, including Alabama, in banning kratom outright as more aggressive than Edney's plan. Edney's October 14 remarks do not address the testimony Hosemann cited, and the Clarion Ledger did not ask him to reconcile the two. Whether a Schedule III placement that removes kratom from retail stores amounts to a ban in practice, or leaves a lawful channel open through FDA oversight, is the distinction Edney drew and the one the Legislature would have to resolve.

Attorney General Lynn Fitch, the third statewide official to weigh in, said October 1 that her office will promote legislation to ban kratom "in all forms," as KNT [reported][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc]. The Clarion Ledger story does not quote Fitch.

## Yancey and the 2027 session

Rep. Lee Yancey, R-Brandon, has sponsored most of Mississippi's kratom legislation since 2022, according to the paper. His 2022 bill would have placed kratom in Schedule I; it passed the House and died in the Senate, and similar attempts failed in the two following years. In 2025 the Legislature passed his law prohibiting sales to anyone under 21 and banning the sale of synthetic kratom products. On September 25, four days after the deaths of two University of Mississippi students in Oxford, Yancey posted on social media that the deaths show it is time to investigate "whether statewide prohibition should be considered in 2027," the paper reported. It also noted that Yancey's kratom push may become a signature issue in his bid to replace retiring Sen. Dean Kirby, R-Pearl.

The Clarion Ledger said kratom entered public attention in Mississippi in late September after the two deaths, and that Lafayette County officers reported finding packaged kratom in both investigations, the finding KNT [covered on September 23][two-university-of-mississippi-students-dead-oxford-packaged-kratom-found-metro-narcotics-no-confirmed-link]. The paper's October 14 story does not identify the product. A federal court filing reported by Fox News Digital on October 12 [listed mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] in both students' toxicology and tied the deaths to Smax Mango Breeze tablets, a manufactured product containing compounds the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) describes as lab-made and placed in Schedule I in August, and distinct from natural leaf kratom. Edney's comments about "the plant packages" address natural leaf products directly. As the paper describes it, his proposal would cover kratom generally, which would reach the leaf products Mississippi's 2025 law currently permits for adults 21 and older, rather than only the synthetic products that law already bans.

The Clarion Ledger reported that kratom industry lobbyists and some veteran advocacy groups have pressed lawmakers nationwide to keep kratom legal and accessible. The story does not quote the American Kratom Association or the Global Kratom Coalition, both of which have said they support restrictions on synthetic products while opposing bans on natural leaf, and KNT found no response from either group to Edney's proposal as of publication.

Mississippi was one of three states where officials moved this week toward broader restrictions following the Oxford deaths. Georgia's attorney general [called for a default ban on all kratom-derived products][georgia-attorney-general-carr-ajc-op-ed-default-ban-all-kratom-derived-products-approved-list-contraband-seizure-poison-center-lopez-315-calls-four-deaths-2026-wsb-synthetic-20-to-30-times-more-potent] in an op-ed, and Castle Rock, Colorado, south of Denver, citing the death of the student from nearby Englewood, [gave final approval to a retail sales ban][castle-rock-colorado-ordinance-final-passage-october-6-retail-kratom-sales-ban-effective-november-2650-fine-364-days-brooks-dead-kid-englewood-bracken-natural-leaf-not-synthetic].
