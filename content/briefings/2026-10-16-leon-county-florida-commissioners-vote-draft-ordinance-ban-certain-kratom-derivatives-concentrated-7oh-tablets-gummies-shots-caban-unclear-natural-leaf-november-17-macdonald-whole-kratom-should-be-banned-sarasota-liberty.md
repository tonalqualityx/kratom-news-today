The Leon County Commission voted Tuesday night, October 13, 2026, to have staff draft an ordinance banning "certain derivatives" of kratom, [WCTV's Jay Harrison reported](https://www.wctv.tv/2026/10/14/leon-county-commissioners-vote-draft-ordinance-banning-certain-derivatives-kratom/) on October 14. Commissioner Christian Caban, whose office brought the item, told the station the main goal is to take "more concentrated, synthetic forms of the product off the shelves," and WCTV identified the target as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products sold as tablets, gummies, and shots. Those are manufactured preparations in which 7-OH is concentrated far beyond the trace amounts found in natural kratom leaf, a distinction the WCTV report draws explicitly. The station did not report a vote count.

The scope of the eventual ordinance is unsettled. According to WCTV, Caban said "it is unclear if the final wording will include natural, raw forms of kratom." The draft is expected to return to the commission at its November 17 meeting, with a goal of finalizing it by the end of the year, the station reported.

## What prompted the item and who spoke

WCTV reported that Caban's office raised the issue over concerns about addiction, regulation, and the lack of Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval. The station's report describes kratom as commonly sold as leaves, pills, gummies, or liquids, says in its own words that it is used "for pain relief, anxiety and to treat withdrawal symptoms," and notes the FDA's warning that regular or excessive use can cause addiction and other serious medical problems. Those characterizations are WCTV's and the FDA's; the station did not cite a specific FDA document.

Two speakers at the meeting urged a full ban rather than a derivatives-only ordinance, according to WCTV. One was Dana Macdonald, whose son died in Tallahassee last year in what the station described as a kratom overdose. Macdonald told commissioners that losing a child's life deserves a major discussion even when the right decision is unpopular, and said, "The whole kratom should be banned." The WCTV report does not specify whether the product involved in her son's death was a concentrated 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom, and it does not describe a medical examiner's finding. The report includes no speaker opposing the ordinance and no comment from a kratom retailer, manufacturer, or advocacy organization.

## Where Florida law already stands on 7-OH

WCTV's report states that "in August 2026, the state added additional, specified 7-OH products and related compounds into Schedule I drug classification." That date does not match the record in the attorney general's own announcements, and the station did not cite a document. Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier's office [announced an emergency rule on June 22, 2026](https://www.myfloridalegal.com/newsrelease/attorney-general-james-uthmeier-signs-emergency-rule-immediately-scheduling-dangerous-7) placing 7-OH in Schedule I under state law, [expanding an earlier emergency order that had been set to expire at the end of June][florida-emergency-rule-7oh-schedule-one]. KNT could not identify an August 2026 Florida scheduling action matching WCTV's description. Separately, the Florida Kratom Consumer Protection Act, [section 500.92 of the Florida Statutes](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0500-0599/0500/Sections/0500.92.html), makes it unlawful to sell kratom products to anyone under 21 and defines a kratom product to include leaf, extracts, and synthetic alkaloids. WCTV did not report whether commissioners or county attorneys discussed how a county ordinance would interact with the state's Schedule I rule or the state statute.

Commissioners also reviewed ordinances adopted in Sarasota and Liberty counties, WCTV reported, without describing their terms. Elsewhere in Florida, Seminole County Sheriff Dennis Lemma [urged the Legislature last week to outlaw kratom entirely][seminole-county-sheriff-dennis-lemma-urges-florida-legislature-outlaw-kratom-wkmg-kristie-dijak-williston-partner-attributes-death-7oh-overdose-smith-gas-station-heroin], a position that, like Macdonald's, extends to leaf kratom as well as concentrated derivatives.

## Local governments acting before the federal decision

Leon County is one of several local bodies that have taken up kratom ordinances this month while the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) 7-OH scheduling remains pending. According to the DEA's [notice of intent for 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine), [published in the Federal Register on October 14][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline], the notice's 30-day period puts the earliest date for a temporary order on or after November 13, four days before the Leon County draft is due back. Castle Rock, Colorado [gave final approval to a retail sales ban][castle-rock-colorado-ordinance-final-passage-october-6-retail-kratom-sales-ban-effective-november-2650-fine-364-days-brooks-dead-kid-englewood-bracken-natural-leaf-not-synthetic] on October 6, and Franklin County, Georgia commissioners [planned a vote on barring kratom-derived products from convenience stores][franklin-county-georgia-commissioners-plan-vote-ban-kratom-derived-products-convenience-stores-amended-alcohol-ordinance-hart-county-sayer-regional-effort-7oh-mgm-15-mgm-16] through an amended alcohol ordinance. The same week as the Leon County vote, Missouri's attorney general [issued nine more civil investigative demands][missouri-attorney-general-hanaway-nine-more-civil-investigative-demands-31-total-cease-and-desist-kc-distro-karmic-genesis-concentrated-7oh-schedule-i-analogue-missouri-adopted-mgpi-mgm-15-september-25-vapor-maven-joplin] over concentrated 7-OH products.

The question Caban left open, whether the ordinance will stop at concentrated derivatives or extend to leaf, is left to the November 17 draft. The American Kratom Association, in a policy advisory [earlier this year][aka-urges-ag-enforcement-7oh-florida-ban-expiry], urged state attorneys general to enforce against concentrated 7-OH products sold as kratom, with senior adviser Mac Haddow saying "7-OH is not kratom" and describing those products as chemically manipulated rather than natural leaf preparations. No advocacy organization was quoted in the WCTV report, and KNT has not located a public statement from the association on the Leon County vote.
