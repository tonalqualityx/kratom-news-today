Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway has added nine companies to her office's investigation into concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and MGM-15 products, according to a [release from the attorney general's office](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-investigates-nine-additional-companies-over-7-oh-products/) dated October 15, 2026. The office issued nine new civil investigative demands (CIDs), which it said brings its total to 31 demands directed at kratom manufacturers, distributors, and retailers. It also sent cease-and-desist orders to two of the nine, KC Distro and Karmic Genesis, "demanding they stop selling 7-OH products immediately."

The products at issue are preparations the release describes as containing "added or isolated 7-OH" at "artificially elevated levels," marketed as supplements or wellness products. That is a category distinct from natural kratom leaf, in which 7-OH occurs only in trace amounts, according to the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) [October 14 Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine).

"Missourians are being led to believe that 7-OH, MGM-15, and similar chemicals are safe supplements. They are not. Do not consume these deadly products," Hanaway said in the release. She described the products as illegal and dangerous drugs and said people, including children, are being harmed. No specific adverse event, death, or poison-center report tied to the nine companies appears in the release.

## How the office says concentrated 7-OH becomes a Schedule I analogue in Missouri

The release lays out a legal theory that extends state controlled-substance law to 7-OH products without a 7-OH-specific scheduling action. According to the office, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15 "were added to Schedule I through an emergency DEA order on August 26, 2026, and Missouri adopted the designation on September 25, 2026." The release then states that "7-OH, in high concentrations, has a similar effect to MGM-15, and has a similar chemical structure to that drug," and concludes that "products with artificially elevated levels of 7-OH are Schedule I controlled substance analogues subject to criminal penalties in Missouri."

That position is the attorney general's reading of Missouri's analogue provisions. No court decision adopting that reading is cited, and the office did not say whether it intends to pursue criminal referrals or civil enforcement under the Missouri Merchandising Practices Act, which it names as the statute the nine companies "or others associated with them" may have violated along with "other state and federal laws." The DEA's August 26 temporary order covered three compounds, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, according to the agency's Federal Register filings; the Missouri release mentions only the first two.

According to the DEA's Federal Register filings, the August 26 order did not cover 7-OH, and the agency's action on 7-OH remains at the notice-of-intent stage. In its notice of intent for 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, [published in the Federal Register on October 14][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline], the agency proposes to place products above 1 milligram per gram in Schedule I, and the notice states that a temporary order may issue no sooner than 30 days after publication, which falls on November 13. The release does not tie the office's analogue position to the federal notice.

## The nine companies

The release lists the recipients of the new demands as:

- KC Distro, LLC, Independence, Missouri
- Main Street Traders, LLC, doing business as Main Street Smoke Shop, Kansas City, Missouri
- Spark Wholesale LLC, doing business as Spark Tobacco & Vape, Saint Peters, Missouri
- Cloud Wholesale LLC, Saint Louis, Missouri
- S&A Wholesale, Inc., Saint Louis, Missouri
- JSST Enterprises LLC, doing business as Ultimate Vapor, Desloge, Missouri
- AK Wholesale, Inc., Bensenville, Illinois
- Karmic Genesis LLC, Cape Girardeau, Missouri
- Vapor Maven MO LLC, Joplin, Missouri

The Turner Report, a Joplin-area news blog written by Randy Turner, [reported the inclusion of Vapor Maven MO](https://rturner229.blogspot.com/2026/10/attorney-general-announces.html) on October 15 and reproduced the office's statement. Neither the release nor the Turner Report includes a response from any of the nine companies, and KNT has not independently confirmed whether the companies have been served or have replied.

## What the demands require

According to the release, the CIDs require information about kratom products sold in Missouri, the alkaloid levels in those products, "the presence of added or isolated 7-OH," and the methods used to manufacture, test, source, and distribute them. The office said it is also seeking records of communications with regulators, consumer complaints and adverse-event reports, and any safety or dosage claims the companies made. The release says the office "will continue to identify companies selling products containing concentrated psychoactive compounds, including 7-OH," and describes those substances as acting "on the brain in ways similar to traditional opioids."

The demands ask about added or isolated 7-OH and about alkaloid levels. The DEA's October 14 notice, for its part, uses a 1 milligram per gram threshold and a 100-to-1 mitragynine-to-7-OH ratio, which the agency says is meant to separate leaf material from concentrated and synthesized products. Whether any of the nine companies sold leaf products goes unstated, and nothing in the release indicates that leaf products are a target of the demands.

## Where this sits in the Missouri investigation

In July, Hanaway [added 10 entities across the supply chain][missouri-ag-expands-kratom-investigation-american-shaman-suspends-sales] as Shaman Botanicals, described by the office as the state's largest kratom distributor, agreed to suspend in-state sales. In August, the office [barred The Green Dragon and its owners from any Missouri business selling 7-OH or kratom products][missouri-ag-bars-green-dragon-kratom-7oh-sales] through an Assurance of Voluntary Compliance filed in Cole County Circuit Court, following similar agreements with CBD American Shaman, EDP Kratom, and VEGA 7X.

The release's statement that Missouri adopted the federal MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl designation on September 25 had not previously appeared in KNT's coverage. How the adoption was carried out, whether through an emergency rule, a statutory trigger, or another mechanism, is not described, and KNT has not located the underlying Missouri instrument.

MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl are the same compounds Utah officials [identified in Buzzers Cat's Claw tablets that, according to the Utah Department of Health and Human Services, caused four deaths][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] earlier this month. The Missouri release does not reference those products or any specific brand.

## Where the American Kratom Association stands

The American Kratom Association has supported restrictions on concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products while opposing bans on natural leaf kratom, and in a policy advisory earlier this year it [urged state attorneys general to enforce against concentrated 7-OH products sold as kratom][aka-urges-ag-enforcement-7oh-florida-ban-expiry], with senior adviser Mac Haddow stating that "7-OH is not kratom." The association had not issued a statement specific to the October 15 Missouri action in the coverage reviewed for this briefing. The release does not mention natural leaf kratom.

## Other action on concentrated 7-OH this week

In Florida, Leon County commissioners [voted October 13 to have staff draft a local ordinance banning certain kratom derivatives][leon-county-florida-commissioners-vote-draft-ordinance-ban-certain-kratom-derivatives-concentrated-7oh-tablets-gummies-shots-caban-unclear-natural-leaf-november-17-macdonald-whole-kratom-should-be-banned-sarasota-liberty], with Commissioner Christian Caban telling [WCTV](https://www.wctv.tv/2026/10/14/leon-county-commissioners-vote-draft-ordinance-banning-certain-derivatives-kratom/) the goal is to remove concentrated, synthetic 7-OH tablets, gummies, and shots from shelves while leaving open whether the final wording reaches natural leaf. The draft returns to that commission on November 17.
