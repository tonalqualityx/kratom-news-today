Two more Michigan townships moved against kratom sales this week, one with a ban and one with an age limit, while the statewide bill that would settle the question remains parked in the Senate.

In Wayne County, the Plymouth Township Board of Trustees voted unanimously on Tuesday, October 13, 2026 to advance an ordinance that would prohibit kratom sales throughout the township, [WDIV reported](https://www.clickondetroit.com/news/local/2026/10/16/plymouth-township-moves-to-ban-kratom-sales-amid-addiction-teen-safety-concerns/) on October 15. According to the station, the ordinance carries three levels of civil fines "starting between $1,000 and $2,500 and ranging up to $7,500," and businesses that still refuse to comply could face misdemeanor charges. The vote was an advancement rather than final passage; the final vote is scheduled for October 27.

In Macomb County, the Chesterfield Township Board of Trustees passed the first reading of an ordinance that would limit kratom sales to buyers 21 and older, the [Macomb Daily reported](https://www.macombdaily.com/2026/10/16/chesterfield-township-officials-vote-to-regulate-kratom/) on October 16. The paper described Chesterfield as the fifth Macomb County community to address kratom sales this year. The ordinance would take effect if a second reading is approved next month, according to the report.

Neither report says whether the ordinance text distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in raw leaf. WDIV's report refers to "kratom and its derivative 7-OH" in its background on addiction concerns, and the Macomb Daily's summary of Food and Drug Administration (FDA) material notes that the compound "is sometimes concentrated or synthesized in products." Whether either township's ordinance reaches one category, the other, or both is not stated in the coverage, and KNT has not obtained the ordinance texts.

## Plymouth Township: the police chief's case

Police Chief James Knittel is the ordinance's public advocate, according to WDIV. He told the station that mixing kratom with other substances "can be hazardous and it can be potentially deadly," and that the department has seen two local incidents in which "both times there was addiction issues similar to heroin." The report does not describe the two incidents further or say what products were involved.

Knittel framed the ordinance around young buyers. "The area that we're really concerned about is high school teenagers as well as college students," he told WDIV. He said the products are often placed alongside nutritional supplements and energy drinks, and that "many times the kratom piece is written very small on the package, so they might not even see it." The station's report does not include comment from any retailer, trade association, or resident opposed to the ban, and does not say whether public comment was taken at the October 13 meeting.

WDIV noted that neighboring Canton and Clinton Township have already passed ordinances regulating kratom sales and barring sales to anyone under 21. Canton's trustees [advanced their ordinance in September][canton-township-trustees-advance-kratom-ordinance-21-plus-500-foot-buffer] with a 500-foot buffer from schools and parks and provisions addressing synthetic and high-potency products, following a joint inspection that seized kratom and concentrated 7-OH products from a township tobacco shop. Plymouth's proposal, as described by WDIV, goes further than Canton's by banning sales outright.

## Chesterfield Township: the fifth in Macomb County

According to the Macomb Daily, Chesterfield's ordinance would put it alongside Clinton Township and Mount Clemens, which limit sales to buyers 21 and older, while Sterling Heights and Warren have banned sales outright. Warren's City Council [approved the first reading of its ban in July][warren-michigan-kratom-ban-north-dakota-special-session] and gave final approval in August, according to the paper. "We want people to be safe," Warren council President Mindy Moore told the Macomb Daily. "We had so many complaints that it was causing people problems and people were becoming addicted."

Chesterfield Supervisor Brad Kersten said in a written statement quoted by the paper that kratom "has been linked to fatal overdoses across the state, with additional concerns related to addiction, contamination, and other adverse health effects." He added: "Fortunately, this is not an issue that has affected the youth in our community at this point, but we are taking a proactive step to prohibit this unsafe substance from being purchased by minors in the township." The statement does not cite a source for the overdose link.

The paper reported one voice asking for more. Carol Grant of Harrison Township, who the Macomb Daily said spoke at two public meetings in favor of banning kratom, said she was pleased Chesterfield was moving to regulate it but added, "They should totally ban it." The paper characterized people who work in substance-use treatment as opposed to kratom's use and sale, without naming any beyond Grant.

## The statewide bills have not moved

Both reports place the local actions against a stalled state process. According to WDIV, House Bill 5537, which would ban kratom sales statewide, passed the Michigan House in March and awaits a Senate vote, while Senate Bill 1181, introduced in September, would ban synthetic 7-OH products, require clear labeling, and mandate third-party lab testing, and has not been voted on. The Macomb Daily likewise reported that the House-passed ban "has stalled in the state Senate."

KNT reported earlier this month that the [Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center logged 347 kratom and 7-OH cases from 2021 through 2025][michigan-poison-center-347-kratom-7oh-cases-2021-2025-87-in-2025-81-percent-vohra-science-informed-framework-not-blanket-ban-hb-5537-sb-1181-cherry-detroit-council-resolution-committee], with its senior director telling Axios Detroit that regulation "should be based on a science-informed framework" rather than a blanket ban, and that the Detroit City Council sent a resolution supporting HB 5537 back to committee after commenters urged regulation of natural leaf instead. As WDIV described them, the two bills represent that split: HB 5537 would ban the plant, and SB 1181 would regulate leaf and ban the synthetic derivatives. The American Kratom Association, which has supported bans on synthetic 7-OH products while opposing bans on natural leaf, had not commented on either township action in the coverage reviewed for this briefing.

With no state law in effect, the practical result for Michigan retailers is a township-by-township map. Plymouth's final vote comes October 27, and Chesterfield's second reading is expected in November.
