Three agencies served search warrants on October 14 at seven Colorado Springs-area stores that share an owner, and the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) listed "7-OH product" alongside THC, psilocybin, and cash among the items seized, [The Gazette reported](https://gazette.com/2026/10/14/police-raid-businesses-believed-to-have-illegally-sold-thc-psilocybin-3/) in a story by Cleo Westin. The 7-OH reference points to concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural kratom leaf. Neither The Gazette nor [KRDO](https://krdo.com/news/2026/10/14/speedys-smoke-shop-and-los-compadres-liquor-store-raided-accused-of-selling-thc-and-psilocybin-to-minors/), which also covered the raids, described the form, quantity, or concentration of the 7-OH items, and neither report stated the legal basis on which they were taken. The THC and psilocybin were the stated focus of the investigation.

## The stores, the agencies, and the tip

The warrants covered five Speedys Smoke Shop locations and two Los Compadres Liquor stores, one of them in unincorporated El Paso County, according to The Gazette, which named the DEA Rocky Mountain Field Division, the Colorado Springs Police Department (CSPD), and the El Paso County Sheriff's Office as the agencies involved. KRDO's Danny Mata reported that the investigation began in May 2025, when a shipping company tipped CSPD to suspicious packaging, and that the department's Metro Task Force later recovered more than 200 pounds of THC and psilocybin products addressed to the Speedys store at 3960 Airport Road. The Gazette said the packages came from New York. None of the stores holds a marijuana license, and investigators believe THC vapes and edibles were sold without one, the paper reported, adding that investigators are examining whether psilocybin was sold illegally at the same locations.

"We seized a lot of evidence today," CSPD's Ira Cronin told KRDO. "It's going to take us a few days to really tally it up." He added, "The sky's the limit here based on initially what we found today." No arrests had been made as of the reports, and Cronin said that could change.

## The minor behind the counter

Police allege some of the locations sold to minors. "At one of the locations we hit today, we found a 16-year-old juvenile" alone in the store working behind the counter, Cronin told KRDO. The Gazette described the person as a minor without giving an age. The store owner, who is not named in either report, told KRDO the teenager is her daughter and had been covering for about an hour after another worker called out sick. She did not respond to the station's questions about the minor-sales allegations and said by text, "We're a serious business and are working/cooperating to resolve this matter," adding that the business wants a better understanding of which rules apply to which type of license.

The DEA's Rocky Mountain division said on X that "many of the seized items are blatantly marketed to those under 21 and young adults" and that "the investigation continues," The Gazette reported. Special Agent in Charge Mark Putnam said in a release quoted by the paper that "operations like this show DEA's commitment to making sure customers are safe."

## Where 7-OH sits in Colorado law

Colorado's Daniel Bregger Act caps 7-OH at 2 percent of a kratom product's total alkaloids, Colorado Politics reported in September, and that cap was the basis on which Attorney General Phil Weiser [reached a $152,000 settlement][colorado-attorney-general-weiser-152000-settlement-dnb-distro-million-smoke-n-vape-7oh-limit-labeling] with the owners of a wholesale distributor and 15 smoke shops over products exceeding it. Whether the Colorado Springs seizure rests on that cap, on the products' marketing, or on something else is not stated in either report. At the federal level, a Congressional Research Service sidebar [summarized by Legis1 this week][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] states that 7-OH is not a controlled substance absent final action by the DEA or Congress, and the DEA's October 14 notice of intent sets November 13 as the earliest date a temporary order could issue.

Local governments in Colorado have acted on retail kratom this fall. Castle Rock's council [gave final passage on October 6 to a retail sales ban][castle-rock-colorado-ordinance-final-passage-october-6-retail-kratom-sales-ban-effective-november-2650-fine-364-days-brooks-dead-kid-englewood-bracken-natural-leaf-not-synthetic] that takes effect in November, and [deaths in the Yampa Valley linked to concentrated 7-OH tablets][yampa-valley-deaths-concentrated-7oh-tablets-colorado-shops] drew attention to what smoke shops in the state were stocking in September. The Colorado Springs reports do not say whether any natural-leaf kratom product was among the items taken.
