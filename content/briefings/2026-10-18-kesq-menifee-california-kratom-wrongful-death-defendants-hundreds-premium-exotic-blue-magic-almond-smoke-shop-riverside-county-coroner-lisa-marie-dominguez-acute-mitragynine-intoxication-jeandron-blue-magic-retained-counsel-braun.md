The wrongful-death suit [filed in September over a Menifee, California woman's death][family-lawsuit-kratom-death-menifee-california] names Hundreds Premium LLC, doing business as Exotic Blue Magic, and Almond Smoke Shop in Menifee as defendants, [KESQ News Channel 3 reported](https://kesq.com/news/crime/stronger-together/2026/10/16/family-sues-kratom-manufacturer-and-retailer-after-riverside-county-mothers-death/) on October 16 in a segment by Karen Devine. The initial ABC7 coverage did not identify the defendants, the product, or the decedent. KESQ's report fills in all three: Lisa Marie Dominguez, 55, died in July 2025, the product was Exotic Blue Magic kratom capsules her family says she bought at the smoke shop, and the Riverside County coroner attributed her death to acute mitragynine intoxication, according to the complaint as the station describes it.

The reporting does not say whether the capsules were natural leaf kratom, an extract, or a product with added 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The coroner's finding names mitragynine, the principal alkaloid in natural kratom leaf, rather than 7-OH, and KESQ states that the complaint does not establish that the capsules contained added or enhanced 7-OH. That leaves the product category open in a way that distinguishes this case from the concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products at the center of current federal scheduling.

## The allegations and the defense's first move

The plaintiffs are Dominguez's three children, including her daughter Lauren Dominguez, who told KESQ she found her mother after unanswered calls and texts. The complaint alleges unsafe mitragynine levels, inadequate warnings, and misleading claims about the product's safety, allegations the station noted have not been proven in court. The report does not give the court or a case number.

"They're making it seem like it's all just a happy, delicious herbal supplement. And they're not telling the truth," family attorney Michael Jeandron told the station. He said the toxicology and autopsy findings support the family's claim. On the defendants' response, Jeandron said, "Blue Magic has responded. They've hired a lawyer. They've reached out to me," and that the company had not filed a formal answer at the time of the interview. KESQ did not report a statement from either defendant.

Lauren Dominguez said her mother described the capsules as an over-the-counter herbal supplement. "She took it because she said that it made her feel good, and she said it was just an over-the-counter herbal supplement," she told the station, adding, "You shouldn't profit from something that causes harm like this." KESQ reported that earlier ABC7 coverage identified chronic alcohol use as a contributing factor in the coroner's report.

## What the station put around the case

KESQ quoted Michael Braun, a former DEA chief of operations, who said kratom "contains a couple of pretty powerful alkaloids" and that a buyer is "really playing Russian Roulette because you have no earthly idea, you know, what is contained in that packaging." The station also summarized the Food and Drug Administration's position: the agency distinguishes products with added or enhanced 7-OH from the trace amounts naturally present in leaf, describes concentrated 7-OH products as potent opioid products it advises consumers to avoid, and warns against kratom use generally, citing risks it lists as liver toxicity, seizures, and substance use disorder. The segment is the second installment of a 12-part series the station is producing with Mothers for Awareness and Prevention of Drug Abuse.

## A second California mitragynine case

The Dominguez complaint joins [Soria v. Ashlynn Marketing Group][soria-v-ashlynn-marketing-group-krave-kratom-wrongful-death-los-angeles-county-acute-mitragynine-intoxication-100x-200x-extracts-california-superior-court-aboutlawsuits], filed in California Superior Court on September 30 over a Los Angeles County death that complaint also describes as acute mitragynine intoxication. Both complaints, as reported, cite acute mitragynine intoxication as the cause of death and center on warnings and marketing language; the Soria complaint also alleges undisclosed 7-OH levels in extract products, while the Dominguez reporting makes no 7-OH allegation. That puts the two suits on different footing from the enforcement actions and legislation aimed at concentrated 7-OH, a distinction is the one the American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow pressed in [an interview with Gizmodo published this week][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled], where he said "the natural plant has limitations in terms of what its effects can be." The Dominguez and Soria complaints, as reported, attribute deaths to the plant's principal alkaloid; neither has been adjudicated, and neither report describes the mitragynine concentration of the products involved.
