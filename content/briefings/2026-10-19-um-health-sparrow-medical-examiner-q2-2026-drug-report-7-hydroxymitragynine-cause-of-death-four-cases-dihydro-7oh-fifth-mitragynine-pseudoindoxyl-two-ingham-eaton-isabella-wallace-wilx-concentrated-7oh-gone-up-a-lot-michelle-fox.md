The University of Michigan Health-Sparrow Office of the Medical Examiner lists 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) among the substances causing death in four of the 38 drug-related deaths it recorded across seven mid-Michigan counties in the second quarter of 2026, and a 7-OH derivative in a fifth, according to [the office's second-quarter drug report](https://www.uofmhealthsparrow.org/sites/default/files/2026-10/2026-q2-ome-drug-report.pdf), published September 11, 2026 and covered by [WILX News 10](https://www.wilx.com/2026/10/15/mid-michigan-drug-deaths-stay-above-last-years-pace-new-report-shows/) on October 15, 2026. The report lists the compounds found in each case and does not identify the products involved. It does not say whether the 7-OH in those cases came from concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products, which the Food and Drug Administration distinguishes from the trace amounts of the alkaloid naturally present in kratom leaf, or from leaf itself. Amanda Wallace of the Department of Forensic Pathology told WILX that examiners have been seeing mitragynine and a concentrated form of 7-OH in several cases over the past year, and that such findings have "gone up a lot."

## What the tables show

The report, which covers Clinton, Eaton, Ingham, Ionia, Isabella, Livingston, and Shiawassee counties, presents each death as a row listing sex, race, age, county of residence, the substances the assigned medical examiner determined caused the death, and manner of death. KNT reviewed the tables. Five entries list a kratom alkaloid or derivative:

- An Eaton County man, 60, whose death was attributed to diphenhydramine, 7-hydroxymitragynine, mitragynine, and trazodone. Manner: accident.
- An Ingham County man, 33, attributed to 7-hydroxymitragynine, alprazolam, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, desvenlafaxine, ethanol, ketamine, xylazine, and zolpidem. Manner: accident.
- An Ingham County woman, 62, attributed to 7-hydroxymitragynine, alprazolam, fluoxetine, gabapentin, and quetiapine. Manner: accident.
- An Ingham County man, 62, attributed to 7-hydroxymitragynine, clonazepam, gabapentin, mitragynine, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl. Manner: accident.
- An Isabella County man, 30, attributed to dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, mitragynine, and "multiple medication." Manner: indeterminate.

Two of the five cases list mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, the semi-synthetic kratom compound that, according to the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary scheduling order, [has been in Schedule I since August 26, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] along with MGM-15 and MGM-16. Two of the four 7-OH cases do not list mitragynine at all, and the report offers no comment on that pattern. Every one of the five lists at least one other drug, which is consistent with the report's own methods note that "the majority of the drug deaths reported are due to more than one substance." Michelle Fox, the forensic pathology supervisor and chief investigator for the office, told [The New Citizens Press](https://tncpnews.com/ingham-county-records-43-drug-related-deaths-in-first-half-of-2026/) that "most of the drug deaths we see involve multiple substances" and that investigators "work carefully to determine which substances actually caused or contributed to each death." The report states that the extent of toxicology testing is set case by case and that Axis Forensic Toxicology performed the testing during the period.

The report does not include the "kratom products" quote that WILX and [WOWO](https://wowo.com/michigan-drug-deaths-remain-above-last-years-levels-new-report-shows/) attributed to it. Both outlets quoted the report as saying, "We are seeing a lot of the Kratom products now in our deaths," and both reported that four Ingham County deaths involved kratom products. The published PDF contains no narrative about kratom, and its Ingham County table lists three entries with 7-OH among the causing substances; the station did not explain how it arrived at four. KNT could not resolve the discrepancy from the published documents.

## The wider numbers and the policy backdrop

WILX reported nearly 30 drug-related deaths in the greater Lansing area during the quarter, with 23 in Ingham County, opioids present in about half of the deaths, and no deaths in Clinton or Ionia counties. The New Citizens Press put the seven-county total at 38, with Ingham at 23, Eaton at six, Livingston at five, and Isabella at four, and reported that Ingham County recorded 43 drug-related deaths in the first half of the year after 20 in the first quarter. The second-quarter total fell from the first quarter but remained above the same period in 2025, according to WILX, which did not give the comparison figures. The report's charts compare the quarter with the first quarter of 2026 and the second quarter of 2025 by drug class, and kratom alkaloids are not a listed class.

Wallace's comment to WILX about concentrated 7-OH came days after the Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center's Varun Vohra [told Axios Detroit on October 13, 2026][michigan-poison-center-347-kratom-7oh-cases-2021-2025-87-in-2025-81-percent-vohra-science-informed-framework-not-blanket-ban-hb-5537-sb-1181-cherry-detroit-council-resolution-committee], when he said exposures to concentrated 7-OH products were associated with more severe withdrawal and greater intensive care needs than other kratom exposures. Mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, listed in two of the Ingham County deaths, was also the subject of the Center for Forensic Science Research and Education study KNT [covered on October 17, 2026][cfsre-nms-labs-journal-of-analytical-toxicology-method-mitragynine-7oh-mgpi-mgm-15-200-cases-7oh-fell-89-percent-112-days-blood-mgpi-rose-testing-extremely-limited-fatalities-under-investigated-krotulski], which reported that pseudoindoxyl was turning up more often in casework while testing for the newer compounds remained limited. Neither the Sparrow report nor the broadcast coverage identifies the products involved in any of the five deaths.

WILX and WOWO both noted that a statewide kratom sales ban passed the Michigan House earlier this year and has not been taken up by the Senate, where senators introduced a separate regulatory bill in September. In the Detroit suburbs, Plymouth Township trustees [advanced a township-wide sales ban on October 13, 2026][plymouth-township-michigan-trustees-unanimous-advance-kratom-sales-ban-civil-fines-1000-7500-misdemeanor-final-vote-october-27-knittel-chesterfield-township-first-reading-21-and-older-fifth-macomb-county-community-kersten], with a final vote set for October 27, 2026. Kratom advocacy groups have not treated those measures and 7-OH restrictions as one question: the American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow [told Gizmodo this week][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] that the association supports federal scheduling of synthetic 7-OH while opposing bans on natural leaf. Wallace, for her part, used the WILX interview to urge people with substance use disorder to contact their local health departments, which she said have "plenty of resources."
