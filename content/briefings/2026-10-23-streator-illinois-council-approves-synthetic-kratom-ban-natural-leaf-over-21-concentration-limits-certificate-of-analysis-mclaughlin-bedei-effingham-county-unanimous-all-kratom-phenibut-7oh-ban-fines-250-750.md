The Streator, Illinois City Council approved an ordinance on the night of October 21, 2026 that bans the sale of synthetic kratom in the city, [Shaw Local reported](https://www.shawlocal.com/illinois-valley/2026/10/22/streator-joins-other-la-salle-county-communities-in-banning-synthetic-kratom/) on October 22. According to the outlet, the ordinance establishes concentration limits for mitragynine, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and total kratom alkaloids, requires kratom products to carry specific labeling and product information, and requires retailers to obtain a certificate of analysis for the kratom products they sell. According to Shaw Local, the ordinance treats kratom as synthetic once it has been chemically altered or concentrated. The outlet did not specify whether the 7-OH limits apply only to concentrated or chemically produced 7-OH, and it did not report the vote count, penalty amounts, an effective date, or the final concentration numbers. The Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) [October 14 Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/citation/91-FR-65370), which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered at the time][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline], proposed controlling 7-OH only above a concentration threshold of 1 milligram per gram, an approach the Department of Health and Human Services said targets concentrated 7-OH "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I."

City Manager Yates McLaughlin told Shaw Local that natural kratom remains available for purchase to people older than 21. Shaw Local reported that the ordinance describes natural kratom as kratom in its natural, plant-based form, including raw or dried leaf and minimally processed powder that has not been chemically altered or concentrated. Shaw Local attributed to the University of Colorado a statement that synthetic kratom can be up to 50 times more powerful than ordinary kratom powder; the outlet did not identify a study, researcher, or department behind that figure.

## Why the October 6 draft was revised

The vote followed [McLaughlin's October 6 preview of the ordinance][streator-illinois-october-21-vote-ordinance-ban-synthetic-kratom-concentration-limits-mitragynine-7oh-total-alkaloids-natural-leaf-21-mclaughlin-merrimack-new-hampshire] at a Committee of the Whole meeting, which [Shaw Local reported on October 11](https://www.shawlocal.com/illinois-valley/2026/10/11/streator-moves-to-ban-synthetic-kratom-in-city-limits/). McLaughlin said that draft had to be changed, Shaw Local reported on October 22. According to the outlet, the October 6 version would have prohibited the sale of kratom containing 17 milligrams of total kratom alkaloids per gram while separately allowing up to 30 milligrams of mitragynine per gram. McLaughlin said the two limits conflicted because mitragynine is itself a kratom alkaloid. KNT could not locate the adopted text on the city's website as of this briefing.

Mayor Tara Bedei said she had received a lot of emails asking the city to preserve the natural products, the outlet reported. Shaw Local did not say who sent them, and neither Shaw Local nor WCIA reported a position from the American Kratom Association or any other kratom trade group on either of this week's Illinois measures.

Shaw Local's headline described Streator as joining other La Salle County communities in banning synthetic kratom. The outlet's October 11 report said Seneca, Ottawa, and Marseilles already had kratom bans in place, and the October 22 story did not describe the scope of those ordinances.

## Effingham County bans all kratom products

Downstate, the Effingham County Board approved a broader measure. The board unanimously approved an ordinance that bans the sale of kratom products across the county, [WCIA reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/effingham-county-bans-possession-sale-224808612.html) on October 21. According to the station, the ordinance's findings cite "significant health and safety concerns" with the "sale, possession, purchase, distribution, delivery, and use of Kratom, Phenibut, and 7-Hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products." WCIA reported that the ordinance defines kratom as any part of the Mitragyna speciosa plant, "whether growing or not," along with any derivative, mixture, or preparation of it, and the station's report described no exception for natural leaf. WCIA's report also did not say whether the "7-OH products" named in the ordinance are limited to concentrated or synthetic preparations, as distinct from the trace 7-OH in natural leaf. The measure went to the full board on a unanimous recommendation from the Administrative Committee, and the board's approval was also unanimous, the station reported.

Fines are $250 for a first violation, $500 for a second violation within three years, and $750 for a third and subsequent violations, WCIA reported. The station reported that the ordinance takes effect immediately and that businesses have a 30-day grace period to remove prohibited items. Any law enforcement agency with jurisdiction in the county may enforce it, and the ordinance states it is meant to supplement rather than conflict with federal and Illinois law, according to WCIA. The station also noted that the Illinois General Assembly has considered legislation that would add restrictions on kratom.

The two ordinances, as the two outlets described them, take different shapes. Streator's restricts synthetic products and sets testing and labeling rules for natural kratom, according to Shaw Local. Effingham County's bans all kratom products, according to WCIA. [Naperville's council voted 8-0 in September][naperville-city-council-votes-ban-kratom-sales] to prohibit retail sale of both natural and synthetic kratom, as KNT reported at the time. In Washington, [state Rep. Kristine Reeves told the Seattle Times](https://www.seattletimes.com/seattle-news/politics/as-kratom-proliferates-in-legal-gray-area-wa-lawmakers-eye-drug-ban/) this week that she is working on a bill to ban synthetic kratom and regulate natural leaf, [KNT reported][washington-rep-kristine-reeves-working-on-bill-ban-synthetic-kratom-regulate-natural-leaf-liquor-cannabis-board-hb-2291-seattle-times-king-pierce-spokane-medical-examiners-no-rise-75-king-county-deaths-fentanyl], after the Times said four cities in that state had banned kratom and a fifth had approved a sales ban.
