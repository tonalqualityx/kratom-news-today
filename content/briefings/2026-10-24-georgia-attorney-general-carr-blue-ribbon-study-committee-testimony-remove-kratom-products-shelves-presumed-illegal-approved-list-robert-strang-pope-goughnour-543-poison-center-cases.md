Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr asked the state's convenience stores, gas stations, and smoke shops on October 23, 2026 to take kratom products off their shelves without waiting for a new law, and asked legislators to pass one as well, [his office announced](https://law.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-10-23/carr-calls-convenience-stores-remove-dangerous-kratom-products) the same day. Carr made the request in testimony to the Georgia House Blue-Ribbon Study Committee on Youth Exposure to Kratom and Retail Available Substances at the state Capitol. "It shouldn't take legislation or law enforcement to do the right thing," he said in the release. "This problem could be solved today if all of our convenience stores step up, remove this poison from their shelves, and put safety first." The testimony puts before the committee the proposal Carr [first published as an Atlanta Journal-Constitution op-ed on October 15, 2026][georgia-attorney-general-carr-ajc-op-ed-default-ban-all-kratom-derived-products-approved-list-contraband-seizure-poison-center-lopez-315-calls-four-deaths-2026-wsb-synthetic-20-to-30-times-more-potent], Kratom News Today (KNT) reported at the time. The release describes the products at issue as synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) sold as "kratom" in tablets, gummies, shots, and powders. The Justice Department said in an [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) temporary scheduling of three 7-OH-related compounds was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. Neither the release nor the three news reports on the hearing specify whether the products involved in the deaths Carr cited were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations, other manufactured compounds, or natural leaf kratom.

## The proposal: a state-approved list

Carr's argument, as laid out in the release, is that Georgia's existing Kratom Consumer Protection Act already bans synthetic 7-OH but cannot be enforced at scale. Under the current statute, the release says, law enforcement "must find and identify a product, seize it as evidence, test it, and then prove what is in it." [Capitol Beat News Service reported](https://www.mdjonline.com/state/georgia-attorney-general-kratom-products-should-be-presumed-illegal/article_a90a02af-36ce-5df8-a63b-dd3970ebb134.html) that the Kratom Consumer Protection Act, as enacted, also prohibits sales to people under 21 and requires products to be kept behind the counter or otherwise secured, and that the lab-testing requirement "has stymied enforcement."

Carr asked lawmakers to "reverse the model," according to the release: all kratom-related products would be presumed illegal for retail sale unless the state has affirmatively approved them, with a "short, public list" of products meeting standards such as independent lab testing, accurate labeling, and federal marketing authorization. Anything off the list would be treated as contraband subject to seizure by any law enforcement agency in the state. "There'd be no lab work required and no need to prove what's inside that package because if it's not on the list, it comes off the shelf," Carr said at the hearing, according to Capitol Beat. The release compares the approach to how Georgia handled gray-market and counterfeit-stamped cigarettes.

The release describes 7-OH as "an opioid-like substance that is addictive and roughly 13 times more potent than morphine," and says manufacturers "now synthesize 7-OH into tablets, gummies, shots and powders, then sell it as 'kratom' at gas stations and smoke shops." The release does not address natural leaf kratom separately. The Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement drew that line, describing the three compounds it scheduled as manufactured and concentrated into opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom, and the Global Kratom Coalition said the order did not schedule natural kratom leaf, [KNT reported on August 26, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. Carr's proposed list, as described in the release and the three news reports, would apply to all kratom-derived products rather than to the synthetic 7-OH category alone.

## The cases Carr cited

The release cites the deaths last month of two University of Mississippi students, one of them from Georgia, and says law enforcement "recovered packaged kratom at the scene, the same type of kratom product that is sold in communities throughout the state." [Atlanta News First identified](https://www.atlantanewsfirst.com/2026/10/24/georgia-attorney-general-calls-removal-kratom-store-shelves/) the Georgia student as Robert Strang, 20, a metro Atlanta native, and reported that investigators believe he bought pills at a store 20 miles outside Oxford, Mississippi. A federal court filing in the Oxford case, [which KNT covered on October 12, 2026][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond], said toxicology found mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students and tied the substances to Smax Mango Breeze MGM-15 tablets sold at an Oxford vape store, according to KNT's report on the filing. The Justice Department described MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl as manufactured 7-OH-related substances when it announced their temporary scheduling in August 2026. Neither Carr's release nor the Georgia news reports describe the product's contents beyond "packaged kratom" and "pills."

The release also cites the 2021 death of 23-year-old Ethan Pope "after consuming a liquid kratom product," without further detail on the product. Pitter Goughnour testified that her former husband, Bill, died six years ago after taking what he believed was a natural supplement, [Fox 5 Atlanta reported](https://www.fox5atlanta.com/news/georgia-kratom-ban-state-attorney-general-urges-retail-prohibition). "When it was sold to Bill, it was under the false pretense that it was a healthy product," Goughnour said, according to the station. Atlanta News First quoted her saying "the people in the gas station don't know how to sell it" and "the people who are buying it don't have the knowledge either."

The numbers in the four accounts differ in scope. The attorney general's release says the Georgia Poison Center "has handled roughly 543 kratom exposure cases since 2021," a 66 percent increase between 2022 and 2025, "with the vast majority requiring treatment at a healthcare facility." Fox 5 reported that Carr cited at least four Georgia deaths and more than 315 emergency calls in 2026, the same figures Georgia Poison Center director Dr. Gaylord Lopez gave WSB-TV on October 14, 2026, KNT reported. None of the reports says how many of the 543 cases or four deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom, and none says whether the deaths involved other substances.

## What else the committee heard

Carr told the committee that "consumers cannot tell the difference between a lawfully registered, regulated product or something that can stop your breathing," and that "the same is probably true with the shop owners," Capitol Beat reported. He added that buyers assume a product sold in a store has been approved by someone, and he described that assumption as a deadly one in Georgia today, according to the outlet. Rep. Michelle Au, a Johns Creek Democrat who serves on the committee, said reducing availability would make people less likely to buy kratom without knowing the risks, and called the signage at gas stations "implicit messaging" that kratom is safe, according to Capitol Beat.

The committee has heard a different view at earlier meetings, Capitol Beat noted, from kratom users who praised the substance, and on Friday a Marietta man, whom the outlet did not name, spoke in favor of kratom he described as "natural," Capitol Beat reported. Capitol Beat reported that kratom is not listed as a federally controlled substance and that, under Carr's proposal as the outlet described it, people could still buy kratom products online. The Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement said the DEA's temporary order covered three manufactured compounds, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16, and described the action as directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. Committee chairman Rep. Rick Townsend said at a [September 25, 2026 hearing in Athens][georgia-house-study-committee-kratom-hearing-university-of-georgia-athens-townsend-enforcement] that the state's remaining question is enforcement, KNT reported; the four October 23 reports do not quote Townsend on Carr's proposal.

Fox 5 reported that Friday's testimony "is set to translate into legislation during the upcoming legislative session," which convenes in January. Capitol Beat noted that Carr leaves office in January when his term ends. The accounts differ on how they describe the panel: the attorney general's office and Capitol Beat call it a House study committee, while Fox 5 described a "joint legislative study committee" and, in a subheading, a "Senate study committee."

None of the four reports carried a response from the American Kratom Association (AKA), the Global Kratom Coalition, or any Georgia retailer or manufacturer. The AKA filed a formal complaint in August alleging bias by the committee's chairman, [KNT reported at the time][georgia-brunswick-kratom-hearing-west-chester-ordinance], and its senior fellow Mac Haddow [told Gizmodo in an October 17, 2026 report][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] that the association is "absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics." The Independent Florida Alligator [described the association](https://www.alligator.org/article/2026/10/uf-kratom-researcher-under-fire-for-his-ties-to-lobbying-group) on October 23, 2026 as a lobbying group that supports regulated access to kratom. Carr's proposal, as described in the release and the three news reports, would apply to natural leaf products as well as synthetic 7-OH products. The release says Georgia's Kratom Consumer Protection Act already bans synthetic 7-OH.
