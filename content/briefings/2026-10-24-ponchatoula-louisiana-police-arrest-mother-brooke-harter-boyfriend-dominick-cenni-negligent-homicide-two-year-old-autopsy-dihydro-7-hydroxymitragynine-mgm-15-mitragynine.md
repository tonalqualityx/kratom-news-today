Police in Ponchatoula, Louisiana have arrested a two-year-old's mother and her boyfriend after an autopsy attributed the child's death to the toxic effects of dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine and mitragynine, [WVUE Fox 8 reported](https://www.fox8live.com/2026/10/23/police-arrest-mother-boyfriend-over-kratom-related-death-two-year-old/) on October 23, 2026. Dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine is the chemical name the [Louisiana Department of Health's October emergency rule](https://www.ldh.la.gov/assets/oph/Rulemaking/er/LAC-46-LIII.Added-Controlled-Dangerous-Substance.ER---2026-09.pdf) gives for MGM-15. The Drug Enforcement Administration described MGM-15 as a lab-made substance related to the kratom alkaloid 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) when the Justice Department [announced on August 25, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] that the agency was placing the compound in temporary federal Schedule I. The agency said MGM-15 and two related compounds were manufactured and concentrated into opioid products, and that its order did not reach unaltered natural kratom leaf, Kratom News Today (KNT) reported at the time. The Ponchatoula police account, as carried by Fox 8, does not identify the product the child ingested or say how the child was exposed. The reporting does not specify whether the product was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom.

## The charges

Ponchatoula police arrested Brooke Harter, 31, and Dominick Cenni, 27, after interviewing the mother and her boyfriend about the August 24, 2026 death, according to Fox 8. Cenni turned himself in on October 22, 2026 and was charged with negligent homicide, cruelty to juveniles, criminal abandonment, and obstruction of justice, police said. Harter turned herself in on October 23, 2026 and was booked on negligent homicide, cruelty to juveniles, and criminal abandonment. Cenni is being held on a $150,000 bond on the negligent homicide count, and the station reported that "no bond has been posted for the remaining charges." Police asked anyone with information to contact the department. The report, which [KSLA also carried](https://www.ksla.com/2026/10/23/police-arrest-mother-boyfriend-over-kratom-related-death-two-year-old/), does not name the child, say where the death occurred, or describe the evidence behind the obstruction charge.

## What the autopsy named

Fox 8 wrote that the autopsy attributed the death to "the toxic effects of Dihydro-7-hydroxy mitragynine and mitragynine, commonly known as Kratom." The [Louisiana Department of Health's announcement](https://ldh.la.gov/news/7818) describes the compounds it scheduled this month as "related to 7-hydroxymitragynine and mitragynine," and Red River Radio's 2025 account of Act 41 described that law as covering kratom, mitragynine, and 7-OH. The DEA's August order characterized dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, or MGM-15, as a 7-OH-related substance manufactured and concentrated into opioid products, rather than as a constituent of natural leaf. In Utah, the Department of Health and Human Services [linked four deaths over four months][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] to tablets sold as "Buzzers" and "cat's claw" that contained MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, another compound the DEA scheduled in August, [KSL reported](https://www.ksl.com/article/51632638/utah-health-dept-issues-warning-after-4-deaths-linked-to-certain-herbal-supplements) on October 5, 2026. In the deaths of two University of Mississippi students, federal court documents obtained by Fox News Digital said toxicology found mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students, [Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs) on October 10, 2026, and [KNT's October 12, 2026 report on the filing][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] said the documents tied the substances to tablets sold at an Oxford vape store. The police account does not say whether the child was exposed to a tablet, a liquid, a powder, or another form, or whether more than one product was involved.

## Louisiana's legal position

Act 41 of 2025 made it illegal to sell, possess, or distribute kratom products in Louisiana as of August 1, 2025, covering kratom, mitragynine, and 7-OH, with fines of $1,000 to $5,000 per offense, [Red River Radio reported](https://www.redriverradio.org/news/2025-08-04/louisiana-becomes-7th-state-to-ban-kratom-with-its-powerful-psychoactive-compound) at the time. Red River Radio's account of Act 41 did not mention MGM-15, and the department's October 19, 2026 release did not say whether it considered the compound covered before the new rule. The Louisiana Department of Health [added MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl to the state's Schedule I by emergency rule][louisiana-department-of-health-emergency-rule-schedule-i-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-mgm-16-lac-46-liii-2704-greenstein-imminent-peril-act-41-kratom-already-illegal-since-august-2025] announced October 19, 2026, KNT reported this week. The department said in its announcement that preclinical evidence indicates the compounds are potent agonists at the mu-opioid receptor, with risks of dependence and respiratory depression. The department's release and the rule do not mention the Ponchatoula case or any Louisiana death, and the Fox 8 report does not say whether the department was aware of the autopsy finding when it acted.

The case follows a similar one in Florida, where police [arrested two adults on aggravated manslaughter and child neglect charges][florida-toddler-death-kratom-manslaughter-charges] after a two-year-old died of what the New York Post reported as kratom poisoning and mitragynine intoxication, KNT reported on August 3, 2026. Coverage of that case likewise did not specify whether a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom was involved. In the Louisiana case the autopsy named MGM-15, a compound the DEA has characterized as manufactured, but the police account does not identify a product. Fox 8 and KSLA did not report comment from the Tangipahoa Parish coroner, the district attorney, defense counsel for Harter or Cenni, or any kratom trade or advocacy group. Separately, the American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow [told Gizmodo in an October 17, 2026 report][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] that the association is "absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics." The Independent Florida Alligator [described the association](https://www.alligator.org/article/2026/10/uf-kratom-researcher-under-fire-for-his-ties-to-lobbying-group) on October 23, 2026 as a lobbying group that supports regulated access to kratom.
