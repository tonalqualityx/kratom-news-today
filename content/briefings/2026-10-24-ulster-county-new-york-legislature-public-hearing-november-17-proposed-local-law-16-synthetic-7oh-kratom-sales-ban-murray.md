The Ulster County Legislature has scheduled a public hearing for November 17, 2026 on a proposed local law that would prohibit the sale of synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) kratom products in the county, [Mid-Hudson News reported](https://midhudsonnews.com/2026/10/24/ulster-county-to-hold-hearing-on-proposed-kratom-sales-ban/) on October 24, 2026. The hearing is set for 7:05 p.m. in Kingston during the legislature's regular session. The measure, Proposed Local Law No. 16 of 2026, was introduced by Legislator William Murray and targets synthetic and concentrated 7-OH preparations, which Murray said "can contain significantly higher levels of 7-OH than naturally occurring kratom leaves," according to Mid-Hudson News. The Justice Department said in an [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) temporary scheduling of three 7-OH-related compounds was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom, and the Mid-Hudson News report does not describe the bill as reaching natural leaf kratom products.

## What Murray's bill says, as reported

Murray cited potential health risks from the concentrated and synthetic products, including dependence, withdrawal symptoms, liver toxicity, and seizures, according to Mid-Hudson News. He also raised concerns about what the report called the lack of state and federal regulation and the possible presence of harmful additives and heavy metals in the products. The report does not give the bill's definitions, penalty structure, enforcement agency, or effective date. The hearing, Mid-Hudson News wrote, will let residents comment "before the county considers further action."

## Background: the September committee decision

On September 3, 2026, the legislature's health committee [declined to schedule a public hearing on Murray's bill][ulster-county-committee-delays-synthetic-kratom-ban-vote], choosing to wait for state and federal action, [the Daily Freeman reported](https://www.dailyfreeman.com/2026/09/08/ulster-county-delays-ban-on-concentrated-synthetic-kratom/) at the time. Deputy County Executive James Amenta asked for the delay on behalf of County Executive Jen Metzger, saying the county bill "almost exactly mirrors" a state bill then awaiting Governor Kathy Hochul's signature and that the county health department has no existing role in drug enforcement. Murray voted against the delay, arguing there was no guarantee Hochul would sign the state bill or that the federal government would make its temporary scheduling permanent, the Daily Freeman reported. The Justice Department said in its August 25, 2026 announcement that the DEA was temporarily placing MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in Schedule I, describing them as 7-OH-related substances manufactured into opioid products, [KNT reported at the time][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds]. The Mid-Hudson News report does not say what prompted the hearing to be scheduled now, whether the county executive's office has changed its position, or whether the state bill has been signed.

Three nearby counties have acted this year. News 12 reported in September 2026 that Dutchess County Executive Sue Serino [signed a local law banning concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh] after the county legislature rejected her proposal for a full ban that would also have covered natural leaf. WAMC reported that Albany County's ban on kratom sales [took effect September 1, 2026][albany-county-kratom-sales-ban-takes-effect]. News10 reported that the Rensselaer County Legislature [approved a countywide sales ban on September 8, 2026 by a 15-2 vote][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2], with the two dissenters arguing the law should have separated natural leaf from synthetic and concentrated products. The Mid-Hudson News report did not include comment from any Ulster County retailer or from kratom trade or advocacy groups. Separately, the American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow [told Gizmodo in an October 17, 2026 report][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] that the association is "absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics." The Independent Florida Alligator [described the association](https://www.alligator.org/article/2026/10/uf-kratom-researcher-under-fire-for-his-ties-to-lobbying-group) on October 23, 2026 as a lobbying group that supports regulated access to kratom.
