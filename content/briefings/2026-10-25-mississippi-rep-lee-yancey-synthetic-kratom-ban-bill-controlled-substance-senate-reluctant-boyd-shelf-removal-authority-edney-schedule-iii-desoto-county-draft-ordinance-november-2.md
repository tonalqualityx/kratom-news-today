Mississippi Rep. Lee Yancey, a Republican from Brandon, is considering legislation to ban synthetic kratom products, and Sen. Nicole Boyd, a Republican from Oxford, wants to give state officials temporary authority to pull from store shelves substances that Mississippi Today described as potentially harmful, [Mississippi Today reported](https://www.djournal.com/mississippi-today/after-ole-miss-student-deaths-mississippi-lawmakers-eye-tougher-restrictions-on-kratom/article_81323f95-fb02-4b07-ac7a-6d97c5b9438b.html) on October 23, 2026 in a story by Candice Wilder republished by the Northeast Mississippi Daily Journal. The discussion follows the September 21 deaths of University of Mississippi students Aidan Hamilton, 18, and Robert Strang, 20, in separate incidents in Oxford, according to Mississippi Today. A federal court filing [reported by Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs) on October 10 said toxicology found mitragynine and two compounds the Justice Department has described as deliberately manufactured opioid products, MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, in both students. The filing tied the substances to Smax Mango Breeze MGM-15 tablets sold at an Oxford vape and tobacco store and stated there was probable cause to believe those substances caused the deaths, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported at the time][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond]. The Justice Department said in an [August 25 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the Drug Enforcement Administration's temporary Schedule I placement of MGM-15, MGM-16, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl was directed at deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. The Mississippi Today story does not report a cause of death or toxicology findings.

## What Yancey and Boyd are weighing, as reported

Yancey told Mississippi Today that the state Senate has been reluctant to ban synthetic kratom products, so he is also exploring classifying kratom as a controlled substance and banning over-the-counter sales. The story does not say whether that broader option would reach natural leaf products, and it does not describe bill text, penalties, or an age threshold. Boyd's proposal, as Mississippi Today described it, would let state officials temporarily pull substances the outlet characterized as potentially harmful from shelves. Boyd said lawmakers are all looking at and thinking through the steps that need to be put in place, according to the report. Mississippi Today wrote that lawmakers would still need to decide whether violations carry administrative or criminal penalties, what product testing would be required, and what enforcement would cost. The story does not say when the Legislature would take up either proposal.

Sen. Daniel Sparks, a Republican from Belmont, told Mississippi Today that passing another law would not solve the problem and that banning one substance could push manufacturers to sell similar products under different names. The Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit, which is investigating the Oxford deaths, issued a September 30 statement calling on lawmakers to ban synthetic kratom statewide, according to Mississippi Today. University spokesperson Jacob Battle told the outlet that Ole Miss officials encourage students to use drug education and wellness services at the William Magee Center.

## Where Hosemann and Edney stand

Lt. Gov. Delbert Hosemann, who presides over the Senate, [called for a statewide ban][mississippi-lt-gov-hosemann-op-ed-time-to-ban-all-kratom-products-edney-testified-all-kratom-should-be-banned-80-percent-deaths-other-substances-whippets-kosciusko-aldermen-ordinance] in an [October 8 op-ed in the Magnolia Tribune](https://magnoliatribune.com/2026/10/08/hosemann-it-is-time-for-mississippi-to-ban-all-kratom-products/), Mississippi Today noted. In that op-ed, Hosemann wrote that State Health Officer Dr. Dan Edney had testified that all kratom should be banned. Mississippi Today reported that Edney supports classifying kratom leaf as a Schedule III controlled substance, and quoted him as saying that "right now we can't say the kratom supply in the state of Mississippi is safe" and that any potentially addictive substance should be regulated.

Mac Haddow of the American Kratom Association [told Gizmodo in an October 17 report](https://gizmodo.com/the-dea-is-cracking-down-on-kratom-this-time-some-advocates-are-on-board-2000826560) that the association is "absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics" while opposing restrictions on natural leaf, [as KNT reported][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled]. Neither the Mississippi Today story nor the DeSoto Times-Tribune report quoted the association, another trade group, or a Mississippi retailer.

## DeSoto County orders a draft ordinance

The DeSoto County Board of Supervisors authorized County Attorney Adam Emerson at its October 20 meeting to draft an ordinance banning kratom sales in the unincorporated areas of the county, [the DeSoto Times-Tribune reported](https://www.desototimes.com/news/desoto-county-to-consider-kratom-ban/article_78389b23-722a-43d4-9ead-b7078b7c7d23.html) on October 22 in a story by Mark Randall. The draft is slated for the board's November 2 meeting for study, according to the report, which did not describe a formal vote, penalties, or whether the measure would cover all kratom products or only synthetic ones. The Times-Tribune wrote that authorities believe a kratom overdose was involved in the two Ole Miss deaths; it did not mention the MGM-15 tablets identified in the federal filing.

Board President Jessie Medlin told the Times-Tribune he had not heard of kratom until the news reports about the student deaths, said many of the county's convenience stores carry it, and said he would be open to a countywide ban. Supervisor Robert Foster said he already supports a statewide ban on sales to people under 21 and wants more regulation. "I believe it should be a scheduled drug that is tested and only available by prescription for limited medical use," Foster said, according to the report. Supervisor Ray Denison said he had received emails from residents on both sides, some describing what they called benefits and others from parents who said they lost children to kratom overdoses, and that he wanted to learn more before deciding. Supervisor Lee Caldwell said few convenience or vape stores operate in the unincorporated county but that retailers could begin selling kratom there if cities ban it, a prospect he told the Times-Tribune concerned him.

## Existing Mississippi restrictions, as the two outlets describe them

Mississippi Today described a 2025 bill signed by Gov. Tate Reeves as prohibiting kratom sales to anyone under 21 and outlawing a synthetic form of the substance; the story did not cite the bill number. The same report described a 2023 law as placing tianeptine, sold under names including "Zaza" and "Tiana," in the same category as drugs such as ketamine and anabolic steroids. At the local level, Mississippi Today wrote that Oxford has prohibited kratom sales and possession since 2019, Lafayette County has had a ban since January 2021, and Lee County and Tupelo have banned possession and sale. The Times-Tribune reported that kratom is banned in Hernando, Oxford, and Madison, that Southaven and Olive Branch have recently voiced support for a statewide ban, and that Tennessee and Arkansas ban it statewide.
