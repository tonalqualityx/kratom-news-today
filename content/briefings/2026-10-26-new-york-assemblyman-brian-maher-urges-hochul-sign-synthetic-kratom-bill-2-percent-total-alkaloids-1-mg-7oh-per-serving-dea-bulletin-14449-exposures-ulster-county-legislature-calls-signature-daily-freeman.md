Assemblyman Brian Maher has urged Gov. Kathy Hochul to sign legislation restricting the sale of synthetic and highly concentrated kratom products in New York, [the Daily Freeman reported](https://www.dailyfreeman.com/2026/10/25/maher-urges-gov-kathy-hochul-to-sign-kratom-crackdown-bill/) on October 25, 2026, citing a news release from his office. Maher, a Republican who represents the 101st Assembly District, pointed to what his office called growing concerns over addiction, overdoses, and the availability of the products in gas stations and vape shops, according to the report. The bill [passed both houses of the Legislature in June][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban] and would prohibit the sale of products containing more than 2% of total alkaloids or one milligram per serving of 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), the Daily Freeman reported. Maher's office said 7-OH occurs naturally in trace amounts in the kratom plant, while many commercially available products contain far higher concentrations, often produced through laboratory processing, according to the report. The Assembly's [June 8, 2026 announcement](https://assembly.state.ny.us/mem/Phil-Steck/story/118530) of the bill's passage described it as targeting synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom.

## What Maher's office said

"We have spent years fighting the opioid epidemic, yet dangerous substances with similar addictive properties are being sold at gas stations and convenience stores across our state," Maher said in a statement quoted by the Daily Freeman. He added that the products "are being marketed and sold in places where families stop for everyday necessities" and that "we cannot afford to wait until more lives are lost before taking action." The release said highly concentrated products "remain readily accessible to consumers" despite what it called serious health risks, and raised concerns about their effect on young people and on communities "already struggling with substance abuse," the paper reported.

Maher's office cited a July 2026 bulletin from the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) that it said documented 14,449 kratom-related exposures reported to America's Poison Centers between 2015 and 2025, according to the Daily Freeman. The release said 3,434 exposures were reported in 2025 alone, an increase of roughly 1,200% from the 258 reported in 2015, the paper reported. The release also said the DEA had warned about highly concentrated 7-OH products that "can be up to 13 times stronger than morphine" and that carry what the release described as risks of addiction and withdrawal similar to traditional opioids, according to the Daily Freeman. The report did not reproduce the DEA bulletin itself, and KNT has not independently reviewed it.

The release pointed to the federal threshold approach as well. The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) [recommended on October 9, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) that the DEA place 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in Schedule I only above a threshold of one milligram per gram in solid products or one milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the ratio of mitragynine to those compounds falls below 100 to one, [KNT reported at the time][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. Maher's office said New York's bill "would establish its own restrictions based on the amount of 7-OH per serving or its percentage of total alkaloids," giving the state a mechanism to prohibit products that exceed those limits, the Daily Freeman reported.

## Where the bill stands

The measure, sponsored by Assemblyman Phil Steck, a Schenectady Democrat, passed the Assembly unanimously in the closing days of the June session, according to the Assembly's announcement, and the Daily Freeman reported that it passed both houses that month. The Daily Freeman report does not say whether the bill has been delivered to the governor or give a deadline for her to act, and it does not include a response from Hochul's office. The New York Post [reported on October 10, 2026](https://nypost.com/2026/10/10/us-news/illegal-kratom-products-sold-in-nyc-smoke-shops-investigation/) that the bill [remained under review by the governor][new-york-post-investigation-52-nyc-smoke-shops-eight-sold-mgpi-mgm-15-tablets-kama-opia-rush-7oh-legal-new-york-34-stores-steck-hochul-synthetic-ban-review] and that Steck said he expected a signature early next year.

## Hudson Valley counties act on their own

The Ulster County Legislature has called on Hochul to sign the state law, the Daily Freeman reported, and announced this week that it will hold a public hearing at its November 17, 2026 session at 7:05 p.m. on [Legislator William Murray's proposed local prohibition][ulster-county-new-york-legislature-public-hearing-november-17-proposed-local-law-16-synthetic-7oh-kratom-sales-ban-murray] on the sale of synthetic kratom products. Murray said in a statement quoted by the paper that "an increasing body of health research has shown that kratom and its derivatives pose serious risk of liver toxicity, seizures and substance use disorder," and that the products are not regulated by the state or federal government. "By banning the sale of synthetic kratom, we can reduce access to this harmful opiate," Murray said, according to the Daily Freeman. A county legislative committee had declined in September to move the proposal forward, saying it would wait for state and federal action, the paper reported.

In Dutchess County, County Executive Sue Serino [signed a law in September banning the sale of synthetic and highly concentrated kratom products][dutchess-county-executive-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban-7oh] while allowing natural kratom to remain on sale, [News 12 Hudson Valley reported](https://hudsonvalley.news12.com/dutchess-county-executive-sue-serino-signs-partial-kratom-ban) at the time. Serino said the law did not go far enough, and wrote in a September 10, 2026 memo to county legislators that she signed it because "any step forward is better than maintaining the status quo," the Daily Freeman reported.

The Daily Freeman report did not include comment from kratom retailers or from trade or advocacy groups. The American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow [told Gizmodo in an October 17, 2026 report](https://gizmodo.com/the-dea-is-cracking-down-on-kratom-this-time-some-advocates-are-on-board-2000826560) that the association is ["absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics,"][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled] a position that separates the natural leaf products the association defends from the concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations that New York's pending bill and Ulster County's proposed local law would restrict, according to the Daily Freeman, and that Dutchess County's enacted law restricts, according to News 12 Hudson Valley.
