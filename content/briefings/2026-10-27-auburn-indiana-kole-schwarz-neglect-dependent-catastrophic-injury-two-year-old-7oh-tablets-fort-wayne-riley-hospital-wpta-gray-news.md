Police in Auburn, Indiana have charged a 20-year-old man with neglect of a dependent resulting in catastrophic injury after a two-year-old boy was hospitalized on September 6, 2026 with what a doctor described as a catastrophic brain injury, and a detective called tablets sold as "7-OH" the likely source of the child's exposure, according to an affidavit described by [WPTA and Gray News](https://www.wcax.com/2026/10/26/2-year-old-suffers-catastrophic-brain-injury-after-exposure-7-oh/) on October 26, 2026. The Food and Drug Administration (FDA), in a statement cited by WPTA, describes 7-OH as a potent opioid concentrated from kratom and distinguishes ordinary botanical kratom from products marketed with added or enhanced 7-OH. The report does not identify the brand, say whether the tablets were tested, specify which kratom-related compounds the lab found in the child's blood, or say whether the tablets were a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation.

## What the affidavit says

The child was taken to a hospital in DeKalb County on September 6, 2026, where he was unresponsive and placed in a medically induced coma, according to the affidavit described by WPTA. He was later moved to Riley Hospital in Indianapolis, the report said. Court papers noted an unidentified green substance around the child's mouth, and a doctor found MRI evidence meeting the criteria for catastrophic injury, according to the report.

Court records identify Kole Schwarz as the live-in boyfriend of the child's mother, WPTA reported. The mother told investigators she believed Schwarz had used "7-OHs" and feared the toddler may have swallowed one, according to the affidavit. Detectives said Schwarz admitted buying tablets sold as "7-OH" under the counter through a contact in Fort Wayne, and a detective called that product the likely source of exposure, the report said. Schwarz told police the tablets had stayed sealed in his pocket, according to the affidavit.

A specialized lab test found kratom-related compounds in the child's blood, WPTA reported. Auburn police said the mother has cooperated with the investigation and is not a suspect, according to the report. The report does not say whether Schwarz has entered a plea or retained counsel.

## The family's account

Josie Casebere, the boy's grandmother, told WPTA she spent six weeks at the hospital with him. A wheelchair has been ordered to fit him until age six, and doctors do not know whether he will walk again, she said. "I pray every day that he keeps getting healing," Casebere said, according to the report. She said she is speaking publicly to raise awareness and criticized sellers of the products, saying manufacturers are "selling it to our kids."

## Berrien County, Michigan's warning

Berrien County, Michigan health officials warned in October 2026 that kratom-related hospitalizations are rising, according to WPTA. The county's warning described 7-OH as acting like an opioid, said the products are sold at gas stations and smoke shops in candy-flavored forms, and listed seizures, liver toxicity, substance use disorder, and overdose among the risks, the report said.

The FDA statement cited by WPTA warned of a "concerning proliferation of concentrated 7-OH products" sold over the counter and online and called for policies to educate consumers and limit access to them. The report did not include comment from kratom trade or advocacy groups.

The Auburn case follows the October 2026 arrests in Ponchatoula, Louisiana, where, [as KNT reported at the time][ponchatoula-louisiana-police-arrest-mother-brooke-harter-boyfriend-dominick-cenni-negligent-homicide-two-year-old-autopsy-dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine-mgm-15-mitragynine], police charged a mother and her boyfriend with negligent homicide after an autopsy attributed a two-year-old's death to dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, a compound known as MGM-15 that the Drug Enforcement Administration has described as lab-made, and to mitragynine. In the Indiana case, the reporting names 7-OH tablets rather than MGM-15, and it does not say whether the Auburn tablets were analyzed.
