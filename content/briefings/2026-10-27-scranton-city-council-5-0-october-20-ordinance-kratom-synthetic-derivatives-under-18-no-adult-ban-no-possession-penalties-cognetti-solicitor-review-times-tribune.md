Scranton City Council voted 5-0 on October 20, 2026 to adopt an ordinance barring kratom and its synthetic derivatives for people under 18, and the ordinance does not ban adult use, [the Times-Tribune reported](https://www.thetimes-tribune.com/2026/10/26/scranton-council-passes-kratom-ban-but-only-regarding-youths/) on October 26, 2026. Council untabled and amended the measure before adopting it, according to the report by Jim Lockwood. The report did not define "synthetic derivatives" or say whether the ordinance reaches concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, natural leaf kratom, or both. Voting yes were Councilman Patrick Flynn, Council President Tom Schuster, Mark McAndrew, Sean McAndrew, and Jessica Rothchild, the paper reported.

## What the amendments changed

The amendments define a minor as a person under 18, remove penalties for possession of the products, and state that a ban passed by the Pennsylvania Legislature would make the city action moot, according to the Times-Tribune. The report did not state the penalty amounts for violations or the ordinance's effective date. Flynn said the ease with which minors could buy the products at gas stations was more pressing than adult use, according to the report. Council hopes the Legislature passes a pending kratom bill before the session ends in a few months, and members felt adult regulation was better handled at the state level, the Times-Tribune reported. The Pennsylvania House passed House Bill 2657 by a 202-0 vote on October 5, 2026, according to the [House roll call](https://www.palegis.us/house/roll-calls?sess_yr=2025&sess_ind=0&rc_body=H&bill_body=H&bill_type=B&bill_number=2657) published by the General Assembly. The [Pennsylvania Capital-Star reported](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/bill-regulate-kratom-sales-and-ban-synthetic-compounds-passes-pa-house) that the bill would cap 7-OH at 0.05 percent by dry weight, set a minimum purchase age of 21, and impose a 12 percent tax, as [Kratom News Today reported at the time][pennsylvania-house-202-0-hb-2657-kosierowski-kratom-bill-amended-0-05-percent-7oh-dry-weight-no-extracts-12-percent-tax-kratom-regulatory-fund-haddow-restraint-senate-seven-days]. The Times-Tribune report did not say whether the Senate has scheduled the bill.

## The mayor's review

Mayor Paige Cognetti said her solicitor would review the amended ordinance to confirm it is enforceable and something she should sign, the paper reported. She said the city wants to keep the products away from minors but must stay within what a municipality can regulate, according to the report. Her options are to sign the ordinance, veto it, or let it take effect without her signature after a set period, and council can override a veto with at least four votes, the Times-Tribune reported.

## How the ordinance got here

According to the Times-Tribune, Schuster, Flynn, the McAndrews, and Lackawanna County District Attorney Brian Gallagher announced plans for a citywide ban on April 6, 2026. The next day council voted 4-1 to introduce two companion ordinances, one for adults and one for minors, drafted without input from the administration. Rothchild dissented at the time, saying the administration had been excluded, and questioned enforcement. Council tabled both ordinances on April 28, 2026 for further review, the paper reported. The report did not say what council intends to do with the adult ordinance if the state bill fails.

## Public reaction

Public reaction was mixed, according to the Times-Tribune. Some residents supported the step, while others who said they rely on pure leaf kratom opposed a ban, and many in both groups supported restricting synthetic forms and sales to minors, the paper reported. The Times-Tribune noted that kratom is not approved by the Food and Drug Administration. The Times-Tribune did not include comment from kratom trade or advocacy organizations.
