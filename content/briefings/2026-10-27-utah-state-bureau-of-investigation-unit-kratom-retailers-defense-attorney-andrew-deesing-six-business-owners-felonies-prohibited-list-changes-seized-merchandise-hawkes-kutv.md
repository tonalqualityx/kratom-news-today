[KUTV reported](https://kutv.com/news/local/defense-attorney-says-retailers-want-to-comply-as-utah-pursues-businesses-selling-kratom) on October 26, 2026 that Utah's State Bureau of Investigation (SBI) is pursuing retailers it accuses of selling kratom products the station describes as prohibited, and that the attorney for several of them says the list of prohibited products changes faster than stores can follow. The Utah Legislature passed a bill and funded a special unit at the SBI to police smoke shops, convenience stores, and other retailers selling controlled substances illegally, according to the report by Nate Carlisle. The report did not name the bill or say when the unit began operating.

## What is prohibited, as KUTV describes it

KUTV reported that pure leaf kratom is legal to sell in Utah and that kratom drinks and products with kratom as an additive are illegal. KUTV did not cite a statute or rule for that distinction or name the official who described it, and KNT has not independently confirmed it. The report said the unit has also found mushroom products containing controlled substances. A spokesperson for Attorney General Derek Brown's office [told Fox 13](https://www.fox13now.com/news/politics/lawsuit-challenging-utahs-ban-on-kratom-products-is-dropped) on October 9, 2026 that the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food had reviewed and approved a new pure leaf kratom product submitted by Botanic Tonics, after which the company dismissed its lawsuit against the state's Kratom Regulation Act, [KNT reported on October 10, 2026][botanic-tonics-global-kratom-coalition-utah-attorney-general-brown-dismiss-kratom-regulation-act-lawsuit-tenth-circuit-appeal-udaf-approved-pure-leaf-product-fox-13].

## The defense account

Andrew Deesing, an attorney who told KUTV he represents about a half dozen business owners, some of whom are charged with felonies, said the list of banned products changes faster than retailers can keep up. "We've got people that are trying to comply," Deesing said, according to the report. He said police sometimes seize merchandise that stores have already removed from shelves and are holding in back rooms while the merchants decide what to do with it, KUTV reported. Deesing said products are delisted at eight o'clock and the SBI "raids" at 10 o'clock, according to the report. The report did not identify the businesses, the charges by statute, or the products seized, and it did not include a response from the SBI to Deesing's seizure allegation.

## The enforcement side

Lt. Jalaine Hawkes of the SBI told KUTV the agency receives complaints from parents, local police departments, and schools. Chloe DiLuigi, a senior at Crimson Cliffs High School in St. George and a member of the Washington County Youth Coalition, spoke to KUTV about community concern and said seeing the products at gas stations every day was "normalizing it."

KUTV reported that the Mayo Clinic describes kratom as "unsafe and ineffective." KUTV did not specify where or when the Mayo Clinic made that statement. KUTV did not include comment from the Utah Department of Agriculture and Food or from kratom trade groups.

## Context

The enforcement push follows the Utah Department of Health and Human Services' confirmation, [reported by KSL](https://www.ksl.com/article/51632638/utah-health-dept-issues-warning-after-4-deaths-linked-to-certain-herbal-supplements) on October 5, 2026, of [four deaths in four months][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] that the department tied to tablets sold as "Buzzers" and "cat's claw" and found to contain mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15, compounds it described as lab-made. The KUTV report did not say whether any of Deesing's clients are accused of selling those tablets or whether the felony charges involve natural leaf products, kratom beverages, or synthetic preparations.
