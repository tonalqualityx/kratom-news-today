Washington State University (WSU) researchers will lead a five-year project funded by a $7.5 million grant from the National Institutes of Health (NIH) to study kratom in relation to chronic pain, sleep, risks, and quality of life, [the university announced](https://research.wsu.edu/news/wsu-awarded-7-5-million-grant-to-study-kratom-chronic-pain-and-sleep) on October 26, 2026. Mary Paine, a professor in the Department of Pharmaceutical Sciences in WSU's College of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, will lead the Botanical Dietary Supplements Translational Research Team, according to the announcement. The university did not name the NIH institute making the award.

## Study design

WSU described a project with three components. Natural products chemists at the University of North Carolina Greensboro will select and authenticate a pure form of kratom, without the additives and compounds often found in market products. Scientists at the University of Arizona and WSU will conduct studies in mouse models. Paine's group and colleagues in the WSU Sleep and Performance Center plan a sleep study in humans with chronic low back pain. The results are intended to inform the design of future full-scale clinical efficacy trials of kratom, the university said. The announcement did not describe the authentication method, the dose form, or the enrollment target for the human study, and it did not say whether the project will examine 7-hydroxymitragynine separately from whole leaf.

Partners include researchers from WSU's colleges of Pharmacy and Pharmaceutical Sciences, Medicine, and Nursing, the university said.

## What Paine said

Paine estimated in the announcement that roughly 20% of people worldwide suffer from chronic pain, a figure she called conservative, and the announcement did not cite a source for it. She said kratom acts like a stimulant at lower doses and like an opioid at higher doses, according to the announcement. Paine said the science has lagged behind regulation and that some decisions are being made without definitive evidence, according to the announcement. She called the project "a beautiful example of an interprofessional collaboration at WSU."

The announcement said kratom is marketed as a pain reliever and an alternative to opioids, is not approved by the Food and Drug Administration for any medical use, and carries overdose and addiction risks, and it noted that several states and cities have banned it. WSU also said chronic pain is linked to poor sleep, anxiety, and reduced quality of life.
