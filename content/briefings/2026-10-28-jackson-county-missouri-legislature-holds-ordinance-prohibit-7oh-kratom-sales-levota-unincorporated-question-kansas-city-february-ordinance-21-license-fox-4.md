Jackson County, Missouri legislators discussed a proposed ordinance at a legislative meeting on October 26, 2026 that would prohibit the sale of products containing concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) throughout the county, then decided to hold the measure for further review, [Fox 4 Kansas City reported](https://fox4kc.com/news/jackson-county-leaders-discuss-new-restrictions-for-kratom-7-oh-products/) on October 27, 2026. The station's report by Sophia Ortiz described the targets as highly concentrated 7-OH products, which contain the alkaloid at levels far above the trace amounts in natural kratom leaf, and said the proposal comes as products containing kratom and 7-OH continue to be sold in vape shops and convenience stores across Missouri. Fox 4 reported that if approved, the ordinance would require businesses in Jackson County to stop selling products containing kratom and 7-OH, with penalties for violations; the report did not state the penalty amounts or say whether natural leaf kratom products would be treated differently from concentrated 7-OH products.

## What Levota said

Phil Levota told Fox 4 that county leaders want to avoid a patchwork of rules across jurisdictions and instead establish a consistent approach. "So we are all on the same goal and that is to make it illegal in Jackson County," Levota said, according to the station. The report did not state Levota's title. Questions remain about whether the ordinance would apply countywide or only to unincorporated areas of the county, and lawmakers said additional revisions are needed before moving forward, Fox 4 reported. "The ordinance would make kratom illegal in Jackson County, but the legislator and I want to make sure we fine-tune that. Does that just mean the unincorporated parts of Jackson County?" Levota said, according to the report. Levota said lawmakers expect to finish the measure within the next couple of weeks and described the issue as a serious problem for the community, according to Fox 4.

## Kansas City's existing ordinance

Kansas City passed an ordinance in February 2026 that banned synthetic kratom and restricted sales of natural kratom to people 21 and older, Fox 4 reported. According to Fox 4, businesses in the city that sell products containing kratom or 7-OH must obtain a license under the ordinance. The station's report did not say how a county measure would interact with the city ordinance or with Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway's enforcement campaign, which has issued 31 civil investigative demands to kratom businesses over concentrated 7-OH products and products containing MGM-15, a kratom-derived compound that the Drug Enforcement Administration said in its August 2026 notice it was placing in Schedule I, including Kansas City-area wholesalers, as [Kratom News Today reported on October 16, 2026][missouri-attorney-general-hanaway-nine-more-civil-investigative-demands-31-total-cease-and-desist-kc-distro-karmic-genesis-concentrated-7oh-schedule-i-analogue-missouri-adopted-mgpi-mgm-15-september-25-vapor-maven-joplin]. Fox 4 did not include comment from retailers, kratom trade organizations, or the county executive's office.
