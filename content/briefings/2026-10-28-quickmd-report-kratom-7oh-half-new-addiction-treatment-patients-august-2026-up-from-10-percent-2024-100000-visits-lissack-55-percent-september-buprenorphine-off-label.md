Patients seeking treatment for kratom and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) dependence accounted for half of QuickMD's new addiction treatment patients by August 2026, up from 10 percent in 2024, the telemedicine company said in a [press release](https://www.morningstar.com/news/accesswire/1241810msn/kratom-and-7-oh-now-half-of-quickmds-new-addiction-patients-up-5x-as-federal-restrictions-loom) distributed by ACCESS Newswire on October 27, 2026. The company said the figures come from a report titled "Kratom & 7-OH: An Emerging Treatment Population," based on aggregated, anonymized data from more than 100,000 completed visits related to kratom and 7-OH on its platform. The release describes 7-OH as a concentrated kratom alkaloid that acts on opioid receptors and is sold in tablets and shots at gas stations, smoke shops, and online; it does not say whether patients used concentrated 7-OH products or natural leaf kratom, a distinction the release does not address. The numbers are the company's own analysis of its patient population and have not been independently reviewed.

## What the company reported

"The surge has continued since the period covered in our report. In September, 55% of our new addiction treatment patients sought help for kratom or 7-OH dependence," QuickMD CEO Ryan Lissack said in the release. Lissack said some patients "turned to these substances hoping to move away from other opioids," while others "began using them without recognizing the potential for dependence." The company said that among patients who answered a question about prior opioid use, more than 80 percent reported previously using opioids other than buprenorphine, and that the share of patients with no prior buprenorphine experience rose from roughly 30 percent in 2024 to 50 percent in 2026.

The release states that buprenorphine-naloxone is approved by the Food and Drug Administration for treatment of opioid use disorder, that published case reports and case series have described its use for kratom and 7-OH dependence, and that its use for those conditions is off-label and the subject of ongoing research. QuickMD describes itself as the largest U.S. telemedicine provider of opioid use disorder care by patient visits and geographic reach, with more than 2 million visits across 44 states and the District of Columbia, and says it offers medication-assisted treatment for opioid use disorder as well as for 7-OH and kratom dependence, according to the release.

## The federal timeline the company cited

The release notes that the Drug Enforcement Administration published a notice of intent on October 14, 2026 to temporarily place products containing 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above specified thresholds in Schedule I, and that the order could take effect as early as November 13, 2026, as [Kratom News Today reported on October 14, 2026][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline]. "As federal restrictions tighten, people need to know that this dependence is treatable and that help is available," Lissack said in the release. [The New York Times reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/28/us/politics/trump-opioid-kratom.html) separately on October 28, 2026 that the administration adopted those thresholds after industry lobbying, as [Kratom News Today reports today][new-york-times-trump-administration-backs-down-mgpi-ban-kratom-industry-lobbying-gabbay-2-9-million-haddow-white-house-chittiboyina-25-to-500-times-kolodny-56-overdose-cases-48-fatal]. The release did not include comment from kratom trade organizations, and it did not say what share of the company's kratom and 7-OH patients also reported using other substances.
