The town of Seabrook, New Hampshire is researching a possible ban on synthetic kratom products after a resident recommended it, [WMUR reported](https://www.wmur.com/article/seabrook-ban-kratom-synthetic-102626/74253556) on October 26, 2026. The station's report by Kelly O'Brien said some critics, whom it did not name, contend that products marketed as kratom may contain synthetic or enhanced versions of the plant's alkaloids. WMUR's report included the nickname "gas station heroin" for these products but did not identify who uses it or provide data on addiction risk. The report did not say whether a Seabrook measure would reach natural leaf kratom as well as synthetic products, or how the town would define a synthetic product.

## What the town said

"We're reacting to substances that we feel are actually a danger to the public," Planning Board Chairman Jason Janvrin told WMUR. Janvrin told WMUR he understands the governing statute, which the station did not cite by number, to require him to consider the health, safety, and general welfare of the town's residents, and said he takes that "very, very seriously." Town officials are continuing to research the effect on residents' health and safety as they work to define what a ban could look like, WMUR reported. Officials plan to hold a public hearing, and if the Planning Board passes a ban it would go to the March Town Meeting ballot, according to the report.

WMUR reported that Franklin, New Hampshire banned kratom in 2019 after several kratom-related calls, and that the city's police chief said those calls have declined significantly since the ban. WMUR reported that Massachusetts has banned kratom and that an attempted statewide ban in New Hampshire failed. The report did not cite the Massachusetts law or the New Hampshire bill.

## The Global Kratom Coalition's position

Matthew Lowe of the Global Kratom Coalition told WMUR that kratom "is a leaf from Indonesia" that "has been sold in America for the last 50 years," and that the coalition's view is that natural kratom has benefits people have relied on for decades. "What is driving a lot of the concern today are synthetic alkaloid products that are not kratom but are calling themselves kratom," Lowe said, according to the station. The coalition works with towns and cities to explain the difference between natural leaf and synthetic products, WMUR reported, and Lowe said natural leaf "is getting into a situation with the babies being thrown out with the bathwater." The report did not attribute to the coalition a position on the specific measure Seabrook is drafting, and it did not include comment from local retailers or from state health officials.
