The Lynnfield, Massachusetts Board of Health voted unanimously at its October 22, 2026 meeting to uphold a pair of penalties against Centre Market, a convenience store in the town that was found twice to be selling kratom products in violation of a local regulation adopted in December 2025, [Lynnfield Weekly News reported](https://lynnfieldweeklynews.com/2026/10/27/centre-market-fines-upheld-on-appeal/) on October 27, 2026. The store's tobacco license will be restricted for seven days in November, and it must pay a cumulative fee of $3,000, according to the outlet's report by Sunny Ramos. The article did not say how the $3,000 is divided between the two violations. The report does not specify whether Feel Free contained a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom.

## How the violations arose

Regional Tobacco Inspector Ron Beauregard reported during a routine inspection that the store was illegally selling Feel Free, which the outlet described as a tonic of kratom and kava, Lynnfield Weekly News reported. Health Department Director Coral Hope reported that the products were still on display when she delivered the first violation notice, which led to the second violation, according to the outlet. The report did not give the dates of the inspection or of either violation.

Lynnfield's regulation, adopted in December 2025, prohibits the sale of products made from kratom, the outlet reported. The town's local measure predates the state action: in August 2026, the Massachusetts Department of Public Health issued a one-year ban on all forms of kratom, according to Lynnfield Weekly News. As Kratom News Today previously reported, the governor's office said [the statewide emergency order, which took effect on August 28, 2026][massachusetts-emergency-kratom-ban-takes-effect], covers natural leaf as well as concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products and authorizes local boards of health to enforce it. The Lynnfield penalties were issued under the town's own regulation, according to the outlet's account.

## What the owner and the board said

Centre Market owner Vinod Zaveri attributed the second violation to an employee's misunderstanding and told the board the penalty was unfair, Lynnfield Weekly News reported. Michael Fisher, the distributor who supplied the Feel Free sold at the store, attended the meeting with Zaveri, the outlet reported; the article did not attribute any statement to Fisher.

Board of Health Chair Rocco Iocco said the board's job is to protect public health and that the store showed no respect for the regulation, according to the outlet. Lynnfield resident Sarah Kelley urged the board to consider permanently revoking the store's license, citing what she described as kratom's potency and addictive potential, the outlet reported. The Select Board and A Healthy Lynnfield, a local group, both sent letters that Hope read aloud criticizing the store's violations and what they described as a pattern of decisions incompatible with public health, according to Lynnfield Weekly News. The board said Centre Market's license would be closely scrutinized going forward, the outlet reported.

Zaveri had appeared before the same board last year because he co-owns the gas station Pump and Pantry, which was found to have sold nicotine vapes to minors at least once, according to the outlet. Lynnfield Weekly News did not report whether Zaveri plans any further appeal, and the article did not include comment from the product's manufacturer.
