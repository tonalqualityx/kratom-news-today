The Peru, Illinois City Council moved toward an ordinance prohibiting synthetic kratom products at its October 26, 2026 meeting, with several aldermen signaling immediate support after Police Chief Sarah Raymond recommended drafting a measure modeled on neighboring Streator's, [Shaw Local reported](https://www.shawlocal.com/illinois-valley/2026/10/27/peru-council-agrees-to-prohibit-synthetic-kratom/) on October 27, 2026. The proposal would reach only synthetic kratom and would keep natural, plant-based forms available, according to the outlet. Shaw Local reported that an earlier proposal to ban kratom outright in Peru was withdrawn after public pushback. Shaw Local did not report a vote count, penalties, or an effective date, and described the draft ordinance as still to come. Raymond cited two of La Salle County's kratom-related deaths that occurred in Peru, and the report does not specify whether those deaths involved natural leaf kratom, a concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product, or another synthetic preparation.

## What the chief and the mayor said

Raymond told the council of personally supporting the regional effort and pointed to the two Peru deaths, Shaw Local reported. Raymond told the council, according to Shaw Local, that kratom "is beneficial in its natural state." Shaw Local did not report any medical or scientific basis for the statement, and the FDA has not approved kratom for any use. Shaw Local did not cite toxicology for either death.

Mayor Ken Kolowski said he supports a limited ban, and City Attorney Scott Schweickert said a draft ordinance would be ready for a vote at the full council's November 2, 2026 meeting, the outlet reported. Shaw Local did not identify the aldermen who voiced support and did not report any opposition at the meeting.

## The Streator model

The ordinance Peru is drafting is modeled on the one [Streator's council approved on October 21, 2026][streator-illinois-council-approves-synthetic-kratom-ban-natural-leaf-over-21-concentration-limits-certificate-of-analysis-mclaughlin-bedei-effingham-county-unanimous-all-kratom-phenibut-7oh-ban-fines-250-750], according to Shaw Local. As the outlet described it, Streator's ordinance sets concentration limits on synthetic forms of kratom, permits plant-based kratom that has not been chemically altered or concentrated, and keeps natural kratom available to people 21 and older. Shaw Local's October 27 report included the statement that synthesized kratom can be up to 50 times more potent than natural kratom and can be fatally toxic. The outlet did not name a study, agency, or expert as the source of the statement, and Kratom News Today has not independently verified it.

## A countywide push in December

La Salle County Coroner Rich Ploch has urged municipalities to restrict kratom and wants the county to extend a ban to unincorporated areas, Shaw Local reported. Ploch is set to present to the county board on December 3, 2026 in Ottawa and hopes a draft ordinance from the La Salle County State's Attorney's Office will be ready for the board by then, according to the outlet. Shaw Local did not report whether Ploch's countywide proposal would follow Streator's synthetic-only model or reach natural leaf as well.

Shaw Local reported that Ottawa, Seneca, Marseilles, and Mendota have enacted full or partial bans. The outlet did not cite the ordinances or describe the scope of each, and Kratom News Today has not reviewed the ordinance texts. The outlet reported that Oglesby has informally agreed to a prohibition and is expected to pass an ordinance at its November meeting. Shaw Local's report did not include comment from kratom retailers in Peru or from industry or advocacy organizations.
