Every one of 46 products labeled as cat's claw that a University of Mississippi laboratory bought in September 2026 contained at least one kratom-derived alkaloid, and none of the 46 labels declared one, according to a preprint posted October 22, 2026 by the university's National Center for Natural Products Research, [Behavioral Wire reported](https://behavioralwire.com/articles/cats-claw-labeled-products-kratom-alkaloids-detection-gap) on October 26, 2026. According to Behavioral Wire, the products were sold as cat's claw and were not labeled as kratom. By the outlet's account, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) appeared in 35 of the products at 0.01 to 95.4 milligrams per unit, and 39 contained at least one of two compounds that, according to Behavioral Wire, the Drug Enforcement Administration placed in Schedule I by temporary order on August 26, 2026. According to Behavioral Wire, the DEA's October threshold proposal is designed to distinguish concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations from the trace 7-OH naturally present in raw kratom leaf. The outlet's account does not say whether the 7-OH in each product was synthesized or concentrated from extract. The preprint has not been peer reviewed, Behavioral Wire noted. The outlet did not name the authors or link the preprint, and Kratom News Today was unable to independently verify it; the figures below are as reported by Behavioral Wire.

## What the lab found

The lab bought 46 chewable or unit-dose products from online retailers and three gas stations in Mississippi, spanning 17 brands, often with several flavors per brand, Behavioral Wire reported. According to the outlet, the researchers analyzed methanol extracts by ultra-high-performance liquid chromatography with photodiode array detection, used mass spectrometry for additional compounds, quantified nine compounds against reference standards, and compared the results with a botanically verified extract of Uncaria tomentosa bark, the plant sold as cat's claw.

Mitragynine was found in 41 of the 46 products, 7-OH in 35, MGM-15 in 24, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in 22, the outlet reported. Thirty-nine products contained mitragynine pseudoindoxyl or MGM-15, a share the outlet put at 85 percent. The 7-OH content ranged from 0.01 to 95.4 milligrams per unit, with 16 products at 22.2 milligrams or more and 12 at 40 milligrams or more, according to Behavioral Wire. In one five-flavor product line, 7-OH ranged from 45.2 to 84.2 milligrams depending on flavor, the outlet reported.

Eight products claimed on their labels to contain no kratom, no 7-OH, or both, Behavioral Wire reported. All eight contained mitragynine, 7-OH, and 3-dehydromitragynine, according to the outlet. Five products contained tianeptine at 27.9 to 40.2 milligrams per unit, and one product also contained yohimbine, melatonin, and lidocaine, the outlet reported.

Behavioral Wire reported that the labeled ingredient was largely absent: Uncaria alkaloids, the markers of authentic cat's claw, were not detected in 41 of the 46 products. Five products did contain Uncaria alkaloids, each alongside kratom-derived compounds, which the authors read as deliberate addition, according to the outlet. A single Uncaria marker compound appeared in 13 products, but none had the alkaloid profile of authentic bark, Behavioral Wire reported.

## Limitations the outlet flagged

Behavioral Wire listed several limits on what the preprint can show. It is a single time point covering 46 products and 17 brands, it reports no clinical outcome data, it did not assess batch-to-batch variability, and the authors did not link any product to a specific case, the outlet reported. The analyte panel did not include MGM-16, so the study cannot say whether that compound was present, according to Behavioral Wire. The outlet also said the sample does not represent the cat's claw category as a whole.

## The regulatory backdrop

Two of the compounds the lab reported, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15, are among the three kratom-derived compounds the DEA placed in Schedule I by temporary order on August 26, 2026, along with MGM-16, Behavioral Wire reported. On October 14, 2026, the DEA published a proposal that would control 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above one milligram per gram of a solid product, or whenever mitragynine is less than 100 times their concentration, the outlet reported; KNT covered [the Federal Register notices and the November 13 earliest effective date][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline] when they were published. The DEA said the aim of the thresholds is to separate products "intentionally enhanced or concentrated" from those whose alkaloid mix still resembles the plant, according to Behavioral Wire.

Behavioral Wire also listed state alerts about the same product category issued before the preprint. Behavioral Wire reported that the Utah poison center issued its first alert on adulterated cat's claw on July 22, 2026, naming the Buzzers and Homiez brands, and that Utah's Department of Health and Human Services on October 5, 2026 linked four deaths to high-potency illegal compounds in products including kratom products and cat's claw. KNT reported on [those four Utah deaths][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] and on [a San Francisco death that the city's health department and medical examiner attributed to MGM-15][san-francisco-first-fatal-mgm-15-overdose-script-botanicals-maui-labs-cats-claw-tablets], which, according to the department's alert, involved tablets whose packaging did not mention kratom. The Tennessee Bureau of Investigation logged 18 submissions since July of cat's claw-labeled tablet packs containing kratom, the Virginia Department of Health issued a notice on September 17, 2026, and the Washington Poison Center published a public advisory on October 7, 2026, according to Behavioral Wire. The outlet reported that the FDA's public page on hidden drug ingredients, titled "Hiding in Plain Sight," did not mention cat's claw or Uncaria as of October 12, 2026.

Behavioral Wire described the lab's botanical authentication work as supported by an FDA cooperative agreement. The outlet's report did not include comment from the FDA, from any of the 17 brands, or from kratom industry or advocacy organizations.
