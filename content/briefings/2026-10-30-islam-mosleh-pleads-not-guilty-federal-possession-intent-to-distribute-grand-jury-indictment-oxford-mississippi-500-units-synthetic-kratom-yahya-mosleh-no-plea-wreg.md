Islam Mosleh, 18, pleaded not guilty on Monday, October 26, 2026 to a federal charge of possession of a controlled substance with intent to distribute, [WREG reported](https://wreg.com/news/local/ms-man-pleads-not-guilty-to-federal-charge-of-posessing-kratom/). According to the station, the case involves products that agents described as "synthetic kratom," and a grand jury indicted Mosleh the previous Tuesday. Earlier court filings, [reported by Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs) on October 10, 2026 and [covered here][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond] on October 12, 2026, identified the products as Smax tablets containing the lab-made Schedule I compounds MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl. The filings described those compounds as distinct from natural leaf kratom.

According to WREG, the charge involves kratom and other substances alleged to have been sold at an Oxford, Mississippi business. Islam Mosleh and Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, 51, were arrested on September 24, 2026 after the Lafayette County Metro Narcotics Unit investigated alleged sales at a retail business and made an undercover purchase, and agents seized more than 500 units of synthetic kratom along with THC cartridges, gummies, and other suspected narcotics, the station reported. Yahya Mohamed Mosleh, who is also federally charged, had not yet entered a plea as of the station's report. Kratom News Today's earlier reporting identified the business as Puffs Vape, Tobacco and Beer Store, in [coverage of the raid and the two arrests][mississippi-tindell-ole-miss-toxicology-next-week-parkers-law-oxford-puffs-raid-500-kratom-units-two-arrests] in September 2026.

The station identified the business owner as Amer Alowdi, 40, and reported that Alowdi was indicted on narcotics trafficking charges in 2022 and left the country before prosecution. Kratom has been banned in Oxford since 2019, according to WREG.

The case followed the September 2026 deaths of two University of Mississippi students, and police said kratom from a retail store was found nearby, WREG reported. According to the station, officials have not confirmed a link between the kratom and the deaths or said where it was bought, and police say the investigation remains active. WREG's report did not name the compounds or the store, identify the court or defense counsel, say whether the kratom tied to the deaths was a synthetic preparation or natural leaf kratom, or include comment from attorneys for either defendant.
