The Tennessee Alcoholic Beverage Commission (TABC) said on October 31, 2026 that a fraudulent email designed to look like an official commission notice was sent to licensees that morning, falsely claiming the agency had "authorized the retail sale of kratom products," lifted all restrictions on hemp-derived cannabinoid products, and ended mandatory inspections, according to [Chattanoogan.com](https://www.chattanoogan.com/2026/10/31/525474/Halloween-Prank-No-All-Hemp-Product.aspx) and [WKRN](https://www.wkrn.com/news/tennessee-news/tennessee-alcoholic-beverage-commission-lifts-retail-limits-on-thca-delta-9-and-kratom-cannabis-flower-marijuana-weed/). In a statement addressed to licensees and issued hours later, the commission said it "has learned that a fraudulent email was distributed today that falsely appeared to come from TABC," and told recipients: "Please disregard the fraudulent message. Do not click any links, reply to it, or rely on its contents." The commission said all of its laws, rules, licensing requirements, product restrictions, inspection requirements, and enforcement practices remain in effect, WKRN reported.

Kratom is a small part of the fake message, which was built mainly around hemp. Its inclusion is notable because Tennessee's Matthew Davenport's Law has prohibited kratom since July 1, 2026, as KNT has reported, so a claim that the state's alcohol and hemp regulator had authorized retail kratom sales contradicted a criminal statute rather than an agency rule.

## What the email claimed

According to the text reproduced by Chattanoogan.com, the email carried the headline "TABC Lifts All Hemp Product Restrictions, Ends Mandatory Inspections" and said product-level restrictions on hemp-derived cannabinoids were lifted "effective immediately," so that retailers could sell THCA, Delta-8, Delta-9, Delta-10, HHC, THCP, and other hemp-derived cannabinoids at any concentration, including flower, edibles, beverages, and inhalable products. It said the change superseded restrictions under Public Chapter 526 and TABC's THCA product rules that took effect July 1, 2026, that no additional authorization, permit, or licensure was required, and that routine inspections were no longer mandatory, with no adverse action for products sold without one. The email said the rules applied statewide and required no action from licensees. It was signed in the name of Executive Director Russell F. Thomas and listed the commission's help desk email address and main phone number as media contacts, Chattanoogan.com reported.

WKRN reported that the commission's statement also said the email falsely claimed TABC licensing accounts had been deleted. Chattanoogan.com noted that the version of the email it reproduced did not contain that claim.

## What the commission and the hemp trade group said

Aaron Rummage, TABC's director of legislation, policy, and communication, called the email fake and said hemp restrictions remain in effect, Chattanoogan.com reported. Rummage said the commission had sent a communication to those affected to correct the misinformation. The commission described the sender as "an unidentified outside party," according to Chattanoogan.com, and neither outlet reported how the sender obtained what appeared to be TABC letterhead or a licensee distribution list.

Officials with the Tennessee Healthy Alternatives Association, a hemp industry trade group, suggested the agency had been "hacked," WKRN reported. The station said the commission had not determined how the email was sent, and neither outlet reported confirmation of a breach. The commission's statement apologized "for any confusion," according to WKRN. Neither report said how many licensees received the message or whether any retailer acted on it before the correction went out.

## Where Tennessee's kratom rules actually stand

Neither outlet reported that TABC has any role in regulating kratom. The state's kratom restrictions come from Matthew Davenport's Law, Public Chapter 950, which took effect July 1, 2026, as KNT reported when [the ban and Kansas's Schedule I classification took effect][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one]. Under that law, knowing possession of kratom is a Class A misdemeanor and knowing manufacture, delivery, sale, or possession with intent to sell is a Class C felony, according to the [Shelby County District Attorney's Office release](https://www.scdag.com/news-releases/da-steve-mulroy-west-tn-violent-crime-and-drug-task-force-announce-major-kratom-enforcement-operation) that KNT covered when [the West Tennessee Violent Crime and Drug Task Force seized nearly 30,000 packages of kratom products from a Memphis wholesaler][shelby-county-da-mulroy-west-tennessee-task-force-novelty-king-memphis-30000-packages-synthetic-kratom-klaz-cats-claw-tipton-county-4700-pills] on October 1. That release listed TABC among the agencies that assisted the Memphis operation alongside the Drug Enforcement Administration's Memphis office and the Tennessee Counterdrug Task Force.

The hemp framework the email targeted is newer. WKRN reported that lawmakers approved Public Chapter 526 earlier this year and that TABC drafted regulations creating a tiered licensing system, strict packaging and dosage caps, and mandatory product testing, all of which took effect July 1, 2026. The station also reported that federal hemp restrictions passed during the government shutdown negotiations take effect December 11, 2026 and cap total THC at 0.4 milligrams per container.

The commission's own statement framed the episode as a verification problem for licensees, telling them not to rely on the message's contents. The commission had not, as of the two reports, said whether it has referred the matter to law enforcement or identified the sender.

The disavowal came the same day California's governor's office published updated kratom and 7-OH enforcement figures, including a $10 million embargo-and-destruction total from the state's public health department, [which KNT covers separately today][california-newsom-halloween-release-cdph-embargoed-destroyed-10-million-kratom-7oh-products-legal-action-manufacturers-abc-9209-visits-8230-products-removed-97-97-percent-compliance-erica-pan-mgm-15].
