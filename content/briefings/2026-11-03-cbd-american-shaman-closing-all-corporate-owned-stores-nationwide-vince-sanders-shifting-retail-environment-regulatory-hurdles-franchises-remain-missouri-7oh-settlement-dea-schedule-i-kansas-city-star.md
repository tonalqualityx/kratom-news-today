Kansas City-based CBD American Shaman is shutting down its corporate-owned stores nationwide, [Kansas City Star columnist David Hudnall reported on November 2, 2026](https://www.kansascity.com/opinion/opn-columns-blogs/david-hudnall/article317887506.html) in a column published in the paper's opinion section. The column reported that founder Vince Sanders confirmed the decision in a statement and noted that the closures come as the company faces regulatory pressure over hemp and kratom products, including 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. The Missouri attorney general's office described the products at issue in its lawsuit against the company as concentrated 7-OH products. A perspective published in Frontiers in Pharmacology by Oliver Grundmann, Christopher McCurdy, and Kirsten Brown, [covered by KNT on October 30, 2026][frontiers-in-pharmacology-defining-kratom-perspective-grundmann-mccurdy-brown-four-product-categories-40-mg-mitragynine-per-serving-0-01-percent-7oh-leaf-ceiling-global-kratom-coalition-blanket-bans], distinguishes concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products from the trace 7-OH found in natural leaf kratom and proposes a 0.01 percent 7-OH ceiling for products sold as natural leaf kratom. The Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement of federal emergency scheduling said that action was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

## What Sanders said

"A shifting retail environment and persistent regulatory hurdles forced the difficult decision to close our corporate-owned brick-and-mortar locations and part ways with dedicated team members who served our brand admirably," Sanders said in the statement carried by the Star. Sanders added that the company regretted the impact on staff and looked forward to continuing to serve customers as the brand "moves into its next chapter," according to the column.

The Star reported that the chain includes both corporate-owned stores and independently owned franchises, and that the franchises are not affected by the decision. Franchise locations in Liberty, Missouri and Lawrence, Kansas will remain open, the column said. The corporate-owned store on Metcalf Avenue in Mission, Kansas closed last week, and a sign on the door directs customers to buy the company's products online, Hudnall reported.

American Shaman once had more than 40 shops in the Kansas City area, the column said, citing the Star's reporting in 2019, and the column said the nationwide network eventually grew to several hundred stores, most of them franchises. The column did not report how many corporate-owned stores are closing, how many employees are affected, or a timeline for the remaining closures.

## The regulatory pressure the Star described

Hudnall wrote that the closures come as American Shaman faces regulatory pressure on several fronts. On the hemp side, Hudnall wrote that the company sells hemp-derived products, including THCa flower, and described them as occupying a legal gray area. The column also referred to a new federal law narrowing the definition of hemp that Hudnall wrote takes effect in December; the column did not identify the statute.

On the kratom side, the column recounted that Missouri Attorney General Catherine Hanaway [sued American Shaman in March 2026](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-files-suit-against-american-shaman-for-unlawfully-manufacturing-and-selling-deadly-opioid-7-oh/), alleging the company illegally manufactured and sold 7-OH products, and that the lawsuit ended in June with American Shaman agreeing to stop selling any kratom product to Missouri customers in stores and online. According to the attorney general's release, the agreement covers all kratom products and alkaloids sold to Missouri consumers, natural leaf as well as concentrated 7-OH products; the Star's column did not separate the two categories. Kratom News Today (KNT) covered the suspension when [the attorney general's office announced it][missouri-ag-expands-kratom-investigation-american-shaman-suspends-sales]. According to the [attorney general's June 4, 2026 release](https://ago.mo.gov/american-shaman-agrees-to-suspend-sales-of-kratom-and-7-oh-in-missouri/), Shaman Botanicals, LLC, operating as American Shaman, also agreed to abandon Missouri-targeted retail advertising, including billboards, and the office may invoke an agreed $5 million penalty if the company breaches the agreement and fails to remedy the breach immediately. The same release said the office opened its investigation in November 2025 and that the Justice Department had seized thousands of the company's products.

The Star's column cited the paper's own investigation last year into Sanders' role in the 7-OH industry. According to the column's summary of that investigation, Sanders' affiliated company, Shaman Botanicals, made 7-OH for numerous other brands at a facility in Riverside, Missouri, while American Shaman sold its own Advanced Alkaloids products online and in its stores. Sanders told the Star that the cannabis business generated about $100 million annually and that the 7-OH business was "significantly larger," the column said.

## The pending federal order

The column said the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) plans to classify concentrated 7-OH as a Schedule I controlled substance and could issue that order as early as this month. KNT reported on October 10, 2026 that the [DEA's notice of intent][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14], scheduled for Federal Register publication on October 14, 2026, proposes placing products in Schedule I when 7-OH, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, or both exceed 1 milligram per gram in solids or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when mitragynine is present at less than 100 times those compounds. The DEA's notice states that a temporary scheduling order cannot issue until 30 days after the notice is published in the Federal Register, according to the [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine), which cites 21 U.S.C. 811(h).

The column did not report the positions of kratom advocacy or trade organizations on the pending order. The American Kratom Association (AKA) said in an [October 1, 2026 letter](https://fortune.com/press-releases/aka-urges-mississippi-ag-fitch-amend-kratom-ban-recommendation-2026-10-01/) to Mississippi Attorney General Lynn Fitch that it supports legislation specifically prohibiting synthetic and chemically manipulated opioid products and opposes bans on natural kratom. KNT's October 10, 2026 briefing reported that the AKA's August 2026 lawsuit against the DEA concerned the risk that the mitragynine pseudoindoxyl order could reach natural leaf rather than the scheduling of concentrated 7-OH itself. The AKA said in an October 9, 2026 statement that the litigation had been resolved, according to that briefing. The Justice Department said the threshold approach targets concentrated 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I," the briefing reported.

Two earlier developments involving the company have been covered by KNT. In August 2026, [KCUR reported](https://www.kcur.org/health/2026-08-29/kansas-city-kratom-company-american-shaman-cbd-dea) that a Kansas City-area company it identified as American Shaman [destroyed roughly $10 million in products][kansas-city-kratom-company-destroys-10-million-products] after the DEA's emergency Schedule I order for three synthetic kratom-related compounds took effect. In October 2026, Hanaway's office said it had issued [31 civil investigative demands][missouri-attorney-general-hanaway-nine-more-civil-investigative-demands-31-total-cease-and-desist-kc-distro-karmic-genesis-concentrated-7oh-schedule-i-analogue-missouri-adopted-mgpi-mgm-15-september-25-vapor-maven-joplin] to kratom manufacturers, distributors, and retailers over concentrated 7-OH products.

## What is not in the reporting

The Star published the piece as an opinion-section column, which the paper separates from its newsroom reporting. It is the only account of the closures KNT identified as of November 3, 2026. The column did not say whether American Shaman's online sales outside Missouri continue, whether the Riverside manufacturing operation is affected, or how franchisees learned of the decision. No franchise owner, trade association, or Missouri regulator was quoted in the column, and American Shaman had not issued a public release about the closures as of publication of this briefing.
