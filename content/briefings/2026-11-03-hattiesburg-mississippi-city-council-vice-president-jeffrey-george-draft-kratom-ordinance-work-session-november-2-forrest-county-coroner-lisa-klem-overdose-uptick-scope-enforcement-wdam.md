Hattiesburg City Council Vice President Jeffrey George presented a draft ordinance on kratom during the council's work session on Monday, November 2, 2026, opening a discussion among city leaders about whether the products should be allowed in the city, [WDAM reported on November 3, 2026](https://www.wdam.com/2026/11/03/hattiesburg-city-council-opens-discussion-kratom-ban/). City leaders told the station they are in the early stages of the conversation, and no ordinance has been approved. WDAM's report does not say whether the draft would apply to natural leaf kratom, to extracts, or to concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. The Justice Department drew that distinction in its August 25, 2026 announcement of federal emergency scheduling, which said the action was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. The report also does not specify which type of product was involved in the overdoses described at the meeting.

## What George and Klem told the council

George framed the proposal around Hattiesburg's student population, according to WDAM. George said that, as a college town with many young people, Hattiesburg should consider and discuss how far the city wants to go on these products, according to the station. George is in a third term representing Ward One, which includes the University of Southern Mississippi, [according to the city's council page](https://www.hattiesburgms.com/?p=17468).

Forrest County Coroner Lisa Klem also addressed the meeting, WDAM reported. Klem told the council that the coroner's office has seen an uptick in overdoses involving kratom over the last couple of years, and that bottles of kratom found at scenes once drew little notice because responders knew nothing about the product, but now register as a possible factor. Klem said the accessibility of the products is part of the concern, telling the council that most people take kratom because it is sold over the counter and assume it is all natural, and that combining it with other medications can cause an overdose, according to the station. WDAM did not report figures for the overdoses Klem described, whether toxicology attributed any death to kratom alkaloids alone, or whether the products involved were leaf, extract, or concentrated 7-OH preparations.

George raised a separate concern about marketing and oversight, telling WDAM that a product marketed for health uses without regulation or approval by the Food and Drug Administration is a concern because consumers do not know what is in the products or what is being added to them.

## The questions the council is weighing

WDAM reported that the city is looking at what other Mississippi communities have done and what approach might make sense for Hattiesburg. George described the open questions as the scope of the products the city might ban, who enforcement would target, and how the ordinance would be enforced, and said the council was hearing from law enforcement and coroners so that members could weigh those opinions before deciding how to address the products, according to the station. WDAM did not report the text of the draft, proposed penalties, a timeline for a vote, or any comment from retailers, a trade association, or kratom consumers.

## Where Hattiesburg fits in Mississippi

[Hoodline reported in September 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/09/tupelo-police-set-sept-12-deadline-to-enforce-long-delayed-kratom-ban/) that Mississippi's House Bill 1077 was signed in April 2025 and took effect that July. According to Hoodline, the law set a minimum purchase age of 21, capped 7-OH content at one percent, added a five percent tax on kratom sales, and preserved municipal authority to enact total local bans. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on October 25, 2026][mississippi-rep-lee-yancey-synthetic-kratom-ban-bill-controlled-substance-senate-reluctant-boyd-shelf-removal-authority-edney-schedule-iii-desoto-county-draft-ordinance-november-2], citing the [DeSoto Times-Tribune](https://www.desototimes.com/news/desoto-county-to-consider-kratom-ban/article_78389b23-722a-43d4-9ead-b7078b7c7d23.html), that DeSoto County supervisors ordered a draft ordinance banning kratom sales in unincorporated areas for review at the board's November 2, 2026 meeting, and that Rep. Lee Yancey is considering a bill to ban synthetic kratom products in the 2027 session.

According to KNT's earlier reporting, Tupelo police [began enforcing a ban on all kratom products][tupelo-mississippi-police-begin-enforcing-kratom-ban-september-12] on September 12, 2026. [WDAM reported on July 28, 2026](https://www.wdam.com/2026/07/28/magee-police-chief-discusses-recently-passed-kratom-ban-local-business-weighs-impact/) that Magee, in Simpson County, passed Ordinance No. 301 in late April 2026, that the ordinance bans the use, purchase, and sale of kratom, that a violation is a misdemeanor carrying a fine of up to $1,000 and/or six months in jail, and that Police Chief Dennis Borges said officers had not found any business in violation. A manager at a Magee tobacco store told the station that kratom had been the store's biggest seller.

According to KNT's earlier coverage, the September 2026 deaths of two University of Mississippi students in Oxford prompted renewed debate over kratom in the state. A federal court filing [reported by Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs), [which KNT covered on October 12, 2026][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond], said toxicology detected mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students. The Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement described MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, two 7-OH-related compounds the Drug Enforcement Administration placed in temporary Schedule I, as manufactured products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

Lt. Gov. WDAM's Hattiesburg report did not connect the draft ordinance to the Oxford deaths or to any specific product category, and George told the station only that the city is looking at what other Mississippi communities have done.
