The Mississippi Department of Public Safety announced that agents executed 14 search warrants at locations across Madison, Rankin, and Hinds counties on November 2, 2026 and seized more than 200 pounds of kratom-labeled products that investigators believe contain MGM-15, MGM-16, or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, [WJTV reported on November 3, 2026](https://www.wjtv.com/news/local-news/mississippi-authorities-seize-over-1000-pounds-of-suspected-illegal-products/). According to the Justice Department's [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), the Drug Enforcement Administration temporarily placed those three compounds in Schedule I, and the department described them as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH)-related substances and as manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. The reports from WJTV, [SuperTalk Mississippi](https://www.supertalk.fm/agents-seize-suspected-illegal-thc-kratom-like-products-in-central-mississippi-raids/), and [Darkhorse Press](https://darkhorsepressnow.com/news/2026-11-03/authorities-seize-more-than-1000-pounds-of-illegal-thc-kratom-products-in-metro-raids/) do not say whether any natural leaf kratom was among the seized products, and none reports that laboratory testing has confirmed the compounds the department named.

## What the department said was seized

According to the department's statement as reported by WJTV, the operation, which agents called Operation Preferred Purp, followed a six-month investigation that included undercover purchases. WJTV reported that agents seized more than 800 pounds of THC products packaged and marketed as legal merchandise intended for sale at businesses throughout Mississippi, and that investigators determined some of those products were destined for distribution and resale in other states. The department put the combined seizure at more than 1,000 pounds, according to WJTV.

The kratom-labeled portion came to more than 200 pounds, WJTV reported. The station identified the suspected compounds as MGM-15 (dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine), MGM-16 (9-fluoro-dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine), and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, and reported that the products will be transferred to the DEA and Homeland Security Investigations for further investigation. SuperTalk Mississippi reported that officials did not identify the locations where the warrants were executed, and that its reporters saw a large law enforcement presence at Magnolia Wholesale Vapor and Wellness in Jackson on the day of the raids. SuperTalk did not report that the department confirmed that business as a search location. Darkhorse Press published a photo caption describing a raid on a local vape distributor without naming it.

## Who took part, and what the accounts say about arrests

WJTV listed 10 participating agencies: the Mississippi Bureau of Narcotics, the Madison Police Department, the Ridgeland Police Department, the Rankin County Sheriff's Office, the Commercial Transportation Enforcement Division, the Mississippi Bureau of Homeland Security, the Mississippi Bureau of Criminal Investigations, Capitol Police, Homeland Security Investigations, and the DEA. Darkhorse Press referred to the state investigative agency as the Mississippi Bureau of Investigation.

The three outlets describe arrests differently. WJTV reported only that the investigation remains active and that additional arrests are anticipated. SuperTalk reported that arrests were made in the raids but that officials did not provide identities. Darkhorse Press reported that officials did not say how many people had been arrested. None of the three reports named a defendant, a charge, or a court.

## What officials said

Public Safety Commissioner Bo Luckey said in the department's statement that the operation demonstrated the importance of coordinated efforts among law enforcement agencies at every level, and that such operations keep dangerous, illegal substances from reaching Mississippi streets, SuperTalk reported.

Rankin County Sheriff Bryan Bailey said he was worried about dangerous products being sold to teens and young adults, Darkhorse Press reported. "Our young people's safety must come before anyone's profits," Bailey said, according to Darkhorse Press. None of the reports included comment from the businesses searched, from a kratom trade association, or from a cannabis or hemp trade group.

## Where the operation fits in Mississippi

SuperTalk reported that the push to remove suspected illegal products from retail shelves statewide follows the deaths of two University of Mississippi students who were reported to have had synthetic kratom-like products in their systems. A federal court filing [reported by Fox News Digital on October 10, 2026](https://www.foxnews.com/us/toxicology-reports-reveal-controlled-substances-ole-miss-students-found-dead-court-docs), [which Kratom News Today (KNT) covered on October 12, 2026][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond], said toxicology detected mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students. The same two compounds appear in the department's description of the November 2, 2026 seizure. None of the three reports on the raids said the department connected the seized products to the Oxford deaths.

According to KNT's earlier coverage, Attorney General Lynn Fitch [said on October 1, 2026 that she would ask lawmakers to ban kratom in all forms][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc] in the 2027 session, and Islam Mosleh, charged in federal court in connection with the Oxford case, [pleaded not guilty on October 26, 2026][islam-mosleh-pleads-not-guilty-federal-possession-intent-to-distribute-grand-jury-indictment-oxford-mississippi-500-units-synthetic-kratom-yahya-mosleh-no-plea-wreg].
