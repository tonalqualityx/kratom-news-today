The Irvine City Council gave final approval by a unanimous vote on October 27, 2026 to an ordinance that bans synthetic kratom products within city limits and prohibits the sale or distribution of kratom containing more than 2 percent 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), [Voice of OC reported on November 3, 2026](https://voiceofoc.org/2026/11/irvine-bans-synthetic-kratom/). The ordinance is directed at synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom: Voice of OC described 7-OH as a minor component of the kratom leaf that can produce opioid-like effects when synthetically amplified, and the ordinance leaves natural kratom legal for sale to people 21 and older, according to the report. Voice of OC did not publish the ordinance number, an effective date, or how the city intends to test products against the 2 percent line.

## What the ordinance does

According to Voice of OC, the ordinance limits sales of natural kratom products to people 21 or older, requires age verification for all kratom sales, bars the sale or distribution of kratom with more than 2 percent 7-OH, and prohibits synthetic kratom products within Irvine. Violators may face a misdemeanor, a fine of up to $1,000, up to six months in jail, or both, and businesses that violate the ordinance may have their licenses revoked, the outlet reported.

The council first approved the ordinance 5-2 on October 13, 2026, with Councilmembers Mike Carroll and James Mai voting no, and then gave final approval unanimously on October 27, 2026, Voice of OC reported. The same article also describes the final vote as having taken place "last week," which would place it in the last days of October; the outlet did not reconcile the two descriptions.

## How the measure got to a vote

Councilmember Melinda Liu proposed a similar ban in 2025, and it failed for lack of support from colleagues, according to Voice of OC. Liu brought the proposal back in July, and the council voted 4-3 on July 28, 2026 to direct staff to return with two ordinances, one on synthetic kratom and one on nitrous oxide canisters, [Voice of OC reported at the time](https://voiceofoc.org/2026/07/irvine-moves-to-ban-synthetic-kratom-and-whippets/). Councilmembers Betty Martinez Franco and James Mai and Mayor Larry Agran voted no in July, according to that report, and Martinez Franco said the city should determine whether there was a demonstrated need in the community before adopting new ordinances.

At the October 13, 2026 meeting, Liu said the city had received many emails thanking it for looking into an ordinance and argued the council should act before something bad happened, Voice of OC reported. Mai questioned whether any shops in Irvine sell synthetic kratom and argued a local ban was redundant because the products are already set to be federally scheduled, according to the report. "We could put an ordinance to ban cocaine and fentanyl as well under the same premise here," Mai said, according to Voice of OC. The report did not say what changed between the 5-2 vote and the unanimous final vote.

## Where the federal threshold stands

Voice of OC reported that Mai argued the products are already set to be federally scheduled. According to the [Justice Department's October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid), the Department of Health and Human Services recommended that 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) be placed in Schedule I when either substance exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when a product contains less than 100 times as much mitragynine as those compounds, and the Drug Enforcement Administration said it would publish a revised notice of intent. Separately, the Justice Department said in an [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the DEA had temporarily placed three 7-OH-related compounds in Schedule I and that the action was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

The American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow told [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/the-dea-is-cracking-down-on-kratom-this-time-some-advocates-are-on-board-2000826560) in an October 17, 2026 report that the association is "absolutely supportive" of banning or scheduling synthetic 7-OH products, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 18, 2026][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled]. According to Gizmodo, Haddow also asked for an amnesty for people who used 7-OH or other synthetic products marketed as kratom. Voice of OC did not report any trade association comment on the Irvine ordinance.

## The Orange County pattern

Irvine joins roughly a dozen Orange County cities that have adopted bans or restrictions, according to Voice of OC. The outlet reported that Fountain Valley banned synthetic kratom earlier in 2026, a measure [KNT covered on July 17, 2026][fountain-valley-restricts-high-7oh-kratom-products]; that Los Alamitos, Anaheim, Buena Park, Huntington Beach, and Laguna Niguel restrict kratom with more than 2 percent 7-OH; that Newport Beach, Dana Point, Tustin, and Stanton have banned kratom sales and distribution entirely; and that Dana Point and Garden Grove had also considered bans. La Habra began exploring a similar ordinance in July, [KNT reported on July 26, 2026][la-habra-weighs-kratom-whippet-ban]. KNT's July coverage reported that the Fountain Valley ordinance set its limit at 2 percent 7-OH in the alkaloid fraction, the same figure Voice of OC reported for Irvine and for the five cities it listed as restricting high-7-OH products.

## Local enforcement elsewhere in California

The Irvine vote arrived as another California jurisdiction weighed whether to take kratom enforcement into local hands. Yolo County supervisors were scheduled to consider on November 3, 2026 whether to adopt a county kratom ban that county staff or peace officers could enforce, [Abridged by PBS KVIE reported the same day](https://www.abridged.org/news/yolo-county-kratom-enforcement/). Abridged reported that state law prohibits the sale of kratom for consumption in California, without citing the statute, and that the county currently has no enforcement authority of its own and refers suspected violations to the state. Abridged reported that 26 percent of the county's tobacco retailers sold kratom in 2025, that seven people in the county have died with kratom in their systems since 2017, and that a December 2025 letter from county health officials produced a 20 percent drop in brick-and-mortar sellers. The Abridged report does not specify whether the products involved in those deaths were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom. Former Yolo County public health officer Aimee Sisson told the Board of Supervisors in April that kratom is illegal in the state, Abridged reported; the outlet did not report which statute Sisson cited. Abridged described the tradeoffs as greater flexibility and recourse for the county on one side, and cost, possible legal challenges, and duplication of state efforts on the other. No outcome of the November 3, 2026 meeting had been reported as of publication.

Governor Gavin Newsom's office said in an [October 31, 2026 release](https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/10/31/trick-treat-or-illegal-product-california-keeps-cracking-down-on-dangerous-products-hiding-in-plain-sight/) that the California Department of Public Health had embargoed or destroyed approximately $10 million worth of kratom and 7-OH products, [KNT reported on November 1, 2026][california-newsom-halloween-release-cdph-embargoed-destroyed-10-million-kratom-7oh-products-legal-action-manufacturers-abc-9209-visits-8230-products-removed-97-97-percent-compliance-erica-pan-mgm-15]. Abridged cited a July statement from Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control director Paul Tupy that nearly 98 percent of the department's licensed locations were in compliance after five months of kratom and 7-OH enforcement.
