The Mississippi State Medical Association (MSMA) is asking the Legislature to ban retail kratom sales statewide in the 2027 session and to permanently schedule concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and related compounds in state law, [the association said in a November 4, 2026 release](https://www.einpresswire.com/article/954509545/mississippi-physicians-call-on-legislature-to-implement-stronger-kratom-safeguards) distributed through EIN Presswire. The release describes the 7-OH ask as directed at intentionally concentrated or synthetic products rather than the trace amounts that occur naturally in botanical kratom, and it says that plant-based kratom remains legal for adult sale in most of Mississippi. The statewide ban the association seeks would cover that natural leaf product as well.

## What the association is asking for

According to the release, the ban request reaffirms a policy the association adopted in 2019 that commits it to advocate for a ban on kratom sales while supporting research into the substance's safety and efficacy. A companion policy supports a state-level screening and approval process for substances with potential for recreational use or abuse, along with public education about unregulated products, the release said.

The association listed seven steps for lawmakers to consider in 2027, according to the release:

- Ban the retail sale of kratom statewide, which the association said would replace what it called a patchwork of local ordinances with one rule while leaving room for approved research.
- Permanently schedule concentrated and synthetic 7-OH and related compounds in state law so that protections do not lapse if the federal temporary order expires.
- Create a state screening and approval process for over-the-counter substances with abuse potential so that new products are reviewed before reaching shelves.
- Strengthen enforcement of current law with penalties and regular compliance checks for sales to anyone under 21 or sales of products that violate the state's synthetic ban.
- Have coroners and medical examiners routinely test for kratom compounds in suspected overdose deaths and report findings to the Mississippi State Department of Health.
- Fund public education campaigns aimed at college campuses and young adults, wider naloxone availability, and coverage for treatment of substance use dependence.
- Preserve the authority of cities and counties that have already acted to set stronger rules.

"Mississippi made real progress in 2025, but a substance with opioid-like effects should not be sold next to candy and energy drinks," MSMA President Lucius Lampton said in the release. Lampton said physicians see the consequences of the products in clinics and emergency rooms and urged the Legislature to act in 2027, according to the release. The association described itself as the state's largest physician advocacy organization, representing nearly 5,000 physicians and medical students.

## What the association says current law covers

According to the release, the Legislature in 2025 banned synthetic kratom, limited sales of natural kratom to adults 21 and older, and required products to be kept behind the counter. The release said nearly 40 Mississippi cities and counties have gone further and banned kratom outright. In the 2026 session, lawmakers filed several bills to place kratom on Schedule I or Schedule III of the state's Uniform Controlled Substances Act, and none became law, the release said.

At the federal level, the release said three synthetic opioid-related compounds were temporarily scheduled on August 26 and that the Drug Enforcement Administration issued revised notices of intent on October 9 addressing 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI). The release characterized those federal actions as not comprehensively addressing botanical kratom and said temporary scheduling is not permanent. According to the [Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), the DEA's temporary scheduling of three 7-OH-related compounds was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. The [Justice Department's October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) said the Department of Health and Human Services recommended Schedule I control for 7-OH and MGPI above a threshold of 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when a product contains less than 100 times as much mitragynine as those compounds.

## Where the ask sits among other Mississippi proposals

The association's request is broader than the synthetic-only measures other Mississippi officials have discussed this fall, and it aligns with the position of the state's attorney general. Attorney General Lynn Fitch said on October 1, 2026 that she would ask lawmakers to ban kratom in all forms in the 2027 session, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported][mississippi-attorney-general-fitch-ban-kratom-all-forms-next-session-lafayette-metro-narcotics-hearings-synthetic-haddow-misled-gkc]. According to that coverage, the American Kratom Association's Mac Haddow said at an Oxford press conference the same day that the product tied to two University of Mississippi student deaths was "a federally controlled synthetic opioid and not traditional botanical kratom," and the Global Kratom Coalition said it supported a focus on synthetic compounds rather than a broad ban on natural leaf. The MSMA release does not mention either organization, and neither organization had publicly responded to the release as of publication.

Rep. Lee Yancey, a Brandon Republican, told Mississippi Today in October that Yancey was considering a synthetic kratom ban and exploring classifying kratom as a controlled substance because the Senate had been reluctant to ban the products, [KNT reported on October 25, 2026][mississippi-rep-lee-yancey-synthetic-kratom-ban-bill-controlled-substance-senate-reluctant-boyd-shelf-removal-authority-edney-schedule-iii-desoto-county-draft-ordinance-november-2]. The release's call to preserve local authority arrives as local governments continue to act: the Hattiesburg City Council [opened discussion of a draft kratom ordinance on November 2, 2026][hattiesburg-mississippi-city-council-vice-president-jeffrey-george-draft-kratom-ordinance-work-session-november-2-forrest-county-coroner-lisa-klem-overdose-uptick-scope-enforcement-wdam], KNT reported, and the Mississippi Department of Public Safety said agents seized more than 200 pounds of kratom-labeled products believed to contain MGM-15, MGM-16, or MGPI in raids the same day, [KNT reported on November 4, 2026][mississippi-department-of-public-safety-operation-preferred-purp-14-search-warrants-madison-rankin-hinds-november-2-200-pounds-kratom-labeled-products-mgm-15-mgm-16-mgpi-800-pounds-thc-dea-hsi-luckey-bailey-wjtv-supertalk]. The release does not reference the raids or the Hattiesburg discussion.

The association's position also tracks national physician advocacy. The American Medical Association's October 2, 2026 state advocacy update urged state legislatures to ban synthesized, purified, or derivative kratom products including 7-OH and MGM-15 in their 2027 sessions, [KNT reported][american-medical-association-october-2-state-advocacy-update-kratom-issue-brief-2027-sessions-ban-synthesized-purified-derivative-7oh-mgm-15]. The MSMA release goes further than that AMA brief by asking for a ban on retail sales of all kratom, including natural leaf, while the AMA update as KNT reported it focused on derivative products. The release did not say whether the association has a legislative sponsor for the 2027 proposals or whether a bill has been drafted.
