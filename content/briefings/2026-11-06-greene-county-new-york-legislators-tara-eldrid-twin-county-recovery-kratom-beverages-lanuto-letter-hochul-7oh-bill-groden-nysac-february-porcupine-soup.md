Greene County, New York legislators heard a presentation on kratom on the night of November 4, 2026 from Tara Eldrid, director of prevention services for Columbia and Greene counties, who described kratom beverages in dedicated refrigerated displays at local gas stations and a growing number of people seeking treatment for kratom use, [Porcupine Soup News reported](https://porcupinesoupnews.com/news-articles/kratom-concerns-raised-before-greene-county-legislators). The report identifies concentrated and synthetically produced 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) as the particular concern, distinguishing those products from the small amounts of 7-OH naturally present in kratom leaf, and says that distinction has complicated regulation, according to Eldrid.

## What the prevention director told legislators

Eldrid, whose organization Twin County Recovery Services merged with the Addictions Care Center of Albany in September, said her concerns intensified after hearing from a parent whose 26-year-old son, a public works employee in Rensselaer County, died after reportedly beginning to use kratom for energy and developing an addiction, according to the report. "He wound up aspirating on his vomit and died at the age of 26 years old," Eldrid said, as quoted by Porcupine Soup News. The report does not say whether the product involved in that death was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom, and Eldrid said she was unaware of any kratom-related deaths in Greene County itself.

Eldrid said she visits gas stations to see what is being sold and found kratom beverages in their own refrigerated display at one Greene County retailer, the outlet reported. She said products are often placed near the front counter with cigarettes and chewing tobacco, and she raised the popularity of energy drinks among middle and high school students as a reason the beverage format concerns her, according to the report. Eldrid also said that where kratom was once encountered among people trying to stop using other substances, "now we are finding that this is their drug of choice," and that "withdrawing from kratom is no different than withdrawing from any other opioid," according to Porcupine Soup News. Those characterizations are Eldrid's.

The report cited a Centers for Disease Control and Prevention analysis of poison center reports from 2015 through 2025 that identified 233 deaths associated with kratom exposure, 184 of which involved multiple substances, and said poison centers received 3,434 kratom exposure reports in 2025, an increase of roughly 1,200 percent from 2015. The outlet did not link to the CDC publication. The report also stated that the Food and Drug Administration has not approved kratom or 7-OH products for medical use.

## The state law and whether retailers know about it

New York enacted two kratom laws earlier in 2026, including a prohibition on sales to anyone under 21 and product labeling requirements, the report said. "Before that time anyone could purchase this," Eldrid said, according to the outlet, but she questioned whether local retailers are aware of the restrictions, particularly the labeling rules. "What I can do is provide education to our local retailers," she said. "As for enforcement, I think that is going to be the hardest question because there hasn't been a clear picture sent down from the state."

## Why the county sent a letter instead of passing a law

Legislator Michael Lanuto, a Republican from Catskill, said he has received numerous emails from concerned parents and has pushed for local legislation, but that "we were advised by counsel that our best course of action was the letter we sent to Albany," according to the report. That letter, sent last month after the Legislature adopted a resolution, urges Governor Kathy Hochul to sign the bill passed by the state Senate and Assembly that would prohibit businesses from knowingly selling products with 7-OH exceeding 2 percent of total alkaloid content or one milligram per serving, Porcupine Soup News reported. Assemblyman Brian Maher and the Ulster County Legislature made the same request of Hochul in late October, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 26][new-york-assemblyman-brian-maher-urges-hochul-sign-synthetic-kratom-bill-2-percent-total-alkaloids-1-mg-7oh-per-serving-dea-bulletin-14449-exposures-ulster-county-legislature-calls-signature-daily-freeman]. The report did not say why counsel advised against a local law.

Other New York counties have gone further. The report said Rensselaer County enacted a law prohibiting the commercial sale and distribution of both natural and synthetic kratom products, with exceptions for FDA-approved or physician-prescribed products and civil penalties of $500 per item for a first offense and $1,000 per item thereafter, with possible criminal prosecution for willful violations. Ulster County scheduled a November 17 public hearing on a proposed local law banning synthetic 7-OH kratom sales, [KNT reported on October 24][ulster-county-new-york-legislature-public-hearing-november-17-proposed-local-law-16-synthetic-7oh-kratom-sales-ban-murray]. The Greene County letter, as described in the report, targets high-concentration 7-OH products rather than natural leaf, while the Rensselaer County law covers both.

Lanuto, a former law enforcement officer, compared the situation to the K2 synthetic cannabinoid products of the late 2000s and recalled police and public health officials identifying and shutting down sellers, according to the report. The outlet noted that enforcing any kratom restriction could strain agencies with substantial workloads.

County Administrator Shaun Groden said he would circulate Greene County's letter to Hochul to other counties and ask the New York State Association of Counties to put kratom on the agenda for its February conference, the report said. The report said 10 states had enacted outright bans on kratom-derived products as of October 2026 and that the Drug Enforcement Administration had not finalized its proposed Schedule I action on concentrated kratom-related compounds as of November. The outlet did not report any comment from retailers or from a kratom trade association, and it did not report that the Legislature took any formal action at the meeting beyond the letter already sent.
