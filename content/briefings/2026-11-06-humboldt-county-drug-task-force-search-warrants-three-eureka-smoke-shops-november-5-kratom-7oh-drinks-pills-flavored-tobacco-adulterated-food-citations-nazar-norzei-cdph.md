The Humboldt County Drug Task Force served search warrants on three Eureka-area smoke shops on November 5, 2026 and seized kratom and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products sold as drinks and pills, along with flavored tobacco, according to a Humboldt County Sheriff's Office press release [published by the Times-Standard](https://www.times-standard.com/2026/11/05/warrants-served-at-3-humboldt-county-smoke-shops-over-suspected-kratom-cannabis-sales/) and [Lost Coast Outpost](https://lostcoastoutpost.com/2026/nov/5/kratom-krackdown-drug-task-force-raids-eureka-area/). The release describes 7-OH as an alkaloid found in trace amounts in kratom leaf and says a chemical extraction process is used to convert mitragynine into 7-OH, a description that matches concentrated or semi-synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf. The release does not say which of the seized products were concentrated 7-OH preparations and which were natural leaf kratom, and it groups the two together throughout as "Kratom/7-OH products."

## What the task force says it found

According to the release, task force agents received information earlier in 2026 that local tobacco shops were selling flavored tobacco, 7-OH, and kratom products, which the release says violates state codes and local regulations. Undercover agents bought kratom products at all three locations before the warrants were served on November 5, the release says. The task force was assisted by the sheriff's office POP team, its Marijuana Enforcement Team, and the Eureka Police Department, according to the release.

Agents found "a large amount of flavored tobacco being sold and products containing Kratom also being sold," and "these Kratom and 7-OH products were found to be in the form of drinks and in pill form," the release says. All products containing illegal substances were seized as evidence, and marijuana packaged for sale was found at the EZ Smoke Shop on W 14th Street, according to the release.

An employee at each location who sold kratom or 7-OH products to undercover agents was cited under California Health and Safety Code section 110620, which the release describes as the sale, offering for sale, or holding for sale of adulterated food, and under Eureka Municipal Code section 123.04 for the sale or possession with intent to sell flavored tobacco products, the release says. The release names Mohammed Nazar and Ratib Norzei as the owners of all three locations and says the same two charges will be requested against them. The release does not say whether anyone was arrested, and it does not quantify the seized products beyond "a large amount."

## What a reporter saw on the scene

Redheaded Blackbelt reporter Ryan Hutson was outside Cigarettes Cheaper in Eureka's Henderson Center on the afternoon of November 5 and reported multiple Eureka Police Department vehicles outside the store, numerous officers inside, and customers being turned away, [the outlet reported](https://kymkemp.com/2026/11/05/kratom-investigation-brings-officers-to-henderson-center-smoke-shop/). Officers at the scene told Hutson the sheriff's office was leading the investigation, that officers were at three separate locations, and that they were primarily looking for kratom products as well as cannabis products the business was not authorized to sell, according to the report. Sheriff's office spokesperson Summer Hansen told the outlet a task force press release would follow, and the outlet appended the release when it arrived.

A photo caption in the Redheaded Blackbelt report says an officer displayed a can labeled "Big Mit Kratom" among boxes of canned drinks inside Cigarettes Cheaper. That label points to a kratom beverage rather than a product marketed as 7-OH, which is why the question of which seized products were concentrated 7-OH and which were natural leaf kratom matters: the citations issued under the adulterated-food statute apply, according to the release, to "Kratom/7-OH" without distinguishing between them. The task force release itself names only EZ Smoke Shop; the Henderson Center store was identified by the reporter on scene, and the third location was not identified in any of the three reports.

## The state position the task force cites

The release says the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) "has declared Kratom/7-OH supplements illegal and considers them unsafe in any form" and sent notice to all licensed tobacco retailers in the state advising them of the prohibition. Redheaded Blackbelt reported that CDPH considers foods and dietary supplements containing kratom or 7-OH adulterated under federal and state law, and that the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control announced in January that it would educate licensees and take enforcement action against businesses continuing to sell prohibited products. Neither the release nor the reports cite the specific statute or regulation CDPH relies on for that position.

The Eureka warrants follow a statewide campaign that Governor Gavin Newsom's office described on October 31, 2026 as having embargoed or destroyed approximately $10 million in kratom and 7-OH products, with the Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control reporting a 97.97 percent compliance rate among its licensees, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 1, 2026][california-newsom-halloween-release-cdph-embargoed-destroyed-10-million-kratom-7oh-products-legal-action-manufacturers-abc-9209-visits-8230-products-removed-97-97-percent-compliance-erica-pan-mgm-15]. The Humboldt County action is a criminal-citation enforcement by a county drug task force rather than a licensing action by the state, and the release does not say whether the three stores hold alcohol licenses.

The release also cites the Drug Enforcement Administration's [July 1, 2026 notice](https://www.dea.gov/press-releases/2026/07/01/dea-temporarily-schedule-7-oh-and-related-substances-protect-public) of intent to temporarily place 7-OH and related substances in Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act. That action remains a notice of intent rather than a final scheduling order, as the American Kratom Association emphasized in its October 31 letter to The New York Times, [KNT reported][aka-haddow-demands-new-york-times-correction-or-retraction-october-28-mgpi-article-sulzberger-combined-7oh-mgpi-threshold-100-to-1-ratio-notice-of-intent-not-final-order]. The Humboldt County citations were issued under state food law and a city tobacco ordinance, according to the release. No federal controlled-substance provision was cited.

## How the release characterizes the products

The release says 7-OH "is commonly referred to as 'gas station heroin'" and describes the products as "highly addictive," with stimulant-like effects at low doses and opioid-like effects at higher doses. It says Humboldt County "has already begun to see the negative effects of Kratom/7-OH products" and that the overdose process "is similar to an opioid overdose and can be deadly," without citing case counts or specific incidents. Those characterizations are the task force's. The release does not cite a poison center, coroner, or medical source, and it does not distinguish between natural leaf kratom and concentrated 7-OH in describing those effects even though it separately describes natural 7-OH as a trace component of the leaf.

Lost Coast Outpost's headline describes the seized items as "kratom-extract products," a characterization that goes beyond the release text, which refers to kratom products and 7-OH products in drink and pill form. None of the three reports carried comment from the store owners or employees, from a kratom trade association, or from the manufacturer of any seized product.

The Eureka action is the second California local enforcement story this week. Irvine's city council gave final approval to an ordinance banning synthetic kratom and capping 7-OH at 2 percent, [KNT reported on November 5][irvine-california-city-council-final-approval-synthetic-kratom-ban-2-percent-7oh-cap-natural-kratom-21-age-verification-misdemeanor-1000-fine-liu-mai-carroll-voice-of-oc]. Unlike Irvine, Humboldt County proceeded under existing state food law rather than a new local ordinance, according to the release.
