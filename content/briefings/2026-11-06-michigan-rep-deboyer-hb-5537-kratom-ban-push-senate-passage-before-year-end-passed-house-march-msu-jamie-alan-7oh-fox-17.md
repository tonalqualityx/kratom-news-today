Michigan Rep. Bryan DeBoyer is renewing his push to get House Bill 5537, a statewide ban on kratom and synthetic variants such as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), through the state Senate and signed into law before the end of 2026, [Fox 17 reported on November 6](https://www.fox17online.com/news/local-news/grand-rapids/michigan-lawmakers-still-want-to-ban-kratom-push-to-pass-bill-before-end-of-the-year). The bill would prohibit growing, synthesizing, selling, distributing, or importing kratom and synthetic variants like 7-OH, according to the report, which means it covers natural leaf kratom as well as the concentrated and synthetic 7-OH products that have been the focus of federal action.

The House passed the bill in March, according to Fox 17, a vote [Kratom News Today (KNT) covered on May 24][new-york-signs-kratom-age-limit-michigan-house-passes-ban], and it has sat in the Senate since. Fox 17 reported that with the election season over, lawmakers in Lansing are turning to legislation they want passed before year-end, and DeBoyer is among them. "These are the kinds of issues that are not political, and we need to look at them for the health, safety, and welfare of the state of Michigan," DeBoyer said, according to the station.

## What DeBoyer said about the Senate and the governor

"It's not a blue or red issue," DeBoyer said, according to Fox 17. "I would love to believe that if it were passed out of the Senate, that Governor Whitmer would sign it, and then we can start to get our arms around what this product is, and try to differentiate between what is safe and what is not safe. But right now, we don't have any of those things." Fox 17 did not report any statement from Senate leadership on whether the bill will get a vote, and it did not report a position from Governor Gretchen Whitmer's office.

The Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center at Wayne State University recorded 347 kratom and 7-OH cases from 2021 through 2025, and its senior director, clinical toxicologist Varun Vohra, told Axios Detroit he favored a science-informed framework rather than a blanket ban, [KNT reported on October 13][michigan-poison-center-347-kratom-7oh-cases-2021-2025-87-in-2025-81-percent-vohra-science-informed-framework-not-blanket-ban-hb-5537-sb-1181-cherry-detroit-council-resolution-committee]. Fox 17's report did not mention that testimony or the competing Senate bill, SB 1181, that KNT's October coverage described.

## The data the station cited

Fox 17 reported that legislators point to state data showing that of the nearly 2,000 overdose deaths in Michigan in 2024, nearly half had kratom in their system. The station did not identify the dataset, did not say whether "in their system" meant kratom was a cause of death or was detected alongside other substances, and did not say whether the products involved were natural leaf kratom or concentrated 7-OH preparations. KNT could not locate the dataset behind the figure, which is the station's summary of legislators' claims.

The station also reported that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention says kratom use increased 1,200 percent nationally last year, without linking to a CDC publication. Porcupine Soup News, reporting on a Greene County, New York presentation this week, described the same CDC analysis as showing an increase of roughly 1,200 percent in poison center kratom exposure reports between 2015 and 2025, a ten-year comparison of poison center calls rather than a one-year increase in use, [KNT reported today][greene-county-new-york-legislators-tara-eldrid-twin-county-recovery-kratom-beverages-lanuto-letter-hochul-7oh-bill-groden-nysac-february-porcupine-soup]. Fox 17 did not reconcile its description with that analysis.

## The pharmacologist's distinction

Jamie Alan, a professor in the Department of Pharmacology and Toxicology at Michigan State University's College of Human Medicine, told Fox 17 that the leaf extract known as 7-OH can be addictive and deadly when mixed with other drugs. "Kratom by itself is a natural product; it's a leaf. Now, 7-OH is an opioid agonist, so this is going to work like something like oxycodone or hydrocodone or hydromorphone, so this can lead to addiction," Alan said, according to the station. "On its own it may not be as troubling, but when you combine it with these things that can lead to respiratory depression that can lead to death."

Alan's distinction between the leaf and 7-OH is the one that federal action has drawn: the [Justice Department said in August](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the DEA's emergency scheduling was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. HB 5537, as Fox 17 describes it, does not draw that line and would ban both. The station's report, which it said was converted to its platform with AI assistance and verified by its editorial team, did not include comment from a kratom trade association, a retailer, or any opponent of the bill. Fox 17 reported that nearly half of U.S. states already regulate kratom in some fashion.
