The Yolo County Board of Supervisors asked county staff on November 3, 2026 to develop amendments to the county's Tobacco Retail Licensing ordinance that would prohibit licensed tobacco retailers from selling kratom, [the Davis Enterprise reported on November 4](https://www.davisenterprise.com/news/supes-discuss-kratom-juvenile-detention-programs/article_e7f2086e-e85d-4291-9080-aaa18e54cba9.html). The direction stops short of the county-wide ban that [Abridged by PBS KVIE](https://www.abridged.org/news/yolo-county-kratom-enforcement/) reported was on the table ahead of the meeting: according to the Enterprise, the prohibition would be limited to businesses covered by the tobacco license, and its purpose is to give the county enforcement authority over those retailers that it does not currently have.

## What the board directed

According to the Enterprise, the amendment would prohibit kratom sales by licensed tobacco retailers and "provide additional local enforcement authority over licensed tobacco retailers." The report says existing state tobacco grant funds cannot be used for kratom enforcement, and that staff will continue coordinating with state agencies on local education, outreach, and enforcement. The Enterprise did not report a vote count, a timeline for the draft amendments to return to the board, or any discussion of penalties. The board's next meeting is November 17, according to the report.

The Enterprise described kratom products as sold in gummies, powders, beverages, extracts, and concentrates, with most sales occurring online or in vape and smoke shops. Neither the Enterprise nor Abridged reported whether the proposed amendment would distinguish natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, a distinction other California jurisdictions have drawn. Irvine's ordinance, which the city council gave final approval on October 27, bans synthetic kratom and caps 7-OH at 2 percent while leaving natural kratom legal for adults 21 and older, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 5][irvine-california-city-council-final-approval-synthetic-kratom-ban-2-percent-7oh-cap-natural-kratom-21-age-verification-misdemeanor-1000-fine-liu-mai-carroll-voice-of-oc]. The Yolo County direction, as reported, covers kratom as a category.

## The county's numbers

Yolo County Public Health has identified seven fatal overdoses involving kratom since 2017, and kratom has been identified in overdose deaths often in combination with other substances, the Enterprise reported. Abridged reported the same figure as seven people who have overdosed with kratom in their systems since 2017, along with a finding that 26 percent of the county's tobacco retailers sold kratom in 2025. Neither report specifies whether the products involved in those deaths were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom, and neither identifies the other substances involved.

In December 2025 the county health agency sent a letter to licensed tobacco retailers advising them to remove kratom products, according to the Enterprise. Abridged reported that county staff told the board in April that the letter alone produced a 20 percent drop in brick-and-mortar retailers selling kratom, and that products remain available online with delivery to the doorstep.

## Why the county is acting

Abridged reported before the meeting that the county has no enforcement authority of its own over kratom sales and relies on the state, with local efforts limited to retailer education and referring suspected violations to the California Department of Public Health. Abridged reported that selling kratom for consumption is illegal in California, without citing a statute, and quoted former county public health officer Aimee Sisson telling supervisors in April that "Kratom contains opioids. Kratom is dangerous. Kratom is illegal." Abridged did not report which statute Sisson relied on.

Abridged framed the choice before the board as a tradeoff: a local ban would give the county greater flexibility and recourse, at the cost of money, possible legal challenges, and potential duplication of state efforts. Tying the prohibition to the tobacco license, rather than adopting a standalone ban, is the narrower of those options. The state's own enforcement figures have come largely through Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control licensees. Abridged cited a July statement from Department of Alcoholic Beverage Control director Paul Tupy that nearly 98 percent of the department's licensed locations were in compliance after five months of kratom and 7-OH enforcement, and Governor Gavin Newsom's office said on October 31 that the state had embargoed or destroyed approximately $10 million in kratom and 7-OH products, [KNT reported on November 1][california-newsom-halloween-release-cdph-embargoed-destroyed-10-million-kratom-7oh-products-legal-action-manufacturers-abc-9209-visits-8230-products-removed-97-97-percent-compliance-erica-pan-mgm-15].

Abridged also reported that kratom and 7-OH "are often sold as remedies to pain, anxiety or opioid withdrawal, all of which are unproven," a characterization that is the outlet's. Neither report carried comment from a kratom trade association, a retailer, or a manufacturer, and neither reported public comment at the meeting.

The Yolo County action came two days before a county drug task force in Humboldt County served search warrants on three Eureka smoke shops and cited employees under state adulterated-food law for selling kratom and 7-OH products, [KNT reported today][humboldt-county-drug-task-force-search-warrants-three-eureka-smoke-shops-november-5-kratom-7oh-drinks-pills-flavored-tobacco-adulterated-food-citations-nazar-norzei-cdph]. That action relied on existing state law; Yolo County's approach, as reported by the Enterprise, would create a local licensing rule the county itself can enforce.
