Ohio Governor Mike DeWine and Attorney General Andy Wilson released a public safety bulletin from the Ohio Narcotics Intelligence Center (ONIC) and the Ohio Bureau of Criminal Investigation on November 6, 2026 warning that some dietary supplements sold at gas stations and vape shops contain illegal substances, [Spectrum News 1 reported](https://spectrumnews1.com/oh/columbus/news/2026/11/06/gov--dewine-warns-residents-of-mislabeled-product-containing-kratom). According to Spectrum News, a forensic analysis found synthetic kratom in a package of "Happy Daze Botanical Mango," labeled as containing 375 milligrams of cat's claw, that was found at the scene of a fatal overdose. According to the Justice Department's [August 25, 2026 emergency scheduling announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), the federal action against three synthetic kratom-derived compounds was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. Neither Spectrum News nor the [Springfield News-Sun](https://www.springfieldnewssun.com/local/ohio-officials-urge-against-taking-cat-s-claw-gray-market-supplements/article_0b721b57-1ea6-5bda-be00-bca02525b91a.html) reported which compound the analysis identified, and the News-Sun reported that toxicology results in the death were pending.

## What the bulletin says

The News-Sun reported that the bulletin describes cat's claw, the herb Uncaria tomentosa, as a legal herbal supplement, and that the state has confirmed multiple cases in which products labeled as cat's claw contained synthetic kratom. According to the News-Sun, the bulletin describes gray-market supplement packaging as often brightly colored, with claims of benign, naturally occurring ingredients, and says such products are often laced with illegal substances including synthetic opioids and LSD. Spectrum News listed the same warning signs, adding eye-catching designs.

The bulletin, as relayed by both outlets, says synthetic kratom compounds produce opioid-like effects, are highly addictive, and have been linked to psychosis, seizures, insomnia, hallucinations, and death. A photo caption supplied by ONIC and published by the News-Sun said one set of supplements pictured contained tianeptine, which the caption said the Drug Enforcement Administration calls "gas station heroin," and another contained 4-AcO-DET, which the caption described as an unregulated synthetic psychedelic. Neither outlet reported deaths or hospitalizations beyond the single fatal overdose, and neither gave overall case counts.

According to Spectrum News, DeWine said the state strongly encourages people to avoid buying herbal supplements from places like gas stations and vape shops, and that there is no quality control on the products. "Selling these unregulated products puts lives in danger and exposes retailers to serious legal liability," Wilson said. Wilson also urged lawmakers to work with law enforcement to keep mislabeled products off store shelves. The News-Sun reported that the bulletin directs tips to ONIC at 1-833-OHIO-NIC and points to the state's free naloxone program.

## Ohio's existing ban and earlier cat's claw reports

Spectrum News reported that synthetic kratom was banned in Ohio in 2025. According to a [Kratom News Today (KNT) report on May 14, 2026][ohio-synthetic-kratom-ban-takes-effect], a permanent Ohio Board of Pharmacy rule banning synthetic kratom took effect that month, replacing a temporary emergency ban the board had issued earlier; Spectrum News did not say which of those measures it was referring to. Neither outlet reported whether the bulletin cites that ban or whether any enforcement action against the Happy Daze product's seller or manufacturer is underway.

KNT has reported on cat's claw products linked to synthetic kratom alkaloids in Utah and Mississippi this fall. Utah health officials linked four deaths over four months to tablets sold as cat's claw under the Buzzers name that lab testing found contained mitragynine pseudoindoxyl and MGM-15, [KNT reported on October 6, 2026][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products]. A University of Mississippi Natural Products Center preprint reported undeclared kratom alkaloids in all 46 products labeled cat's claw that the researchers bought online and at Mississippi gas stations, with 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) up to 95.4 milligrams per unit, [KNT reported on October 29, 2026][university-of-mississippi-natural-products-center-preprint-46-cats-claw-products-all-contain-undeclared-kratom-alkaloids-7oh-up-to-95-mg-mgm-15-mgpi-in-39-tianeptine-five-eight-labeled-no-kratom-behavioral-wire]. The Ohio outlets did not report whether the Happy Daze package was tested for those specific compounds.

The American Kratom Association said in an October 9, 2026 statement that it applauded the DEA and the Justice Department for what it called targeted scheduling of 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, [KNT reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. Neither Ohio outlet reported an industry or advocacy response to the bulletin.
