The Pennsylvania Department of Health is alerting healthcare providers to a likely rise in patients with opioid-like withdrawal symptoms, [the Butler Eagle reported on November 6, 2026](https://www.butlereagle.com/20261106/health-dept-warns-of-increase-in-withdrawal-patients-from-planned-7-oh-rescheduling/). According to the Eagle, the cause is the Drug Enforcement Administration's (DEA) stated plan to classify 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), the related substances MGM-15 and MGM-16, and tianeptine as Schedule I controlled substances, a change the Eagle said could take effect as soon as November 13, 2026. According to the Eagle, the department said 7-OH occurs naturally in kratom only in very small amounts and that the danger comes from concentrated and synthetic 7-OH derivatives sold as tablets, gummies, and drink mixes, products distinct from natural leaf kratom.

## What the department told providers

The department said 7-OH and its related substances act similarly to opioids, are found in the leaves of the kratom tree, and are commonly sold at vape shops and convenience stores, an availability the department said may have led customers to believe the products were safe, the Eagle reported. The Eagle did not report the advisory's formal title, number, or issuing date.

Beyond the risks of concentrated products sold at gas stations and other retailers, the department warned that rescheduling itself could raise overdose risk because people seeking the substance may turn to street supplies or other illicit drugs, according to the Eagle. The department said people who use these substances for pain management, and people who do not identify as having a substance use disorder, may face a heightened risk of negative health outcomes and difficulty getting care, the Eagle reported. According to the Eagle, the department said buprenorphine and methadone, which are used to treat opioid use disorder, are also commonly used for 7-OH withdrawal.

Michael Lynch, a UPMC emergency medicine physician, told the Eagle that clinicians have experience treating withdrawal from these substances using approaches similar to those used for opioid withdrawal. "Our focus is on education, awareness and preparation, as well as adapting existing treatment protocols," Lynch said, according to the Eagle. A representative of the West Virginia University Health System declined to comment, the Eagle reported.

## The numbers

According to health department data cited by the Eagle, 7-OH contributed to 17 overdose deaths in Pennsylvania in 2026 through July, compared with 33 in 2025, 38 in 2024, and 65 in 2023. The Eagle reported that current overdose death data does not distinguish natural kratom from synthetic 7-OH, and that some labs have begun testing specifically for 7-OH and related substances. The Eagle did not say how the earlier years' figures were derived.

Pennsylvania poison control centers recorded 284 kratom exposure cases and 66 7-OH exposure cases from 2022 through June 30, 2026, the Eagle reported; of those cases, 156 involved significant illness, 38 required Narcan, and 26 required a ventilator, according to the Eagle. The Eagle did not break those outcomes down between the kratom exposure cases and the 7-OH exposure cases, so the report does not establish how many involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products rather than natural leaf kratom.

## The federal timeline

The DEA's notice of intent to temporarily place 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above a specified threshold in Schedule I, along with a separate notice of intent covering MGM-15 and MGM-16, was scheduled for publication in the Federal Register on October 14, 2026, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14]. According to that KNT report, the threshold recommended by the Department of Health and Human Services is 1 milligram per gram for solids, 1 milligram per milliliter for liquids, or a mitragynine ratio below 100 to 1, and Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. described the approach as drawing a line between natural kratom and enhanced products. The Eagle's report also lists tianeptine, which the Eagle did not describe as a kratom alkaloid; the two October 14, 2026 notices KNT reported on did not cover tianeptine, and the Eagle did not identify a separate federal action on it. The Justice Department announced on August 25, 2026 that the DEA had temporarily placed mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 in Schedule I, [KNT reported on August 26, 2026][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds].

The American Kratom Association said in an October 9, 2026 statement that it applauded the DEA and the Justice Department for what it called targeted scheduling of 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, according to the October 10, 2026 KNT report. The Eagle did not report any industry or advocacy response to the department's alert.

## Pennsylvania's own track

Separately, Pennsylvania's House and county prosecutors have acted on 7-OH in recent weeks. The Pennsylvania House voted 202-0 on October 6, 2026 to pass HB 2657, Rep. Bridget Kosierowski's kratom regulation bill, which as amended would limit natural kratom products to 0.05 percent 7-OH by dry weight and ban synthetic products, [KNT reported on October 6, 2026][pennsylvania-house-202-0-hb-2657-kosierowski-kratom-bill-amended-0-05-percent-7oh-dry-weight-no-extracts-12-percent-tax-kratom-regulatory-fund-haddow-restraint-senate-seven-days]. The Bucks and Chester County district attorneys urged the state in September 2026 to emergency-schedule 7-OH and kratom derivatives under state law, [KNT reported on September 1, 2026][bucks-chester-das-urge-pennsylvania-emergency-7oh-scheduling]. The Eagle's report does not mention the state bill or the prosecutors' request.
