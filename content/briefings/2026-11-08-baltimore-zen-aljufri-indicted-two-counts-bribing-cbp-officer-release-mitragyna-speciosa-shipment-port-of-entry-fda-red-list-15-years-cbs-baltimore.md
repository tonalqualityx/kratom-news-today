A Baltimore man has been indicted on two counts of bribing a federal official for allegedly offering money to a U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) officer to release a shipment of kratom held at a port of entry, [CBS Baltimore reported on November 7, 2026](https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/baltimore-man-indicted-bribery-customs-kratom/). According to the U.S. Attorney's Office for the District of Maryland, as cited by CBS, the defendant is Zen Aljufri, 61. The shipment was identified as Mitragyna speciosa, the kratom plant, and the report describes it as botanical kratom; it does not mention any concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product.

## The allegation

"According to the indictment, Aljufri corruptly offered to pay a CBP officer to release goods from a hold at a port of entry," CBP said in a statement quoted by CBS Baltimore. CBP said Aljufri intended to influence the officer to help defraud the United States, which the agency described as a violation of the officer's official duty, according to CBS. The CBP Baltimore Field Office investigated the case, CBS reported.

The shipment was being held because kratom is on the Food and Drug Administration's (FDA) "Red List" of substances subject to detention at the border, CBP said, according to CBS. The [FDA's kratom page](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/public-health-focus/fda-and-kratom), which CBS linked in its report, states the agency's position that kratom is not lawfully marketed in the United States as a drug, a dietary supplement, or a food additive. CBS did not report which port of entry held the shipment, the shipment's size or country of origin, the amount of the alleged bribe, or when the alleged offer was made.

Each count carries a maximum sentence of 15 years in prison if Aljufri is convicted, according to CBP as quoted by CBS. An indictment is an allegation, and the CBS report does not indicate whether Aljufri has entered a plea or is represented by counsel. CBS did not name the U.S. Attorney or report the date the indictment was returned, and the U.S. Attorney's Office statement was described as released on a Friday, which would be November 6, 2026. CBS also did not identify Aljufri as the operator of any kratom business or say whether the shipment was intended for commercial sale.

## A bribery case, not a drug case

As CBS Baltimore described it, the charges concern the alleged offer to pay a federal officer, and the report mentions no drug charge tied to the shipment itself. The kratom was held under an FDA import hold rather than seized as a controlled substance, according to CBP's account in the CBS report. The pending Drug Enforcement Administration action on kratom alkaloids, which [Kratom News Today reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14], concerns 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl above a threshold rather than leaf kratom, and the CBS report does not connect the indictment to it.

The CBS Baltimore report does not connect the indictment to that litigation.
