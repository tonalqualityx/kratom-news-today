The Wilmington, Illinois City Council adopted an ordinance at its November 4, 2026 meeting that bans the sale of kratom, 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and tianeptine within the city, [Shaw Local reported on November 6, 2026](https://www.shawlocal.com/wilmington-area/2026/11/06/wilmington-bans-sales-of-kratom-and-other-potentially-dangerous-substances/). According to Shaw Local, the ordinance's kratom definition covers the Mitragyna speciosa plant, its leaves, extracts, alkaloids, and derivatives, and any compound or mixture containing mitragynine or 7-OH. That language reaches natural leaf kratom, which contains 7-OH only in trace amounts, as well as the concentrated and synthetic 7-OH tablets and shots that have been the focus of recent federal and state action. Shaw Local did not report a vote tally or an effective date.

## What the ordinance covers

Beyond kratom and 7-OH, the ordinance bans tianeptine, which Shaw Local described as a synthetic substance that is not approved by the Food and Drug Administration for medical use and can act on opioid receptors. It also bans what the ordinance calls novel psychoactive drugs, meaning products with psychedelic, psychotropic, or hallucinogenic effects similar to federally controlled drugs, and novel synthetic drugs designed to mimic controlled stimulants, depressants, and hallucinogens, according to Shaw Local. Nitrous oxide cartridges, cylinders, balloons, and other devices intended for recreational inhalation are covered too, with exemptions for medical, dental, industrial, automotive, and culinary uses, Shaw Local reported.

The prohibition extends past retail sales. According to Shaw Local, the ordinance also bars processing, manufacturing, labeling, marketing, advertising, giving away, or delivering the listed products. Violations carry fines of $250 to $750 per offense, and a business can face suspension or revocation of its business, tobacco, or liquor license under the city's municipal code procedures, Shaw Local reported.

## The debate at the council table

Alderman Marty Orr voted against the ordinance, the only dissent Shaw Local reported. "It's something that I think that people need to make their own decisions," Orr said, according to Shaw Local. "Some of it is very beneficial for people." Orr asked whether residents who use kratom for chronic pain or acute anxiety should be denied access, and he said he knew of no deaths attributed to kratom in Wilmington, Shaw Local reported.

Paula Ekstrom, program director of the Wilmington Coalition for a Healthy Community, argued for the ban. "I want to remind you that even though kratom is billed as natural, it is not always natural," Ekstrom said, according to Shaw Local. She said much of the kratom on the market is synthesized and still acts on opioid receptors, Shaw Local reported. Alderwoman Rachel Conforti, who Shaw Local identified as a pharmacist, said buying natural products "does not guarantee it's safe," and said there are no manufacturing regulations for these products and that people seeking pain relief may not know about the risk of dependence, according to Shaw Local.

Shaw Local did not report any comment from retailers, manufacturers, or kratom advocacy organizations, and the article does not say whether the council considered a narrower measure limited to synthetic or concentrated 7-OH products.

## A different model than Streator and Peru

Wilmington's approach differs from the one other Illinois municipalities have taken in recent weeks. The Streator City Council approved an ordinance on October 21, 2026 that bans synthetic kratom and sets concentration limits while allowing natural leaf products for adults over 21, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 23, 2026][streator-illinois-council-approves-synthetic-kratom-ban-natural-leaf-over-21-concentration-limits-certificate-of-analysis-mclaughlin-bedei-effingham-county-unanimous-all-kratom-phenibut-7oh-ban-fines-250-750]. The Peru City Council signaled support for a ban built on the Streator model that would leave natural leaf on shelves, [KNT reported on October 29, 2026][peru-illinois-council-signals-support-synthetic-kratom-ban-natural-leaf-stays-streator-model-raymond-two-county-deaths-schweickert-november-2-vote-kolowski-ploch-la-salle-county-board-december-3-shaw-local]. Wilmington's ordinance, as Shaw Local described it, makes no such distinction and bans the plant itself along with its derivatives. Its fine range of $250 to $750 matches the range KNT reported for Effingham County's all-kratom ban in that October 23 briefing.

Wilmington is in Will County, about 60 miles southwest of Chicago. Shaw Local did not report whether neighboring municipalities or Will County are considering similar measures.
