The Czech Health Ministry counted 515 people treated for kratom overdoses between April and June 2026, according to data compiled by the Institute of Health Information and Statistics and [reported by Expats.cz on November 7, 2026](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/kratom-overdoses-send-515-people-for-treatment-as-czechia-plans-tighter-rules). The ministry counted 609 treatment cases in the quarter, because some patients were treated more than once, Expats.cz reported. The report does not describe the products involved, so it does not indicate whether patients had used leaf powder, extracts, or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations.

## Who was treated

People under 20 made up the largest group, at 196 patients, another 102 were between 20 and 24, and around two-thirds of patients were male, according to the ministry figures as reported by Expats.cz. More than 60 percent of recorded cases involved serious health problems, and 203 people received acute inpatient care, including intensive care, Expats.cz reported. More than 330 of the reports came from hospital care, a count of reports rather than individual patients, according to the outlet.

Expats.cz also reported that police recorded 17 deaths associated with kratom in the Czech Republic last year, involving high levels of kratom alone or in combination with alcohol, medicines, or THC. The report does not say how the deaths were attributed or whether toxicology distinguished mitragynine from 7-OH.

## The ministry's case for tighter rules

Health Minister Adam Vojtěch said the data show that "despite regulated sales, kratom is still reaching children and adolescents," and that continued access for minors is a clear reason to strengthen the rules, according to Expats.cz. Dita Protopopová, the ministry's senior director for mental health and addictions, warned against treating kratom as a harmless natural product and called for clearer sales rules, enforcement, prevention, and access to professional help, Expats.cz reported.

The figures arrive as the government's amendment to the addictive substances law awaits parliamentary action. The cabinet approved the measure, known as lex kratom, on September 14, 2026, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on September 16, 2026][czech-cabinet-approves-lex-kratom-age-21-online-sales-ban-excise-tax]. As KNT reported then, the bill would raise the purchase age from 18 to 21, ban online and cross-border sales, confine sales to licensed physical stores, and add an excise tax scaled to concentration, and it drew opposition from the Chamber of Commerce and from addiction specialists who argued an online sales ban would push buyers to unlicensed foreign sellers. Expats.cz described the amendment as raising the minimum age to 21 starting next year, tightening requirements for sellers, and strengthening the powers of customs officers and public health inspectors. The outlet did not report whether parliament has voted on it.

Expats.cz did not report comment from kratom sellers or from the addiction specialists who opposed parts of the bill in September.
