The owner of a Jonesboro, Arkansas gas station faces a felony charge after state regulatory officers reported finding kratom pills displayed for sale during an inspection, [NEA Report reported on November 6, 2026](https://neareport.com/2026/11/06/gas-station-owner-felony-kratom-possession-jonesboro/). Arkansas classifies both mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) as Schedule I controlled substances, according to the Arkansas Department of Health list cited by NEA Report. NEA Report described the seized items only as kratom pills and did not say whether they were leaf capsules or a concentrated 7-OH product, a distinction the state list as described by the outlet does not draw.

## The inspection

Arkansas Regulatory Police inspected the Jonesboro Exxon at 3109 Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. Drive on November 5, 2026 and reported seeing pills they identified as kratom displayed for sale, according to NEA Report. Officers called in the Second Judicial District Drug Task Force to assist, the outlet reported. Mirzali Lakhani, whom investigators identified as the station's owner and permit holder, cooperated and consented to a search of the rest of the inventory, according to NEA Report. Officers seized 62 suspected kratom pills in total, packaged as 31 two-pill packages, the outlet reported.

## The charge

Lakhani faces one count of possessing a Schedule I or II controlled substance, a Class C felony, according to Craighead County court records cited by NEA Report. Judge David Boling set bond on Lakhani's own recognizance on Thursday, and Judge Tommy Fowler left that bond in place at a Friday court appearance, NEA Report reported. Lakhani is scheduled to appear in Craighead County Circuit Court on January 25, according to his first-appearance notice as reported by the outlet, which did not state the year. NEA Report did not report any statement from Lakhani, an attorney, or the Arkansas Regulatory Police beyond the court records, and it noted that all suspects are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

## Similar enforcement in Utah

In Utah, a State Bureau of Investigation unit funded to police smoke shops and convenience stores has pursued kratom retailers, some of them charged with felonies, [KUTV reported on October 26, 2026](https://kutv.com/news/local/defense-attorney-says-retailers-want-to-comply-as-utah-pursues-businesses-selling-kratom), and a defense attorney representing about a half dozen owners told the station the list of banned products changes faster than his clients can follow, [Kratom News Today reported on October 27, 2026][utah-state-bureau-of-investigation-unit-kratom-retailers-defense-attorney-andrew-deesing-six-business-owners-felonies-prohibited-list-changes-seized-merchandise-hawkes-kutv]. NEA Report did not indicate that the Jonesboro inspection was part of a broader sweep.
