The Los Angeles County Medical Examiner's Office has attributed the August death of a 22-year-old Southwest Airlines passenger to the "combined effects of 7-Hydroxymitragynine and Mitragynine," [the New York Post reported on November 6, 2026](https://nypost.com/2026/11/06/us-news/cause-of-death-revealed-for-22-year-old-who-died-mid-air-during-flight/). The Post described both compounds as found in kratom and said the manner of death is listed as an accident. [People also reported the finding on November 6](https://people.com/cause-of-death-revealed-for-southwest-passenger-22-who-experienced-mid-flight-medical-emergency-12240554). Neither outlet reported what product Rain McMillen Walmsley consumed. The reporting therefore does not establish whether a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom was involved.

## The flight

Walmsley was aboard Southwest Flight 958 from Dallas to Los Angeles on August 7, 2026 when he suffered a medical emergency and stopped responding, according to the Post. Southwest described the event at the time as a "medical incident involving a customer" and gave no further detail about the passenger, the Post reported. [10News reported in August](https://www.10news.com/news/authorities-investigate-22-year-old-mans-death-on-southwest-flight-from-dallas-to-la) that paramedics met the plane after landing and pronounced him dead, and that the medical examiner had deferred the cause of death pending toxicology results.

## What the finding leaves open

According to [a STAT News explainer published November 6, 2026](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/06/what-is-kratom-addiction-risks-explained-7-oh/), 7-OH is both a minor constituent of dried kratom and a metabolite the body forms from mitragynine, while the concentrated tablets, gummies, shots, and drink powders now sold commercially can contain far more 7-OH than ordinary leaf. STAT reported that federal policy discussions have focused on those concentrated products rather than on traditional leaf. The Post and People reports do not say which kind of product was involved, and they do not say whether the medical examiner measured the concentrations of either alkaloid or whether investigators recovered a product.

In the deaths of two University of Mississippi students in September, a federal filing reported toxicology showing mitragynine alongside MGM-15 and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 12, 2026][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond]. The Post and People reports on the Walmsley case do not include that level of detail.

## Other cases the Post cited

The Post placed the finding alongside an ordinance in California and two deaths in Mississippi. It cited the Irvine City Council's ordinance restricting kratom sales to adults 21 and older and banning products with more than two percent 7-OH, which [KNT reported on November 5, 2026][irvine-california-city-council-final-approval-synthetic-kratom-ban-2-percent-7oh-cap-natural-kratom-21-age-verification-misdemeanor-1000-fine-liu-mai-carroll-voice-of-oc], and the Mississippi student deaths. The Post did not report any statement from Walmsley's family, from Southwest beyond its August statement, or from the medical examiner's office beyond the cause and manner of death.
