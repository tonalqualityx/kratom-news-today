The Merrimack, New Hampshire Town Council put off action on a proposed local ban on certain kratom products until its November 19, 2026 meeting after a law firm representing the American Kratom Association (AKA) threatened to take the town to court, [the New Hampshire Union Leader reported on November 8, 2026](https://www.unionleader.com/news/local-news/discussion-of-proposed-kratom-ban-delayed-in-merrimack-a069338b). The Union Leader described the targets as certain synthetic or semisynthetic kratom products and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), which the paper called a concentrated kratom compound. Natural leaf would stay on shelves for adults 21 and older under the draft, according to the paper.

## The letter

Town Council Chair Nancy Harrington told the council the town had received a letter from Eric Evans, a partner at a law firm in Menlo Park, California, that represents the AKA, the Union Leader reported. According to Harrington's account as reported by the paper, the firm argued the ordinance would harm local businesses, asked the town to remove the item from Thursday's agenda, and said it was "prepared to seek declaratory and injunctive relief in superior court."

Harrington said other municipalities had already adopted similar ordinances and that she expected a legal review to move quickly, the Union Leader reported. "I also think we need to be cautious about going ahead without some legal advice," she said, according to the paper. Councilor Peter Albert asked whether the town had vetted the law firm. Harrington replied that the town's attorneys would probably do that, the Union Leader reported.

## What the ordinance would do

The proposal came out of presentations at recent council meetings by state Rep. and Town Councilor Nancy Murphy and Merrimack Police Department Prevention, Enforcement and Treatment Officer Justin Agraz, who argued for a ban on certain synthetic or semisynthetic kratom products sold in stores, according to the Union Leader. Agraz said the goal was to prevent the sale or distribution of kratom and 7-OH, and he had previously told councilors that the risk of addiction to kratom is severe, the Union Leader reported.

As drafted, the ordinance would exempt "natural, unadulterated, botanical-form kratom leaf and ground, natural kratom leaf," which adults 21 and older could still buy, according to the Union Leader. [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 12, 2026][streator-illinois-october-21-vote-ordinance-ban-synthetic-kratom-concentration-limits-mitragynine-7oh-total-alkaloids-natural-leaf-21-mclaughlin-merrimack-new-hampshire], citing earlier local reporting, that the council was weighing a ban on most kratom products with an exemption for natural leaf for adults 21 and older. The Union Leader's November 8 account describes the targets as synthetic or semisynthetic products and 7-OH.

The Union Leader also noted that SB 557, a statewide bill that would have made synthetic and semisynthetic kratom illegal in New Hampshire, died in the Legislature earlier this year. In Seabrook, the planning board has been considering its own synthetic kratom ban for a March town meeting ballot, [KNT reported on October 28, 2026][seabrook-new-hampshire-planning-board-considers-synthetic-kratom-ban-janvrin-public-hearing-march-town-meeting-ballot-lowe-global-kratom-coalition-franklin-2019-wmur].

## Where the AKA sits

The AKA's public position separates natural leaf from concentrated 7-OH. Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy, has argued that the AKA should not defend every concentrated 7-OH product and should instead draw a "clear, bright line" between natural kratom and concentrated 7-OH products, [STAT News reported on November 6, 2026](https://www.statnews.com/2026/11/06/what-is-kratom-addiction-risks-explained-7-oh/). In October the association said its Capitol press conference and congressional briefing would explain how the federal threshold plan for 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl differentiates botanical kratom from synthetic and chemically manipulated products, [KNT reported on October 13, 2026][american-kratom-association-capitol-press-conference-october-13-rayburn-congressional-briefing-october-14-doj-dea-october-9-thresholds-media-mischaracterizations-ole-miss-synthetics-posing-as-kratom].

The Union Leader's account of the Merrimack letter describes an objection framed around harm to local businesses, and the paper did not report which provisions the letter objected to. Its account does not show whether the AKA's objection reaches the ordinance's synthetic and 7-OH provisions or only its scope and local effect.

The Union Leader did not identify the law firm beyond Evans and its Menlo Park location, did not report the letter's text, and did not report comment from the AKA, Merrimack retailers, or the town's attorneys. The council is scheduled to take the matter up again on November 19.
