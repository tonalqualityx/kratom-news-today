Hillsborough County, Florida commissioners scheduled a public hearing for 10 a.m. on November 10, 2026 on a proposed ordinance that would ban the sale of concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, [Bay News 9 reported](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/11/10/hillsborough-county-considers-ban-on-concentrated--oh-products). According to Bay News 9, 7-OH is found naturally in the kratom plant at low levels and can be concentrated into more potent forms, and the proposed ordinance targets concentrated, chemically enhanced products while excluding natural kratom teas. The outlet reported that commissioners could vote on the ordinance after the hearing. The report was published before the hearing and does not include its outcome.

## The county's stated reasons

Commissioner Harry Cohen described the products at issue as separate from kratom, Bay News 9 reported. "This is a synthetic derivative of kratom that people are using," Cohen said, according to the outlet. Cohen said the county medical examiner's office has found that the substance killed or was implicated in the deaths of 34 people in Hillsborough County in the last two years, Bay News 9 reported. The outlet did not publish the medical examiner's data.

According to Bay News 9, Florida Attorney General James Uthmeier issued an emergency rule in 2025 declaring 7-OH at certain thresholds a Schedule I controlled substance. Cohen said the proposed county ordinance would provide local coverage if that rule expires in 2027, the outlet reported. Bay News 9 did not report the thresholds or cite the rule.

## A clinician and a kratom tea seller

Dr. Jason Fields, an addiction psychiatrist at BayCare Behavioral Health, told Bay News 9 that 7-OH can be addictive and that the number of people seeking treatment after using it has risen sharply in the last year. Fields said some patients return to street opioids when 7-OH is unavailable, according to the outlet. Fields also said natural forms of kratom can be a problem for people with substance use disorders, Bay News 9 reported.

Tom Ponzo and Ian Beckles, co-owners of Dignitary Cafe, told Bay News 9 that the business sells kava and kratom teas brewed in-house from powdered kratom and does not sell synthetic 7-OH products. The two owners said they support a ban that specifically targets concentrated 7-OH while keeping natural kratom teas legal, according to the outlet. Ponzo said the cafe does not want to see addiction problems come from 7-OH sold on its own in concentrated pills or liquids, Bay News 9 reported.

Bay News 9's report did not include a position from a kratom trade or advocacy organization, and it quoted no seller of concentrated 7-OH products.
