The coroners of Hamilton, Butler, and Warren counties in southwest Ohio said on November 9, 2026 that kratom and related alkaloids are appearing more often in postmortem toxicology results, [WVXU reported](https://www.wvxu.org/health/2026-11-09/southwest-ohio-coroners-kratom-overdose-deaths). Hamilton County Coroner Dr. Lakshmi Sammarco counted about 14 suspected cases involving kratom and kratom alkaloids in the region in the last couple of months, according to WVXU. The reports do not specify, case by case, whether the products involved were concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations or natural leaf kratom. Butler County Coroner Dr. Lisa Mannix said the concentration of alkaloids increases when the compounds are extracted and processed into liquids, tablets, and other products, according to [a WCPO report republished by the Springfield News-Sun](https://www.springfieldnewssun.com/local/greater-cincinnati-coroners-warn-of-deadly-drugs-sold-at-gas-stations-vape-shops/article_e8d31c87-e6eb-5fdd-9c26-5a0b66fb3a08.html).

## The counts each outlet reported

The outlets described the regional figure in different terms. WVXU quoted Sammarco as counting about 14 suspected cases. WCPO reported the figure as about 14 suspected deaths. [Fox 19 reported](https://www.fox19.com/2026/11/09/tri-state-coroners-raise-alarms-about-kratom-dietary-supplements/) that more overdose deaths in the three counties are being attributed to kratom and did not give a number.

WCPO reported county-level figures from the news conference. According to WCPO, Butler County has confirmed two deaths involving kratom-related substances and has a third suspected case under investigation. Warren County Coroner Dr. Russell Uptegrove's office has recorded three confirmed deaths involving mitragynine, the primary alkaloid in kratom, out of 19 confirmed drug overdose deaths in the county in 2026, WCPO reported. Hamilton County is investigating four suspected overdose deaths involving kratom-related compounds, and Sammarco said one of those cases also involved fentanyl, according to WCPO.

Sammarco also said the Hamilton County Crime Laboratory has received 38 drug seizure cases involving mitragynine and MGM-15, a synthetic compound, in 2026, along with four cases involving 7-OH, WCPO reported. Mannix told WVXU that deaths in Butler County from fentanyl, other opioids, methamphetamine, and cocaine are down in 2026.

## What the coroners said about the products

According to WCPO, the coroners said products sold in concentrated forms can be substantially different from the plant's natural leaf, and Sammarco said laboratory testing has identified alkaloids in products whose labels did not clearly disclose them. Mannix said the people in Butler County's cases obtained the substances at retail establishments, WCPO reported. "You don't know exactly what you're getting," Uptegrove said, according to WVXU. The coroners also said the legal status of natural kratom does not mean it is free of health risks, WCPO reported.

WVXU reported that the coroners pointed to deaths elsewhere that have drawn news coverage, including that of a 22-year-old passenger on a Southwest Airlines flight, a case [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 9, 2026][los-angeles-county-medical-examiner-southwest-flight-958-passenger-rain-mcmillen-walmsley-22-combined-effects-7-hydroxymitragynine-mitragynine-accident-new-york-post-people].

## Legal status as the coroners described it

According to WCPO, the coroners said Ohio has classified certain kratom-related alkaloids as Schedule I controlled substances while natural leaf and ground leaf powder remain legal under state law. WCPO did not cite the statute or rule. Fox 19 reported that forms other than leaf or powdered leaf have been illegal in Ohio since May 2026, also without citing a statute or rule. Sammarco said Ohio stores have been directed to remove non-leaf and non-powder products from shelves and that enforcement seems unclear, according to WVXU.

WCPO reported that the Ohio Attorney General's Office and the Ohio Narcotics Intelligence Center have issued their own warnings about illegal drugs in retail products marketed as herbal supplements, a bulletin [KNT reported on November 7, 2026][ohio-governor-dewine-attorney-general-wilson-onic-bci-bulletin-happy-daze-botanical-mango-cats-claw-synthetic-kratom-fatal-overdose-gas-stations-vape-shops-spectrum-news-springfield-news-sun].

WVXU reported that leaders in some states, including Ohio and Kentucky, have called for banning kratom and 7-OH products. None of the three reports included a response from a kratom trade or advocacy organization.
