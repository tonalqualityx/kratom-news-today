The DeSoto County, Mississippi Board of Supervisors approved an ordinance on November 2, 2026 that prohibits selling, manufacturing, possessing, distributing, or possessing with intent to sell kratom anywhere in the county, [DeSoto County News reported](https://desotocountynews.com/mississippi-news/desoto-county-kratom-ban-passes/) on November 10, 2026, citing the DeSoto Times-Tribune. Each violation carries a penalty of six months in jail and a $1,000 fine, according to the outlet. Neither DeSoto County News nor [Action News 5](https://www.actionnews5.com/2026/11/10/desoto-county-passes-kratom-ban/) reported whether the ordinance distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and neither outlet published the ordinance text or quoted a kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## Scope and timing

DeSoto County News reported that the ordinance takes effect 30 days after passage and that stores carrying kratom products may get a grace period of up to 30 days to clear the products from shelves. Action News 5 reported on November 10, 2026 that kratom sales "are now banned" in the county and did not mention an effective date. Action News 5 described the ordinance as covering sale, distribution, and manufacturing, while DeSoto County News also listed possession.

Supervisors had discussed a ban limited to unincorporated areas in October 2026, [as Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 25, 2026][mississippi-rep-lee-yancey-synthetic-kratom-ban-bill-controlled-substance-senate-reluctant-boyd-shelf-removal-authority-edney-schedule-iii-desoto-county-draft-ordinance-november-2]. County Attorney Adam Emerson told the board that research changed that plan, according to DeSoto County News. Emerson said a similar unincorporated-only ordinance in Pike County was tied up by a legal challenge for about a year, the outlet reported.

The county ordinance does not override city rules, and a city's own kratom ordinance takes precedence, Emerson said, according to DeSoto County News. The DeSoto County Sheriff's Office will enforce the ordinance and cases will go to Justice Court, the outlet reported.

## What supervisors said

Supervisor Lee Caldwell said the main concern is that kratom is unregulated, DeSoto County News reported. "Even though some say it helps them, it is unregulated and may have other additives added to it," Caldwell said, according to the outlet's account of the Times-Tribune report.

Supervisor Robert Foster voiced support for the ban while opposing searches of homes for kratom packages by local law enforcement, DeSoto County News reported. Foster said the county should act because the Legislature has no timeline, according to the outlet.

County Coroner Josh Pounders has said kratom-related deaths are rising in the county, mostly among young people, according to Supervisor Mark Gardner's account at the meeting as relayed by DeSoto County News. The outlet published no figures from the coroner and did not specify whether the deaths Pounders cited involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products or natural leaf kratom.

## Other Mississippi action

Action News 5 reported that the city of Hernando and more than 30 other Mississippi municipalities and counties have already prohibited kratom. The Mississippi State Medical Association counted nearly 40 local bans in a release that [KNT reported on November 5, 2026][mississippi-state-medical-association-lampton-urges-2027-legislature-statewide-kratom-sales-ban-permanent-state-scheduling-concentrated-synthetic-7oh-otc-screening-nearly-40-local-bans].

DeSoto County News reported that kratom drew new attention in north Mississippi after the deaths of two University of Mississippi students in September 2026, which the outlet said authorities believe were linked to the substance. DeSoto County News did not specify whether the product involved was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom. A federal court filing reported by Fox News Digital said toxicology found mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl in both students, [according to KNT's October 12, 2026 report][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond].
