The Hillsborough County, Florida commission voted 7-0 on November 10, 2026 to prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom products containing concentrated or chemically altered compounds, including 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), [WUSF reported](https://www.wusf.org/health-news-florida/2026-11-11/hillsborough-bans-sales-of-concentrated-and-chemically-altered-kratom-products). According to WUSF, the products at issue are concentrated or synthesized 7-OH preparations sold as tablets, gummies, capsules, or liquid shots, and 7-OH occurs naturally in the kratom plant only in trace amounts. The ordinance exempts kratom in its natural, unaltered form, the outlet reported. The vote followed the public hearing that [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 10, 2026][hillsborough-county-florida-commissioners-november-10-hearing-concentrated-7oh-sales-ban-cohen-34-deaths-bay-news-9].

## What the ordinance covers

According to WUSF, the measure amends the county's existing synthetic drug ordinance to cover kratom products that have been chemically altered or concentrated, along with synthetic derivatives such as MGM-15 and MGM-16. The ordinance applies in unincorporated areas of the county and prohibits production, manufacturing, distribution, sale, display for sale, marketing, and advertising, WUSF reported. Violations are misdemeanors with penalties of up to 60 days in jail, a $500 fine, or both, according to the outlet. WUSF did not report an effective date.

[Fox 13 Tampa Bay reported](https://www.fox13news.com/news/hillsborough-county-ban-synthetic-kratom-compound-7-oh) that the ordinance targets manufactured kratom products containing concentrated synthetic compounds and places no restrictions on the natural, herbal form of kratom.

## The state rule behind the county vote

WUSF reported that Florida placed 7-OH and several related compounds on the state's Schedule I list in August 2026 under a temporary emergency rule that expires June 30, 2027, and that Hillsborough commissioners wanted a permanent prohibition. The outlet did not cite the rule. Fox 13 reported that county officials acted because the state order could expire in 2027.

## Public comment

The vote came after about two hours of public comment from more than 50 speakers, some supporting the restrictions and some opposing them, according to WUSF. Joseph Petri, whom WUSF identified as a former chief executive of a Palm Beach County substance abuse treatment center, asked commissioners to keep natural kratom separate from the concentrated products. "If you go to a gas station and you pull out a tab or a gel pack or a gummy, that's not kratom anymore," Petri said, according to WUSF.

Other speakers asked the commission to ban the natural plant as well, WUSF reported. The outlet reported that most of those speakers described family members or friends who had suffered addiction or died after using kratom products. WUSF's account of that testimony did not specify in each case whether the product was a concentrated 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom, and the outlet cited no medical examiner records.

Commissioner Chris Boles called the vote a first step, WUSF reported. Commissioner Donna Cameron Cepeda said after the public comment that Cameron Cepeda would favor a total ban, according to the outlet.

## Where the death counts differ

Fox 13 reported that, according to Hillsborough County, the medical examiner linked mitragynine to more than 30 deaths in the county. Commissioner Harry Cohen put the figure at 34 deaths over two years and tied it to concentrated products, [Bay News 9 reported on November 10, 2026](https://baynews9.com/fl/tampa/news/2026/11/10/hillsborough-county-considers-ban-on-concentrated--oh-products). None of the three outlets published the medical examiner's data.

Neither WUSF nor Fox 13 quoted a kratom trade or advocacy organization or a seller of concentrated 7-OH products.
