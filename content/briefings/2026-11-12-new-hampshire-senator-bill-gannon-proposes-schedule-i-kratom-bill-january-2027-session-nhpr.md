New Hampshire state Sen. Bill Gannon, a Republican from Sandown, has proposed a bill to classify kratom as a Schedule I drug under state law, [New Hampshire Public Radio (NHPR) reported](https://www.nhpr.org/politics/2026-11-11/nh-newhampshire-kratom-dangerous-drug-classification) on November 11, 2026. The bill is not yet drafted and would be prepared ahead of the legislative session that begins in January 2027, according to NHPR. NHPR did not report whether the proposal would distinguish natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and the outlet quoted neither Gannon nor a kratom trade or advocacy organization directly.

## What the proposal would do

Gannon wants the state to place kratom under the strictest level of drug controls, according to NHPR. The outlet described Schedule I drugs as those deemed to have a high potential for abuse, no accepted use for medical treatment, and no accepted safe use under medical supervision. Schedule I drugs cannot be prescribed, NHPR reported.

NHPR reported that the state Senate and House debated bills earlier in 2026 that were intended to make synthetic and semi-synthetic kratom illegal to prepare, distribute, make, sell, possess, or advertise, with an exception for research. The outlet did not report the outcome of those bills.

## How the proposal compares with federal action

NHPR reported that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) filed a notice of intent in July 2026 to temporarily place synthetic 7-OH products in Schedule I. That temporary scheduling would not apply to botanical kratom products from the Mitragyna speciosa plant, according to NHPR.

The American Kratom Association (AKA) issued support for the DEA scheduling of synthetics, NHPR reported. The association maintains that synthetics should be separated from natural kratom leaf products, according to the outlet. NHPR did not report an AKA position on Gannon's proposal.

## Local measures in New Hampshire

Several New Hampshire communities are considering steps to regulate or prohibit kratom products, NHPR reported. The Manchester Board of Mayor and Aldermen is weighing a ban, according to NHPR, which attributed that detail to the New Hampshire Union Leader.

The Merrimack Town Council tabled its proposed ordinance until November 19, 2026 after a law firm representing the AKA sent the town a letter, [as Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 9, 2026][merrimack-new-hampshire-town-council-tables-kratom-7oh-ordinance-november-19-american-kratom-association-law-firm-eric-evans-threatens-declaratory-injunctive-relief-harrington-agraz-union-leader]. Seabrook's planning board is researching a ban on synthetic kratom products, [KNT reported on October 28, 2026][seabrook-new-hampshire-planning-board-considers-synthetic-kratom-ban-janvrin-public-hearing-march-town-meeting-ballot-lowe-global-kratom-coalition-franklin-2019-wmur].
