The Peoria, Illinois City Council voted 8-3 on November 10, 2026 to defer the first reading of a proposed ordinance prohibiting retail sales of kratom and hemp-based THC products until its December 8, 2026 meeting, [WCBU reported](https://www.wcbu.org/local-news/2026-11-11/peoria-to-consider-a-ban-on-kratom-hemp-based-thc-products) on November 11, 2026. Council members want the draft split into separate measures on kratom and hemp-based THC so each can be considered individually, according to WCBU. WCBU did not report whether the draft distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and the outlet quoted no kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## What the draft would do

The proposed ordinance would block stores from offering kratom, hemp-based THC, and other synthetic alternatives, WCBU reported. City Attorney Patrick Hayes told the council that the ordinance would prohibit kratom sales and would leave possession alone, according to WCBU.

Hayes told the council that a number of fatalities have been partially attributed to the use of kratom mixed with other substances, WCBU reported. WCBU's report does not specify whether the products in the fatalities Hayes cited were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom. Hayes also cited the Food and Drug Administration's position that kratom is not lawfully marketed in the United States as a drug product, a dietary supplement, or a food additive in conventional food, and said that position has created a regulatory gray area, according to WCBU.

Peoria would join East Peoria, Washington, Pekin, and Peoria Heights among area communities that prohibit kratom sales, WCBU reported.

## Natural leaf and synthetic 7-OH at the meeting

Council member Alex Carmona stressed the difference between natural kratom and synthetic versions, WCBU reported. Carmona said the state is not outlawing kratom and that adults over 21 should be able to make their own choices, according to the outlet.

Kenny Dobson, vice president of Ware Brands, spoke during public comment in favor of natural kratom and against synthetic 7-OH products, WCBU reported. The outlet identified Ware Brands as the operator of two tobacco and vape stores in Peoria. Dobson told the council that the company has never sold the synthetic products and has lobbied for years to have them prohibited, according to WCBU.

"They are engineered in a lab, concentrated into flavored tablets and shots, and they stole the name kratom," Dobson said of 7-OH products, according to WCBU.

Dobson also told the council that the company has sold natural leaf kratom for more than a decade with no record of a complaint, hospitalization, or police incident, WCBU reported. That statement is the retailer's account of the company's own sales record.

## The vote and the hemp provisions

Council members Tim Riggenbach, Denis Cyr, and John Kelly opposed the deferral, WCBU reported. Riggenbach said the city should follow the state and federal governments on hemp-based THC, according to the outlet. Hayes noted that pending court cases seek to delay or block the new state and federal hemp laws, WCBU reported.
