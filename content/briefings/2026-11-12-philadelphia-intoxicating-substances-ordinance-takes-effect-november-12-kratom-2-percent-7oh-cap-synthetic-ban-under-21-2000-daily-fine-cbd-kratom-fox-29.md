A Philadelphia ordinance restricting sales of kratom and intoxicating hemp products takes effect on November 12, 2026, with fines of up to $2,000 for each day a violation continues, [Fox 29 reported](https://www.fox29.com/news/philadelphias-kratom-ban-hemp-restrictions-take-effect-thursday-heres-what-we-know) on November 11, 2026. According to Fox 29, the ordinance makes it unlawful to sell a kratom product that contains more than 2 percent 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) as a share of its overall alkaloid content or that contains any synthetic version of kratom compounds. Fox 29's report does not specify whether the 2 percent cap would reach natural leaf kratom products. Retailer CBD Kratom argues that a separate provision requiring Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approval reaches natural leaf kratom, according to Fox 29.

## What the ordinance prohibits

City Council passed the bill on June 4, 2026 and Mayor Cherelle Parker signed it on September 15, 2026, Fox 29 reported. The outlet identified the measure as Bill No. 260163 and said it adds a chapter to the city code covering "intoxicating substances," a category that includes kratom products and intoxicating hemp products.

Fox 29 listed four kinds of kratom product that the ordinance makes unlawful to sell:

- a product mixed or packed with a non-kratom substance to the point that it is harmful to a consumer
- a product containing a kratom extract with solvent residue above a national pharmaceutical standard
- a product containing more than 2 percent 7-OH as a share of its overall alkaloid content
- a product containing any synthetic version of those compounds

The ordinance also bars the sale of any intoxicating substance that is not approved as a dietary supplement or safe food additive, or that lacks an approved application on file with the FDA, according to Fox 29. Sales of covered products to anyone under 21 are prohibited, the outlet reported.

## Penalties and permits

A violation is a Class III offense under the city code, which carries a maximum fine of $2,000, and each day a violation continues counts as a new offense, Fox 29 reported. The ordinance includes no grace period and does not say whether the city will inspect stores or respond to complaints, according to the outlet.

Fox 29 reported that the ordinance creates a retailer permit with a $300 application fee and a $300 renewal fee. The permit is available only to a fixed storefront that is not within 500 feet of a school, playground, park, swimming pool, recreation center, library, or child care facility, according to the outlet. The bill also names roughly 180 commercial corridors and business improvement districts where no permit can be issued, Fox 29 reported. Permit holders would have to get products tested at a federally registered lab licensed in Pennsylvania, the outlet reported.

The permit section takes effect only once the city issues regulations, and those regulations have not been announced, according to Fox 29. The city has not released a count of affected businesses, the outlet reported.

## Retailer response

CBD Kratom, which says it operates three Philadelphia stores, asked Council to reconsider the bill before it took effect, Fox 29 reported. The company argues that requiring FDA approval effectively ends sales of natural leaf kratom because no practical approval pathway exists for those products, according to the outlet.

CBD Kratom chief operating officer Jason Brandl said in a June 10, 2026 statement that the company wants a regulatory framework that protects consumers while leaving a path for compliant operators, Fox 29 reported.

Fox 29 did not quote a city official on how the FDA-approval provision will be applied to natural leaf products, and the outlet did not quote a kratom trade or advocacy organization. Allison Smith of the Global Kratom Coalition told a Council hearing in May 2026 that the legislation would amount to a de facto ban of all kratom products in Philadelphia because of the testing and licensing requirements it would place on sellers of natural leaf, [as Kratom News Today reported on May 21, 2026][philadelphia-council-clashes-with-kratom-advocates-over-smoke-shop-bills], citing The Philadelphia Inquirer. Neither Fox 29 nor that earlier report establishes whether the coalition or CBD Kratom opposes the 2 percent 7-OH cap itself.
