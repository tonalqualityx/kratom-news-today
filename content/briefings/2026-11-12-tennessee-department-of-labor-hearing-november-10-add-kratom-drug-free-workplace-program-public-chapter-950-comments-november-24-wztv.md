The Tennessee Department of Labor and Workforce Development held a public hearing on November 10, 2026 on a proposal to add kratom to the definition of a drug in the state's Drug-Free Workplace Program, [WZTV Fox 17 reported](https://katv.com/news/nation-world/kratom-crackdown-continues-across-tennessee-months-after-statewide-ban). The department told Fox 17 that the change would not require employers to test for kratom, [according to the station's earlier report](https://fox17.com/news/local/tennessee-considers-kratom-workplace-rule-change). Neither Fox 17 report specified whether the proposed text distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and neither quoted a kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## Why the department is acting

The department told Fox 17 that kratom is not currently mentioned in the program's rules. Public Chapter 950, known as Matthew Davenport's Law, requires the department to add kratom to the definition of a drug, according to the department's statement to Fox 17. Section 6 of the law requires the labor commissioner to adopt a rule making that addition, Fox 17 reported.

Fox 17 reported that the law took effect on July 1, 2026 and separately prohibited knowingly possessing, manufacturing, delivering, or selling kratom in Tennessee. The proposed workplace amendment is separate from those possession and sales prohibitions, according to the station.

## What the rule would change for workers

The rule would affect employers that voluntarily participate in the Drug-Free Workplace Program and would not apply to every employer in the state, the department told Fox 17. The program's required testing list comes from the U.S. Department of Transportation, and the proposal does not add kratom to it, according to the department.

The department told Fox 17 that a positive drug test for an injured worker at a participating employer can create a legal presumption that the drug caused the injury, which could affect a workers' compensation claim. The worker can challenge that presumption with clear and convincing evidence that the drug did not cause the injury, according to the department. Discipline is governed by employer policies and by state and federal employment law, and the amendment does not separately address off-duty use, Fox 17 reported.

## Next steps

No effective date has been determined, the department told Fox 17. The remaining steps include review and approval by the attorney general's office and review by the legislature's Joint Government Operations Committee, according to the station. Written comments on the proposal are accepted until 4:30 p.m. on November 24, 2026, Fox 17 reported. Employers would be responsible for notifying their employees, department officials told the station.

## Enforcement since July

WZTV's November 12, 2026 report said law enforcement agencies have continued to find kratom products in Tennessee businesses since the prohibition took effect. The station cited the Lincoln County Sheriff's Office operation at 21 businesses, [which Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on September 12, 2026][lincoln-county-tennessee-sheriff-seizes-kratom-21-businesses]. The WZTV report does not specify whether the products seized in Lincoln County were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom. WZTV also cited the West Tennessee Violent Crime and Drug Task Force's report of nearly 30,000 packages of what the station called prohibited synthetic kratom products at a Memphis warehouse, [which KNT reported on October 2, 2026][shelby-county-da-mulroy-west-tennessee-task-force-novelty-king-memphis-30000-packages-synthetic-kratom-klaz-cats-claw-tipton-county-4700-pills]. WZTV described the Memphis products as synthetic kratom and did not specify whether they were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations. WZTV reported that a comprehensive statewide count of inspections, arrests, and criminal charges has not been established.
