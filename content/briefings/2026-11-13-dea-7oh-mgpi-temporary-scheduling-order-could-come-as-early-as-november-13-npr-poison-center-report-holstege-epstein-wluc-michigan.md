A federal restriction on some concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products could go into place as early as November 13, 2026, [NPR reported](https://www.wusf.org/2026-11-13/kratom-and-7-oh-can-feel-like-a-miracle-they-can-also-be-addictive) that day. According to NPR, the planned restriction covers products that contain higher concentrations of 7-OH or of mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), and 7-OH is found naturally in kratom in very small amounts. The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) and the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) say they are not focusing on natural kratom products, NPR said. WLUC TV6 [gave the same timing](https://www.uppermichiganssource.com/2026/11/12/tv6-investigates-dea-could-classify-synthetic-kratom-products-schedule-i-drugs/) on November 12, 2026. Neither outlet reported that the order had been published.

## How the federal action reached this point

NPR reported that the DEA in August 2026 restricted the making and sale of three compounds: MGM-15, MGM-16, and MGPI. The American Kratom Association (AKA) filed a federal lawsuit against the DEA on August 26, 2026, asking the agency to ensure that natural kratom products with traces of MGPI would remain legal, according to NPR. Kratom News Today (KNT) covered that suit [on August 27, 2026][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order].

HHS issued a new recommendation on 7-OH and MGPI on October 9, 2026, and the DEA now plans to restrict a narrower range of products, NPR reported. According to the [Justice Department's October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid), HHS recommended Schedule I control when 7-OH or MGPI exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the ratio of mitragynine to those compounds is less than 100 to 1. KNT reported the details of that recommendation and the DEA's notice of intent [on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14].

The AKA, a kratom trade and advocacy group, said in an [October 9, 2026 statement](https://www.americankratom.org/news/dea-and-department-of-justice-scheduling-of-7-oh-and-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl) that it applauded the targeted scheduling of 7-OH and MGPI and welcomed the resolution of its litigation, according to its release. NPR did not name an organization opposing the restriction. The opposition NPR described came from individual users of kratom and 7-OH products.

## Poison center data cited by NPR

NPR reported that a recent report of data from 53 U.S. poison control centers, co-authored by Chris Holstege, a physician and professor at the University of Virginia School of Medicine, found that kratom-related calls increased about 1,200 percent between 2015 and 2025. NPR did not name the report's publisher. Holstege told NPR the authors were surprised by the findings.

According to NPR, the poison center report counted 233 deaths associated with kratom from 2015 through 2025, 79 percent of which involved multiple substances. NPR said the report's findings do not distinguish between kratom and products like 7-OH, and NPR's account does not say how many of those deaths involved natural leaf kratom or concentrated 7-OH products. The same report found that 62 percent of the calls involved people using kratom without other drugs, according to NPR, which said the authors drew on the National Poison Data System.

## What researchers told NPR

David Epstein, a senior investigator in the National Institute on Drug Abuse Intramural Research Program, told NPR that some of his ongoing research suggests the risk of addiction or dependence is higher with 7-OH products than with whole leaf kratom. Epstein also told NPR that some people report benefits from kratom or 7-OH. NPR reported that estimates of U.S. kratom use range from more than 5 million people to as many as 15 million, and that those estimates usually do not separate whole leaf kratom from 7-OH products.

## What users told NPR

NPR also interviewed three people who have used 7-OH products. According to NPR, two former users, identified by first name only, said they experienced withdrawal symptoms after stopping. Angela Wilson of Delaware told NPR that she uses 7-OH products to manage chronic pain and that losing access would be hard on her family. That is Wilson's own account, and NPR did not report verifying it.

## Michigan

WLUC TV6 reported on November 12, 2026 that Eliza Hutchinson, an assistant professor of family medicine at the University of Michigan, said the federal rule could go into effect as soon as November 13, 2026. According to TV6, the DEA has issued an intent to classify 7-OH and related compounds as Schedule I at concentrations above a certain threshold for two years.

Legislation to ban the sale of kratom derivatives passed the Michigan House and has stalled in the Senate, according to TV6. KNT reported on a push to move that bill [on November 6, 2026][michigan-rep-deboyer-hb-5537-kratom-ban-push-senate-passage-before-year-end-passed-house-march-msu-jamie-alan-7oh-fox-17]. TV6 also cited U.S. Poison Control Center data showing 165 exposure cases involving 7-OH between January 1, 2025 and July 31, 2025, of which 35 percent resulted in serious health problems. TV6's report did not specify whether those exposures involved concentrated 7-OH products or natural leaf kratom.
