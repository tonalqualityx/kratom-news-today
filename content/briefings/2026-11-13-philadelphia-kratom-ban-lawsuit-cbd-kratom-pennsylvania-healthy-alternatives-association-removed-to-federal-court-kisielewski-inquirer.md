Retailer CBD Kratom and the Pennsylvania Healthy Alternatives Association sued Philadelphia officials on November 10, 2026, asking a Common Pleas Court judge to pause enforcement of the city's ordinance on kratom and intoxicating hemp products, [The Philadelphia Inquirer reported](https://www.inquirer.com/news/philadelphia/kratom-ban-philadelphia-legality-20261112.html) on November 12, 2026. City attorneys moved the case to federal court 40 minutes before a hearing on the request was set to begin on November 12, 2026, the day the ordinance took effect, according to the Inquirer. The Inquirer's report does not specify whether the kratom products at issue in the suit are natural leaf products or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. Fox 29 reported on November 11, 2026 that the ordinance lists products above a 2 percent 7-OH cap among the kratom products it makes unlawful to sell, [as Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on November 12, 2026][philadelphia-intoxicating-substances-ordinance-takes-effect-november-12-kratom-2-percent-7oh-cap-synthetic-ban-under-21-2000-daily-fine-cbd-kratom-fox-29].

## Who sued and what the suit says

The Inquirer described the Pennsylvania Healthy Alternatives Association as a nonprofit that advocates for hemp and kratom sellers, and CBD Kratom as a St. Louis-based retailer with three stores in Philadelphia. The two plaintiffs named Mayor Cherelle Parker and the co-commissioners of the Philadelphia Department of Licenses and Inspections as defendants, according to the Inquirer.

The complaint says the ordinance would force businesses to close and eliminate jobs, the Inquirer reported. According to the Inquirer, the complaint also says the ordinance fails to separate law-abiding retailers from sellers of dangerous products and that the city passed a ban in place of regulations on kratom sellers.

## The move to federal court

Judge Brian R. Kisielewski had scheduled a hearing on the injunction request for the afternoon of November 12, 2026, the Inquirer reported. City attorneys said in a court filing that the case belongs in federal court because the sellers claim the ordinance violates the U.S. Constitution, according to the Inquirer.

Kisielewski told the retailers' lawyers that the city had not given notice of the move to the court or to the plaintiffs, the Inquirer reported, and the hearing was adjourned. The Inquirer did not report a federal hearing date or any ruling on the injunction request.

Craig Katz, whom the Inquirer identified as president of the association and a member of CBD Kratom's management, spoke to the outlet after the adjournment. "We have several stores now that we are hoping to get some kind of answer as to whether they can stay in business and sell products after today," Katz said, according to the Inquirer.

The city did not immediately respond to the Inquirer's request for comment.

## How the two outlets describe the ordinance

The Inquirer reported that the ordinance requires store owners to hold licenses that do not currently exist. A spokesperson for City Councilmember Katherine Gilmore Richardson, who introduced the bill, told the Inquirer that the Philadelphia Department of Public Health will not create the license because the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has not approved kratom or intoxicating hemp for consumption, and that as a result no products are allowable for sale. The Inquirer characterized that outcome as an effective end to the Philadelphia market for those products.

[Fox 29 reported](https://www.fox29.com/news/philadelphias-kratom-ban-hemp-restrictions-take-effect-thursday-heres-what-we-know) on November 11, 2026 that the ordinance creates a retailer permit that takes effect once the city issues regulations, and that it lists specific kinds of kratom product that are unlawful to sell, including products above the 2 percent 7-OH cap and products containing synthetic versions of kratom compounds. Fox 29 also reported that CBD Kratom argues the FDA-approval provision reaches natural leaf kratom.

Stores found selling the covered products can be fined $2,000 a day by the Philadelphia Department of Public Health, according to the Inquirer. Whether the department had begun enforcement on November 12, 2026 was unclear, the Inquirer reported.

## Positions on 7-OH

Neither the Inquirer's report nor KNT's May 21, 2026 report states whether the Pennsylvania Healthy Alternatives Association, CBD Kratom, or the Global Kratom Coalition opposes the ordinance's 2 percent 7-OH cap. Allison Smith of the Global Kratom Coalition told a Council hearing in May 2026 that the legislation would amount to a de facto ban of all kratom products in Philadelphia because of the requirements it would place on sellers of natural leaf, [as KNT reported on May 21, 2026][philadelphia-council-clashes-with-kratom-advocates-over-smoke-shop-bills], citing the Inquirer.
