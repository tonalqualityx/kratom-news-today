The American Kratom Association (AKA) is urging Georgia lawmakers to regulate kratom products instead of making them all illegal, [WSB Radio reported](https://www.wsbradio.com/news/local/georgia-lawmakers-encouraged-make-changes-kratom-regulations/X77QRT3OZRARHJHDLEWOBPYCEA/) on November 12, 2026. AKA spokesperson Mac Haddow told the station that concentrated and synthetic products marketed as pure kratom are harming people and that the natural kratom plant is not the cause. That is the AKA's position: the association supports enforcement against concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, which it describes as distinct from natural kratom leaf, and opposes a ban that would also cover leaf, according to a [November 10, 2026 AKA press release](https://lifestyle.middletownlifemagazine.com/story/824965/federal-government-clarifies-the-line-between-botanical-kratom-and-synthetics-former-congressman-and-the-american-kratom-association-to-explain-what-the-new-federal-policy-means-for-georgians/).

## What the AKA asked for

Haddow told WSB Radio that Georgia could treat kratom the way it treats alcohol and cigarettes, as an age-restricted product. Haddow also said kratom products should be subject to an excise tax in addition to sales taxes, the station reported.

"We are asking the Georgia legislature to take appropriate steps to pass legislation that will have enforcement mechanisms embedded in them," Haddow said, according to WSB Radio.

The AKA's release lists the measures the association supports: age restrictions, accurate labeling, contaminant testing, good manufacturing practices, and enforcement against synthetic and adulterated products marketed as kratom. The release says the association opposes blanket bans, which it says push consumers to an unregulated market.

## The proposal the AKA is answering

Georgia Attorney General Chris Carr is proposing changes to the state's Kratom Consumer Protection Act that would make all kratom products illegal unless specifically approved by the state, WSB Radio reported. Carr presented that proposal to a House study committee on October 23, 2026, according to a [release from Carr's office](https://law.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-10-23/carr-calls-convenience-stores-remove-dangerous-kratom-products) that [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 24, 2026][georgia-attorney-general-carr-blue-ribbon-study-committee-testimony-remove-kratom-products-shelves-presumed-illegal-approved-list-robert-strang-pope-goughnour-543-poison-center-cases]. That release described the products at issue as synthetic 7-OH sold as kratom in tablets, gummies, shots, and powders, KNT reported.

According to the AKA's release, the association supports enforcement against synthetic and concentrated products marketed as kratom and opposes bans that would also cover natural leaf. Carr's proposal would presume every kratom-derived product illegal until the state approves it, according to the release from Carr's office. The AKA's release calls Carr's request for the removal of all kratom products "a position at odds with the federal record."

## The Atlanta press conference

The AKA's release announced a press conference with former U.S. Rep. Jack Kingston at the Hyatt Regency Atlanta on November 12, 2026, the day of the WSB Radio report. The release says a kratom ban bill is expected in Georgia's 2027 legislative session. It does not identify a bill number or sponsor, and WSB Radio's report does not mention one.

According to the release, the Justice Department, the Drug Enforcement Administration, and the Department of Health and Human Services in October 2026 drew a line between natural kratom leaf and synthetic, concentrated products sold under the kratom name. That is the AKA's characterization of the federal action. WSB Radio's report did not include a response from Carr's office to the AKA's remarks, and it did not quote any other kratom trade or advocacy organization.
