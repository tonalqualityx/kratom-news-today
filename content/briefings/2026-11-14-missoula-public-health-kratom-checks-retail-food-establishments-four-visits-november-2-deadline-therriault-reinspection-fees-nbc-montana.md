Missoula Public Health has begun checking retail food establishments for kratom products after a November 2, 2026 removal deadline, and inspectors have visited four locations in two weeks, [NBC Montana reported](https://nbcmontana.com/news/local/missoula-public-health-begins-enforcing-kratom-restrictions-in-all-retail-food-establishme) on November 13, 2026. Environmental Health Director Shannon Therriault told the station that the department has found more compliance than non-compliance so far. NBC Montana's report does not specify whether the products involved are natural leaf kratom or concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, and it carried no comment from a retailer or from a kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## What the inspections found

According to NBC Montana, the inspections check compliance with the 2013 Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Food Code, which the station described as requiring that no unsafe or adulterated food products be sold. NBC Montana reported that the FDA has taken the position that kratom is an unsafe food additive, and the station did not cite a specific FDA action or document.

Therriault told NBC Montana that inspectors visited one store that had not taken kratom products off its shelves. The department sent the owner additional information and will return to check that the products have been removed, Therriault said, according to the station. An establishment begins to incur reinspection fees after the department's third visit, NBC Montana reported. The station did not report the fee amount or name any of the four locations.

The department has "a lot more" checks planned over the next two or three weeks, Therriault told the station.

## How the deadline came about

The department notified retail food businesses of the prohibition in a September 25, 2026 letter, [Kratom News Today (KNT) reported on October 21, 2026][missoula-county-health-department-prohibits-kratom-food-drink-products-retail-food-businesses-november-2-twelve-of-23-stores-therriault-29-montana-deaths-riverstone-yellowstone-kelli-mccann-helena-son-benjamin-all-counties], citing [Montana Free Press](https://montanafreepress.org/2026/10/19/missoula-county-to-prohibit-sale-of-kratom-in-retail-food-businesses/). Department staff found kratom products in 12 of the 23 retail food businesses they visited in late August 2026, according to that Montana Free Press report. Montana Free Press also reported that some retailers complained of unfair enforcement because businesses without retail food licenses, including vape shops, are not subject to the restriction.

NBC Montana's November 13, 2026 report gives the first inspection results since the deadline passed. The station did not report how many establishments the department plans to check or how many retail food businesses are in the county.

## A recovery group founder's account

NBC Montana also interviewed Greg Kiesow, whom the station identified as founder and executive director of Kratom Anonymous, a 12-step program. Kiesow told the station that the program grew out of Kiesow's own addiction to 7-OH. That is Kiesow's own account, and NBC Montana did not report verifying it.

Kiesow said some people in the program bought a kratom extract at a gas station where it was sold like an energy supplement, according to the station. NBC Montana described 7-OH as one of the forms of kratom and did not report whether the extract Kiesow described was a concentrated 7-OH product or a natural leaf product.
