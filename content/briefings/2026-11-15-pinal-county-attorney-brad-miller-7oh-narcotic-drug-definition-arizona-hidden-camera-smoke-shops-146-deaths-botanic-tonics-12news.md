Pinal County Attorney Brad Miller wants Arizona lawmakers to add concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) to the state's definition of a narcotic drug, [12News reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/people-dying-pinal-countys-top-232143515.html) on November 12, 2026. Miller told the Phoenix station that the concern is the concentrated 7-OH products made from the kratom plant. According to 12News's description of Miller's position, the proposal targets those concentrated products, which the station described as a concentrated form of a compound found in kratom, and Miller separated them from the plant itself. The station's report did not include comment from a retailer or from a kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## What Miller said

"What is being sold in stores is actually not kratom," Miller said, according to 12News. Miller described 7-OH products to the station as a synthetic opioid comparable to fentanyl. That comparison is Miller's, and 12News did not cite a study or a laboratory analysis in support of it.

Miller told 12News that young people are obtaining the products and becoming addicted and that people are dying. The station did not report a number of deaths in Pinal County or identify a case. The report does not specify whether any death Miller referred to involved a concentrated 7-OH product or natural leaf kratom.

## The store visits

Miller sent investigators from the county attorney's office to convenience stores and smoke shops to ask how kratom and 7-OH products are marketed, 12News reported. On hidden-camera video from those visits, one clerk told an investigator that 7-OH is "basically Percocet," according to the station. 12News did not name the stores or report how many the investigators visited.

Miller told the station that the recordings show store owners know what the products are. 12News did not report that any citation or charge resulted from the visits.

## The proposal

Miller told 12News that the first step is to add 7-OH to the definition of a narcotic drug that Arizona already uses. Miller said the change would let prosecutors charge sellers as they charge other drug dealers and would keep 7-OH from being sold over the counter, according to the station. 12News did not identify a bill or a legislative sponsor. The next step is getting state lawmakers to sign on during the next legislative session, the station reported.

## The state's kratom case and death count

12News also noted that the Arizona Attorney General's Office has sued Botanic Tonics, a maker of kratom drinks, over how the company describes its products. Botanic Tonics says its drinks contain natural kratom with only a trace of 7-OH, according to the station. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported that lawsuit on September 24, 2026][arizona-attorney-general-mayes-sues-botanic-tonics-feel-free-consumer-fraud-act-first-in-nation], citing the Attorney General's office.

The Attorney General's office says the Arizona Department of Health Services documented 146 deaths involving kratom in the state from 2020 to 2023, 12News reported. The station's report does not specify whether those deaths involved concentrated 7-OH products or natural leaf kratom, or whether other substances were present. Miller told 12News that the figure is likely an undercount, and the station did not report a basis for that estimate.
