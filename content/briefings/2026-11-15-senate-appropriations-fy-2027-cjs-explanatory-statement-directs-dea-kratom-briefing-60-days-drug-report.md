The Senate Appropriations Committee's [explanatory statement](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/cjs_explanatory_stmt.pdf) for its fiscal year 2027 Commerce, Justice, Science (CJS) spending bill directs the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) to brief the committee on kratom within 60 days of the bill's enactment. The committee [released the bill text](https://www.appropriations.senate.gov/news/majority/senate-appropriations-committee-releases-five-fy-2027-funding-bills) on November 12, 2026. The directive is report language in a bill that, according to The Drug Report, the full committee has not yet voted on. The statement asks DEA for a briefing and does not direct DEA to schedule any substance.

## What the statement says

The kratom paragraph appears in the DEA section of the statement. It reads: "The Committee directs DEA to provide a briefing, within 60 days of the date of enactment of this act, on the health and safety impacts of kratom products, including patterns of use, adverse events, and any enforcement or regulatory actions taken."

The statement says the committee is aware that in 2025 the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) referred "a byproduct of the kratom plant" to DEA for possible scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act. The statement does not name the byproduct. It refers to "kratom products" as a group and does not distinguish natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products.

According to the Justice Department's [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), the DEA's temporary scheduling of three 7-OH-related compounds was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

## Where the bill stands

[The Drug Report wrote](https://thedrugreport.org/2026/11/13/senate-cjs-bill-wins-kratom-marijuana-grows-dea/) on November 13, 2026 that the full Senate Appropriations Committee still has to vote on the bill and that a final version would have to be reconciled with the House's. The Drug Report, a drug policy news site, described the kratom directive as a win for drug prevention advocates and wrote that it "opens the door to a renewed push for scheduling." That reading is the site's own.

The Drug Report tied the directive to pressure from Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley for the administration to finalize its pending 7-OH order. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on October 8, 2026][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply], citing a release from Grassley's office, that Grassley and Sens. Mike Lee and Susan Collins asked DEA to finalize that order and to consider scheduling the entire kratom plant. The explanatory statement does not mention the senators' letter.

The Drug Report's article did not include comment from a kratom trade or advocacy organization, and the statement does not cite one.
