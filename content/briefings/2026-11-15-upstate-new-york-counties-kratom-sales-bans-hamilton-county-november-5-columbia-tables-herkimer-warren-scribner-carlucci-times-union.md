Hamilton County, New York adopted a kratom ban on November 5, 2026, and Columbia County tabled a similar measure on November 10, 2026, the [Times Union reported](https://www.timesunion.com/news/article/counties-banning-kratom-across-upstate-new-york-22509402.php) on November 15, 2026. The newspaper reported that as many as three more counties, including Herkimer and Warren, and three municipalities could act by the end of November 2026. The measures differ in what they cover. According to the Times Union, Rensselaer, Saratoga, and Schoharie counties passed outright bans that include natural leaf kratom, while Suffolk and Dutchess counties limited their bans to synthetic products with high levels of concentrated compounds such as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The Times Union did not report which approach Hamilton County took.

## Where the counties stand

Kratom sales have been prohibited or temporarily prohibited in Albany, Rensselaer, and Saratoga counties within the last three months, the Times Union reported. Since May 2026, three neighboring counties, starting with Washington County, have also banned sales, and three others have considered a ban or looked into one, according to the newspaper.

The Rensselaer County measure passed 15-2 in September 2026 and County Executive Steve McLaughlin signed it in October 2026, the Times Union reported. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported the Rensselaer vote on September 9, 2026][rensselaer-county-legislature-bans-kratom-sales-15-2], citing News10. Albany County's prohibition is a public health emergency order that has to be renewed every few days, WAMC's Capital Region bureau chief, Grant Ashley, said in a [November 12, 2026 WAMC program](https://www.wamc.org/podcast/northeastnotebook/2026-11-12/kratom-bans-are-passing-quickly-across-new-york-but-are-they-really-necessary).

The two outlets give different dates for Saratoga County. The Times Union placed the Board of Supervisors' vote in September 2026, while WAMC said the county joined the list on October 21, 2026.

Schenectady County has no restrictions in the works on synthetic or raw kratom, according to the Times Union. County spokesperson Erin Laiacona told the newspaper that the county supports the pending statewide 7-OH measure and expects Gov. Kathy Hochul to decide on it before the county could finish adopting a local law.

## Who is asking for the bans

The Times Union reported that Cari Scribner of Ballston Spa has spent more than a year traveling to county legislature meetings to ask for bans. Scribner's son Nick died in late 2024 at age 27 from choking on vomit, and only ice cream and raw kratom were found in Nick Scribner's system, according to Cari Scribner's account to the newspaper. The Times Union reported that account without citing an autopsy or toxicology report.

Scribner wants a full statewide ban and described the lack of action in Schenectady County as a hole in a patchwork, the Times Union reported.

Rensselaer County Legislator Bruce Patire, a Republican who wrote that county's measure, told the Times Union that legislators worked across the aisle on it. Schoharie County Board of Supervisors Chairman William Federice, a Republican whose board passed an outright ban in October 2026, told the newspaper that a product being natural does not settle the question. "I mean, if you really want to get down to it, opium poppies are natural," Federice said, according to the Times Union.

## The split over natural leaf

Saratoga County Supervisor Minita Sanghvi, a Democrat, was one of four votes against that county's outright ban, the Times Union reported. Sanghvi told the newspaper that the issue called for more precision and was open to a measure limited to synthetic products with high levels of concentrated compounds such as 7-OH.

Oneida County Legislator Christopher Newton, a Republican, told the Times Union that more than 50 emails arrived from kratom proponents outside the county before an August 2026 public hearing, and called the decision a tough call.

Assemblyman John McDonald, a pharmacist, told WAMC that a ban on leaf products for adults is a step McDonald is "not there yet" on, and said banning a product grows an underground market. McDonald worked on the state legislation that bars kratom sales to people under 21, according to WAMC.

## Where the advocacy organizations sit

The American Kratom Association (AKA) wants federal and state regulation, David Carlucci, a former state senator who lobbies for the association, told the Times Union. Carlucci said county legislatures are not typically in the business of regulating consumer products and predicted that many counties are unlikely to reverse their bans, according to the newspaper.

WAMC aired testimony from Allison Smith, director of government affairs for the Global Kratom Coalition (GKC), at a Saratoga County public hearing. Smith told supervisors that concentrated synthetic opiates should not be available at convenience stores, and that botanical kratom powder and crushed leaves are not interchangeable with the concentrated, chemically modified substances the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) has scheduled or is working to schedule.

Tracey Hudson, who owns the Kratom Shop in Schenectady and sells raw kratom, told the Times Union that bans in surrounding counties have brought more customers to the store. Hudson described synthetic products sold at gas stations as dangerous, according to the newspaper.

## The state bill

The state Legislature voted in June 2026 to ban synthetic forms of kratom and Hochul has not signed the bill, the Times Union reported. KNT [reported the Legislature's passage of that bill on June 11, 2026][new-york-legislature-passes-synthetic-kratom-7oh-ban], citing a New York State Assembly release. A spokesperson for the governor told the Times Union that Hochul is reviewing the bill.
