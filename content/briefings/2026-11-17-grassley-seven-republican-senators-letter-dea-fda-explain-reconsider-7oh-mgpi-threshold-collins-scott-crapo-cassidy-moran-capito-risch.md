Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley and seven other Republican senators are asking the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to explain and reconsider the pending federal proposal on concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, according to a [November 16, 2026 release](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-leads-colleagues-in-seeking-explanation-urging-reconsideration-of-changes-to-kratom-derived-scheduling) from Grassley's office. The proposal at issue covers concentrated 7-OH and the semi-synthetic compound mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) above set thresholds. According to the Justice Department's [October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid), those thresholds were recommended by the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), and HHS [stated the same day](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that the thresholds target concentrated 7-OH and MGPI "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I."

## What the senators asked for

The release from Grassley's office states that the DEA's October 2026 proposal "doubled the threshold originally established for 7-OH and removed MP from Schedule I of the Controlled Substances Act." The release uses "MP" for mitragynine pseudoindoxyl. According to the release, the October proposal "would allow both chemicals to be present in over-the-counter kratom products at levels higher than those found naturally in the kratom plant." That description is the senators' office's own.

The release quotes the letter as stating: "Kratom is a drug in any form with addictive properties, and we believe this combined threshold level for 7-OH and MP set by DEA is inappropriately high and unsound." The senators also wrote that if the administration's goal is to remove synthetic or enhanced kratom products from shelves, the order "misses the mark," according to the release.

The release lists the signers as Grassley of Iowa, Susan Collins of Maine, Rick Scott of Florida, Mike Crapo of Idaho, Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, Jerry Moran of Kansas, Shelley Moore Capito of West Virginia, and Jim Risch of Idaho. The release does not state a date by which the senators asked the agencies to reply.

## The risks the release cites

The release attributes to the FDA a list of known side effects of kratom that includes liver toxicity, seizures, substance use disorder, and, in rare cases, death. It attributes to the DEA a statement that state health authorities have linked 7-OH products and concentrated extracts to dependence, addiction, acute psychosis, slowed breathing, cardiac arrest, and death. The release does not specify whether the deaths it references involved natural leaf kratom, concentrated extracts, or synthetic 7-OH products, and it cites no case counts.

## The proposal the letter addresses

Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14], citing the Justice Department's announcement, that HHS recommended Schedule I control for 7-OH and MGPI when either substance, or both combined, exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the ratio of mitragynine to those substances is below 100 to 1. The DEA's [notice of intent](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine) states that "neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels."

The new letter follows an earlier one. KNT [reported on October 8, 2026][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply], citing a release from Grassley's office, that Grassley, Collins, and Sen. Mike Lee of Utah asked the DEA to finalize its original 7-OH order and to consider scheduling the entire kratom plant. Lee is not among the signers listed in the November 16, 2026 release.
