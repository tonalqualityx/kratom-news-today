The Reason Foundation published a [policy paper](https://reason.org/policy-brief/kratom-and-its-alkaloids-a-balanced-review-of-the-science-and-state-regulation/) on November 17, 2026 that recommends states regulate kratom products in place of banning them. The [full paper](https://reason.org/wp-content/uploads/kratom-alkaloids-balanced-review-science-state-regulation.pdf) lists five authors, with Michelle Minton as project director, and describes the foundation as an organization that promotes libertarian principles. The paper is a review of existing studies and state laws. It reports no new clinical data.

## What the paper recommends

According to the foundation's summary, the paper recommends that states adopt age restrictions, good manufacturing practice standards, mandatory product testing, and clear labeling requirements. The summary states that those recommendations cover both leaf kratom and concentrated products.

The foundation also published a [model bill](https://reason.org/commentary/model-legislation-for-kratom-product-regulation/) the same day, titled the "Kratom Product Regulation and Accountability Act." The foundation's description states that the model bill would specify which kratom products could be sold, would set manufacturing and testing standards, would impose labeling requirements, would restrict sales to people 21 and older, and would set civil and criminal penalties for manufacturers and retailers that do not comply.

## How the paper describes state laws

The paper states that, as of June 2026, eight states ban all kratom and 17 have adopted regulatory frameworks. The authors write in the paper's summary that "most of these state approaches are improperly calibrated."

## How the paper treats concentrated products

The paper distinguishes products with an alkaloid makeup close to unprocessed leaf from concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. The authors describe mitragynine as the main alkaloid in the former. The authors write that the overdose evidence on 7-OH and concentrated products is "more mixed" than the evidence on leaf. The foundation's summary states that the paper's recommendations for testing and labeling apply to concentrated products as well as leaf.

## Federal context

The paper's conclusions on risk are the authors' reading of the literature. The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) issued its October 2026 [notice of intent](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine) on 7-OH after a recommendation from the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). According to the notice's title, the proposed temporary Schedule I placement applies to 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl "above a specified threshold." HHS [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that the threshold targets concentrated 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I." The notice states that "neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels." The notice does not address the Reason Foundation paper.

The paper arrived one day after eight Republican senators asked the DEA to reconsider that proposal, according to a [release from Sen. Chuck Grassley's office](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-leads-colleagues-in-seeking-explanation-urging-reconsideration-of-changes-to-kratom-derived-scheduling) that Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered on November 17, 2026][grassley-seven-republican-senators-letter-dea-fda-explain-reconsider-7oh-mgpi-threshold-collins-scott-crapo-cassidy-moran-capito-risch]. KNT [reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] on the thresholds in the federal proposal.

The American Kratom Association (AKA) [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.americankratom.org/news/dea-and-department-of-justice-scheduling-of-7-oh-and-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl) that it applauded the DEA and the Justice Department for the targeted scheduling of 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl.
