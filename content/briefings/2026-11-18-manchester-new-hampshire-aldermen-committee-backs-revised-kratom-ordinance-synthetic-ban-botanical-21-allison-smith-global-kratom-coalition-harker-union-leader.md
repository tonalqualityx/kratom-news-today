A committee of the Manchester, New Hampshire Board of Mayor and Aldermen voted unanimously on November 16, 2026 to recommend a revised kratom ordinance that would ban synthetic kratom products, including synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), while allowing botanical kratom for adults 21 and older, the [New Hampshire Union Leader reported](https://www.unionleader.com/news/local-news/committee-basks-revised-kratom-ban-sends-to-manchester-aldermen-for-review-8b5226c1). The proposal now goes to the full board. It has not been adopted.

## How the proposal changed

According to the Union Leader, an earlier version of the proposed ordinance would have banned the use, possession, purchase, sale, delivery, or public display of kratom products in Manchester. The revised version targets manufactured products containing concentrated synthetic compounds and, for now, places no restrictions on the natural, herbal form of kratom, the newspaper reported. The Special Committee on Alcohol, Other Drugs and Youth Services amended and approved the measure, and the city solicitor's office has reviewed the language, according to the Union Leader.

Aldermen Bill Barry and Kelly Thomas brought the original proposal forward in fall 2026, the Union Leader reported. In a memo to fellow board members quoted by the newspaper, Barry and Thomas wrote that the retail market is flooded with highly concentrated extracts, liquids, capsules, tablets, and gummies, in contrast with traditional preparations brewed from raw leaves.

## Who spoke

Allison Smith, director of government affairs for the Global Kratom Coalition (GKC), told the committee that the original proposal grouped botanical kratom with concentrated synthetic derivatives, according to the Union Leader. Smith said the coalition gave the city a marked-up alternative that would allow qualifying botanical products to be sold under testing and labeling requirements and would prohibit synthetic 7-OH and other derivatives. "The big thing is ensuring that 7OH and all these synthetic derivatives are off of our shelves," Smith said, as quoted by the newspaper.

Travis Harker, a Manchester resident who works as a family doctor in Portsmouth, told the committee about patients seen in an emergency department with depressed breathing, the Union Leader reported. Harker said those patients "most likely" had taken the 7-OH form of kratom, according to the newspaper, and added that there is no good test for kratom toxicity. Harker's account as reported does not include confirmed test results identifying the products involved. Harker said the proposed ordinance contains "a lot of really good things," the Union Leader reported.

## The lawsuit threat

Manchester officials said the city had received a letter from a California law firm threatening a lawsuit and an injunction if the city held a meeting on the proposal, according to the Union Leader. Smith told the committee that the head of the organization behind the letter had reviewed the revised ordinance and approved of it, the newspaper reported. The Union Leader did not name the firm or its client.
