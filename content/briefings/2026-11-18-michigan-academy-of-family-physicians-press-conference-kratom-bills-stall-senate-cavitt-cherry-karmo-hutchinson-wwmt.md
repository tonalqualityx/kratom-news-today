Michigan doctors held a press conference in Lansing on November 17, 2026 at which the doctors voiced concerns about kratom use while two bills on the substance remain in state Senate committees, [WWMT reported](https://wwmt.com/news/state/doctors-highlight-dangers-kratom-bills-ban-regulate-substance-stall-michigan-drugs-dea-addiction-cavitt). One physician at the event drew a line between natural kratom and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) or synthetic products, which the physician described as far more potent, according to the station. The two pending bills treat that line differently: one would ban all kratom, and the other would ban high-potency products and regulate the rest, WWMT reported.

## What the doctors said

Dr. Brandon Karmo, president of the Michigan Academy of Family Physicians, said at the press conference that kratom use is a growing concern in Michigan and across the country, according to WWMT.

Dr. Eliza Hutchinson, medical director of addiction services at Packard Health, said kratom products are often marketed as safe and natural and can be harmful, WWMT reported. Hutchinson said the problems seen in practice include seizures, liver failure, overdose, and addiction, according to the station. Hutchinson compared natural kratom to weakly brewed coffee and 7-OH or synthetic products to a "quadruple shot latte," WWMT reported.

WWMT cited the Michigan Poison and Drug Information Center as reporting a 123 percent increase in documented medical cases involving kratom between 2024 and 2025. The WWMT report does not specify how many of those cases involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products and how many involved natural leaf kratom.

## The two bills

WWMT reported that a House bill sponsored by Rep. Cam Cavitt, a Republican from Cheboygan, would essentially ban all kratom statewide, passed the House in March 2026, and is stalled in a Senate committee. A separate Senate bill sponsored by Sen. John Cherry, a Democrat from Flint, was introduced in September 2026 and would ban high-potency kratom, require product testing, and mandate potency labels, according to the station. That bill also remains in committee, WWMT reported.

WWMT did not give bill numbers. [Michigan Advance reported](https://michiganadvance.com/2026/11/17/lame-duck-ultimatum-get-kratom-off-michigans-store-shelves/) on November 17, 2026 that House Bill 5537 passed the House in March 2026 by a 56-48 vote and is pending in the Senate Government Operations Committee. KNT has previously covered [House Bill 5537][michigan-rep-deboyer-hb-5537-kratom-ban-push-senate-passage-before-year-end-passed-house-march-msu-jamie-alan-7oh-fox-17] and [Senate Bill 1181][michigan-poison-center-347-kratom-7oh-cases-2021-2025-87-in-2025-81-percent-vohra-science-informed-framework-not-blanket-ban-hb-5537-sb-1181-cherry-detroit-council-resolution-committee], which was introduced on September 22, 2026.

## Cavitt blames lobbying

Cavitt told WWMT that lobbyists are holding up the House bill and said the kratom industry has hired six lobbying firms to press senators against it. "There is no good kratom, only bad kratom," Cavitt said, as quoted by the station. Cavitt called on Senate Majority Leader Winnie Brinks to bring the bill to the Senate floor and said House Speaker Matt Hall regards the effort as a priority, according to WWMT. Cavitt said the bill would be reintroduced next session if it fails, the station reported.

The claims about lobbying are Cavitt's. WWMT's report includes no statement from a kratom company or trade group, and the station reported that Senate Democrats did not respond to questions about the bill.

Cavitt's position and Hutchinson's description differ on a point of substance. Hutchinson distinguished natural kratom from 7-OH and synthetic products by potency, while Cavitt rejected any distinction between kinds of kratom, according to WWMT's account of the remarks.

## Federal context

WWMT reported that the Drug Enforcement Administration has moved to ban the sale of kratom products other than natural leaf kratom and that the federal measure has not taken effect. Both natural kratom and 7-OH products remain on sale at smoke shops and gas stations in Michigan, according to the station.
