The Mississippi Association of Chiefs of Police is pushing for a statewide ban on kratom, board member Chief Landon Tucker told [WTVA](https://www.wtva.com/2026/11/17/police-chiefs-push-statewide-kratom-ban-advocate-urges-regulation/) in a report published on November 17, 2026. Lawmakers could take up a ban during the next legislative session, the Tupelo station reported. WTVA's report does not specify whether the association's proposal would cover natural leaf kratom, concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, or both.

## The police chiefs' position

"We as an association are absolutely pushing for a statewide ban," Tucker said, as quoted by WTVA. Tucker told WTVA that kratom products sold at gas stations are dangerous, according to the station's report.

About 40 Mississippi cities and counties have banned kratom, WTVA reported. The station said the substance drew scrutiny in the state after the deaths of two University of Mississippi students who were suspected of using it. WTVA's report does not specify whether the products in those cases were concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations or natural leaf kratom.

Nettleton Mayor Phillip Baulch told WTVA about concerns over the effects on younger people in particular.

## The advocate's position

WTVA also interviewed Glenn Johnson, a kratom advocate from Denver, Colorado. Johnson opposes an outright ban and wants stronger regulation of pure leaf kratom, the station reported. Johnson told WTVA that derivatives and synthetics, including 7-OH, have given kratom a bad name and that natural leaf kratom, synthetic products, and the products sold in smoke shops are separate categories. Johnson asked lawmakers and police to research those differences before acting, according to the station. The report did not name an organization that Johnson represents.
