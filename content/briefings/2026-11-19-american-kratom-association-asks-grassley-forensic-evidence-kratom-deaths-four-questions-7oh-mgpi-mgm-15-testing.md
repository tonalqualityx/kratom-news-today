The American Kratom Association (AKA) is asking Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley to release the forensic records behind deaths the senator has attributed to kratom, according to a [release](https://www.americankratom.org/news/senator-grassley-should-produce-the-forensic-evidence) the association datelined November 18, 2026. The association argues in the release that a toxicology finding of mitragynine alone does not show whether a death involved natural leaf kratom, a concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) product, or a synthetic compound sold under the kratom name. The release states that Grassley held an event with families who lost relatives. It does not quote the senator's remarks, and no response from Grassley's office appears in it. The release does not establish which product type was involved in each death, and Kratom News Today (KNT) has not reviewed the underlying records.

## The four questions

According to the release, the AKA asks Grassley to answer four questions for each death: whether 7-OH was identified and quantified, whether the sample was tested for mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15, and MGM-16, whether the product actually consumed was recovered and analyzed, and whether other substances and medical conditions were fully evaluated. The release identifies MGPI, MGM-15, and MGM-16 as substances the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) placed in Schedule I on a temporary basis on August 26, 2026.

The release also calls on medical examiners, coroners, laboratories, and public health agencies to reassess past kratom-associated deaths with current quantitative testing where the evidence allows. It names Mac Haddow, the association's senior fellow on public policy, as its spokesperson.

## The cases the release disputes

The release disputes how four deaths were classified. In one case, the AKA states that federal prosecutors' records show the product purchased was an MGM-15 product. In a Mississippi case, the release states that federal court filings show toxicology detected MGM-15 and MGPI alongside mitragynine, and that the product recovered was labeled as containing MGM-15. In the two other cases, the AKA argues that medical examiners did not weigh underlying health conditions.

Those descriptions are the association's. The release does not include accounts from the families or the medical examiners, and KNT has not reviewed the autopsy, toxicology, or court records in any of the four cases.

## The study the release cites

The release cites a [2016 study](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13181-016-0588-y) by Lydecker and colleagues in the Journal of Medical Toxicology, titled "Suspected Adulteration of Commercial Kratom Products with 7-Hydroxymitragynine." The AKA describes that study as finding commercial products with 7-OH above the levels in natural leaf, in some cases by more than 500 percent, and states that the researchers suggested adulteration. According to the release, limited testing panels can miss the compounds involved in a death.

## Where the two sides stand on scheduling

The release describes the DEA's August 26, 2026 temporary scheduling of MGPI, MGM-15, and MGM-16 as appropriate. It states that the AKA supports Connor's End Gas Station Heroin Act (S. 5383 and H.R. 10272) and the Kratom Consumer Protection Act.

KNT [reported on November 17, 2026][grassley-seven-republican-senators-letter-dea-fda-explain-reconsider-7oh-mgpi-threshold-collins-scott-crapo-cassidy-moran-capito-risch] that Grassley and seven other Republican senators asked the DEA and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) to explain and reconsider the October 2026 threshold proposal for concentrated 7-OH and MGPI. A [November 16, 2026 release](https://www.grassley.senate.gov/news/news-releases/grassley-leads-colleagues-in-seeking-explanation-urging-reconsideration-of-changes-to-kratom-derived-scheduling) from Grassley's office quoted the senators' letter as stating: "Kratom is a drug in any form with addictive properties." The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that its recommended thresholds target concentrated 7-OH and MGPI and are written to leave natural botanical kratom outside Schedule I.
