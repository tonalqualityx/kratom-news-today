The Gainesville, Georgia City Council approved an ordinance under which businesses licensed to sell alcoholic beverages may no longer sell kratom products, WSB-TV [reported](https://www.wsbtv.com/news/local/hall-county/it-isnt-bake-with-georgia-city-sets-new-limits-controls-sales-kratom-synthetic-urine/UODODNVCN5ETPBBBQ2DPK6MR3Q/) on November 18, 2026. The ordinance takes effect on January 1, 2027, according to the station. WSB-TV reported that the measure focuses on how products such as 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, MGM-15, and MGM-16 are sold. The station's report does not say whether the ordinance treats natural leaf kratom differently from concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products.

## What the council approved

City Attorney Abbot S. Hayes Jr. told the council that the city "cannot just totally ban it since the state does allow it," WSB-TV reported. According to the station, Hayes said the change would end kratom sales by any business holding an alcohol license. WSB-TV reported that the same ordinance prohibits businesses in the city from selling synthetic urine.

The council approved the ordinance with little discussion and no public comment, according to WSB-TV. The station did not report a vote tally or any penalties. WSB-TV reported that the January 1, 2027 effective date gives city staff time to notify the businesses affected.

Mayor Zack Thompson described kratom sales at convenience stores and gas stations as a public safety issue, according to the station.

## The attorney general's role

Council members said the kratom change was made at the request of Attorney General Chris Carr and others, WSB-TV reported. According to the station, Carr is seeking to end kratom sales statewide by any business that sells alcohol. WSB-TV did not report a comment from Carr on the Gainesville ordinance.

Carr's office [stated on October 23, 2026](https://law.georgia.gov/press-releases/2026-10-23/carr-calls-convenience-stores-remove-dangerous-kratom-products) that the attorney general is pushing for legislators to require that all kratom-related products be presumed illegal for retail sale unless the state has approved them. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on November 14, 2026][american-kratom-association-haddow-urges-georgia-lawmakers-regulate-kratom-age-restriction-excise-tax-enforcement-carr-approved-list-wsb-radio] that the American Kratom Association is urging Georgia lawmakers to regulate kratom products instead, citing a [WSB Radio report](https://www.wsbradio.com/news/local/georgia-lawmakers-encouraged-make-changes-kratom-regulations/X77QRT3OZRARHJHDLEWOBPYCEA/) in which association spokesperson Mac Haddow attributed harm to concentrated and synthetic products marketed as kratom. WSB-TV's report on Gainesville included no response from kratom trade or advocacy organizations.
