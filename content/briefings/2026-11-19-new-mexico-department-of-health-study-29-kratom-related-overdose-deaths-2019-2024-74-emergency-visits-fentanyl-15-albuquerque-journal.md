A New Mexico Department of Health study identified at least 29 kratom-related overdose deaths in the state from 2019 through 2024, the Albuquerque Journal [reported](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/health-department-study-links-kratom-200400803.html) on November 18, 2026. Most of the deaths involved other substances, and fentanyl or its analogues appeared in 15 of them, according to the Journal. The Journal's account does not specify whether the products involved were natural leaf kratom or concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations. The Journal's report included no response from kratom trade or advocacy organizations.

## What the study counted

The Journal reported that the department published the study the week before, and that its authors are Percis Drew and Malinda Gowin, epidemiologists in the department's Center for Health Protection. According to the Journal, the researchers used state death certificates and a federal public health database. They also identified 74 kratom-related emergency department visits that did not end in death, the Journal reported.

According to the Journal, four of the 29 people died with no other substance detected. The other substances found alongside kratom included methamphetamine, antidepressants, alcohol, and cocaine, the Journal reported. The Journal reported that all but two of the deaths were classified as accidental, that about three-quarters occurred in the person's home, that nearly 80 percent of those who died were male, and that most were between 25 and 44 years old.

The Journal reported that the authors called the research base on kratom "extremely limited" and described their data as a baseline for tracking future trends. According to the Journal's account of the study, the count may be low because standard postmortem toxicology panels do not test for kratom or mitragynine. For scale, the Journal cited National Center for Health Statistics data showing 961 drug overdose deaths statewide in 2025.

## State and local rules

The Journal reported that New Mexico has no state-level regulation of kratom. According to the Journal, the state Environment Department barred kratom sales at state-permitted food establishments in December 2025, after Attorney General Raúl Torrez issued a warning about the substance, and found one establishment selling it. The Journal reported that Albuquerque's Environmental Health Department prohibited retail kratom sales in the city and confiscated more than 5,400 items.

Environment Department staff are drafting proposed legislation, according to the Journal. Department spokesperson Drew Goretzka told the Journal that the next steps will be decided by the new state administration in 2027. The Journal did not report what the draft legislation would contain.
