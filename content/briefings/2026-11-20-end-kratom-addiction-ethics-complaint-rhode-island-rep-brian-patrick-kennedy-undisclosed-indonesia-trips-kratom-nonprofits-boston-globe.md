End Kratom Addiction, a national organization, filed an ethics complaint on November 18, 2026 against Rhode Island state Rep. Brian Patrick Kennedy over two trips to Indonesia paid for by kratom-related nonprofits, the Boston Globe [reported](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/11/18/metro/ri-kratom-law-kennedy-travel/). Kennedy was the lead sponsor of the 2025 law that ended Rhode Island's 2017 kratom ban, according to the Globe. The newspaper reported that the 2023 and 2024 trips were missing from his state ethics filings. Kennedy confirmed the trips to the Globe and said there was "nothing nefarious to be inferred" from what he called an "inadvertent" omission.

## The trips and the filings

Kennedy took three trips to Indonesia in three consecutive summers, visiting kratom farms and processing plants and meeting government officials involved in regulating exports to the United States, the Globe reported. According to the newspaper, he disclosed the first, a nine-day trip in 2022 that cost $12,000 and was paid for by the International Plant and Herbal Alliance, which the Globe described as a nonprofit that supports kratom legalization.

The 2023 and 2024 trips cost a combined $21,000 and did not appear on his annual filings, the Globe reported. Lora Romney, the alliance's president, told the newspaper that her group paid nearly $15,000 toward Kennedy's July 2023 travel. The Center for Plant Science and Health paid $6,000 for the August 2024 trip, according to the Globe, which described the center as a research nonprofit associated with the kratom lobby.

Kennedy told the newspaper by email that he amended his 2023 and 2024 disclosures on November 17, 2026, after the Globe contacted him about the trips.

## The complaint and the responses

Hilary Tesluck, executive director of End Kratom Addiction, told the Globe that the group is asking the Rhode Island Ethics Commission to investigate whether Kennedy violated any disclosure or gift rules. The commission is expected to decide on December 15, 2026 whether to investigate, executive director Jason Gramitt told the newspaper. No finding has been made.

John Marion, executive director of Common Cause Rhode Island, told the Globe the travel should have been disclosed. Kennedy pointed out that neither group that paid for the trips is a lobbyist, according to the newspaper.

Mac Haddow of the American Kratom Association (AKA) confirmed to the Globe that the association, which lobbied for the Rhode Island law, organized and arranged the trips and did not pay for the travel. The Globe reported that the AKA shares office space in Gainesville, Virginia with the Center for Plant Science and Health. Romney described the alliance's trips as "fact-finding" missions for lawmakers. "We follow all of the rules," Haddow told the newspaper.

## The law Kennedy sponsored

The law is the Rhode Island Kratom Act, chapter 21-28.12 of the Rhode Island General Laws. [Section 21-28.12-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE21/21-28.12/21-28.12-4.htm) of the act prohibits the sale of kratom products to anyone under 21, and the section's history lists an effective date of April 1, 2026. [Section 21-28.12-3](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE21/21-28.12/21-28.12-3.htm) prohibits kratom products that contain synthetic alkaloids, including synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH), and products with more than 1 milligram of 7-OH per serving or more than 1% 7-OH as a share of total kratom alkaloids. The Globe described the act as allowing small amounts of naturally occurring 7-OH and excluding highly concentrated or synthetic products. Gov. Dan McKee vetoed a legalization bill in 2024 and signed Kennedy's bill the following year, the Globe reported.

The state Department of Health has licensed 57 retailers, three distributors, and three manufacturers since April, according to the Globe. Neil Hytinen, the department's chief of staff, told the newspaper that the department does not have the money to proactively inspect retailers for the synthetic and concentrated products. Hytinen said the department's concerns about kratom "are the same as they ever were." Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported in July 2026][rhode-island-kratom-licensing-public-health-opposition] on public-health opposition as the department reviewed its first license applications.

Haddow told the Globe he was not calling kratom risk-free and said deaths attributed to kratom typically involve derivatives. The Globe placed the complaint in the context of wider scrutiny of kratom lobbying, including a 60 Minutes report that KNT [covered on November 16, 2026][60-minutes-kratom-report-deaths-industry-lobbying-haddow-brower-botanic-tonics-donations-makary-means-resignations-cbs].
