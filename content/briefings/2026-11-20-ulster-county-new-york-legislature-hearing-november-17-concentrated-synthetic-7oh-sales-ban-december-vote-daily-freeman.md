Nearly a dozen speakers urged Ulster County, New York legislators on November 17, 2026 to prohibit sales of concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products, the Daily Freeman [reported](https://www.dailyfreeman.com/2026/11/18/ulster-lawmakers-urged-to-ban-synthetic-kratom/). The proposed local law targets concentrated and synthetic 7-OH preparations and would leave natural leaf kratom on sale to buyers 21 and older, according to the newspaper. The Legislature is expected to vote in December, the Daily Freeman reported.

## What the proposed law would do

The proposal would prohibit the sale and distribution of what its text calls "concentrated and synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products," the Daily Freeman reported. Violators would face a civil penalty of up to $1,500 for a first violation and up to $3,000 for later violations within 36 months, according to the newspaper. The Daily Freeman reported that the proposed penalties would rise by $500 for sales to anyone under 21.

The Legislature held the hearing during its November 17 meeting and did not vote on the measure, according to the Daily Freeman. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported in September 2026][ulster-county-committee-delays-synthetic-kratom-ban-vote] that a county committee delayed an earlier vote on the proposal, and [reported in October 2026][new-york-assemblyman-brian-maher-urges-hochul-sign-synthetic-kratom-bill-2-percent-total-alkaloids-1-mg-7oh-per-serving-dea-bulletin-14449-exposures-ulster-county-legislature-calls-signature-daily-freeman] that the hearing had been scheduled.

## What speakers told legislators

Speakers who asked for the prohibition described dependence among family members, the Daily Freeman reported. Jordan Davis said a relative who had been in recovery for five years became dependent on a product bought at a local gas station, where it was advertised as an "all natural mood booster and energy enhancer," according to the newspaper. The report does not specify whether that product was a concentrated 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom. Hurley resident Patty Goodwin said someone close to her began with kratom marketed as natural and moved on to 7-OH, the Daily Freeman reported.

Pierette Farber, a legislator-elect and a mental health and substance abuse counselor, told lawmakers they "can control the legal distribution" of the substance in the county, according to the newspaper.

Several speakers supported the prohibition on concentrated and synthetic products and urged lawmakers to keep natural leaf sales legal, the Daily Freeman reported. Tracy Hudson, who runs a kratom botanical shop in Schenectady, said the synthetic and chemically enhanced products "are not the same" as the natural botanical, according to the newspaper. Kathleen Dinsmore also spoke in favor of the natural leaf product, the Daily Freeman reported.

The newspaper's account quoted no speaker who opposed the 7-OH prohibition and no kratom trade or advocacy organization.

## The federal distinction

The Daily Freeman's report cited the Food and Drug Administration's description of 7-OH as a compound "that occurs naturally in very small amounts in the kratom plant." The Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that its recommended federal scheduling threshold targets concentrated 7-OH and a related compound, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl, "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I."
