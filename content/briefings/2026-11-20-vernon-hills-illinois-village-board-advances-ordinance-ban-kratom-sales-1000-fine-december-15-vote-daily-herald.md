The Vernon Hills, Illinois village board this week advanced a proposed ordinance that would prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom products in the village, the Daily Herald [reported](https://www.dailyherald.com/2026/11/19/vernon-hills-could-become-next-suburb-to-ban-kratom-sales/) on November 19, 2026. The proposal makes no distinction between natural leaf kratom and concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. "The definition is broad that we've incorporated," Village Attorney James Ferolo said, according to the newspaper. "It covers the plant and its derivatives."

## What the proposal would do

The proposed ordinance would prohibit businesses or people from "advertising, displaying, selling, offering for sale or giving away" any product containing kratom, the Daily Herald reported. Violations would carry a minimum $1,000 fine, and a store's business license could be revoked, according to the newspaper.

Ferolo said the measure, if approved, would be added as an amendment to a section of village code that prohibits the sale of THC-related products, the Daily Herald reported. He said the proposal is based on ordinances used in other communities.

The Daily Herald reported that the ordinance was written to cover both kratom and its derivatives because of the difficulty of telling them apart for enforcement purposes. Deputy Police Chief Shannon Holubetz told the board that the issues include a lack of clear labeling and inconsistent dosage listings. "There's no real regulatory agencies looking at these," Holubetz said, according to the newspaper.

The board has not adopted the ordinance. A vote is expected at its next meeting, scheduled for December 15, 2026, the Daily Herald reported.

## How the proposal reached the board

Some village leaders attended a recent town hall hosted by the Stand Strong Coalition, according to the Daily Herald. Earlier in November, student members of the Stevenson High School Catalyst Club gave the board a presentation on kratom and its derivatives, including 7-OH, the newspaper reported.

The club presented a review that found 38% of 88 retailers in 10 northern Illinois towns, including Vernon Hills, sold kratom, according to the Daily Herald. Just over half of those retailers were smoke or tobacco shops, the club said. The newspaper reported that the findings prompted the board to ask for a draft ordinance.

Mayor Thom Koch said an ordinance is appropriate given the number of places where kratom is sold and the lack of regulation, according to the Daily Herald. The draft ordinance cites Food and Drug Administration warnings about kratom products, the newspaper reported.
