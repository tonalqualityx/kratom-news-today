At least two overdose deaths in Grand Traverse County, Michigan in the summer of 2026 were connected to kratom, the Traverse City Record-Eagle reported in an article [The Detroit News published](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/news/local/michigan/2026/11/18/kratom-overdose-deaths-grand-traverse-county-michigan/92847193007/) on November 18, 2026 and [Yahoo News republished](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/kratom-overdose-cited-least-two-000039582.html). Each death involved a second substance, according to the newspaper: alcohol in one case and inhalants in the other. Some friends of the first man told deputies that the man had mentioned a "kratom 7-OH pill from a smoke shop," according to the Record-Eagle. The newspaper did not report that any product was recovered or tested, and the reporting does not specify whether either death involved a concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparation or natural leaf kratom. The Justice Department drew that distinction in [its August 25, 2026 emergency scheduling announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds), which described that federal action as directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

## The two deaths

A 21-year-old Grand Traverse County man died on June 14, 2026 after a night of drinking with friends, the Record-Eagle reported. The autopsy listed the cause of death as an accidental overdose caused by alcohol and kratom, according to the newspaper. None of the friends told sheriff's deputies that they saw the man with kratom that night, and some said the man had mentioned having a 7-OH pill bought at a smoke shop, the Record-Eagle reported. The newspaper did not report whether investigators recovered or tested any product, so its account leaves open whether the product was a concentrated 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom.

A 38-year-old Grand Traverse County man was found dead on August 10, 2026 from an accidental overdose involving kratom and inhalants, according to the Record-Eagle. People who knew the man told deputies that the man had a history of kratom use and had resumed daily use shortly before the death, the newspaper reported. The Record-Eagle did not describe the product involved, and its report does not specify whether it was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom.

The Record-Eagle did not name either man. The newspaper did not identify the official who determined the causes of death, and it reported no toxicology concentrations for either case.

## What local clinicians told the newspaper

Joe Santangelo, chief medical, quality and safety officer for Munson Medical Center in Traverse City, told the Record-Eagle that sedation associated with kratom is magnified when another substance is present. Santangelo told the newspaper that mixing substances is where serious problems arise for people who had otherwise handled a kratom product.

Paula Lipinski, chief executive of Addiction Treatment Services in Traverse City, told the Record-Eagle that the organization has heard from people about kratom for about eight years and saw use increase starting in 2020. "We're seeing at least 100 people per year coming to us with kratom or 7-OH addiction," Lipinski said, according to the newspaper. That figure is Lipinski's own, and the Record-Eagle did not cite records for it. Emmy Newth, the organization's chief quality officer, told the newspaper that 7-OH is a particular concern because of its potency.
