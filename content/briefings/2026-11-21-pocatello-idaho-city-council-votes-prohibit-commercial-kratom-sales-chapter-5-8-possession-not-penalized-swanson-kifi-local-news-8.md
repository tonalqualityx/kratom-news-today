The Pocatello, Idaho City Council voted on November 19, 2026 to prohibit the commercial sale of kratom within the city, [KIFI Local News 8 reported](https://localnews8.com/news/local-news/2026/11/19/pocatello-bans-commercial-sale-of-kratom-throughout-city-limits/). All council members voted in favor on the final roll call, according to the station. KIFI's report describes the ordinance as covering kratom as a category. The report does not say whether the ordinance distinguishes concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products from natural leaf kratom.

## What the council adopted

The ordinance amends Title 5 of the Pocatello Municipal Code by adding a Chapter 5.8, KIFI reported. According to the station, the council read the ordinance by title and placed it on final passage at the same meeting. KIFI reported that the ordinance restricts commercial transactions and permits personal possession and use.

Council Member Ann Swanson addressed that point before the vote. "This ordinance does not make it illegal to have kratom or to use kratom," Swanson said, according to KIFI. The station reported that the ordinance takes effect after its passage, approval, and required publication. KIFI did not report penalties for violations or an effective date.

## What the council heard

Swanson said the council received a large amount of public input and that residents who wrote in were closely divided, KIFI reported. According to the station, Swanson said many of those residents wrote that kratom had helped with pain and with stepping down from opiate use. Those are the residents' accounts as Swanson relayed them, and KIFI did not report verifying them. Swanson said the dangers outweigh the benefits for the city, according to KIFI.

The vote followed a public hearing earlier in November 2026, KIFI reported. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on November 7, 2026][pocatello-idaho-city-council-directs-staff-proceed-kratom-sales-ban-ordinance-no-possession-penalty-bascom-124-idaho-deaths-since-2020-hope-solier-schei-idaho-state-journal] that the council directed city staff on November 6, 2026 to proceed with the ordinance, citing the Idaho State Journal. KIFI reported that a licensing enforcement officer with the Pocatello Police Department presented the department's support for the prohibition at that hearing. The two outlets spell the officer's name differently: KIFI gave it as Mallory Bascom, and the Idaho State Journal gave it as Mallarie Bascom.

KIFI reported that speakers at the hearing described as medical professionals raised concerns about addiction. The station did not name the speakers or say which products they were discussing. KIFI also reported that business representatives urged the council to wait for possible action by the Idaho Legislature, and the station did not name those speakers. According to the Idaho State Journal's account of the hearing, Pat Solier, representing the Idaho company Animal Farm, asked the council to remove synthetic 7-OH products and to wait for state lawmakers on natural leaf kratom.

KIFI's report included no comment from retailers or from kratom trade and advocacy organizations on the final vote.
