The Warren County, New York Board of Supervisors voted unanimously on November 20, 2026 to enact a local law prohibiting the sale and distribution of kratom in the county, according to a [county news release](https://www.warrencountyny.gov/news/warren-county-passes-laws-ban-kratom-nitrous-oxide-sales) issued the same day. The county's release states that the prohibition covers "natural leaf, powder or otherwise modified kratom-derived products." Neither the release nor the text of the law separates natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products. The board enacted a second local law on nitrous oxide chargers at the same meeting, according to the release.

## What the local law says

The board adopted the measure as [Local Law No. 9 of 2026](https://www.warrencountyny.gov/sites/default/files/R432%202026.pdf), titled "A Local Law Prohibiting the Sale of Kratom in Warren County," through Resolution No. 432 of 2026. Section 3 of the law defines kratom as the plant Mitragyna speciosa and its parts, products containing mitragynine, and products derived from the plant's leaves. The same section defines a derived product as any substance created from kratom or its naturally occurring alkaloids through extraction, isolation, concentration, chemical conversion, synthesis, or modification.

Section 4 states that no person shall sell, offer for sale, or otherwise distribute kratom in Warren County. The section exempts kratom approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) and kratom dispensed by a licensed pharmacist under a physician's prescription.

Section 5 authorizes law enforcement agencies with jurisdiction in the county to conduct regulatory inspections and compliance checks of suspected retailers and their vehicles. Under that section, kratom found during a check is confiscated and is destroyed one year after a finding of a violation.

## Penalties and effective date

Section 6 of the law sets a civil penalty of up to $500 per item confiscated, and up to $1,000 per item confiscated for each subsequent violation. The same section states that a willful violation may result in criminal prosecution under New York Public Health Law Section 12-b. The county's news release summarizes the penalty as a fine of up to $1,000 and describes a violation as a civil offense.

Section 8 states that the law takes effect immediately upon filing with the New York Secretary of State. The county's release did not give a filing date.

## What county officials said

Board Chairman Kevin Geraghty said in the release that supervisors had heard from numerous residents about the two substances. "Our Board of Supervisors decided we could no longer wait for New York State to take action with a statewide ban," Geraghty said in the release. Patricia Belden, the county's director of health services, said in the release that Warren County Health Services fully supports the two laws.

The release states that kratom and nitrous oxide products "have been blamed for addictions, serious health problems and at least one death in Warren County." The release does not say which of the two products that death involved. It also does not specify whether any kratom product involved was a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom. The release cites no autopsy or toxicology report.

The legislative intent section of the law states that the FDA has warned about kratom use and has issued an alert on dietary supplements that contain kratom. The section does not cite a specific FDA document.

## Other New York counties

Warren County was among the upstate counties expected to act on kratom by the end of November 2026, the Times Union reported in an article that Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered on November 15, 2026][upstate-new-york-counties-kratom-sales-bans-hamilton-county-november-5-columbia-tables-herkimer-warren-scribner-carlucci-times-union]. According to that Times Union report, Rensselaer, Saratoga, and Schoharie counties passed bans that include natural leaf kratom, while Suffolk and Dutchess counties limited their bans to synthetic and concentrated 7-OH products.

David Carlucci, a former state senator who lobbies for the American Kratom Association, told the Times Union that the association wants federal and state regulation and that county legislatures are not typically in the business of regulating consumer products. The Warren County release included no comment from retailers or from kratom trade and advocacy organizations.
