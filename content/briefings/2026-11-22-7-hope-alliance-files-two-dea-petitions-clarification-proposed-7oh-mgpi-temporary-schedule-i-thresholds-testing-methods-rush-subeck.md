The 7-HOPE Alliance announced on November 20, 2026 that it has filed two petitions asking the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) to clarify its proposed temporary Schedule I order for 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), according to a [release](https://7hopealliance.org/peition-filing-announcement/) from the organization. The proposal applies to concentrated 7-OH and MGPI products above set thresholds, according to the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS), which [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that its recommended thresholds target concentrated 7-OH and MGPI "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I." The Justice Department [announced the HHS recommendation](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) the same day. The release does not include the text of the petitions, and Kratom News Today (KNT) has not reviewed them.

## What the petitions ask

The release describes the 7-HOPE Alliance as a consumer protection nonprofit focused on 7-OH and kratom. It states that the petitions respond to the DEA's October 14, 2026 [notice of intent](https://www.federalregister.gov/public-inspection/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine) and that attorney Robert T. Rush prepared and filed them.

According to the release, the petitions cover three areas:

- **Thresholds and testing.** The petitions ask how the DEA intends to apply the proposed chemical thresholds and ratios, which laboratory testing methods it will use or accept, and how it will handle results the release describes as too close to call.
- **Possession and disposal.** The petitions ask how consumers and businesses without DEA registration can lawfully possess, surrender, or dispose of affected products, the release states. The same item asks how a separate August 2026 order applies when MGPI forms as 7-OH degrades in a product, according to the release.
- **Manufacturing, retail, and laboratories.** The petitions ask which business activities require DEA registration and what independent laboratories need in order to receive and test samples, the release states.

The Justice Department said in an [August 25, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) that the DEA had temporarily placed three 7-OH-related compounds in Schedule I and that the action was directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

The release states that the petitions are supported by sworn declarations from consumers of 7-OH and kratom. It does not name the declarants or include the declarations.

## What the organization said

"The agency drew that line before explaining how anyone is supposed to know when they've crossed it," Rush said in the release, referring to the ratio in the DEA's proposed order. Rush also said the DEA has not established laboratory standards for measuring 7-OH in products, according to the release. That statement is Rush's own, and the release cites no DEA document for it.

Jackie Subeck, whom the release identifies as the organization's executive director, said the federal government has a responsibility to explain what is prohibited, what remains legal, and how people are expected to comply, according to the release. The release states that the organization advocates for age restrictions, product testing, and scientific research in place of what it calls blanket criminalization.

## The proposal the petitions address

KNT [reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14], citing the Justice Department's announcement, that HHS recommended Schedule I control for 7-OH and MGPI when either substance, or both combined, exceeds 1 milligram per gram in solid products or 1 milligram per milliliter in liquids, or when the ratio of mitragynine to those substances is below 100 to 1. The DEA's notice of intent states that "neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels."

The release does not say when the petitions were filed, under which regulation they were filed, or whether the DEA has acknowledged them. No DEA response appears in the release.
