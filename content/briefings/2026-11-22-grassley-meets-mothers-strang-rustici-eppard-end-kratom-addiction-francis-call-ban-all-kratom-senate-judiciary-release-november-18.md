Senate Judiciary Committee Chairman Chuck Grassley met on November 18, 2026 with three mothers who attribute their children's deaths to kratom, according to a [November 20, 2026 release](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/press/rep/releases/video-grassley-hears-from-mothers-who-lost-children-to-deadly-kratom) from the committee's majority office. The release describes the meeting as one with parents "whose children were killed or harmed by botanical kratom and kratom-related substances, including 7-OH." Two of the mothers stated in remarks quoted in the release that their children used kratom powder, and one of them said her daughter was not using 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) or synthetic products. Beyond the mothers' own statements, the release does not specify whether the product involved in each death was natural leaf kratom, a concentrated extract, or a synthetic 7-OH product. The release cites no autopsy report, toxicology report, or product testing for any of the three deaths, and Kratom News Today (KNT) has not reviewed those records.

## Who attended

The release lists four attendees. It names Rebecca Strang of Georgia, whose son Robert died in September 2026 while a student at the University of Mississippi. It names Tia Rustici of Idaho, whose daughter Kielee died in 2025, and Susan Eppard of Michigan, whose son Matthew died in 2021. The fourth attendee was Dean Francis, whom the release identifies as the founder of End Kratom Addiction.

KNT [reported on November 20, 2026][end-kratom-addiction-ethics-complaint-rhode-island-rep-brian-patrick-kennedy-undisclosed-indonesia-trips-kratom-nonprofits-boston-globe], citing the Boston Globe, that End Kratom Addiction filed an ethics complaint against a Rhode Island state representative.

## What the parents said

According to the release, the four attendees delivered public remarks after the meeting. "My position is very clear: all kratom products should be banned," Strang said, according to the release.

Rustici said her daughter was using powder kratom and was not using 7-OH, synthetic products, alcohol, or street drugs, according to the release. Eppard said her 22-year-old son died from natural kratom leaf powder and that his toxicology showed no street drugs, prescription drugs, or alcohol, the release states. Eppard called for a nationwide ban on kratom leaf, according to the release. Those accounts are the mothers' own. The release does not include the toxicology or autopsy records the mothers described, and it does not identify the products involved in any of the three deaths.

Francis said the kratom industry uses dietary supplement law to sell an addictive drug as an herbal supplement, according to the release. That characterization is Francis's.

## What Grassley said

"We know that kratom is an opioid that kills people. We have to have strong enforcement of that," Grassley said ahead of the meeting, according to the release. Gray DC [reported on November 19, 2026](https://www.wthitv.com/2026/11/19/grieving-parents-call-kratom-crackdown/) that Grassley supports the group's request for tighter restrictions.

The release states that the meeting follows a letter Grassley sent to the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) and the Food and Drug Administration (FDA). KNT [reported on November 17, 2026][grassley-seven-republican-senators-letter-dea-fda-explain-reconsider-7oh-mgpi-threshold-collins-scott-crapo-cassidy-moran-capito-risch], citing a release from Grassley's office, that Grassley and seven other Republican senators asked the two agencies to explain and reconsider the October 2026 proposal on concentrated 7-OH and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI). The committee release states that the proposed changes "would allow deadly kratom products to remain widely sold in gas stations and convenience stores across the country." That description is the committee majority office's own.

## The federal proposal

According to the Justice Department's [October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid), the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) recommended Schedule I control for 7-OH and MGPI above set concentration thresholds. HHS [stated the same day](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/hhs-recommends-thresholds-enhanced-kratom-products-7-oh-mgpi.html) that the thresholds target concentrated 7-OH and MGPI "without sweeping natural botanical kratom into Schedule I." KNT [covered that proposal on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14].

## Industry response

The committee release does not include a response from the DEA, the FDA, or a kratom trade or advocacy organization. KNT [reported on November 19, 2026][american-kratom-association-asks-grassley-forensic-evidence-kratom-deaths-four-questions-7oh-mgpi-mgm-15-testing] that the American Kratom Association (AKA) asked Grassley to release the forensic records behind deaths the senator has attributed to kratom, citing a release from the association. The AKA [stated on October 9, 2026](https://www.americankratom.org/news/dea-and-department-of-justice-scheduling-of-7-oh-and-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl) that it applauded the DEA and the Justice Department for the targeted scheduling of 7-OH and MGPI. The AKA's response to the November 18, 2026 meeting is not stated in the committee release.

Gray DC reported that bills addressing the sale of kratom are pending in Pennsylvania, Michigan, Delaware, and Rhode Island. The outlet did not identify the bills.
