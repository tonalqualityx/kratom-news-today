The Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services adopted Emergency Rule 5KER26-16, titled "Requirement to Label Kratom Products," according to a [notice of emergency rule](https://flrules.org/Gateway/View_Notice.asp?ID=31650632) published in the Florida Administrative Register on November 23, 2026. The notice states that the rule requires labeling that discloses a kratom product's 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) concentration and its alkaloid ratio. The notice ties the rule to kratom products containing 7-OH "in high concentrations or imbalanced alkaloid ratios." The notice does not specify whether the labeling requirement applies to natural leaf kratom products as well as to concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparations.

## What the notice says

According to the notice, the Florida Attorney General adopted Emergency Rule 2ER26-2 on August 18, 2026. The notice describes that rule as providing that 7-OH is a Schedule I controlled substance under subsection 893.03(1)(a), Florida Statutes, when it is concentrated above 1,000 parts per million (1 milligram per gram for powders and solids, or 1 milligram per milliliter for liquids), or when a product contains less than 100 parts of mitragynine per one part of 7-OH.

The department's notice describes kratom products as food products sold in Florida that may contain 7-OH. Without the concentration and ratio on the label, the notice states, "consumers may not know how much of a potentially controlled substance they are consuming or that a product is adulterated, and businesses may not know whether they are buying and selling prohibited products."

The notice also states that the emergency rule reiterates that any controlled substance in a kratom product is an adulterant as provided in section 500.10, Florida Statutes. The notice summarizes the rule and does not set out label format details.

## Dates and earlier rules

The Florida Department of State's [record for the rule](https://flrules.org/gateway/View_Notice.asp?id=31654221) lists a file date of November 20, 2026, an effective date of November 23, 2026, and an expiration date of February 21, 2027. The record identifies the department's Division of Food Safety as the issuing division.

The department's [emergency rule chapter for 2026](https://flrules.org/gateway/ChapterHome.asp?Chapter=5KER26) lists four earlier kratom labeling rules: 5KER26-1, dated March 18, 2026; 5KER26-8, dated July 1, 2026; 5KER26-9, dated August 6, 2026; and 5KER26-11, dated August 24, 2026. The listing marks 5KER26-8 and 5KER26-9 as repealed. The first two carry the title "Requirement to Label the Concentration of 7-Hydroxymitragynine on Kratom Products," and the later three carry the title of the new rule.

Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on June 22, 2026][florida-emergency-rule-7oh-schedule-one] on an earlier emergency rule from the Florida Attorney General on concentrated 7-OH. The notice names no manufacturers or retailers and includes no industry comment.
