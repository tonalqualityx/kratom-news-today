The Town of Amsterdam, New York board unanimously adopted a local law prohibiting the sale and distribution of kratom and kratom-derived products in the town, the [Daily Gazette reported](https://www.dailygazette.com/the_recorder/news/amsterdam/town-of-amsterdam-bans-kratom-sales/article_02ad51a8-f806-43eb-b230-8b30b3c8cbca.html) on November 22, 2026. The newspaper reported that the board voted the week before its report and that the law takes effect after 30 days. The newspaper's description of the law, which refers to "kratom or any kratom-derived products," does not separate natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products.

## What the law provides

According to the Daily Gazette, the law prohibits individuals and businesses from selling, displaying for sale, distributing, or otherwise providing kratom or kratom-derived products in the town. The newspaper reported that the law does not prevent personal possession.

Each offense is subject to a civil penalty of up to $500 for a first violation, the Daily Gazette reported. According to the newspaper, the penalty rises to up to $1,000 for a second violation within two years and up to $2,500 for a third or later violation within two years. The newspaper reported that each day a violation continues counts as a separate violation and that violators would receive a notice of violation or an appearance ticket. According to the report, the law also authorizes the town to seek injunctive relief.

## What the supervisor said

Town Supervisor Thomas DiMezza told the newspaper that businesses that may be selling kratom will be notified before the law takes effect. "We'll give them 30 days to get everything off the shelves, and then after that, if we see it, we'll address it with the code officer," DiMezza said, according to the Daily Gazette. "It shouldn't be in our gas stations. Simple as that," he told the newspaper.

## Regional and state context

The Daily Gazette reported that the town joins Albany, Rensselaer, Saratoga, Warren, and Washington counties in adopting kratom measures and that Gloversville, in Fulton County, is considering a prohibition on kratom sales. Montgomery County Executive Pete Vroman has said the county is monitoring the situation and has no current plans for a ban, according to the newspaper.

The newspaper also reported that the New York Legislature passed a bill to ban the sale of synthetic forms of kratom and that the bill has been on Gov. Kathy Hochul's desk since November 13, 2026.
