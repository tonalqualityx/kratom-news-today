The Whatcom County Council in Washington is scheduled to discuss a draft ordinance on Tuesday, November 24, 2026 that would prohibit the sale and distribution of kratom to anyone under 21, [My Bellingham Now reported](https://mybellinghamnow.com/news/297792-whatcom-county-considers-age-restrictions-on-kratom-sales/). The report refers to kratom products generally. It does not say whether the draft distinguishes natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products.

## The proposal

According to the report, council members Jessica Rienstra, Jon Scanlon, and Elizabeth Boyle are sponsoring the measure. Scanlon credited Rienstra with leading the effort and cited her background in substance use disorder treatment, the outlet reported.

If the council votes to introduce the ordinance, a public hearing would be held at its next meeting on December 8, according to the report. If approved, the ordinance would apply only in unincorporated Whatcom County, the report states.

My Bellingham Now reported that Washington has no statewide age limit on kratom sales. Smoke shops that offer sampling or self-service must restrict entry to people over 21, the outlet reported.

## How the proposal compares with nearby city bans

The county proposal is limited to sales to people under 21, while several cities in the region have adopted full sales bans, according to the report. It states that Lynden passed a ban the week of November 16, joining Bellingham, Blaine, and Mount Vernon, and that Ferndale is expected to consider one in January. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on the Lynden vote][lynden-washington-city-council-unanimous-ban-sale-distribution-kratom-products-gross-misdemeanor-business-license-whatcom-county-my-bellingham-now] on November 18.

Scanlon said the council had heard from adults who use kratom, according to the report. He said that sorting out the different types of products is difficult and would be better handled by the state, and he pointed to state legislation expected to return in the 2027 session, the outlet reported.

The report states that Scanlon spoke with the police chief in Moses Lake about that city's age limits, and that he noted Cheney limited its ban to synthetic forms of kratom.

The report includes no comment from retailers or from kratom trade and advocacy organizations.
