At least three overdose deaths in Fayette County, Pennsylvania this year involved only mitragynine, according to expanded toxicology panels ordered by county Coroner Dr. Bob Baker, [WTAE reported](https://www.wtae.com/article/fayette-county-officials-raise-alarm-over-kratom-overdose-deaths/74524272) on November 24, 2026. Baker told WTAE that kratom contains two compounds he described as opioid-like: mitragynine and 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). WTAE's report does not specify whether the products involved in the three deaths were natural leaf kratom, concentrated extracts, or synthetic 7-OH preparations. "I don't have a problem with the kratom in its true form," Baker told WTAE. "When you start extrapolating the dangerous levels or the dangerous parts of this leaf and compounding it, causing fatal overdoses, I feel like we have a big problem." The Justice Department's [August 25, 2026 emergency scheduling announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds) described that federal action as directed at manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom.

## What the coroner reported

Baker told WTAE that toxicology reports this year have increasingly pointed to kratom, or to 7-OH, as the only drug in overdose cases. The station's account of the testing itself is narrower: it reported that the expanded panels showed at least three deaths involving only mitragynine, and it reported no 7-OH finding or alkaloid concentration for any of the three. WTAE did not say when the expanded panels began, and it gave no total count of overdose deaths in the county for comparison.

Baker said the coroner's office works with a Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) laboratory when more testing is needed. Baker told WTAE that the office sent one sample to the DEA, which checked it for 2,000 different metabolites and drugs, and that the result still showed only mitragynine. The station did not identify the people who died, the dates of the deaths, or the products involved.

Baker described kratom products as completely unregulated, according to WTAE. The station reported that the products are often sold at gas stations and vape shops. The coroner also encouraged having naloxone, sold as Narcan, on hand at a kratom overdose, while telling the station that there is no assurance it would save the person. WTAE cited the National Institute on Drug Abuse as saying that 7-OH carries a higher risk of respiratory depression than mitragynine.

Neither Baker nor anyone else quoted by WTAE called for a specific ordinance or state law, and the report mentioned no pending Pennsylvania legislation.

## A local clinician's account

Jim Piovarchy, a nurse practitioner at Trilogy Wellness and a flight nurse, told WTAE that patients struggling with 7-OH withdrawal come in daily. Piovarchy described the symptoms to the station as gastrointestinal problems, irritability, cramping, and body pain, and said the pattern closely mirrors opioid withdrawal. Piovarchy also told WTAE that a product being legal to buy over the counter does not make it a safe option for pain control. Those are Piovarchy's statements, and WTAE cited no patient records or counts for them.

## Where the American Kratom Association stands

WTAE's report included one industry or advocacy statement, from the American Kratom Association (AKA). According to WTAE, the AKA said it has "consistently supported appropriate federal action against products containing chemically manipulated or synthetically enhanced concentrations of 7-OH and related opioid compounds, which present materially different risks from traditional natural kratom leaf." WTAE did not report an AKA response to the three mitragynine-only findings specifically.

## Federal and regional context

WTAE reported that the DEA placed other synthetic opioid compounds derived from the kratom plant under a temporary Schedule I order in August, and that it remains unclear if or when the agency will act further. The station did not name those compounds.

Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on November 10, 2026][southwest-ohio-coroners-hamilton-butler-warren-14-suspected-kratom-cases-sammarco-mannix-uptegrove] that three southwest Ohio coroners described about 14 suspected kratom cases, in reports that likewise did not say, case by case, what kind of product was involved. That gap in product detail is the subject of [a guest editorial the American Society of Addiction Medicine published on November 24, 2026][asam-guest-editorial-timmons-mun-stable-kratom-prevalence-nsduh-0-68-percent-surveillance-misses-7oh-concentrated-products], whose authors wrote that a person who reports kratom use could mean leaf, a concentrated extract, or another product sold within the kratom marketplace.
