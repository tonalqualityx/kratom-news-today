A 37-year-old San Francisco man who died on September 22, 2026 had a compound called MGM-15 in his blood at 470 nanograms per milliliter, according to [a report in the Morbidity and Mortality Weekly Report (MMWR)](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7546a2.htm) that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) published in its November 26, 2026 issue. The authors described MGM-15, also called dihydro-7-hydroxymitragynine, as a semisynthetic opioid derived from kratom. They wrote that it was the predominant substance in residue from an empty blister card of tablets labeled as cat's claw, and that the label listed neither kratom nor 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH). The report separates this product category from natural leaf kratom: the authors wrote that kratom leaf contains mostly mitragynine and only trace amounts of 7-OH, and they described concentrated 7-OH products and semisynthetic compounds such as MGM-15 as substantially more potent opioid agonists. The authors described the 470 figure as the first reported MGM-15 blood concentration with a comprehensive testing and death investigation.

The report, a three-page "Notes from the Field" item, was written by Luke N. Rodda and Karen Zeigler of the San Francisco Office of the Chief Medical Examiner (OCME) and Jeffrey K. Hom of the San Francisco Department of Public Health (SFDPH). It is the formal case write-up of the death that SFDPH announced in an [October 21, 2026 health alert](https://www.sf.gov/news-fatal-overdose-involving-kratom-derived-opioid), which Kratom News Today (KNT) [covered the next day][san-francisco-first-fatal-mgm-15-overdose-script-botanicals-maui-labs-cats-claw-tablets]. The alert gave no age, sex, or toxicology figures. The MMWR report supplies them.

## What the investigation found

According to the report, the man's partner found him unresponsive at 7:30 a.m. in his residence, and emergency responders pronounced him dead at the scene. The authors wrote that naloxone was not given and that no resuscitation was attempted, because death appeared to have occurred more than an hour earlier.

OCME investigators recovered an empty four-tablet blister card, the report states. The card was labeled as Uncaria tomentosa (cat's claw) extract at 100 milligrams per tablet and sold as "Script Botanicals x Maui Laboratories Paradise Blend." According to the report, the man bought the product the day before he died and had likely taken all four tablets within 24 hours, which exceeded the label's suggested maximum of two servings in that period. The report does not say where he bought it. It states that the product was still for sale online on October 12, 2026 at $34.99 per card.

The report's toxicology results, from testing by the OCME, are as follows:

- **MGM-15:** 470 nanograms per milliliter in blood and about 2,400 nanograms per milliliter in urine.
- **Mitragynine:** 61 nanograms per milliliter in blood.
- **7-OH:** about 6.9 nanograms per milliliter in blood.
- **Not detected:** mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) and cat's claw.

The authors also reported clonazepam at a level they called consistent with prescribed use, trace ketamine, cocaine and cannabis metabolites, and no ethanol. The medical examiner certified the death as accidental and attributed it to the toxic effects of kratom alkaloids, naming MGM-15 and mitragynine, according to the report. The authors wrote that no other anatomic cause of death was found at autopsy.

## What was in the product

Testing of residue from the inner lining of the blister card found MGM-15 as the predominant substance, with mitragynine and the kratom alkaloids speciociliatine or speciogynine in relatively low amounts and trace ketamine, the report states. No cat's claw compounds were detected. In the authors' reading, the presence of the minor kratom alkaloids suggests the MGM-15 in the product was made from kratom-derived starting material.

The report describes a gap between the product's marketing and its contents. According to the authors, the seller's website described the product as a cat's claw supplement and as third-party lab tested, and the lab reports posted there, dated February 24, 2026, listed 7-OH, MGPI, mitragynine, and akuammine as not detected. Third-party testing might not include newer derivatives such as MGM-15, the report notes. The OCME added MGM-15 to its own test panel in September 2026, according to the report, and the authors wrote that routine toxicology does not typically detect MGM-15 or MGPI. Researchers at the Center for Forensic Science Research and Education and NMS Labs wrote in [the Journal of Analytical Toxicology](https://doi.org/10.1093/jat/bkag090) that testing for these compounds remains limited, as KNT [reported on October 17, 2026][cfsre-nms-labs-journal-of-analytical-toxicology-method-mitragynine-7oh-mgpi-mgm-15-200-cases-7oh-fell-89-percent-112-days-blood-mgpi-rose-testing-extremely-limited-fatalities-under-investigated-krotulski].

## Other states and federal status

The authors wrote that Utah and Washington have reported overdoses since July 2026 linked to cat's claw-labeled products sold under the Buzzers and Homiez brand names, including four deaths in Utah, and that those products contained MGM-15, MGPI, or 7-OH. KNT reported on [the Utah deaths][utah-four-deaths-four-months-buzzers-cats-claw-tablets-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-moss-confirmed-deaths-caused-by-these-products] and [the Washington Poison Center alert][washington-poison-center-safety-alert-fake-cats-claw-buzzers-homiez-7oh-mgm-15-mgpi-spokane-stores-utah-four-deaths-tri-city-herald] in October 2026. The Script Botanicals brand has not been named in other states' alerts, according to the report.

On legal status, the report states that the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) temporarily placed MGM-15 and MGPI in Schedule I on August 26, 2026, the month before the death. KNT [reported that order][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] when it took effect. The authors wrote that the Justice Department [announced on October 9, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid) that control of MGM-15 would be maintained, and that it proposed controlling 7-OH and MGPI only above concentration and ratio thresholds meant to distinguish concentrated products from botanical kratom. The report describes kratom leaf as not federally controlled and not approved by the Food and Drug Administration for any medical use.

The report cites a 2025 study in the journal Drug Testing and Analysis for its potency figures. According to the authors' summary of that study, MGM-15 was about 15 to 50 times as potent as morphine in mice and binds the human mu-opioid receptor with about 2.6 times the affinity of 7-OH and 11 times that of mitragynine. KNT has not reviewed that study and relays the figures only as the MMWR report presents them.

## What the authors recommend

The report describes a single case. According to the authors, the 470 figure is the first known reported MGM-15 concentration in human blood, and they interpreted it as consistent with fatal opioid toxicity on the basis of the compound's potency. The report states that products labeled as botanicals, including cat's claw, might contain potent opioid agonists that are not listed on the label. According to the report, questions from clinicians, medical examiners, and coroners about supplement use, along with requests for comprehensive toxicology in unexplained drug-related deaths, could help clarify the extent of the problem.

Rodda, Zeigler, and Hom wrote that naloxone is expected to reverse overdose from these compounds because they act on mu-opioid receptors. The three authors recommend in the report's summary that people who use these products, and anyone present with them, carry naloxone. According to the report, the OCME notified SFDPH on October 9, 2026, and SFDPH then alerted the California Department of Public Health and local clinicians. The report does not include comment from Script Botanicals, Maui Laboratories, or any kratom trade organization.
