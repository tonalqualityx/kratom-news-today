The Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA) had not issued its planned temporary order on concentrated 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products as of November 25, 2026, [Pain News Network (PNN) reported](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/stories/2026/11/25/its-a-mystery-dea-delays-scheduling-of-7-ohnbsp) that day. PNN reported that a notice the DEA published in the Federal Register in October 2026 said the agency would issue the order on or about November 13, 2026. Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on that date][dea-7oh-mgpi-temporary-scheduling-order-could-come-as-early-as-november-13-npr-poison-center-report-holstege-epstein-wluc-michigan] that it had not yet been published. According to the Justice Department's [October 9, 2026 announcement](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/department-justice-announces-updates-emergency-scheduling-actions-7-oh-and-related-opioid), the planned order would apply to products in which 7-OH or mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) exceeds a threshold recommended by the Department of Health and Human Services (HHS). PNN wrote that 7-OH occurs naturally in kratom in trace amounts and that manufacturers have concentrated it in newer products.

The one explanation PNN cited is a sentence in a court filing. In a [joint status report](https://www.painnewsnetwork.org/s/11-13-AKA-filing.pdf) filed on November 13, 2026 in the American Kratom Association's (AKA) lawsuit against the DEA, Justice Department attorneys wrote that the DEA still intends to publish an amended temporary scheduling order consistent with its notice, and that "the administrative process has necessitated ongoing consultations with the Department of Health and Human Services." The filing does not describe what the consultations cover.

## What each side asked the court to do

The case, American Kratom Association v. United States Drug Enforcement Administration, is pending in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia as No. 1:26-cv-02997-AHA. The AKA filed it on August 26, 2026, as KNT [reported at the time][aka-federal-lawsuit-dea-emergency-scheduling-order], and the status report shows that the case has been under a stay.

The two sides made different requests in the status report:

- **The government** asked the court to extend the stay for 30 days, through December 14, 2026, with a new joint status report due that day if necessary. Its attorneys wrote that they expect the notice and the forthcoming order to make further proceedings in the case unnecessary. If the court lifts the stay instead, the government asked for a briefing schedule that runs from December 16, 2026 to January 13, 2027 (the filing prints the final year as 2026).
- **The AKA** wrote that further delay seriously prejudices the association and other affected parties. It proposed that the stay be lifted, that the government respond to the AKA's pending motion for summary judgment by November 30, 2026, and that briefing end on December 21, 2026.

PNN's account and the filing differ on one point. PNN reported that the AKA's attorneys agreed to a delay while asking that the government file all of its motions by December 21, 2026. The filing itself records the AKA asking that the stay be lifted. Neither the filing nor PNN's report says how the court ruled on the competing requests.

## The AKA's position

Mac Haddow, whom PNN identified as the AKA's chief lobbyist and spokesman, told the outlet that the agency has not said why the order is late and that the government will have to explain the delay to the court. Haddow also told PNN that the uncertainty puts the kratom industry, and access to kratom products, in jeopardy. PNN reported that Haddow thinks a recent change of White House chief of staff may be a factor. The outlet presented that as Haddow's view and cited no confirmation from the administration.

PNN reported that the AKA supports the scheduling of 7-OH and sued to change the thresholds in an earlier DEA order so that scheduling would not interfere with sales of natural leaf kratom. KNT [reported on October 10, 2026][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14] that the AKA welcomed the revised thresholds. PNN characterized the revision as the DEA agreeing to everything the AKA wanted, including what the outlet called a descheduling of MGPI. The status report quotes the notice as covering both 7-OH and MGPI when either one, or the two combined, is above the threshold.

## Pressure from other directions

The delay comes while other parties are pressing the DEA from different sides. Sen. Chuck Grassley of Iowa and seven other Republican senators asked the DEA and the Food and Drug Administration to explain and reconsider the revised thresholds in a letter KNT [covered on November 17, 2026][grassley-seven-republican-senators-letter-dea-fda-explain-reconsider-7oh-mgpi-threshold-collins-scott-crapo-cassidy-moran-capito-risch]. PNN reported that Grassley is leading an effort to have all forms of kratom banned. The 7-HOPE Alliance, which describes itself as a consumer protection nonprofit focused on 7-OH and kratom and is a separate organization from the AKA, announced that it filed two petitions asking the DEA to clarify the proposed thresholds, as KNT [reported on November 22, 2026][7-hope-alliance-files-two-dea-petitions-clarification-proposed-7oh-mgpi-temporary-schedule-i-thresholds-testing-methods-rush-subeck].

PNN wrote that the government's request raises the possibility that 7-OH and other kratom derivatives may not be scheduled until early 2027. The filing does not give a date for the order. PNN also reported that some people who use 7-OH products have tapered off them in anticipation of a federal ban, while others have stockpiled supplies.
