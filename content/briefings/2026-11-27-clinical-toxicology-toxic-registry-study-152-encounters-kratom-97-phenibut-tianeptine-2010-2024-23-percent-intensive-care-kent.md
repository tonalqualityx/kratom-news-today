A study published online November 20, 2026 in the journal [Clinical Toxicology](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2026.2761523) identified 152 patient encounters involving kratom, phenibut, or tianeptine that medical toxicologists logged over 15 years, 97 of them involving kratom, according to the paper's abstract. The authors reported that 35 of the 152 encounters resulted in intensive care admission and that one patient died. The abstract does not say whether the kratom cases involved natural leaf products, extracts, or concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) preparations, and it does not identify which substance or substances were involved in the death.

## What the authors examined

The authors analyzed the Toxicology Investigators Consortium (ToxIC) Core Registry, which the abstract describes as a registry that prospectively collects information on patients managed by medical toxicologists. The study covered January 1, 2010 through December 31, 2024 and included every case involving kratom, phenibut, or tianeptine, according to the abstract.

The [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43061855/) lists four authors: J.T. Kent and M. Hacker Teper of the University of Toronto, R. Culbreth of the American College of Medical Toxicology, and Y. Finkelstein of The Hospital for Sick Children in Toronto, writing with the consortium. The record lists Kent's affiliations as St. Michael's Hospital and the University of Toronto.

## What the abstract reports

The abstract counts 97 encounters involving kratom, 44 involving phenibut, and 11 involving tianeptine, and states that case counts increased over time. The median patient age was 35, and 127 patients (83.6 percent) were male, the authors reported.

Co-ingestions were recorded in 73 encounters (48 percent), according to the abstract. The authors reported that those most often involved other psychoactive supplements (27 encounters), stimulants (23), or opioids (22).

The authors classified 131 consultations (86.2 percent) as involving intentional use. The reasons recorded in the abstract include euphoria (60 encounters), perceived therapeutic or wellness benefit (18), and self-management of withdrawal (13).

The abstract reports the intensive care, co-ingestion, and death figures for the three substances combined and gives no separate outcome figures for kratom.

## Treatment and the authors' conclusions

Addiction treatment involved buprenorphine/naloxone in 15 encounters (9.9 percent), the authors reported. At hospital discharge, 16 patients (10.5 percent) received education about a naloxone overdose prevention kit, a prescription for one, or both, according to the abstract.

The authors wrote that the exposures evaluated by medical toxicologists "were associated with substantial morbidity" and that withdrawal was common. The abstract concludes that hospital presentations involving the three substances increased over time, and the authors wrote that the findings point to opportunities to strengthen addiction-focused care, regulatory oversight, and public education.

The registry covers patients whom medical toxicologists managed, according to the abstract. The abstract gives no estimate of how many people use kratom, phenibut, or tianeptine and no rate of hospital presentation among people who use them.
