A bill filed for Montana's 2027 legislative session would add two kratom alkaloids, mitragynine and hydroxymitragynine, to the state's list of Schedule I opiates, according to [the text of Senate Bill 32](https://bills.legmt.gov/#/laws/bill/3/LC0661) published by the Montana Legislature. The bill text states that it was introduced by Senator Mark Noland at the request of the Economic Affairs Interim Committee. The bill names both alkaloids without distinguishing natural leaf kratom from concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products.

## What the bill text says

The bill's title describes it as an act "designating mitragynine and hydroxymitragynine as controlled substances in Montana" and amending Section 50-32-222 of the Montana Code Annotated (MCA). According to the bill text, that section lists the drugs included in Schedule I, and Senate Bill 32 would insert "hydroxymitragynine" and "mitragynine" into subsection (1), the section's list of opiates.

Subsection (1) of the section, as printed in the bill, applies to listed opiates "including isomers, esters, ethers, salts, and salts of isomers, esters, and ethers" unless a substance is specifically excepted or listed in another schedule. According to the bill text, the 12-page bill contains one section and sets no concentration threshold for either alkaloid.

## Where the bill stands

A [legislative tracker](https://missoulagop.org/legislative-tracker/montana-legislature/bills/2027-sb32/) maintained by the Missoula County Republicans, which republishes LegiScan data, lists Senate Bill 32 as introduced in the Senate on November 20, 2026. The tracker identifies Noland as a Republican representing Senate District 6 and shows no committee referrals or roll call votes as of November 23, 2026.

The bill follows interim committee work that Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on September 11, 2026][montana-interim-committee-tables-kratom-consumer-protection-act-8-1], citing the [Daily Montanan](https://dailymontanan.com/2026/09/01/legislators-eye-reining-in-wild-west-kratom-industry/). The Daily Montanan reported then that the Law and Justice Interim Committee tabled a draft Kratom Consumer Protection Act on an 8-1 vote, and that a separate bill designating kratom's chemical compounds as controlled substances had passed the Economic Affairs Interim Committee.

KNT did not find a stated position on Senate Bill 32 from the American Kratom Association or the Global Kratom Coalition in the sources reviewed.

## The Dillon training event

The [Bozeman Daily Chronicle](https://www.bozemandailychronicle.com/news/big-sky/kratom-dangers-outlined-in-overdose-training-event/article_939766bd-33c1-54b9-b68b-6bd96fb67962.html) reported on November 25, 2026 that Chyrel Snedigar, director of external relations and strategic partnerships at the Rimrock Foundation, discussed kratom during a November 18, 2026 visit to Dillon to provide addiction information and naloxone training. Snedigar said there have been between 25 and 35 kratom-related overdose deaths in Montana over the last three years, according to the Chronicle. The Chronicle's report does not cite a source for that range and does not specify whether those deaths involved concentrated or synthetic 7-OH products or natural leaf kratom.

Beaverhead County Public Health Nurse Katie Coppin told the Chronicle that Montana has no state legislation restricting kratom sales by age. Coppin said that, as far as the health department knows, kratom is sold locally only in smoke shops, according to the Chronicle.

The Chronicle reported that community members and activists hope for legislative action in the upcoming session. Lilla Guiberson, president of the Be the Change Youth Coalition, told the Chronicle that the coalition wants legislative action now. The Chronicle's report does not mention Senate Bill 32 by number.
