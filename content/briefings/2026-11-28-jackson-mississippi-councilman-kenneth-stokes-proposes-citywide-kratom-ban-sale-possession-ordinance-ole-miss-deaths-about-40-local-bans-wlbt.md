Jackson, Mississippi Ward 3 Councilman Kenneth Stokes plans to introduce an ordinance banning the sale and possession of kratom in the city, [WLBT reported](https://www.wlbt.com/2026/11/25/councilman-proposes-citywide-kratom-ban/) on November 25, 2026. According to the station, Stokes intends to place the measure on the council's Tuesday agenda. WLBT's report does not say whether the ordinance would treat concentrated or synthetic 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) products differently from natural leaf kratom, and the station did not publish the ordinance text.

## What Stokes said

WLBT reported that the September 2026 deaths of two University of Mississippi students prompted Stokes to introduce the ordinance. Stokes, a city councilman, called kratom "a legal killer that we're gonna make illegal," according to the station, which did not report which products he was describing. According to WLBT, Stokes also said the city had been concentrating on fentanyl before kratom drew his attention.

The station did not report proposed penalties, an effective date, whether other council members support the measure, or comment from retailers or kratom trade and advocacy organizations.

## The University of Mississippi deaths

A federal court filing said toxicology found mitragynine, MGM-15, and mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI) in both students, Fox News Digital reported, as Kratom News Today (KNT) [noted on October 12, 2026][federal-filing-toxicology-mitragynine-mgm-15-mgpi-both-ole-miss-students-probable-cause-fox-news-defense-attorney-cough-syrup-inhalation-asphyxiation-yahya-mosleh-detained-islam-mosleh-20000-bond]. The filing was made by prosecutors in a pending criminal case, and its statements are allegations. According to Fox News Digital's account, the filing tied the substances to manufactured MGM-15 tablets sold at an Oxford store. The Justice Department's August 25, 2026 announcement said the Drug Enforcement Administration had temporarily placed MGM-15 and MGPI in Schedule I and described them as manufactured and concentrated opioid products rather than traditional botanical kratom. WLBT's report on the Jackson proposal does not mention those findings.

## Other Mississippi action

WLBT reported, without listing them, that kratom sales are banned in about 40 Mississippi cities and counties. KNT [reported on November 18, 2026][mississippi-association-of-chiefs-of-police-tucker-statewide-kratom-ban-about-40-local-bans-advocate-glenn-johnson-pure-leaf-regulation-wtva] that the Mississippi Association of Chiefs of Police is pushing for a statewide ban, according to WTVA, and that a kratom advocate interviewed by that station asked lawmakers to separate pure leaf kratom from 7-OH and other synthetic products.

Jackson sits in Hinds County. KNT [reported on November 4, 2026][mississippi-department-of-public-safety-operation-preferred-purp-14-search-warrants-madison-rankin-hinds-november-2-200-pounds-kratom-labeled-products-mgm-15-mgm-16-mgpi-800-pounds-thc-dea-hsi-luckey-bailey-wjtv-supertalk] that the Mississippi Department of Public Safety said agents executed search warrants in Madison, Rankin, and Hinds counties on November 2, 2026 and seized kratom-labeled products that investigators believe contain MGM-15, MGM-16, or MGPI, according to WJTV. Those are the three compounds named in the Justice Department's August 25, 2026 temporary scheduling announcement.
