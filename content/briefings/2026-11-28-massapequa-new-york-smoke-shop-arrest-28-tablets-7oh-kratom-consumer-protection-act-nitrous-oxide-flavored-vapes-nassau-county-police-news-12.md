[News 12 reported](https://newjersey.news12.com/2026/11/25/seaford-man-accused-of-selling-illegal-products-at-massapequa-smoke-shop-resisting-arrest/384lxB4W6YBASob8p8J26F) on November 25, 2026 that Nassau County police charged a 36-year-old Seaford man under New York's Kratom Consumer Protection Act after officers found 28 tablets that police said contain 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) at a Massapequa smoke shop. The report does not say how much 7-OH the tablets contained, and it does not specify whether they were a concentrated or synthetic 7-OH preparation or natural leaf kratom. For context, the authors of a [November 26, 2026 report](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7546a2.htm) published by the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention wrote that kratom leaf contains mostly mitragynine and only trace amounts of 7-OH.

## What police reported

According to police, as reported by News 12, officers who investigated Laxmie Smoke Shop on Merrick Road found about 123 flavored vape products, 18 large nitrous oxide tanks, and the 28 tablets.

News 12 reported that police arrested Rohit Sobti and that, according to police, he resisted by running toward a rear exit and pulling his arms toward his body to keep officers from handcuffing him. According to the station, Sobti was charged with violations of the Kratom Consumer Protection Act and the Nitrous Oxide Consumer Protection Act, and with resisting arrest. News 12 reported that he received an appearance ticket and is scheduled to appear in court on December 10, 2026.

The station did not report which provision of the Kratom Consumer Protection Act the charge cites, and it did not include comment from Sobti, a lawyer for him, or the store.

## The new state 7-OH law

Kratom News Today (KNT) [reported on November 24, 2026][hochul-signs-new-york-law-s8925a-a9156b-banning-sales-concentrated-synthetic-7oh-products-2-percent-total-alkaloids-1-mg-serving-90-days] that Gov. Kathy Hochul signed S8925A/A9156B on November 23, 2026. According to a [release from the governor's office](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-signs-legislation-protect-new-yorkers-against-harmful-effects-concentrated-and), S8925A/A9156B prohibits the sale of products containing 7-OH above two percent of total alkaloids or one milligram per serving. The Albany Times Union reported that industry groups including the American Kratom Association had urged lawmakers to ban only the synthesized, concentrated versions of 7-OH and to leave natural leaf kratom on sale.
