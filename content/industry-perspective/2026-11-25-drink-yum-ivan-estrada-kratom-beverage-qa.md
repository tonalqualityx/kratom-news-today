Ivan Estrada handles online distribution for [Drink Yum](https://drinkyum.com), a Miami company that makes a flavored kratom extract shot. His partner Gunther created the product and started its production. Drink Yum sells 30 ml and 14 ml bottles in two flavors, Bubble Gum and Tropical Breeze. The company's site lists 255 mg of mitragynine per 30 ml bottle and 7-OH below 0.33 mg per serving, under the lab's limit of quantitation, and says every batch is third-party tested. Ivan said the shots are on smoke shop shelves in three western states. The website ships to most of the country, but not to ten states.

We talked by video on November 13. Ivan opened by saying where he stands: "I'm a longtime kratom user. I'm also not a sick fan. I don't think it's for everyone."

## How Drink Yum started

**Is Drink Yum entirely your baby, or do you have partners?**

I have a partner who launched it. He created the product, he's the one who started the production of it, and he brought me on as a salesperson, a distributor. I'm the one who handles the online distribution. He was a close friend of mine. He started off as a marketing client, and then we hung out a few times and I took him to a few kava bars. He had tried kratom a long time ago, but after I brought him to this kava bar and he drank two cups of kratom, he said, "I want to go all in on this." He's a very driven guy, and once he gets his head wrapped around something, he goes all in. So he produced what I would say is a big company.

## Why the kratom beverage starts with flavor

**You sell a shot instead of powder or capsules. What's the driver behind that?**

The whole concept of Drink Yum was always about the flavor. My partner's name is Gunther, and his biggest issue with kratom was that you taste it and it tastes earthy. It tastes like leaf. For a first-time user it was too complicated. You want to get the feeling of it, but it tastes terrible. With leaf, it's too difficult to get the taste right. The only way to get the taste right was with extract.

**The site pitches it as "your unstoppable moment." Who is it for?**

I don't want to put words in Gunther's mouth. The idea was, you're a mom, you've been changing diapers for three or four hours, and you want to have fun with your kids. You can't drink three margaritas and get wasted, because you're still a responsible person. Or you want to go out, but you don't want to get wasted, and at the same time you don't want to be the sober guy. Why not take a shot of kratom?

**How did you land on the flavors?**

A lot of these questions are really for Gunther. I tried to get him on, but it was so last minute. When he decided this is what he wanted to go in on, he said, I want to produce, but I want it to taste good. So he went to the top flavorist in the country, paid him a whole bunch of money and said, "Hey, make this taste good." The guy came back and said, "I could get you two flavors immediately that I promise you taste good." When it was time to test them and see which ones were good, the two that stuck out were bubblegum and tropical.

There's a bit of an issue with bubblegum. Kratom is so closely associated with smoke shops that it gets tangled up with stuff like vapes, where you can't market to underage people, and bubblegum gives that outlook from the outside looking in. So we're trying to find other things. There are a few more flavors in the works that I don't want to release quite yet.

**Can we find out what's coming?**

I could give you a clue. It's very traditional American. That's all I'm saying. It tastes like freedom.

## How Drink Yum keeps 7-OH low

**When you're testing, what numbers and targets are you using?**

When we were doing the math on this stuff, we wanted the least amount of 7-OH possible. That's not what we want to do. Plus there's so much conflict going on with 7-OH, and frankly it's not the type of product we want to sell. Not that I have anything against 7-OH, as long as it's not hyper-concentrated into 700x, where it's going to get banned as a whole. Our whole thought was that's not the avenue we want to go down. We wanted to go in on mitragynine, and extract is what allowed us to play around with the balance of things. The whole idea is to have as few additives as possible and go all in on the mitragynine.

**Are there techniques that keep the 7-OH low, or is it a variable you're always fighting?**

We have to lab test every batch. In terms of mechanical techniques to remove it, I can't tell you. I'm not in the batch-making process. But one thing for sure is we always try to get it below the level of quantification. We want it as little as possible on the lab reports, and we're testing each batch, each barrel.

**So every barrel gets a lab report. What happens if one comes back over?**

We could rework it. We've had situations in the past where a batch had too much mitragynine, not even 7-OH, and we tried to water it down. That was the last time we tried to do something without knowing, because we messed up a lot of stock. It became super foamy. We distributed a bunch of those bottles, and people would open them and they would fizz out. It becomes very sophisticated and very complicated to work the chemical itself. I can't tell you how we go about removing the 7-OH, because it's not my area of expertise. But in every batch, we always try to reach below the level of quantification.

**It sounds like a mitragynine-forward model: as much as you can get in without breaking the limits you've set for yourselves.**

Right. Even though mitragynine is the primary active ingredient we want to increase, we never wanted to go above the taste. The taste is the primary thing we're going after. Imagine you go to a kava bar and you're trying to invite your friends, and they've never heard of the concept. They're like, "What is it, a bar people go to with their laptops and work at?" You offer them a drink and it tastes terrible, and they say, "Look, I'd rather get a Coke and whiskey." The idea was, if we could at least ease that entry into trying it. The taste was always the thing we wanted to push.

## How big is a serving?

**Somebody could down a whole bunch of these. How do you approach serving size?**

We tell people to pour up a cap and mix it. I personally think it has a really good taste, particularly the bubblegum on its own, but if you mix a capful into a big cup of water, you won't even taste the bubblegum. A cap is going to be the normie, standard dose.

**And if somebody takes far more than that?**

I don't want to make any sort of medical claims. Generally we say don't drink the whole 30 ml bottle. It tastes really good and there's very little 7-OH, but kratom is kratom.

## Kratom and alcohol

**I've been straight edge my whole life. How do you think about kratom next to the other things people use to relax?**

From a societal basis, we've normalized all these concepts. People drink coffee every morning, they smoke nicotine, they drink alcohol at night. We have all these things normalized. But kratom gets super targeted, for whatever reason.

## How Ivan would regulate kratom

**You can't buy kratom in Vermont (where I live), and I don't even know who to talk to about changing that.**

Probably the American Kratom Association. They're pretty up to date on that stuff. The legality of a lot of it is the competing industries. They don't want it out. I'm not sure how conspiratorial that is. I do know that when kratom first got banned in Thailand, in the 1940s or something like that, it was because it was getting in the way of opium taxes. They said, this is competing in our market space, and that was the first legal ban of kratom.

It's the same thing with Uber and taxis, with Airbnb and hotels. I think it's okay to set up some regulation, like no, you can't hyper-concentrate it and sell it to six-year-olds. There can be a parameter where this is okay and this isn't. It's fine to regulate it, but to just outright ban it and take the most specialized, hyper-concentrated form and attribute it to the whole thing, it's like saying poppy seed bagels are illegal. And I know I keep using alcohol as an example, but it's on every street corner. There's a whole cultural attitude of, hey buddy, when you're underage, get some guy to buy it for you. I don't see any politicians talking about permanently banning alcohol.

**We tried that once, and it didn't go well.**

Exactly.

## Where Ivan stands on synthetic 7-OH

**There's synthetic 7-OH, which as I understand it is different from what happens on the natural side. Do you have a stance on it?**

I'm not familiar with it, frankly. I see something like synthetic 7-OH and, to me, it looks a little scary. Ours isn't synthetic. Ours is natural leaf derived.

If you do synthetic 7-OH, you're asking to have a two or three year long business. At one point you're going to get sued out of existence, even just from an economic standpoint. I have a few contacts in smoke shops, and I know the people selling it are figuratively making a killing. But what they're capitalizing on is people addicted to drugs on the street. That's not the type of business model I see myself in.

**What's one thing you'd change about how the industry regulates itself?**

In terms of synthetic versus natural, I don't want to pretend I know more than I do. If it turns out that it's basically the same, like lab-grown diamonds versus natural diamonds, the same molecular structure, I wouldn't have any problem with it. But let's test it out and find out. I think lab reports should be something that's a little bit more common.

## Where Drink Yum is sold

**Where can people get Drink Yum? Gas stations?**

Maybe at one point we could get in touch with some of the gas stations and replace some of the ones that aren't that great. The typical thing I hear when people talk bad about it is "gas station crack" or "green crack." Maybe we aim to take away that reputation and make it an old moniker that's not really used anymore. Right now we're in smoke shops in Nevada, Arizona and California. If you want to try it and your state allows it, you go to drinkyum.com. There's a free sample on the site. All you have to do is cover the shipping, because we can't go out of business having you try the product, and you get a free 14 ml bottle.

**You also run affiliate relationships. How does that work?**

We're in a couple of affiliate networks. We didn't get all the systems working right away, but the idea is if you sell our product, we'll give you a certain percent of commission. We go on an individual basis depending on who we're talking to. If you've got a website that has a cajillion visitors and you want to put it on the front banner, we'd probably give you a higher percentage. If you're interested in something like that, send me an email at ivan@drinkyum.com.

## Drink Yum in kava bars

**Where do you see Drink Yum in two years?**

The idea would be to get installed into kava bars and have it as some sort of entry drink. We'd want to be the higher-end quality and taste, basically the top shelf Jack and Coke of extract. Be the high quality, high taste example of what good kratom is, have a positive effect on the industry as a whole, and ideally overcome some of the negative stereotypes people put on it.

## Who Ivan respects in the kratom industry

**Is there anyone in the space you highly respect, or anyone you're nervous about?**

The concentrated 7-OH people, I think we can all look at that objectively and say that's a negative force on everyone. That's not helping anyone. On the other side, there are these cans called Nubrew. This guy is doing a tremendous job at marketing. He wants to normalize, not the daily consumption, but the normal consumption, where you go to have a picnic and bring a couple of Nubrews. He's getting the word out there and getting on big podcasts, and I think that's pretty cool.
