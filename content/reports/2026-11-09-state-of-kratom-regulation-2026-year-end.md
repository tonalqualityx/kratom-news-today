Five months after the midyear edition, kratom regulation in the United States has split along the line federal agencies drew this autumn. The DEA placed three potent kratom-derived compounds, mitragynine pseudoindoxyl (MGPI), MGM-15 and MGM-16, in temporary Schedule I on August 26, [according to the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17429/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-and). On October 14 it published notice of a second order that would schedule 7-hydroxymitragynine (7-OH) and MGPI above a defined threshold, an order the agency could issue on or after November 13. Natural leaf kratom remains federally unscheduled.

The states moved faster and in more directions. Tennessee's ban took effect July 1. Massachusetts placed all kratom in Schedule I by emergency order in August. North Dakota barred all sales until its new licensing rules are written. Wyoming and Virginia put regulated markets into force and Nebraska and West Virginia tightened theirs, while Louisiana, New Jersey and Missouri copied the federal listing of the synthetic compounds into state law. Below the states, dozens of cities and counties passed their own bans.

This report maps the landscape as it stands on November 9, 2026, with a status for all 50 states and the District of Columbia.

## Key Numbers at Year-End

- **8 states** ban kratom by statute or permanent rule: Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Indiana, Louisiana, Tennessee, Vermont and Wisconsin.
- **3 more** stop sales by other means: Massachusetts by emergency order, North Dakota until its licensing rules are adopted, and California through food-safety enforcement without a kratom statute.
- **18 states** run a regulated market for natural leaf, most with a 21-and-older age limit and a cap on 7-OH.
- **6 jurisdictions** restrict 7-OH or related synthetic compounds without regulating leaf sales: the District of Columbia, Florida, Kansas, Missouri, New Jersey and Ohio.
- **16 states** have only narrow rules or no statewide kratom law. In several of them, cities and counties have filled the gap.
- **1 mg per gram** (or 1 mg per milliliter for liquids, or a mitragynine ratio below 100 to 1) is the threshold above which 7-OH and MGPI would become Schedule I under the pending federal notice, [according to DEA](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine).
- **30,346 public comments** had posted on HHS's 7-OH threshold docket by September 12, by Kratom News Today's reading of Regulations.gov. Pain News Network reported nearly 36,000 by September 16.
- **Nearly 40** Mississippi cities and counties ban kratom outright, according to the Mississippi State Medical Association.

## The Federal Picture: One Order in Force, One Pending

At midyear, the federal story was an FDA recommendation. By November it had become two DEA actions, a reversed threshold and an open political fight.

**The order in force.** DEA's temporary order placing MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 in Schedule I took effect August 26 and runs until August 26, 2028, [per the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/26/2026-17429/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-and). The Justice Department described it as "directed at deliberately manufactured and concentrated opioid products, not traditional botanical kratom," and said it would exercise enforcement discretion where only incidental trace amounts of MGPI appear in a product otherwise consistent with botanical kratom, [according to its August 25 release](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/justice-department-announces-emergency-scheduling-three-potent-opioid-compounds). The department added that this policy "does not create a legal exemption." The American Kratom Association applauded the order and then [filed a federal suit][dea-finalizes-schedule-one-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds] warning that the MGPI listing could sweep in natural leaf unless clarified, according to Bloomberg Law. Kratom News Today later reported the litigation as resolved; no dismissal record has been located.

**The threshold that changed.** DEA first published a notice of intent on July 6 to temporarily schedule 7-OH above a lower threshold, [reported at the time][federal-register-publishes-dea-7oh-scheduling-rule] as 0.05 percent by dry weight. HHS took public comment on the threshold through September 10. On October 8, HHS recommended a new standard, and on October 14 DEA published two new notices and said it was withdrawing the July one, [according to the Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/14/2026-20943/schedules-of-controlled-substances-temporary-placement-of-7--hydroxymitragynine-and-mitragynine). HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said the agency was "drawing a clear line between natural kratom and dangerous, enhanced opioid products," [according to the HHS release as reported by Kratom News Today][hhs-dea-new-7oh-mgpi-threshold-1-mg-per-gram-100-to-1-mitragynine-ratio-withdraw-july-notice-kennedy-christine-blanche-aka-litigation-resolved-federal-register-october-14].

**When an order could come.** Under the Controlled Substances Act's temporary scheduling procedure, DEA cannot issue the order until 30 days after the notice, which puts the earliest date on or after November 13, [as Kratom News Today reported][dea-7oh-mgpi-notices-published-federal-register-october-14-91-fr-65370-dea-1570-mgm-15-mgm-16-dea-1644-earliest-order-november-13-harris-lessen-up-regulation-crazy-aka-rayburn-briefing-senators-october-15-deadline]. DEA has not said when it will act. A temporary order lasts two years and can be extended by one, per the notice. The August order on MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 stays in effect until it expires or a new order replaces it.

## The Threshold, in DEA's Words

The October 14 notice quotes HHS's recommendation directly. 7-OH and MGPI would be controlled when either substance alone, or their combined amount, is:

> "(A) Above a specified threshold concentration, that is, greater than 1 mg per gram (for solid powders, encapsulated material, or other solid forms) or greater than 1 mg per milliliter (for liquid preparations), OR (B) The mass of mitragynine present divided by the mass of [7-hydroxymitragynine], or [mitragynine pseudoindoxyl], or the combined amount of [7-hydroxymitragynine] and [mitragynine pseudoindoxyl] if both present, is less than 100."

The same notice adds a caution: "neither HHS nor DEA has made a finding that these substances are safe to use at below-threshold levels." It also describes 7-OH in botanical form as making up less than 2 percent of total alkaloid content or occurring in trace amounts. By Kratom News Today's arithmetic, 1 mg per gram is 0.1 percent by weight, double the July figure.

## The Fight Over the Threshold

The threshold drew criticism from two directions, and the disagreement is the story of the autumn.

Senators Chuck Grassley, Mike Lee and Susan Collins wrote to DEA and FDA on October 7 asking that the final 7-OH order "be expeditiously promulgated" and that DEA consider scheduling "the entire kratom plant," according to Senator Grassley's office and [Kratom News Today's coverage][senators-grassley-lee-collins-letter-dea-cole-fda-diamantas-expeditiously-finalize-7oh-emergency-order-permanent-schedule-i-entire-kratom-plant-october-15-reply]. Representative Andy Harris of Maryland told American Family News, "They're going to lessen up the regulation on that. This is crazy." No public reply from DEA or FDA to the senators had surfaced by November 9.

The New York Times reported on October 28 that the administration [backed away from a planned full MGPI ban after industry lobbying][new-york-times-trump-administration-backs-down-mgpi-ban-kratom-industry-lobbying-gabbay-2-9-million-haddow-white-house-chittiboyina-25-to-500-times-kolodny-56-overdose-cases-48-fatal]. The Times quoted University of Mississippi researcher Amar Chittiboyina calling the HHS threshold "too permissive" and cited DEA data identifying MGPI in at least 56 overdose cases, 48 of them fatal, between February 2025 and May 2026. HHS spokeswoman Emily Hilliard told the Times that "neither litigation nor political contributions influenced H.H.S.' recommendation," and White House spokeswoman Allison Schuster said any suggestion otherwise "is completely false." The American Kratom Association [demanded a correction or retraction][aka-haddow-demands-new-york-times-correction-or-retraction-october-28-mgpi-article-sulzberger-combined-7oh-mgpi-threshold-100-to-1-ratio-notice-of-intent-not-final-order], arguing that the Times omitted the combined threshold and the separate ratio test and that the action is a notice, not a final order. Both of those points match the Federal Register text.

The American Kratom Association does not oppose scheduling the synthetics. Its senior fellow Mac Haddow told Gizmodo the group is "absolutely supportive of banning or scheduling these synthetics," [Kratom News Today reported][aka-haddow-gizmodo-absolutely-supportive-dea-scheduling-synthetic-7oh-amnesty-consumers-congress-preemptive-derivative-ban-mccabe-michigan-ban-high-addiction-potential-tax-natural-leaf-salmon-state-press-crs-7oh-not-controlled], while asking for consumer amnesty and a congressional standard that would ban future derivatives. The New York Post [reported in October][new-york-post-kratom-industry-lobbying-aka-1-million-opensecrets-botanic-tonics-ross-1-1-million-kennedy-pacs-rnc-stop-gas-station-heroin-checkmate-750000-steck-haddow-seat-at-the-table] on lobbying spending by the association, by Botanic Tonics founder Jerry Ross and by the Stop Gas Station Heroin campaign. Haddow said the spending "is to get a seat at the table."

Congress has two bills pending. Senator Bernie Moreno's S. 5383 would [permanently schedule MGM-15, MGM-16 and synthetic 7-OH and MGPI above a threshold][moreno-bill-permanent-scheduling-synthetic-7oh], and Representative Lance Gooden's companion, H.R. 10272, [drew the American Kratom Association's endorsement][gooden-house-bill-hr-10272-connors-end-gas-station-heroin-act-aka-endorsement] in September. Senator Ron Wyden [sent letters in August][wyden-inquiry-kennedy-kratom-vape-policy] to Secretary Kennedy and to the chief executives of Botanic Tonics and Reynolds American, according to STAT.

The American Medical Association [adopted Resolution 201 in June][ama-adopts-policy-targeting-synthetic-7oh-kratom], calling for legislation banning synthesized, purified or derivative kratom products. On October 28 it [wrote to FDA][american-medical-association-letter-fda-botanical-drug-rfi-docket-fda-2026-n-9550-whyte-underwood-real-world-guinea-pigs-kratom-cannabis-kava-kanna-commends-dea-emergency-scheduling-mgpi-mgm-15-mgm-16] urging safety standards for botanical products. AMA President Willie Underwood III said consumers "are becoming the real-world guinea pigs," [according to the AMA](https://www.ama-assn.org/press-center/ama-press-releases/ama-urges-fda-pursue-safety-standards-botanical-products). On the research side, Washington State University announced a [$7.5 million, five-year NIH grant][washington-state-university-7-5-million-nih-grant-kratom-chronic-pain-sleep-mary-paine-mouse-studies-arizona-human-sleep-study-low-back-pain-authenticated-kratom-unc-greensboro] on October 26 to study kratom, chronic pain and sleep, [per the university](https://research.wsu.edu/news/wsu-awarded-7-5-million-grant-to-study-kratom-chronic-pain-and-sleep).

## The Ban States: Eight by Law, Three More by Other Means

Tennessee became the eighth statutory ban state when Matthew Davenport's Law [took effect July 1][tennessee-kansas-bans-take-effect-july-one]. It makes possession a Class A misdemeanor and sale or manufacture a felony, and its definition covers the plant, extracts and synthetic alkaloids, [per the General Assembly](https://wapp.capitol.tn.gov/apps/BillInfo/Default.aspx?BillNumber=HB1649&GA=114). The other seven are Alabama, Arkansas, Connecticut, Indiana, Louisiana, Vermont and Wisconsin. Louisiana added MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 to its Schedule I by [emergency rule on October 19][louisiana-department-of-health-emergency-rule-schedule-i-mitragynine-pseudoindoxyl-mgm-15-mgm-16-lac-46-liii-2704-greenstein-imminent-peril-act-41-kratom-already-illegal-since-august-2025].

Three states reached the same practical result without a permanent ban:

- **Massachusetts.** Public Health Commissioner Robbie Goldstein [placed all forms of kratom in Schedule I][massachusetts-emergency-order-all-kratom-schedule-one] by emergency regulation effective August 28, for up to one year. The order covers natural leaf as well as concentrated and synthetic products. Four retailers [sued to block it][massachusetts-retailers-sue-emergency-kratom-ban]; no ruling had been reported by November 9.
- **North Dakota.** Governor Kelly Armstrong [signed HB 1628 on September 4][north-dakota-armstrong-signs-hb-1628-synthetic-kratom-ban-house-rejects-total-ban], banning synthetic derivatives and creating licensed natural-leaf sales for adults 21 and older. All sales stay prohibited until the attorney general adopts licensing rules, [per the governor's office](https://www.governor.nd.gov/news/updated-armstrong-signs-bill-banning-synthetic-kratom-restricting-natural-kratom-21-and-older). The attorney general's office has estimated about six months, and lawmakers have said the rules [could take until about March 2027][north-dakota-attorney-general-wrigley-hb-1628-kratom-rules-six-months-sales-banned-until-regulations]. The House rejected a total ban 46-47.
- **California.** The state has no kratom statute. Its Department of Public Health treats kratom and 7-OH products for consumption as illegal under existing food law. Governor Gavin Newsom's office said on October 31 that the department had [embargoed or destroyed about $10 million in kratom and 7-OH products][california-newsom-halloween-release-cdph-embargoed-destroyed-10-million-kratom-7oh-products-legal-action-manufacturers-abc-9209-visits-8230-products-removed-97-97-percent-compliance-erica-pan-mgm-15] and reported 97.97 percent compliance across 9,209 retailer visits. The releases do not separate leaf from concentrated products.

## The Regulated Middle

Eighteen states regulate natural leaf through a consumer protection act, licensing or registration. Several of those frameworks took effect or tightened this summer:

- **Wyoming.** SF0056 took effect July 1, [per the enrolled act](https://wyoleg.gov/2026/Enroll/SF0056.pdf): age 21, a 7-OH cap of 2 percent of alkaloid composition, and a ban on candy-like, combustible or vaporizable products. Retailers [cleared 7-OH products from shelves][wyoming-stores-clear-7oh-products-new-state-law] ahead of the deadline.
- **Virginia.** HB 360 [took effect July 1][virginia-kratom-restrictions-take-effect-july-one], barring sales to anyone under 21, requiring products to be kept behind the counter or locked, and banning products that contain 7-OH. The attorney general [launched a Regulated Products Enforcement Unit][virginia-ag-regulated-products-enforcement-unit] on July 30.
- **Nebraska.** Amendments to its Kratom Consumer Protection Act became operative July 18, adding product registration with the Department of Revenue and a ban on synthesized alkaloids, [per the Nebraska Legislature](https://nebraskalegislature.gov/laws/display_html.php?begin_section=71-3801&end_section=71-3815).
- **West Virginia.** SB 985, effective June 12, requires online sellers to verify buyers are 21 or older and sets felony penalties for contaminated product, [per the enrolled bill](https://www.wvlegislature.gov/Bill_Text_HTML/2026_SESSIONS/RS/bills/sb985%20sub1%20enr.htm).
- **Maryland.** New laws effective July 1 gave the Alcohol, Tobacco, and Cannabis Commission seizure and enforcement powers over unauthorized consumable products, including products with more than 1 mg of 7-OH per serving, [per the 2026 chapter law](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/2026rs/Chapters_noln/CH_354_hb1523e.pdf).
- **Utah.** The pure-leaf-only law in force since May 6 now stands without a court challenge. Botanic Tonics and the Global Kratom Coalition [dismissed their federal suit and Tenth Circuit appeal][botanic-tonics-global-kratom-coalition-utah-attorney-general-brown-dismiss-kratom-regulation-act-lawsuit-tenth-circuit-appeal-udaf-approved-pure-leaf-product-fox-13] in late September. An attorney general's spokesperson said the company dropped the case after the state approved a new pure-leaf product, [Fox 13 reported](https://www.fox13now.com/news/politics/lawsuit-challenging-utahs-ban-on-kratom-products-is-dropped). The sponsor, Senator Mike McKell, has suggested he may pursue a full ban.
- **Rhode Island.** The state, which banned kratom in 2017 and now licenses sales, began reviewing manufacturer, importer and distributor applications, according to a July Boston Globe commentary.

Kentucky sharpened its 7-OH rule by regulation: it lists 7-OH above 400 parts per million in Schedule I while exempting mitragynine, [per its administrative code](https://apps.legislature.ky.gov/law/kar/titles/902/055/015/). In Colorado, the attorney general [reached a $152,000 settlement](https://coag.gov/press-releases/colorado-smoke-shop-owners-to-pay-152000-for-selling-illegal-kratom-products/) with a 15-shop chain accused of selling products over the state's 7-OH limit.

## States That Target the Synthetics Alone

Six jurisdictions restrict 7-OH or related compounds without regulating leaf sales. Most of the 2026 movement came from states copying the federal listing:

- **Florida.** Attorney General James Uthmeier's [emergency rule 2ER26-2][florida-emergency-rule-7oh-schedule-one], effective August 18, places 7-OH in Schedule I above 1 mg per gram or 1 mg per milliliter, or below a 100-to-1 mitragynine ratio, matching the threshold DEA later proposed, [per the Florida Administrative Register](https://flrules.org/gateway/RuleNo.asp?title=Emergency%20Rule%20for%20Year%202026&ID=2ER26-2). Natural leaf is not scheduled, and Florida's statute sets a 21-and-older age limit.
- **Kansas.** HB 2365 places 7-OH in Schedule I and was reported to take effect July 1. The enrolled text does not name mitragynine or kratom leaf, [per the Kansas Legislature](https://www.kslegislature.gov/li/b2025_26/measures/documents/hb2365_enrolled.pdf).
- **Missouri.** The state adopted the federal Schedule I listing of MGPI and MGM-15 on September 25, Attorney General Catherine Hanaway said in [an October 15 release](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-investigates-nine-additional-companies-over-7-oh-products/). Her office takes the position that products with "artificially elevated levels of 7-OH" are Schedule I analogues.
- **New Jersey.** The [attorney general's office said on September 25][new-jersey-attorney-general-davenport-public-alert-three-synthetic-kratom-compounds-controlled-dangerous-substances] that MGPI, MGM-15 and MGM-16 are controlled dangerous substances in the state following the federal order, and that natural kratom remains legal.
- **Ohio.** The Board of Pharmacy rule schedules 7-OH, MGPI and MGM-15 and bans extracts, resins, tinctures and, since August 4, blended products. The board says natural leaf stays legal. A Franklin County judge [declined to block the rule][ohio-judge-declines-block-processed-kratom-ban] in August, and a trial is set for June 2027, Signal Ohio reported.
- **District of Columbia.** 7-OH has been in Schedule I by regulation since 2016. LAPPA describes the status of natural leaf as unclear.

Hawaii may follow. Its Department of Law Enforcement posted a notice on November 5 of emergency scheduling for 7-OH, MGPI and MGM-15, which takes effect after 30 days' notice and lapses unless the 2027 legislature acts, [per the department's notice](https://law.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/7OH-updated-11052026-11052026104242-0001.pdf).

## Narrow Rules or No Statewide Law

Sixteen states have an age limit and little else, or no statewide kratom law. Several are weighing more:

- **Pennsylvania.** The House passed HB 2657 [202-0 on October 5][pennsylvania-house-202-0-hb-2657-kosierowski-kratom-bill-amended-0-05-percent-7oh-dry-weight-no-extracts-12-percent-tax-kratom-regulatory-fund-haddow-restraint-senate-seven-days], a regulation bill that would ban synthetic products, cap natural product 7-OH and set a 21 age limit. It sits in the Senate Health and Human Services Committee.
- **Michigan.** HB 5537, an outright ban covering kratom and synthetic variants, passed the House 56-48 on March 18 and is in a Senate committee. Representative Bryan DeBoyer is [pushing for Senate passage before year-end][michigan-rep-deboyer-hb-5537-kratom-ban-push-senate-passage-before-year-end-passed-house-march-msu-jamie-alan-7oh-fox-17], Fox 17 reported.
- **Iowa.** Governor Kim Reynolds [called for a total ban][iowa-governor-reynolds-calls-total-statewide-kratom-ban] on kratom and synthetic variants on September 1, citing 45 kratom-associated deaths since 2024, [per Iowa HHS](https://hhs.iowa.gov/news-release/2026-09-01/governor-reynolds-state-officials-warn-public-about-rising-dangers-kratom-and-synthetic-7-oh).
- **Delaware and Minnesota.** Both raised the minimum age to 21. Delaware's HB 332, [signed September 8][delaware-governor-signs-hb-332-kratom-age-21], also orders a report on testing and retailer licensing by January 1.
- **New York.** Governor Kathy Hochul signed an under-21 sales limit and a labeling law in December 2025, [per the governor's office](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-signs-two-pieces-landmark-legislation-protect-new-yorkers-against-harmful). A bill banning synthetic kratom passed both chambers in June, according to its Assembly sponsor, and awaited the governor's action as of late October. The Senate website still listed it in committee.
- **New Hampshire.** SB 557, which targeted synthetic and semisynthetic products, failed when the Senate refused to concur with House changes.
- **North Carolina.** HB 328 was re-referred to the House Rules Committee on July 30, and the Speaker said it would get no vote until after the election, WFAE reported.
- **Washington, Idaho and Montana.** None has a statewide law, and all three have cities acting on their own (see the local layer below).

## The Courts

**Arizona v. Botanic Tonics.** Attorney General Kris Mayes [sued the maker of feel free][arizona-attorney-general-mayes-sues-botanic-tonics-feel-free-consumer-fraud-act-first-in-nation] on September 23 under the state's Consumer Fraud Act, alleging the company marketed its shots as energy drinks while obscuring kratom content and addiction risk, [per her office](https://www.azag.gov/press-release/attorney-general-mayes-sues-kratom-maker-botanic-tonics-first-nation-consumer). Her office called it a first-in-the-nation consumer protection suit over a kratom product. It seeks an injunction and restitution and does not seek to remove the product from the market.

**Mitra-9 Brands.** A federal judge in the Southern District of New York [denied Mitra-9's motion to dismiss][mitra-9-brands-southern-district-new-york-swain-denies-motion-dismiss-addiction-false-advertising-suit-bursor-fisher-vasquez-adexchanger-transparency] a proposed consumer class action alleging failure to warn about addictiveness, Law360 reported in October. The ruling is procedural and decides no liability.

**Baltimore.** The city's consumer protection suit against sellers of OPMS and ROXY 7-OH tablets was [removed to federal court][baltimore-v-jopen-martian-sales-pur-botanicals-opms-motion-to-dismiss-district-of-maryland-kilpatrick-townsend-city-stepping-into-state-enforcement-shoes-consumer-protection-ordinance] on September 1, and the defendants moved to dismiss.

**Bankruptcy.** Diversified Botanics, the company formerly known as MIT45, filed for Chapter 11 in Delaware on October 7, [per the claims agent's case page](https://cases.stretto.com/DiversifiedBotanics/).

**Wrongful death and insurance.** New suits in California allege deaths from acute mitragynine intoxication; the reporting on one suit describes highly concentrated extracts, and the other does not specify the product type. Separately, an insurer, Kinsale, has asked a federal court to declare it has no duty to defend a kratom seller under a "Kratom, Opioids, and Narcotics" exclusion, according to court records reported by Kratom News Today.

**Local preemption.** Merrimack, New Hampshire, [postponed a vote on a 7-OH sales ordinance][merrimack-new-hampshire-town-council-tables-kratom-7oh-ordinance-november-19-american-kratom-association-law-firm-eric-evans-threatens-declaratory-injunctive-relief-harrington-agraz-union-leader] to November 19 after a law firm representing the American Kratom Association threatened to seek declaratory and injunctive relief, the Union Leader reported. Kratom News Today found no court ruling on whether state law preempts a local kratom ban during the period covered.

## The Enforcement Wave

In Kratom News Today's coverage, most 2026 enforcement has targeted concentrated 7-OH products and mislabeled tablets sold as "cat's claw," with leaf products swept in where states ban all kratom.

- **Missouri.** Attorney General Hanaway's office has issued [31 civil investigative demands][missouri-attorney-general-hanaway-nine-more-civil-investigative-demands-31-total-cease-and-desist-kc-distro-karmic-genesis-concentrated-7oh-schedule-i-analogue-missouri-adopted-mgpi-mgm-15-september-25-vapor-maven-joplin] and sent cease-and-desist letters to two companies, [per her office](https://ago.mo.gov/attorney-general-hanaway-investigates-nine-additional-companies-over-7-oh-products/). A Kansas City company [destroyed about $10 million in products][kansas-city-kratom-company-destroys-10-million-products] after the federal August order, KCUR and the Missouri Independent reported.
- **Alabama.** Operation Southern Shield has executed 83 search warrants and seized nearly 123,000 doses since May, Governor Kay Ivey's office [said September 30][alabama-ivey-taylor-marshall-kratom-disguised-as-drinks-operation-southern-shield-83-warrants-123000-doses-adfs-mitragynine].

## Methodology

**Scope and window.** This edition covers federal, state and notable local kratom regulatory activity in the United States from June 12 through November 9, 2026, and gives a status for all 50 states and the District of Columbia as of November 9.

**Status categories.** Each jurisdiction gets one category describing what is in force on November 9. *Full ban*: kratom itself is prohibited or scheduled statewide, or all sales are barred by order or pending rules. *Regulated market*: a statewide statute or rule regulates natural leaf sales, whether or not it also caps or bans 7-OH. *7-OH restriction only*: no statewide leaf framework, but concentrated or synthetic 7-OH or related compounds are banned, scheduled or capped. *Limited rules*: only an age limit, labeling rule or similar narrow measure. *No statewide law*: no statewide kratom-specific law, though local bans may exist. Laws enacted but not yet in force are noted in the dataset's pending column. Categories are assigned by the editor. California and North Dakota are counted as full-ban states by effect, as described above.

**Sources.** Each state row carries one primary source, usually a statute, enrolled bill, agency rule or official release. Where a state's own page could not be opened, the row cites the Legislative Analysis and Public Policy Association's January 2026 summary of state laws or established news reporting, and the dataset marks it. Federal facts come from the Federal Register, the Justice Department and agency releases. Where reporting from other outlets is the source, the outlet is named in the text. Some figures here rest on news reports Kratom News Today could not check against a primary document, including the CDC poison-center count and the New York Times reporting on DEA data, and the text attributes them.

**Production and corrections.** Kratom News Today reports are produced through the AI-assisted editorial workflow described in our [editorial policy](/editorial-policy/), with review and final responsibility by the Managing Editor. Errors are corrected per our [corrections policy](/corrections/), and material corrections to this report will be noted on this page.

**Updates.** If DEA issues the 7-OH order or a state changes category before the next edition, the change will be added to this report with a date, and the daily [regulation coverage](/regulation/) will carry the news. The midyear edition remains at its own address: [The State of Kratom Regulation: 2026 Midyear Report](/reports/state-of-kratom-regulation-2026/).
